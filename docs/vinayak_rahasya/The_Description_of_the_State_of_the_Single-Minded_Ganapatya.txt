The Description of the State of the Single-Minded Ganapatya

Narada and the assembled sages, having just learned that uninitiated people must worship subsidiary deities to fulfill their material needs, presented a follow-up question to the Sun God, Surya. They asked, "The Ekanistha Ganapatyas—the exclusive, single-minded devotees of Ganesha—do not perform any rituals or worship to appease the local and subsidiary deities (Lokadevas). If they bypass these cosmic officials, how do they attain the physical nourishment and material comforts necessary to sustain their bodies?"

Surya replied, explaining the immense power of formal spiritual initiation (Diksha). He clarified that those who receive formal initiation into the worship of Ganesha and practice undivided devotion are called Ekanistha. Because their surrender is total and absolute, they become the direct recipients of Ganesha's most intimate love.

Surya expanded on the previous "King and Minister" analogy to explain this dynamic. He categorized the initiated devotees into three tiers based on their spiritual path:
Just as an excellent king holds deep affection for his extended relatives, Gajanana holds deep affection for those initiated into the path of devotion (Bhakti-marga). Just as a king treasures his close friends, Gajanana treasures those initiated into the path of ritual worship (Upasana). And just as a fiercely protective king loves his own sons, Brahmanaspati loves those initiated into the path of direct, absolute knowledge (Aparoksha-jnana).

In a royal court, the ministers and government officials do not demand bribes or separate petitions from the king's own relatives, friends, and sons. Out of respect and fear of the king, the royal officials rush to serve the king's family. When the officials fulfill the desires of the royal family, the king is highly pleased with those officials. However, if an official were to thwart the desires of the king's son or friend, the king would become furious.

In the exact same way, the Karmadevas (the subsidiary deities overseeing material nature and cosmic actions) act as the ministers of the Supreme King, Ganesha. When these deities smoothly provide material nourishment and fulfill the desires of Ganesha's single-minded devotees, Ganesha is deeply pleased with them. But if these cosmic officials dare to withhold material success from an initiated Ganapatya, Gajanana becomes enraged. He violently afflicts those arrogant deities with obstacles and completely strips them of their cosmic authority and power!

Therefore, Surya concluded, the five primary deities and all the other cosmic gods are filled with the awe and fear of Ganesha. Acting strictly as the obeyers of His royal command, they proactively rush to fulfill whatever desires arise in the heart of a formally initiated Ganapatya, without ever being directly worshipped by that devotee. Because the cosmic officials serve them automatically out of fear of the Supreme Lord, an initiated, single-minded devotee has absolutely no need to separately consider, appease, or worship the subsidiary gods.

Description of the Worshippers of Ganesha and Others Residing in Kailasa and Other Worlds

Sanaka and the other sages said:

Some say that Heramba resides in Shivaloka, while others claim that He dwells in the glorious palace of Vaikuntha. Some Vedic scholars say He resides in the abode of Brahma, some say in the solar orb, and others assert He lives in Shaktiloka. Yet you, O Shankara, state that He resides in the beautiful Svananda palace, the supreme realm beyond the universe. Please reveal the absolute truth to us!

Lord Shiva said:

This Ganesha, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the Lord of Prayer, is self-revealing in His state and possesses a self-luminous form. He resides in His own Svananda palace alongside His two Shaktis. His attendants there are celebrated as Brahmapriya and others. They are all composed of His true nature and are deeply absorbed in the bliss of the Self. They are well-established across various worlds like Kailasa. To worship Him, they construct temples there according to the rules, installing Ganesha, who bestows his unique grace upon each of them. They perform His worship with devotion, accompanied by chanting and meditation. I shall now explain that cosmic order to you; listen with rapt attention.

In Shivaloka, Parashiva worships Ekakshara Ganesha, while in the beautiful palace of Kailasa, Sadashiva worships Heramba. On Mount Kailasa situated within Jambu-dvipa, Mahesha, the Lord of Parvati, worships Chintamani Ganesha, who grants all desired boons. In the supreme Manidvipa, Paramambika worships Vallabhesha. Durga worships Durga Ganapati, and Kali worships Chandiganeshvara. In Skandaloka, Mahasena worships Lakshesha Gananayaka, while in Rudraloka, Bhairava worships Shulatanka Ganesha. In his own abode, Virabhadra worships Sahasrasya Ganesha, and Shasta, the son of Hari and Hara, worships Margeshvara Ganesha. Nandikesha and the other celestial attendants worship Gajodbhuta, the son of Shambhu, while Hayagriva and other Shakta seekers worship the form born of Gauri's bodily element.

In Vaikuntha, Lakshmi-Narayana Hari worships Ajna Ganesha, while in Goloka, Shri Vasudeva worships the Ganesha known as Krishna Ganesha. In Shvetadvipa, Mahavishnu worships Shveta Vinayaka. Radhamba worships Gavya Ganesha, and Goddess Lakshmi worships Lakshmi Ganeshvara. Tulasi worships Durva Ganesha, the Earth Goddess worships Bhu Vinayaka, Manmatha worships Uchchhishta Ganesha, and the residents of Vishnuloka worship Gaurigarbhaja. In Brahmaloka, Lord Brahma worships Sarvavighnavinashana. In the city of Manovati, He is worshipped as Siddhinatha, and in Satyaloka, He is worshipped as Satya Vinayaka. Goddess Vani worships Sharada Ganapa, Gayatri worships Chandanatmaka, Savitri worships Haridraka, and the personified sacred Mantras worship Mantraganadhipa. All the residents of Brahmaloka worship Varada Gananayaka, and Lord Surya, positioned in the center of the radiant solar sphere, worships Omkara Ganapa. The goddess of twilight worships Shakti Ganesha, Gayatri worships Him as Bala Vighnapa, Savitri worships Him as Taruna, and Sarasvati worships Him in His elder form. Brahmasurya worships Him as Vikata, Shivasurya worships Him as Vakratunda, and Suryanarayana along with all the Sauras worship Him as Vighnapa.

In Tapoloka, Sanaka and the other primordial Yogis worship Avimukta Ganesha, while Marichi and the other mind-born sons of Brahma worship Jnana Vighnapa. In Janoloka, the Ancestors, conjoined with the offering of Svadha, worship Sumangala Ganesha, while in Maharloka, the sages who live for an entire Kalpa worship Ekadanta Ganesha. In Suvarloka, all the Gods led by Indra worship Sumukh Ganesha. In the eight directions of Mount Mahameru dwell the directional lords. In his own city, Devaraja worships Ratnagarbha Ganesha, while in the city of Tejovati, Agni along with his attendants worships Vahni Ganesha. In Samyamini, Kala worships Chandoddanda Ganesha. In Rakshovati, the Lord of Waters worships the noose-wielding Lord of Ganas, Pashapani Gananatha. In Shitavati, He is worshipped as Dhumravarna; in Gandhavati, Samirana worships Him. In the city of Alaka, the king of Yakshas, Kubera, worships Nidhisha Ganesha. On Mount Kailasa, Rudra Ishana worships Prachanda Gananayaka, while in Bhuvarloka, the lord of the planets, Surya, worships Shvetarka Vighnapa. Nishanatha worships Phalachandra; Kuja worships Sankashtaharaka; Somaputra worships Navanita Ganesha; and Brihaspati worships Santana Ganesha. Manda worships Eka-Abhayaprada, Shukra worships Kshipraprasadana, and the deity established in the center of the Pole Star worships the wish-fulfilling Ashapuraka Ganesha, who is composed of shadow-forms. The Seven Great Sages worship Rakta Ganesha, who is ecstatic and free from afflictions. The King of Oceans and Rivers worships Manikarni Ganeshvara.

In Patala-loka, Kalagnirudra worships Vira Ganeshvara; in Mahatala, Kushmandarudra worships Kushmanda Ganesha. In Talatala, Hatakesha Mahadeva worships Pingalabha Ganesha; in Rasatala, the serpent kings and leaders worship Vira Gananadhipa. In Sutala, the Yogi Kapila worships Kapila Ganesha, who grants all thoughts and desires. In Vitala, the demons worship Durmukha Ganesha, and in Atala, the great elephants of the directions worship Him. The immortals worship Chaturdanta Ganesha, the all-pervading wish-fulfilling tree. The Ajanajas worship Pramoda, and the Karmadevatas worship Amoda. Beyond the Lokaloka mountain range, in the land of light, He is worshipped with perpetual joy. In the region enveloped in absolute darkness, they worship the rolling-eyed Lord, Ghurnitaksha Ganeshvara. In the realm of ghosts, spirits, and ghouls, as well as in Pushkara-dvipa, King Chyavana and the lords of sages worship Gajakarna Ganesha and Chaturvaktra Ganeshvara. In Gomedaka-dvipa, King Adhya worships Gajadanta Ganeshvara. The excellent Siddhas worship Him as Siddharupa, and in Krauncha-dvipa, they worship Him as Sthuladantaka. In Shalmali-dvipa, Jyotishman along with the serpents worships Shanmukha Gananayaka, and Dyuti worships Kukshi Ganesha. In Kusha-dvipa, the Garudas worship Trimukha Ganesha and Kutadanta. In Plaksha-dvipa, Vapushman along with the heroic Guhyakas worships Panchavaktra Ganeshvara. In Shaka-dvipa, King Gomedaka worships Lambodara Vinayaka, while the resident demons and Rakshasas along with their groups worship Dvimukha Ganesha.

Jambu-dvipa is the primary land of action, surrounded by the salt ocean. In its exact center stands Mount Meru, known as the golden mountain. This land is divided into two main zones: North and South. In Kuru-varsha, situated to the north of Mount Meru, King Kuru along with all the human inhabitants worships Jyeshtharaja Ganesha, while the groups of Maruts stationed there worship Vanana Gananayaka. In Hiranyaka-varsha, the king worships Pravala Gananayaka, and the Vidyadharas worship Dantahasta. In Ramyaka-varsha, the delightful residents worship Him as Modaka Ganesha. The Gandharvas residing there worship Ganesha in His dancing form. On the southern flank of Mount Meru, He is worshipped as Kapardi Gananayaka. In Ilavrita-varsha, the ruler along with the Yakshas worships Yakshayaksha Gananadhipa. In Hari-varsha, the Sadhyas worship Simhavaktra and Vehali-bhava. In the region of Kimpurusha-varsha, the king and the groups of gods worship Maha-Ganapati and Mitra Ganesha. In the western region of Ketumala-varsha, the king along with the foremost among humans worships Kalanala-prashamana Ganesha, while the Charanas stationed there worship Chitraghanta Ganesha. In the eastern region of Bhadrasva-varsha, the excellent king worships Dhumraketu, while the Kinnaras stationed there worship Vyomavighneshvara.

In Bharatavarsa, the resident gods worship Dhundhiraja Ganeshvara, while the vast assembly of eighty-eight thousand great sages worships Mayuresha. The mercantile community worships Ballala Ganapa, the Shudras worship Mandara Vighnapa, and those who fall outside the framework of Varna and Ashrama worship Kalipriya Vinayaka. They always worship Him with absolute devotion, preceded by Japa and meditation. Furthermore, within Bharatavarsa, in the sacred territory known as Kumari-khanda, one hundred and eight distinct, powerful forms of the Lord of Prayer have been consecrated with deep devotion, and they are worshipped in their respective locations. For this very reason, the excellent Kumari-khanda is proclaimed as supreme. The land situated directly between the sacred rivers Sarasvati and Drishadvati is Brahmavarta. That specific soil is intensely spiritual, bestowing the perfections of Jnana and Yoga. The territories of Kuru, Panchala, and others are remembered as Rishi-desha, the Land of Sages. The earth there is saturated with absolute wisdom, granting sharp intellect for deep inquiry. The region stretching eastward from Rishi-desha, situated between the Vindhya mountains and the Himalayas, is proclaimed as Aryavarta, recognized universally as a deeply holy land. The earth there is described as the perfect ground for Upasana and Yoga. The central zone lying between the Vindhyas and the Krishna river is declared as Madhya-desha. It is a land charged with the energy of Shakti-yoga, inspiring spontaneous devotion to the gods. The region extending to the south of the Krishna river is the land named Dakshina-desha. The soil there is intensely action-oriented, bestowing the perfection of Karma-yoga. Thus, this geographic territory is described as being endowed with five distinct dimensions of spiritual intelligence and consciousness.

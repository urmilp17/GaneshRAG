The Description of Chintamani-Dvipa

Narada and the others said:

O Surya, kindly tell us where that realm called the Karana-Brahma-loka, known as Karya-Svananda, is situated.

Surya said:

O Vipras, the manifested Brahmanda is composed of the five elements, and the knowers of the Veda say that it is surrounded by eight coverings. Mahat, Avyakta, and Ahankara appear in the three states beginning with Jagrat; associated with identification with the tri-deha, they are remembered as having the nature of the five deities. The glory of Brahman connected with the three states—Jagrat, Svapna, and Susupti—within each Brahmanda is known as Brahma-loka. This is called the Karya-Brahma-loka, not the Karana state; in this way countless Brahmandas exist within the Paramatman.

Within the very body of Gunesha these extend horizontally and vertically; bound by the cords of the gunas, He is remembered as the supporter of Turiya. This glory of Gunesha is especially described in the Vedas, and therefore this realm is proclaimed as the Karana-Brahma-loka.

In the Veda, Gunesha is described as fivefold according to distinctions of Prajna; the first is the Prajna consisting of the Turiya-deha. The Prajna identified with that body is called Kshetrajna, and it is said to be the second, possessing similarity with the first. The third is said to be lower, equal to the deha-jiva condition; the fourth, which presides over the three, is described by the wise. The Prajna formed by the union of the four is remembered as the fifth; endowed with these five forms of Prajna, Gunesha is the sustainer of Turiya.

Because the Veda describes His glory according to five distinctions of Prajna, this realm associated with Him is also proclaimed as fivefold. The Maya consisting of the Turiya-deha is the form of Turiya-Brahman; the consciousness identified with it is called Shakta-Brahman. The Turiya that bears the state of the jiva-bhava is called Saura-Brahman; that which possesses equal nature of both is called Vaishnava-Brahman. The Turiya which is the cause of the three is called Shambhava-Brahman; the form consisting of the union of the four is considered the supreme Ganapa-Brahman. Thus the experts in the Veda and Shastra describe Turiya-Brahman as fivefold according to the distinctions of Prajna.

Ambika, Bhaskara, Vishnu, Mahesha, and Gananayaka—by these names the all-pervading Lord exists in His own state. Within the Karana-Brahma-loka, the glory of Kamalapati is known as the abode called Tripad-Vibhuti-Vaikuntha. Likewise the realm of Parvatipati is known as the divine Vibhuti-Kailasa, and the Sarva-loka-Mani-dvipa is proclaimed as the realm of Para-Shakti. The supreme Surya-loka is declared as the realm of Bhaskara, and the highest form is the glorious manifestation arising from the union of the four. That supreme place is called Chintamani-dvipa by those who are well-versed in the Vedas, situated in the Karana-Brahma-loka, which is of the nature of the Turiya state.

In the middle is Chintamani-dvipa, formed by the union of the four; the realms of Vishnu, Shambhu, Shakti, and Surya exist in the eastern and other directions around it. These stand as gateways corresponding to the states of the Purusharthas; yet Guneshana alone is remembered as existing in five forms. Thus His realm too is described in the Vedas as fivefold, and the supreme Chintamani-dvipa stands established at their center. Situated in the middle of the Ikshu-sagara, it is of the nature of the Turiya-jyotis, existing solely by the will of Gananatha, without any support. That excellent island is adorned with jewels made of Chintamani; therefore that place is called Chintamani-dvipa. It is remembered as the abode of Ganesha, who delights in the play of creation and the other cosmic acts; known as Chintamani-dvipa, it is of the nature of the supreme jyotis.

At its center is the mula-sthana, a palace made of Chintamani, where in the central region Guneshana shines with four arms. Gajanana, three-eyed, bearing the phala-chandra on His forehead, and tawny in complexion, stands there in the midst of the jyotis, accompanied by the two Shaktis. On the left side appears the gaja-akara form representing the samashti-bhava, while on the right side the Vibhu bears the vyashti-bhava. In a nara-akara form He shines as the para-jyotis; in the place of jnana, Ganeshana appears resplendent with ten arms.

In the ajna-sthana He is white in complexion and adorned with ten arms, while elsewhere He appears red in complexion seated upon a divine simhasana. Upon a padmasana, within the filaments and central pericarp of the lotus, the Shaktis beginning with Tivra are established, and upon the seat supported by them sits Guneshvara. There stands Ganesha in a divine form, united with the Shaktis; in the mula-sthana He abides together with Siddhi and Buddhi. With five hands He holds pasha, ankusha, phala, His own danta, and modaka; shining with a golden hue and also accompanied by other colors.

In the place of jnana, in the midst of the para-jyotis, the deity Gajanana shines with white complexion adorned with white garments and ornaments. He bears abhaya-mudra, kalpa-valli, jnana-mudra, an aksha-sutra, a purna-patra, a mudgara, a kambu, and a matulunga fruit. Holding also a kuthara, a chuta-kalika, a pushkara, and a kalasha, with His ten hands He grants vidya and jnana. He is three-eyed, adorned with the chandra-chuda, and beautified with a single tusk; on His two sides shine Sarasvati and Mahalakshmi.

Thus in the ajna-sabha, Gajanana appears red in complexion, holding bijapura, gada, ikshu-chapa, shula, and sudarshana. Holding abja, pasha, utpala, a cluster of rice, and His own danta, He bears these in ten hands and is accompanied by Lakshmi and Vani. The palace of Gunapa is said to possess three divisions, and in each of them separate attendants are described. At the gate of the prasada stand the guardians Badasya and Nagna-Bhairava; on the left side of this palace the abode of Siddhi-Lakshmi is described. On the right side is the place of Maha-Vani and Buddhi-devi; in front are the vahanas, and behind are the ganakas.

Within the four mandapas named Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, Vishnu, Shambhu, Shakti, and Surya stand there always serving. Vighnesha, Vira, Shura, Varada, Ibha-vaktra, Lambodara, Eka-danta, and Kshipra-prasadana are present there. These are said to be the Vainayakas, established in a friendly disposition; outside that place stand the deities of the ten weapons.

With Rama is Rameshana, with Gauri is Girijeshvara, with Rati is Ratinatha, and with Bhumi is Bhumi-Varahaka. With Pushti is Pushti-Vighnesha; then are described Moda accompanied by Riddhi-shakti, and Pramoda accompanied by Samriddhi. Sumukha united with Kanti, Durmukha with Madanavati, Vighna with Mahavira, and Madadravapati the excellent one are present there. Then Vighna-karta together with Dravinisha is described; thereafter are Vasudhara, Vasumati, and the treasures Shankha-nidhi and Padma-nidhi.

There are the great attainments called Sarvajnatva, Sarveshatva, the form of Anadi-bodha, and that which is of the nature of Alupta-shakti. Also the Maha-siddhi called Svatantratva, of the nature of Ananta-shakti; thus these six Siddhis are said to exist outside that place. Then many ganas are described, heroic and of fierce valor; among them are Jaya, Vijaya, Nitya, and Ajita. Also Chandi, Vilasini, Sumangala, and Ananta are known as the shaktis situated outside that region. There are also Ichchha-shakti, Jnana-shakti, and Kriya-shakti, along with Subhaga, Lalita, Kama-malini, and Kamini. Also Sarasvati, Gauri, Shri, Vagishvari, Nalini, Vama, Jyeshtha, and Raudri are present there.

The Vak-siddhis stand there carrying out the command of Gunesha; the weapon-deities beginning with Indra's Vajra also remain there. Thus the description of Chintamani-dvipa is remembered in summary.

Thus ends the Description of Chintamani-Dvipa.

The Description of the Palace of Swananda

Sanaka and the other sages said:

O Lord, the Vedas specifically describe the realm of Brahmaloka. Where exactly is the Svananda-bhavana located? Please tell us, O Master!

Ganaka said:

All the worlds up to Satyaloka are remembered as being composed of the five gross elements. They correspond to the waking state of consciousness and are rooted in the state of ego. The realm of the cosmic intellect corresponds to the dream state, and the unmanifest corresponds to the state of deep sleep. The Brahmaloka is remembered as comprising these three states. This is celebrated as the Empirical or Effect-Brahmaloka. In the Vedas, the excellent state known as the Purusha is designated as the Fourth State. The knowers of the Vedas understand this Fourth State to be the Causal Brahmaloka. The supreme abodes of the five Panchayatana deities are located here.

However, the supreme state that is completely devoid of both Cause and Effect is called the Direct, Absolute Brahmaloka, designated as the Fifth. That exactly is celebrated as the Svananda-bhavana. The realm of Svananda-bhavana is remembered as a concentrated mass of infinite light. It shines with the brilliance of tens of millions of suns, yet it is as cooling as tens of millions of moons. Without the direct grace of the Lord of Prayer, Brahmanaspati, it remains utterly inaccessible even to Shiva, the other gods, and the greatest Yogis. This excellent Svananda-bhavana is situated in the very center of the Ocean of Sugarcane Juice.

It is established far above the realms of countless millions of universes. Even the Causal Brahmaloka sits below it; this realm is remembered as the highest of all. The power named Bhramari represents the word Tvam. Above her is the Adhara-Shakti representing the word Tat. Above her is the wish-fulfilling Para-Shakti representing the word Asi. Upon the crown of this Para-Shakti, the Svananda-bhavana is established. It is circular in shape and expands across ten crore yojanas. This supreme place is declared to be made of Mahamaya in the form of the Absolute Point. It is exquisitely constructed entirely of wish-fulfilling Chintamani gemstones.

In the exact center of this personal realm lies the capital city of Brahmanaspati. In the very middle of that city is the celebrated core sanctuary of Ganesha. There, upon a throne made of the Absolute Point, He who is the complete embodiment of the Omkara sits as the Supreme Light, shining as the universal witness. The absolute, attributeless Supreme Brahman, in the form of immaculate, undivided light, sits brilliantly upon a throne made of the void-Omkara. In the middle of both, completely free from the division of duality, the self-luminous Brahman known as Svananda shines in His true essence upon a throne made of the single imperishable syllable. This throne is expansive, measuring a hundred yojanas in height and width.

The masters of Yoga have described its luminous dimensions. The thrones of the other two are said to be exactly half this size. Just as light is inherent in fire, Siddhi and Buddhi reside inherently within Him. In their first enclosure, there are eight specific stations. The eight excellent Vighneshvaras, beginning with Vakratunda, are stationed sequentially in these eight places, corresponding to the eight states starting with the Bindu.

The excellent pavilion of Vakratunda is declared as the Place of Play. In a jeweled pavilion built of the finest Chintamani gems, Gajanana sits comfortably on a jeweled throne with Siddhi and Buddhi. He is adorned with a crescent moon on His crest and possesses a magnificent trunk. He is red-complexioned, three-eyed, and adorned with a single tusk. His four beautiful arms gracefully hold a noose, a goad, His broken tusk, and the gesture of fearlessness. He has a large belly, a conch-like neck, wears red garments and a yellow upper cloth, and shines with all divine ornaments. To delight Siddhi and Buddhi, Vakratunda, the lord of the Bindu, creates infinite universes by His mere glance.

Through His divine play, He establishes the five deities in those universes. Making those five deities perform the five cosmic acts, He Himself remains the detached universal witness, rejoicing with His two Shaktis. Stationed there, He performs many such diverse cosmic plays. Therefore, O lords of sages, this place is called the Place of Play. Next is the incomparable, wondrous place remembered as the pavilion of Ekadanta.

There, in a Chintamani house, Gajanana sits comfortably with Siddhi and Buddhi, radiant with all ornaments. For the pleasure of Siddhi and Buddhi, He playfully reverses cosmic roles in the universes: making Rudra into the lotus-born Brahma, and Brahma into Sadashiva, making Vishnu into the Sun, and the Sun into the Lord of Lakshmi; making the Sun into the Moon, and the Moon into the Sun; making a Chandala into a Brahmin, and a Brahmin into an outcaste; making ice take the nature of fire, and fire take the nature of ice; turning an atom into Mount Meru, and Mount Meru into an atom; making a pauper a king, and a king a pauper; turning Brahma into a pillar, and a pillar into Brahma. Performing such astonishing reversals, He rejoices with Siddhi and Buddhi.

The pavilion of Mahodara is remembered as the Place of Song and Wisdom. Mounted on a divine throne with Siddhi and Buddhi, the personified Rigveda joyfully praises the Lord's qualities using the Brahmanaspati Suktas and Ganesha Sukta. The Yajurveda praises Lord Ganesha with peace chants and the Ganesha Sukta. The Samaveda, accompanied by the Gandharva-veda, respectfully sings to the Lord using the eight-name praise and the Ganesha Sukta. The Atharvaveda praises the Lord of Obstacles with Atharvan mantras, while the personified Upanishad-Purusha, the awakener of supreme knowledge, praises Him with hundreds of texts like the Atharvashirsha. The personified Purusha of the Epics and Puranas, holding a divine form, joyfully praises Him using the Brahmanda, Brahma, Ganesha, Jnana, Maricha, and Mudgala Puranas, and the Brahmajnana-sukhodaya. He praises the Leader of Ganas with the eight Gitas. The truth-seeing Mimamsa praises Gajanana systematically through the philosophy of pure Ganapatya non-dualism. Hearing all this, Ganesha rejoices with His two Shaktis.

The pavilion of Lambodara is remembered as the incomparable Place of Enjoyment. Seated comfortably on a jeweled couch with Siddhi and Buddhi, the Lord takes various delicacies brought by Anima and the other Siddhis with His trunk and lovingly feeds them to Siddhi and Buddhi. The Lord joyfully eats what is offered by them. He gives them betel leaves and happily accepts what they offer. He embraces the Supreme Mother Mahasiddhi with His left arm and Mahabuddhi with His right arm, exulting in bliss.

The pavilion of Vikata is called Ganesha's Place of Intimacy. In the center of a thousand-petaled lotus, resting on a divine bed on its pericarp with Siddhi and Buddhi, the Lord joyfully drinks the nectar of their lips. Though completely free from Maya and delusion, the joyful Gajanana engages in various amorous expressions with Mahasiddhi and Mahabuddhi. Next, the pavilion of Vighnaraja is declared to be the Lord's Place of Rest. Resting on a soft, lovely bed in the center of the pericarp of a thousand-petaled lotus in the middle of the Sugarcane Ocean, the Leader of Obstacles enters into Yoga-nidra while Siddhi and Buddhi joyfully massage His feet.

The pavilion of Dhumravarna is declared as the Supreme Self's Place of Dance. In a jeweled pavilion constructed with a thousand jeweled pillars, the Leader of Ganas places Siddhi and Buddhi on a throne. During the time of cosmic dissolution, with His left foot bent, Ganapa dances joyfully in the sheer bliss of His own Self. Gunesh and Ganaka joyfully play the Mridanga drum and the cymbals, while the four Vedas sing, overflowing with joy. Bending His right foot during the dissolution of nature, the Lord dances joyfully with His foot. During the ultimate unmanifest dissolution, the Lord performs the formidable Urdhva-Tandava dance. Goddess Siddhi provides the rhythmic handclapping and singing, while Supreme Mother Mahabuddhi plays the Veena. Brahmapriya and the other attendants stand watching, filled with absolute bliss. The excellent pavilion of Gajanana is declared to be the Place of Command.

In a gem-built pavilion with a thousand pillars, on a beautiful divine throne supported by four legs, resting on an eight-petaled lotus upheld by the powers named Tivra and others, He sits comfortably on the pericarp with Siddhi and Buddhi. He wears divine garments, is adorned with all ornaments, and wears a divine signet ring bearing the royal seal of Jyeshtharaja. His bodyguards hold white umbrellas and chamara fans over Him. On a slightly lower throne, to the front-left of the Supreme Self, sits the unmanifest Gunesh, the Chief Minister, who is famous as the Crown Prince. Well-seated on a divine throne, He presents all petitions to the Lord of Obstacles, reading aloud the commands of Brahmanaspati to the petitioners. Listening to his words, the Lord executes them instantly, for no one other than him has the capability to speak directly to Brahmanaspati.

To the front-right on a divine throne sits Ganaka joyfully. He is the consciousness conditioned by the aggregate of individuals. He, without a doubt, is also a direct manifestation of Gunesh. This Ganaka, the lord of calculators, systematically records in his book all the details of Brahmanaspati's devotees residing across the universes: their specific universe, world, direction, country, village, lineage, stage of life, caste, guru, personal name, father's name, family deity, their previously worshipped deities, and their specific desires. Lord Brahmanaspati joyfully bestows His grace upon those great souls whose names are recorded in Ganaka's book.

Among the army commanders, Ganaka is remembered as the Commander-in-Chief. Suprabhuta and the others are celebrated as the ministers. Beside Avyakta stand seven crore leaders of Ganas. Commanders like Varada stand there equipped with the four-fold army. Eight crore Ganeshvaras stand under the command of Ganaka, authorized to bring the most excellent devotees from across the universes to the Lord's personal temple. The authorized spirits, led by the excellent Pramoda, stand there adorned with all ornaments, overflowing with bliss.

Brahmapriya, the enjoyer of Svananda; the supreme Jnanamaya; Sarvaga; Sarvavit; Yogapriya; Kala, and Mayika—these heroic servants stand all around with their groups. Vinayaka, Virupaksha, Krurakarma, and Aparajita, Gajagriva, Chandavega, Krura, and Chandaparakrama. These Vinayakas are declared to reside there, endowed with feelings of profound friendship.

The excellent Ganapatyas who had attained lordship over the five etheric realms, and the five famous universal deities—Brahmatma, Vishnvatma, Suryatma, Gajasambhava, and Malaja—having reached Svananda-bhavana at the end of the Kalpa by Ganapa's command, stand in the Lord's assembly, completely endowed with the form of Gajanana. Crores of the five deities who were well-established in their lordships during previous Kalpas across the universes have gone to Svananda-bhavana and stand in the assembly of Brahmanaspati. Others who have attained the Salokya liberation stand all around. The personified Vedas, Upanishads, Mimamsa, Puranas, weapon-deities, and Mantras stand in the Lord's assembly. That is why the highly fortunate ones call this the Place of Command.

Hundreds of famous Avatar-forms of these deities reside with their forms in the intermediate mansions. This central location is celebrated as Ganesha-Pura. Exactly to its left is the excellent city of Mahasiddhi-Pura. There, Siddhi resides with her attendants in proper order. To the right is the exceedingly enchanting city of Mahabuddhi-Pura. In a similar grand manner, the beautiful Buddhi resides there. In the eastern direction lies the utterly enchanting city of Gunesh-Pura. There, the Minister Avyakta Ganapa resides in eternal bliss, surrounded by his own avatar forms and his own attendants.

In the western direction is the excellent city of Ganaka-Pura. This supreme guru of Brahman resides there with his own avatar forms, accompanied by the initiating gurus of the Ganapatya tradition like Gargya and Mudgala, and surrounded by his attendants. To the east of Siddhi-Pura is the great city of Labha—the son of Siddhi and an alternate manifestation of Gunesh. Labha, who bestows all forms of gain and prosperity, resides there. Similarly, to the east of Buddhi-Pura is remembered Laksha-Pura. There resides Mahalaksha, the son of Buddhi, who grants the ultimate target. To the west of Buddhi-Pura, in the city of Kala-Pattana, resides Kala, the servant of the Lord, accompanied by his troops of Vighnas and Avighnas. To the west of Siddhi-Pura is remembered Mayura-Pattana. There resides the world-enchanting Vainayaki Mahamaya Mayura, served by tens of millions of forms of Maya.

Thus, these eight cities are situated in the first enclosure. The celestial vehicles of all these deities are stationed in their immediate vicinity. At the flanks of the city gates stand the two door-keepers. Beyond them are stationed Moda and others, followed by Suprabhuta and others, the heroic deities of the Varnas, the deities of weapons and missiles, and the wall-guards, all arranged systematically with their troops.

Thus is the Svananda-bhavana, located in the middle of the Ocean of Sugarcane Juice. Named after the form of the Svananda Brahman, it was created by Ganesha Himself. Established in His own majesty in the Fifth State, it is declared to be the foundation of all. It was constructed by the Divine Player in response to the prayers of Siddhi and Buddhi.

Thus ends the Description of the Palace of Svananda.

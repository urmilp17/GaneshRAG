The Description of the Descent of Ganaka

The sages, their minds brimming with a newfound curiosity, leaned forward, their collective focus a palpable force in the celestial assembly. They spoke with one voice, their tone reverent yet insistent.

The Sages Said:
“O Lord! O Lotus-born One! You have, in the course of your divine discourse, mentioned in passing that the Ganaka, the supreme knower of Brahman, descends to earth five times during the grand cycle of the four Yugas. Our hearts are stirred by this revelation. Please, with your boundless grace, unveil to us the profound reason for these sacred descents. Tell us of his divine deeds and the wisdom he bestowed upon the world.”
A serene smile graced the lips of the four-faced creator, Lord Brahma. The light of cosmic memory shone in his eyes as he prepared to narrate the glorious chronicle of the great soul, Ganaka.

Brahma Said:
“Hear now, O wise ones, the secret of the descents of the Ganaka, the supreme knower of Brahman, the author of the eternal, three-fold path—the Prasthānatrayī. His coming is ever for the sustenance of Dharma and the illumination of the world.

In the primordial dawn of the Krita Yuga, he first descended to the earthly realm, accompanied by a host of great sages. His purpose was to plant the seeds of supreme knowledge. He is the one who brought forth the dawn of bliss through the knowledge of Brahman in the Vedic path. For the benefit of all humanity, he created the incomparable great epic, the Mahabharata, an Ithihasa that contains within it the vision of the Mahavakyas, revealing the sacred path of Yoga. 
In the Tantric path, this great sage composed the excellent Ganesha Tantra, a masterful synthesis of the wisdom of the Vedas and the practical power of Tantra. And for those treading the path of the heretics, the Pāṣaṇḍa Mārga, this knower of Brahman, who propagated pure non-dualism—Shuddhādvaita—created the profound scripture known as Hairamba. In that first descent, he established all these great Vidyas, these sacred lores, firmly in the world, giving the doctrine of Shuddhādvaita joyfully to the sages.

His second descent occurred in the middle of that same age. This time, his purpose was to refute the false doctrines that had begun to sprout like weeds in a pristine garden. He established non-dualism, Advaita, in the form of the Vedic Siddhanta, restoring the purity of the eternal truth.

Then, at the beginning of the Treta Yuga, the best of sages descended for the third time. The world had grown more complex, and conflicting ideologies had multiplied. Through powerful refutations of false doctrines and by reinstating the authentic Vedic methods of worship, he once again established Advaita, this time fortified by the unassailable conclusions of the Vedas themselves.
His fourth descent was at the commencement of the Dvapara Yuga. The forces of confusion had grown stronger, with demons and those who followed them creating deceptive doctrines to lead souls astray. The great-souled Ganaka came and, after systematically refuting these false paths, he re-established the glorious Vedic path in its complete, four-part splendor—the Chatur-kāṇḍa—upon the earth.

And finally, in the later twilight of the Dvapara Yuga, as the world stood on the cusp of the Kali age, the great sage descended for the fifth time, accompanied by the enlightened inhabitants of Tapoloka. His mission was comprehensive: to re-establish all the Vidyas he had created throughout the ages. To the sages from Tapoloka, he imparted the essence of the secret of Vinayaka, the secret which is the very source of bliss. It was he who instructed the great yogi, Vyasa, to compose the Vedanta Darshana scripture, the philosophical foundation. With great affection, he also taught the Karikas, the explanatory verses, for the Vedanta Shastra, ensuring the teachings would be clearly understood.

Furthermore, at the end of a thousand years into the Kali Yuga, in the sacred confluence of Prayaga, within the serene Badarivana forest, he took birth as the son of a Brahmin named Gaudapada. There, he taught the path of Yoga, which bestows the liberation of knowledge, to the assembled sages. After imparting this wisdom, he became absorbed in the depths of meditation. Later, in preparation for the incarnation of Shankara—of whom he was an amsha, a divine part—he also taught the profound meanings of the ten principal Upanishads. 

He revealed the intended meaning, the Tatparya, of the Vedanta Darshana, which is wholly approved by the Vedas, and gave the exposition of the Mahavakyas through his own illuminating commentary, his Bhāshya. After bestowing upon the world, along with his commentary, the essence of the Gita and the Ganapati Atharvashirsha, and after establishing all the necessary rituals of worship for this dark age, the great yogi departed to his heavenly abode.

O best of sages! Thus, the story of the appearances of the great-souled Ganaka in the four Yugas has been told to you. In other chronicles, the various conquests of directions by Ganaka have been gloriously described, as well as his refutation of the sixty-four heterodox doctrines and the three paths. The systematic refutation of the various erroneous doctrines in the Tantric paths, among the heretics, and among the non-Aryans has also been narrated. Remember this, O Lord of Yogis: the difference in his approach in each age is only due to the difference in time and the needs of the people; in the one who descends, no other distinction is seen.

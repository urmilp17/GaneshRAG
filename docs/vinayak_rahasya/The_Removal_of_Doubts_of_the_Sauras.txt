The Removal of Doubts of the Sauras

Lord Brahma continued his narration, describing the journey of the great Guru Ganaka to the sacred heights of Kedarnath.

Brahma said:
"At the sacred site of Kedarnath, after worshipping Ganesha and Lord Shiva with profound devotion, the Guru of Gurus, Ganaka, took residence there, accompanied by sages and his eminent disciples.

In that holy place, some men approached, their hearts filled with devotion. They worshipped the eminent Guru, bowed before him with folded hands, and spoke in his presence.

The men said:
'O Revered Guru, we have been well-purified here by the great Siddha named Meenanatha. We are all devoted to the Vedic path and faithfully perform the rituals prescribed by the Vedas.

By the command of Meenanatha, we worship with deep devotion the Purusha who is the basis of all actions, known through the Vedas, and who possesses the three Gunas—the qualities of nature.

We understand that Surya, the Sun, is the support of the three Gunas and is called the Fourth, the Turiya. Brahma, Vishnu, and Maheshwara are each associated with one of the Gunas—Rajas, Sattva, and Tamas respectively.

According to the authority of the Vedas, the Trinity was born from that Turiya-Purusha, Surya himself. The Lord of the Day assigned all three to their respective cosmic tasks: Brahma to creation, Vishnu to preservation, and Shiva to destruction.

By a portion of himself, the Sun became the son of Shiva. And to the wise Ganesha, the elephant-faced son of Shiva who was born from his own portion, the Sun-God granted the lordship over all obstacles and the right to be worshipped first by everyone. Thus, Ganesha became worshipful to all beings.

Ganesha and the deities beginning with Shiva are endowed with power through the Sun's boons. The Golden Purusha, Surya, is the all-pervading Lord of all the worlds. This is specifically stated in the Vedas and Upanishads regarding the path to liberation.
Here in this place, the Ganapatyas, also purified by Meenanatha, are his disciples. They proclaim Ganesha as the Fifth Lord, the supreme reality. They speak of the Turiya, the Lord of Gunas, and the five deities who are of the nature of the Gunas.

Similarly, the Smartas, who follow the middle path of the five traditions, speak of the five deities of the Panchayatana as being the Turiya itself.

The Shaivas, Vaishnavas, and Shaktas sing of the Sun as the son of Brahma, the lord of the planets, possessing a nature like Brahman.

O Guru, we are perplexed! The disciples of the same Yogi, Meenanatha, speak so differently from one another. Tell us, which of these views is the truth? Or are they all authoritative in their own way?"

The great Ganaka listened to their words with patience and compassion. His eyes shone with the light of wisdom as he prepared to unveil the profound truth that harmonizes all perspectives.

The Ganaka said:
"O excellent ones, the Upanishads and Puranas are the ultimate authority in this matter. The views of all these sects are based on the authority of the Vedas; of this there is no doubt. The apparent contradictions dissolve when one understands the deeper teachings.

The great souls have described the Six Beginningless Entities in the Vedanta. These are the fundamental principles that have always existed, without beginning. Among these, Mahamaya, the great creative power, is well-known in the Vedas.

These six are: Brahman, the pure consciousness; Ishvara, the personal Lord; Jiva, the individual soul; Maya, the illusory creative power; the distinction between Jiva and Ishvara; and the relationship between Consciousness and Maya.

The substratum of Maya is Brahman, which is of the nature of the Attributeless, the Nirguna. The union of Brahman-Consciousness and Maya is remembered as beginningless, an eternal conjunction.

Ishvara is beginningless, and so is the Jiva, who is also called the Knower of the Field. The universe is manifested in three forms: gross, subtle, and causal. The creation of these manifested worlds is what is called 'Creation'. Among these beginningless entities, Brahman alone is eternal in its own true nature.

The union of the point of Consciousness and Maya is known as Omkara, the sacred primordial sound. The Nirguna Brahman exists, endowed with the attributes of Consciousness and Maya. This union, this primordial reality, is what the wise call Ganapa, Ganesha.

From Him, the excellent Maya, consisting of pure Sattva, was born. The lords of sages call Her the Supreme Power, Para-shakti.

Shiva is the Lord conditioned by this Maya. Vishnu is the Lord characterized by this Maya, associated with its active manifestation, and is the ruler of the universe. Through the Vedas, we know the Kshetrajna, the individual soul, as the self of all worlds. The Lord of Gunas, Guneshvara, is the one who represents the union of all four—Shiva, Vishnu, the Jiva, and Maya.

Therefore, the Vedas speak of the Fourth, the Turiya, in five distinct ways. The play of the four deities—Shiva, Vishnu, Brahma, and others—is within the three Gunas. But the play of the Fifth, Ganesha, is both in the Turiya and in the three Gunas. Thus, O Brahmins, the wise know that the function of the Turiya ultimately belongs to the Fifth alone. The Guna-based tasks belong to the four starting with Shiva.

Therefore, the Ganapatya Brahmins call Ganesha the Fifth. They speak of the Turiya Ganesha in the world as the one who assumes the forms of the Gunas. They refer to the five deities of the Panchayatana based on the authority of the Vedic scriptures.

Now understand the nature of the five deities: Brahma is made of Rajas, the quality of activity and creation. Janardana, Vishnu, is endowed with Sattva, the quality of preservation and goodness. Rudra, Shiva, is made of Tamas, the quality of dissolution and rest. Shakti, however, is of the nature of all three Gunas combined.

Surya, the Golden Purusha, is the bearer of the ego of the three Gunas, the cosmic principle of individuation. Brahma, the grandfather of the worlds, manifests as the fourteen worlds of creation.

By the boon of Brahma, the Sun became his son. Shiva and Vishnu are the lords of gods, and the Lord of Planets, Surya, is also remembered as the son of Kashyapa. The Jiva is born from a portion of the Sun and attains similarity of form through devotion. Therefore, O excellent ones, the Vaishnavas call Shiva the 'Son of Brahma.'

The ritualists, the Karmathas, call Shiva, Vishnu, and the Sun the 'Sons of Brahma.' All this is said according to the authority of the Vedas and not otherwise.

In each of these sects, to perfect one's devotion, the deity of that sect is described as the most supreme. This is the method of the Vedas—to elevate the devotee through focused love and reverence for their chosen ideal. Everyone speaks according to their own sect, O best of Brahmins. The reason for their firm belief in their own path and their dismissal of others' views is simply the intense focus required for their specific spiritual discipline.

Understanding this, one should avoid criticizing other paths and practice devotion toward one's own deity with sincerity and dedication. All paths lead to the same truth when followed with faith and understanding."

Hearing these words of wisdom from the Guru, the Sauras were filled with clarity and peace. Their doubts dissolved, replaced by a broader vision that honored all paths. They bowed deeply to the Guru of Gurus and, by his command, departed, their hearts devoted to the Vedic conduct and their minds established in harmony.

At that moment, Meenanatha himself approached and worshipped the excellent Guru. Bowing with reverence, he sat near him by the Guru's command.

The Guru then spoke to Meenanatha: "O Meenanatha, born of the portion of Guha, Skanda himself, by my command, for the Vedic followers in the land of the Brahmarshis who follow the Six Paths, compose the texts on their traditions and conduct. Provide for their spiritual sentiments and instruct them with grace."

Accepting the Guru's command with reverence and bowing once more, the great Yogi Meenanatha departed joyfully to carry out this sacred mission.

Thus ends the Removal of Doubts of the Sauras.

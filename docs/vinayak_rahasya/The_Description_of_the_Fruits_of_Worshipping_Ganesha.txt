The Description of the Fruits of Worshipping Ganesha

Narada and the assembled sages approached the Sun God, Surya, to resolve a final theological question. Having learned earlier about the necessity of worshipping subsidiary deities, they now asked, "O Lord, please instruct us on the specific fruit and ultimate reward for a devotee who chooses to worship Gananatha alone, without the peripheral deities."

Surya replied by revealing the ultimate cosmic anatomy. He explained that this four-fold universe is comprised of three primary components: insentient material elements (Jada), individual living souls (Jivas), and the governing cosmic deities (Ishvaras). Together, this entire cosmic structure constitutes the actual physical body of Ganesha, while Ganesha Himself serves as the innermost soul (Atman) of the universe.

Endowed with this magnificent four-fold body, Ganesha is the all-pervading Fifth Principle (Vibhu). Surya clarified the hierarchy to the sages: the exalted deities worshipped in the Panchayatana layout (Shiva, Vishnu, Surya, and Shakti) are not independent supreme beings; rather, they are celebrated simply as His individual, functional manifestations (Vyashtis). They act as the Ishvaras, the ruling lords of creation. Meanwhile, all living entities, ranging from the mighty creator Brahma down to a blade of grass or a tiny insect, function as the Jivas. Furthermore, all non-living material elements within the multiverse are classified as Jada.

It is the Fifth Principle, Ganesha, who shines forth by simultaneously assuming the forms of the physical universe, the Jivas, and the Ishvaras. Yet, even while He projects these three states through His divine reflection cast upon the material world, He remains completely untouched. He resides eternally in the supreme realm of Svananda, shining brilliantly in His own absolute majesty.

Because the entire cosmos is His body, Surya declared a profound truth: by the worship of Ganesha alone, the entire four-fold universe is automatically and perfectly worshipped. Therefore, an exclusive devotee of Ganesha (a Ganapatya) does not miss out on anything. Through the grace and excellence of Buddhi (cosmic intelligence), such a single-minded devotee gradually attains absolute purity of mind (Chitta-shuddhi) and supreme spiritual knowledge.

Upon achieving the perfection of this knowledge, the devotee attains the ultimate state of becoming Brahman (Brahmabhuya) in the eternal realm of Svananda. Alternatively, through the strict perfection of Yoga, the devotee achieves Kaivalya—absolute, non-dual liberation.

Surya concluded his discourse by stating that Lord Ganesha, the all-pervading Fifth Principle, is the very fabric of both the material universe and the transcendent Brahman. Therefore, when Vighnesha (the Lord of Obstacles) is worshipped, absolutely everything in existence is worshipped through Him.

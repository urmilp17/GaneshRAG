The Refutation of Pseudo-Vedic Shaktism

Lord Brahma continued his narration to the assembled sages, his voice calm and measured as he described the journey of the great Guru Ganaka.

Brahma said:

"In the sacred cave of Kanchipuram, the eminent Siddha named Ganaka worshipped the Goddess Lalita, who is eternally united with Kameshwara, with profound devotion.

When all were seated, the devoted worshippers of Mother Lalita, led by the learned Ekamranathasharma, approached the Guru of Gurus. After bowing respectfully and standing by his command, they spoke, their minds intent upon their own established views.

The Brahmins said:

'O Revered One, in these southern lands, the people belonging to the four Varnas were once terrified by the fear of the negligent and wicked demons. In their distress, they abandoned the duties prescribed for their Varna.

Resorting to various heretical paths, they worshipped their chosen deities. Yet, to protect the essence of their Varna-dharma, they performed the Sandhya prayers and worshipped the Mother of the Vedas, the Goddess Gayatri, in secret, following the rules of Apad-dharma, the duty prescribed for times of distress. Now, some among those negligent demons who previously tormented them have been destroyed.

Here, in this very region, the great Sage Durvasa purified and reinstated the protectors of the Varna order. Some among the people, however, had fallen so low that they attained the status of Shudras, while others are remembered as completely fallen from their path.

We, the residents of the Kanchi region, worship the Divine Shakti. Our understanding is this: in the beginning, the Supreme Brahman existed and manifested in both male and female forms. The male form is Parameshana, the Supreme Lord, and Shakti is the one who assumes the female form. From a portion of Shiva, the Sun, the regulator of time, was born. From a portion of Shakti, Vishnu, the protector of the universe, was born. And Ganesha, the destroyer of obstacles, was born from portions of both Shiva and Shakti.

From Vishnu himself was born Brahma, the grandfather of all worlds, and everything up to the realm of Brahma was created by that self-born one.

In the abode of Shakti, the Supreme Mother Bhuvaneshvari is independent and sovereign. In the abode of Shiva, Shankara and the other deities reside, and there Shankara is independent in his own domain.

We Brahmins worship the Divine Mother along with Shiva, following the path prescribed by the Vedas, using the auspicious fifteen-syllabled mantra, the Panchadashakshari. We chant it especially along with the Japa of Lord Ganesha.

It is known that the Supreme Shakti is fond of the Kula path and also fond of meat offerings. Following the Samaya tradition of the Supreme Shakti, we partake of meat as part of the consecrated food offerings. We Vedic followers also drink the Kula wine, considering it to be the sacred remains, the Nirmalya, of the Goddess herself. This is our established practice.'"

The great Ganaka listened to their words with patience and compassion. When they had finished, he began to speak, his voice carrying the weight of supreme wisdom.

The Ganaka said:

"O best of the twice-born, there is no inherent flaw in the doctrine you have presented. The origin of the Kula path is indeed said to be of twenty-eight varieties, and the partaking of its offerings is described according to the respective Varnas and Ashramas of the practitioners.

Even in the Kali Yuga, O Brahmins, the consumption of meat is not absolutely forbidden for all. The Shastras prescribe that the meat of specific animals may be eaten by specific men, in accordance with their Varna-dharma. Furthermore, in certain paths of ritual worship, for those initiated as Nirvanis, the consumption of all types of meat is mentioned in the Agamic scriptures.

However, a crucial distinction must be made. Even if certain practices are mentioned in some scriptures, other injunctions must also be considered. Certain drinks are to be avoided. O best of Brahmins, only those foods that are prescribed in the authentic Vedic branches should be consumed by those following the Vedic path.

Now, tell me truly: are you initiated according to the Vedic ritual path, the Veda-kalpa? If you are, then meat mixed with wine must never be offered to Shakti during worship, nor should it be consumed by you. This is the clear conclusion of the Shastras. If one is without such Vedic initiation, the rules are as stated in the Dharma-shastras.

Only that which is fit for Brahmins to eat should be offered in worship. Furthermore, without the Maha-vishesha-diksha, the Great Special Initiation, a Brahmin is not eligible to serve as a priest for rituals performed for the benefit of others in temples. Tell me honestly: are you initiated in this manner, or are you not?"

The Brahmins, hearing the Guru's penetrating words, consulted among themselves and then replied with humility.

The Brahmins said:

"O Guru, there are indeed some Brahmins here who perform worship for the benefit of others. These priests are endowed with the Maha-vishesha-diksha according to the Ushana-kalpa path.

Others among us, however, though devoted to Japa and personal worship, do not possess this specific Shakta initiation. As for the rest of us, without doubt, we all worship the Devi. But by your command, we now understand the proper way.

From this day forward, by these initiated Brahmins among us, meat mixed with wine shall not be offered in the worship of Shakti. It shall neither be eaten nor drunk by those following the Vedic path. This is the truth we accept; there is no doubt.

For others who are initiated in different paths, the offering of meat with wine to the Devi may be performed according to their own Varna-dharma, and they may partake of it without doubt. But by your command, we shall act according to your instructions from now on."
Ganaka, pleased with their sincerity, then revealed a deeper truth.

The Ganaka said:
"Know this, O best of Brahmins: one who indulges in such ritual offerings might indeed attain liberation after five births, following the path of enjoyment. But for one who does not partake, who follows the path of renunciation, there is no such restrictive rule. Liberation is not bound by such conditions.
It seems to me, however, that some of you worship Shakti primarily because you are fond of wine and meat. If you truly have a sincere desire for Moksha, for ultimate liberation, then the worship of the Supreme Shakti should be performed through the path of renunciation, free from such desires."

Hearing these words of wisdom from the Guru, the Brahmins were filled with true devotion. They renounced wine and meat entirely, and seeking liberation through the grace of Shakti alone, they began to perform the worship of the Supreme Power according to the sacred Vedic rules, with purity and dedication.

At that moment, the king of yogis, the great sage Durvasa himself, arrived at the assembly. He bowed respectfully to the Guru of Gurus, and by his command, sat down beside him.

Ganaka then addressed that best of sages: 'To these excellent Shaktas and Shaivas who are worthy, give the authentic scriptures that are firmly based on the Vedas. Having given them the true essence of the paths of Shiva and Shakti, instruct them properly by my command. But for those who are unworthy, for those not yet ready for the highest truths, prescribe the mixed Tantras, those that are not purely Vedic, as a preparatory step.'"

Accepting this divine command by bowing his head in reverence, the best of sages, Durvasa, departed to carry out his sacred mission. Ekamranathasharma and the other Brahmins also bowed deeply to the Guru and left, their hearts filled with joy and their minds clear of doubt.

Thus ends the Refutation of the Pseudo-Vedic Shaktas.

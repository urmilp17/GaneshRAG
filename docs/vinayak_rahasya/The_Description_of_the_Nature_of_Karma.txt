The Description of the Nature of Karma

Sanaka and the other sages approached the Lord and asked, "O Parameshvara, what exactly is Karma, and what is its true nature? Please reveal this to us."

Shiva replied, explaining that action is not merely physical. Whatever is done through the intellect, the mind, the sensory organs, the physical body, or through speech—all of this total output is termed 'Karma' by the wise.

According to the Vedas, Karma is primarily classified into two distinct categories: Prohibited (Nishiddha) and Prescribed (Vihita). When the Shruti and the great sages declare, "This must not be done," such actions fall under Prohibited Karma. Conversely, when the Shruti and the sages decree, "This must be done," those actions are remembered as Prescribed Karma.

This Prescribed Karma is further subdivided into three categories: Daily/Regular (Nitya), Occasional (Naimittika), and Desire-driven (Kamya).

The Nitya or daily duties are themselves two-fold, divided between one's social duty (Varna) and one's specific spiritual denomination (Mata). The daily duties relating to Varna include obligatory rites like the Sandhya prayers, Japa, and Homa (fire offerings). If a person abandons these foundational daily duties of their Varna, they fall from their spiritual standing and attain the state of a Shudra.

On the other hand, the daily duties belonging to one's Mata (spiritual path) consist of chanting the specific mantra of one's chosen deity, performing the deity's ritual worship, and engaging in deep philosophical contemplation of the deity's true nature. O Yogis, if a person abandons these daily spiritual duties, they become a fallen practitioner (Mata-bhrashta).

Beyond the daily rites are the Naimittika or Occasional karmas. These are duties that must be performed only when specific auspicious times, celestial events, or particular life circumstances arrive. Then there is Kamya Karma, which consists of actions performed explicitly with the desire to attain a specific result, fruit, or worldly benefit. Rites of atonement and purification (Prayashchitta) are also encompassed within these frameworks.

Finally, looking at the timeline of how actions bear fruit across lifetimes, Karma is understood in three overarching phases. The vast reservoir of actions performed and accumulated throughout all previous births is known as Sanchita Karma. The specific slice of that accumulated karma which has ripened, is currently bearing fruit, and is actively being experienced in this present lifetime is celebrated as Prarabdha Karma. Lastly, the new actions and intentions being generated from this moment onward, which will bear fruit in the future, are known as Agami Karma.

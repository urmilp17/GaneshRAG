The Description of the State of Shiva and Others

Narada and the assembled sages, having listened to Surya's previous explanations about cosmic bureaucracy, raised a profound philosophical objection. They asked, "O Surya, you have stated that the entire four-fold universe is the physical body of Brahmanaspati. If the universe is His body, then logic dictates that by satisfying the Supreme Lord, the entire universe should automatically attain complete satisfaction. Why, then, did you claim earlier that pleasing Him yields only 'half-satisfaction' for embodied beings?"

Surya smiled and praised the depth of their question. He explained that their logic was absolutely flawless, but it applied only to a specific tier of spiritual seekers. "What you have just described," Surya said, "is the ultimate perspective of the highest Yogis—those who have achieved Ekatma-bhavana, the realization of the One Non-Dual Self. For those awakened souls who no longer perceive any separation between themselves, the universe, and God, your statement is perfectly true. Because they are completely devoid of duality (Bhinna-bhava), they do not need to navigate the cosmic bureaucracy. For them, the One Self, Gananatha, operates directly within their hearts, taking care of all their needs instantly and absolutely."

"However," Surya continued, "the rules I described earlier apply to the vast majority of embodied beings who are still bound by the illusion of separation and duality. As long as a person feels distinct from the universe and desires worldly outcomes, they are subject to the mechanics of the cosmic government."

Surya then detailed the administrative structure of this divine government. In this cosmic state, Gunesh acts as the Chief Executive, carrying out all the functional duties of the Supreme King, Ganesha. Operating directly under Gunesh are the four great cosmic ministers: Vishnu, Shiva (Isha), Surya, and Shakti.

Just as the citizens of a kingdom must respect and follow the decrees of the king's appointed ministers, ordinary devotees must respect the cosmic ministers. Surya concluded that for single-minded followers of specific paths—such as the Shambhavas (worshippers of Shiva)—it is necessary to worship their own chosen deity to fulfill their worldly desires, while simultaneously honoring Ganesha as the Supreme King.

However, Surya offered a final, humbling reminder regarding the status of these great deities. No matter how powerful Shiva, Vishnu, Surya, and Shakti appear, they are ultimately like ministers in a royal court. They possess immense administrative power over the universe, but they are never truly independent. They cannot execute any cosmic action without the underlying command and authorizing grace of the Supreme King, Ganesha.

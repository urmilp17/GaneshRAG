The Decree of Ganesha in Swarga-Swananda

Narada and the other sages said:

In every single Kalpa, for the sake of showering grace upon the worlds, Ganesha, the Lord of Prayer, comes from the city of Svananda with Siddhi and Buddhi and His attendants to hold His assembly there. O Lords of Sages, Bhrushundi, Mudgala, and others speak of this order; please describe that process to us.

Surya said:

In the city of Svarga-Svananda, which is situated to the East of Mount Kailasa, there exists a palace of bliss created by the will of the Lord of Ganas. There, the assembly of Siddhi, Buddhi, and Ganesha takes place. Only the most excellent of the gods are capable of entering this heavenly Svananda palace. Sages and even humans go there to achieve their personal goals. The two gatekeepers of Ganesha are named Shura and Mahashura.

In the center of the Assembly of Command sits Gajanana, the King of Elders. He is seated upon a divine throne alongside Siddhi and Buddhi. Gunesh sits there as the Crown Prince on an excellent but slightly lower throne to the left. Avyakta sits as the Chief Minister on a throne lower than his. Similarly, in due order, sits the excellent Commander-in-Chief named Hamsa. He stays on the right side of the Lord along with the other leaders of the army. With a book in his hand, Ganaka remains near Ganesha. Those gods who receive permission from the two gatekeepers may enter the Lord's assembly; all others are turned away at the gate. Outside the circle of the Svananda-Ganapatyas stand Shiva and the other deities. Those established in the Turiya state stand there by the command of Ganaka. The five deities and the lords of sages stand in the space further outside.

The Lord of Svarga-Svananda, the son of Shambhu, Ganesha, moves through the assembly and signals the people to be silent. Ganaka looks into his book for the name of the petitioner and presents the petition to the Lord. The two messengers, Sumukha and Durmukha, call out the name three times and summon the petitioners in order. They approach and state their business or requests near the Lord. Observing the movement of Ganesha's eyebrows, the Lord Gunesh, in consultation with Avyakta, works to ensure the fulfillment of their desires. Then, Avyakta prepares the command, stamping the petition-scroll with the official seal of Gunesh as well as his own seal. He joyfully places the scroll into the hand of Ganaka. Then Ganaka, the lord of calculators, records everything in his own book.

He then hands that scroll into the hand of the Son of Shiva. If the matter concerns Knowledge, then Ganesha hands it over to the Great Goddess Upanishad. That Goddess reads the letter in the presence of Mahabuddhi. By the decree of Mahabuddhi, the petitioner immediately attains that rare knowledge which is dear to them. If it concerns any other matter, Ganesha, by the command of Ganaka, gives the scroll into the hand of Ishitva-Shakti. That Goddess reads the letter in the presence of Mahasiddhi. By the decree of Mahasiddhi, the devotee attains their desired success. This is why Siddhi and Buddhi are remembered as the cause of everything. In this manner, in the Place of Command within the city of Svarga-Svananda, the Lord grants the fulfillment of all desires to His devotees.

Thus ends the description of Ganesha's command in Svarga-Svananda.

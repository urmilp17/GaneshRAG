Description of Ganapati's Form

Sanaka and the other sages said:

O Ocean of Mercy, please describe clearly and completely the true nature and form of Ganaraja!

Ganaka said:

That in which everything attains ultimate peace, which is of the nature of the Infinite, established in its own majesty, and devoid of both superimposition and de-superimposition, is remembered as Brahman. Through the method of superimposition and subsequent denial, the unmanifest Absolute is explained—resorting to this philosophical maxim, and being well-established in that reality, I shall speak.

Maya, which is the medium of superimposition and takes the form of the bliss of union, arose from the beginningless ocean of Brahman as the cosmic binding force. The reflection of Brahman within that Maya is remembered as the direct Self. This Brahman, termed the Bliss of Self-Union, is the direct implied target of the word Asi. This Self is self-luminous, devoid of both attributes and attributelessness. Yet, it is considered to maintain its true nature by simultaneously embracing both the manifest and unmanifest aspects.

The Maya of the Self is divided into two aspects: Siddhi and Buddhi. That aspect which consists of the five functional modifications of the mind is remembered as Mahabuddhi. That which causes delusion, temporary joy, and sorrow within the mind is called Siddhi. Thus, the Maya of the Bliss of Self-Union Brahman is remembered as two-fold. His head is said to be Mahabuddhi, and His body is composed of Siddhi. That same singular reality became two-fold by its own free will for the sake of cosmic play. For the accomplishment of the cosmic play, that same reality manifested as Saguna, and to illuminate that play, it shone forth as Saguna. Established in its own majesty is the state of non-differentiation between the two. Omkara is known as the Saguna Brahman, and Tat is remembered as the Nirguna.

One's true essential nature is the Existence-Brahman, which directly represents the word Asi. Thus, the Brahman of Ganapati is described as three-fold. The intellect is called the cave, and Siddhi is that which delivers joy and sorrow. That three-fold Brahman, possessing three aspects, is well-established within Siddhi and Buddhi. The Brahman hidden in the cave of the intellect is Truth, Knowledge, and Infinity. Satyam, or Truth, is the Saguna Brahman, which constitutes the meaning of the word Tvam. Jnanam, or Knowledge, is the Nirguna Brahman, which constitutes the meaning of the word Tat. Its true essence is called Anantam, the Infinite, which is remembered as the form of Asi.

Thus, the three-fold Brahman hidden in the cave is described in the Vedas. The Saguna aspect, which assumes various states and expressions, is represented by the letter Ga. The Nirguna aspect, which is completely devoid of diverse expressions, is represented by the letter Na. That which unites both these aspects is called Ishvara, the Lord of both. The ultimate, true essential nature is the Supreme Brahman, and It is called Ganesha. His head is said to represent the Nirguna aspect, symbolized by the Elephant. Below the neck is the Saguna Brahman, symbolized by the Human. In the state of absolute non-differentiation between these two lies the true form of Gajanana.

Through the existence of words and their meanings, names and forms are projected. Names such as Vinayaka and others are declared in the Vedas based on this. His form is envisioned through symbols that awaken the understanding of His true nature. Because it is endowed with Saguna, Nirguna, and Maya, it represents His true essential nature. Out of His own free will, He truly assumes names and forms. Yet, in His true essence, being completely devoid of Saguna, Nirguna, and Maya, His true nature remains. He is completely devoid of name and form; thus do the knowers of the Vedas understand. In the states of cosmic play, He assumes names and forms. When established in His own glory, He is said to be completely free from them. The form of Gajanana is indeed the very embodiment of the Mahavakya, the Great Vedic Statement.

Below the neck is declared to be the word Tvam, represented by the human form that comprises the universe. Above the neck is the word Tat, represented by the elephant form which is Nirguna. In the non-differentiation of these two, there is the flawless and complete realization of the word Asi. This is the direct, visible Brahman called Tat Tvam Asi, the supreme deity of the Ganapatyas. The unmanifested Turiya is remembered as the elephant form. He is the first cause of all actions, and His face is remembered as the elephant face. Gajanana is the generator or source of even this elephant-shaped Brahman. In the Vedas, He is specifically described as the Fifth, the Saguna reality.

Maya is said to be Gaja, the elephant, and that Maya is insentient by nature. Because He grants existence to Maya, He is called the Giver of Life. Therefore, in the Vedas, the Nirguna is remembered as Gajanana. Because He infuses life and consciousness into Maya, He is remembered as Gajanana. Thus, the three-fold Brahman is the very form of Gajanana. Enriched with the form of Gajanana, He is remembered in the Vedas as Ganapati.

Thus, the absolute Brahmanhood of Ganaraja is well-established. Endowed with these three states or aspects, He is the Lord, the Turiya. He is considered three-fold as: Cosmic, Individual, and completely free from both states. The human form is the Individual, while the elephant form is the Cosmic. In the state of non-differentiation between these two, He is Ganesha, this Gajanana. This Lord Ganesha is sometimes called Gajanana. He is termed as the embodiment of pure I-ness by those who have crossed to the further shores of Yoga. This Gajanana is the Lord governing the functions of Ganesha. Both Brahmanhood and Supreme Lordship of Ganapati are declared in the Vedas. He is the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, Ganesha, the Lord of Prayer.

In His own true essential nature, as the embodiment of His own bliss, the Destroyer of Obstacles resides in the form of Supreme Light. Assuming various playful forms for the destruction of the wicked and to bestow grace upon His devotees, the Leader of Ganas, by His own free will, takes birth and manifestation in the cosmic sphere through His own bliss-infusing Maya.

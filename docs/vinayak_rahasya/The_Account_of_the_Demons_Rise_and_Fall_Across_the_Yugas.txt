The Account of the Demons Rise and Fall Across the Yugas
The assembly of sages, their minds filled with the grand history of the cosmos, leaned forward with a new inquiry.

The Sages said:
“O Best of Celestials! In the Krita and the succeeding yugas, which mighty demons arose to challenge the cosmic order? How did they seek to destroy the sacred Vedic rituals? And who, descending to earth, vanquished them? Please tell us all this, O Lotus-born One.”

Lord Brahma, the source of all knowledge, acknowledged their query with a serene nod and began to unravel the epic struggle between light and darkness.

Brahma said:

“Hear now, O wise ones, of the great demons who emerged across the ages and their ultimate downfall. The dawn of the Krita Yuga spans a period of four hundred divine years, plus another one hundred and sixty. Its full duration has been described to you, O great sages, as has that of the Treta Yuga. The Dvapara Yuga lasts half of that, and the Kali Yuga a half yet again.

It was in the Kali Yuga that great demons, intoxicated with power, madness, and negligence, first arose. These wicked beings, having received boons from me, enjoyed the sovereignty of the entire universe. They established their seat of power in the intoxicated city of Mattanagari, located in the southern part of Bhadrashva.

A particularly vile demon named Adhi was appointed by the intoxicated lord Matta to rule over Jambudvipa. He was a tormentor of all races. The deities, deprived of their rightful positions, were forced to reside on Mount Meru, where they engaged in severe penance to counteract his evil.

Through Adhi’s command, a proclamation echoed across the earth: ‘The fourteen great Vedic sciences shall not be studied by the best of men! Humans shall remain without Vedic sacraments. The rituals prescribed in the Shruti, Smriti, and Puranas shall not be performed by any means, and any who perform them shall be punished!’ In the temples of the Panchayatana, he declared that the worship of the Devas was forbidden. Mortals, living in fear, obeyed his decrees.

Yet, some, taking refuge in the Dharma prescribed for times of distress, performed the sacraments for their castes in secret, gradually and mentally. They performed the Sandhya prayers and worshipped their chosen deities in their minds, with deep devotion. In this way, for five thousand years, the Brahmins and Kshatriyas lived in secrecy.

Then, a noble daughter was born to King Daksha in the land of Vidarbha. The great Lord Shiva himself manifested as Somnath. That goddess, Daksha's daughter, in the form of Shakti, killed Adhi with effortless ease. The inhabitants of Jambudvipa breathed a sigh of relief, though their happiness was short-lived.

The sages and the Devas, still fearful of the demon Matta, remained on the mountain, sustained by their penance. Out of fear of the demons stationed everywhere, humans on earth continued to live without Vedic rituals. Kingdoms, led by heroic kings like Daksha, performed only daily and occasional rites, but the great Yajnas were absent.

Hearing of Adhi’s death at the hands of Shakti, the negligent demon Pramattaka grew fearful. In a deceptive move, he ordered the worship of the five names, but through a non-Vedic path. Promoters of these false paths arose, composing and establishing heretical and Tantric scriptures across the globe. The demons, led by Matta, created obstacles and deluded many. Some demons even worshipped Shiva, while certain sages, followers of Shakataayana, established one-sided paths. To further delude the demons, some Devas, precursors to Vishnu, created the six-fold Buddhist scriptures and established this path among the demon races through illusion.

Humans across the world followed these Tantric and heretical paths. The celestial Gandharvas adopted mixed paths, while some humans in the forests took to the ways of the sages. Only a few remained on the Vedic path, sustained by the residual power of the age.

At the end of five thousand years, a son was born to King Soma and Natha, for the benefit of the world. This prince, Ganeshana, was the destroyer of demons. He pleased his parents with his divine childhood play, and the Devas and sages, seeing him, became fearless. Even the promoters of non-Vedic paths came to see the child, and the wise Ganaka recognized him as the Supreme Soul. Yet, the leaders of the false paths, devoid of true knowledge, continued to proclaim their own doctrines as supreme.

Then, the great ones—Sanaka and other yogis, Ganaka, Mudgala, and Bhrigu—established the unparalleled philosophy of Ganeshadvaita. They systematically refuted all non-Vedic paths and, recognizing the child as the Supreme Brahman, departed with joy. The prince Ganeshana then went to the battlefield and slew the mighty demons Matta, Pramattaka, and their hosts of Rakshasas. After subduing demons in heaven, the netherworld, and on earth, and restoring all humans to their righteous duties, the prince, in human form, ruled the world justly, appointing protectors for each caste and performing expiatory rites for those who had fallen.

By the order of Ganapa, sages like Bhrigu were established in the world, and they gradually promoted the growth of the Vedic path. Ganaka, the supreme knower of Brahman, went on a victorious tour across the seven continents to re-establish the Vedic Dharma, accompanied by his disciples. Thus, the four pillars of Dharma were restored throughout the universe.

At the beginning of the middle period of the Krita Yuga, the prince Vinayaka, through the grace of Parvati and Parameshvara, disappeared from sight. The sages, the promoters of the Vedas, Shastras, and Puranas, completed the portions of knowledge that had been lost and remained on earth, joyfully propagating the teachings. In the middle period of the Krita Yuga, people became devoted to their respective castes and duties, taking refuge in the Vedic path, and lived in a state of eternal bliss.

But the wheel of time turns. At the end of the middle period, in the intervening time of Kali, two mighty demon kings arose: Devantaka and Narantaka. Born into a Brahmin family in Bengal, these wicked beings, by the grace of Mahadeva, suddenly attained the wealth of the universe. They enjoyed this power and set about destroying all rituals—the Karma-kanda of the Vedas and the Smarta rites. They destroyed the worship of the Devas, and the portion of Vedic rituals was lost on earth. The Devas, filled with sorrow, approached Gunesha for refuge. The merciful Gunesha, taking birth as the son of the sage Kashyapa, killed Devantaka and Narantaka with ease. By his order, yogis re-established the Vedic path on the seven continents.

This pattern repeated through the ages. At the end of the latter dawn of the Treta Yuga, the demon Ravana appeared. Though devoted to the Vedic path in his own way, he was a destroyer of rituals and sought to uproot all paths except his own. Vishnu himself incarnated as Rama and slew this mighty being. Ganaka refuted the evil doctrines Ravana’s Rakshasas had established, and by Rama’s order, the great sage re-established the Vedic path at the dawn of the Dvapara Yuga.

In the Dvapara Yuga, powerful demons like Bhanda, endowed with boons from Shiva, arose and caused the destruction of the Vedas, Dharma, rituals, and even creation itself. Then, the Goddess Bhuvaneshvari, known as Lalita, manifested from a fire-pit. Herself becoming the queen, she slew the demons and protected the people. By her order, the yogi Mudgala re-established the Vedic path in Jambudvipa, while the great sage Narada did the same in the other islands with five other yogis.

As the Dvapara Yuga waned and Kali advanced, a mighty demon lord named Sindhura, a wretch who had obtained a boon from me, arose. After enjoying the sovereignty of the universe, he caused total destruction—uprooting caste duties, annihilating worship, and destroying the Vedic sciences. He seized and burned the books of the Vedas, Shastras, and Puranas. The world became like a wild forest.

Then, at the beginning of the latter dawn of Dvapara, the lord of Ganas, Ganesha, took birth as the son of Varenya, adorned with an elephant face. He slew the wicked demon Sindhura, who resided in Ilavrita, and protecting the earth from his seat in Mahishmati. The wise sage Bhrigu re-established the Vedic path in Jambudvipa.

Yet another demon king, Vidyavan, endowed with a boon from me, arose and caused the destruction of all sciences. The universe became devoid of knowledge and caste order. Uchchhishtha Ganapati, the lord of virtues, slew him. By my command, the righteous took refuge in Tapoloka. Eventually, they returned to earth and recreated everything as it was before, from the Krita Yuga onwards. The three worlds were restored, and in due course, all was set right.

From the dawn of the Krita Yuga until the end of the Dvapara, the root of Dharma in the Vedic sciences and the three higher castes was never fully destroyed, though it was tested time and again.

At the end of the Dvapara Yuga, for the welfare of the world in the approaching Kali Yuga, Hari himself incarnated as Vyasa. He divided the Vedas and the Puranas, which had previously existed without divisions, and composed the Vedanta and the Mahabharata, thus preserving the Vedic tradition. Yet, this wisdom was largely confined to the land of Bharata. The Kshatriya kings across Jambudvipa were destroyed by Lord Krishna, all except those in Bharata. The mortals in other lands became devoid of Vedic practices, turning to commerce under the influence of non-Vedic rulers. Those from other lands who still followed Varna and Ashrama came to Bharata and mingled with its people.

Thus, at the beginning of the Kali Yuga, only in the sacred land of Bharata did the duties of the castes and the fourteen Vedic sciences remain. In other lands, humans became subdued by base distinctions, devoid of Vedic knowledge, and were known as the Mleccha races, speakers of various tongues and critics of the Vedas. This, O great sages, is the general nature of the different periods of time, from the Krita Yuga until the end of Kali.

The Description of the Kalas

Sanaka and the assembled sages respectfully approached their Master, Ganaka, with a deep theological inquiry. "O Deshika!" they asked, "Please kindly explain to us the exact portions of Brahman (Brahmamsha)—the divine potencies known as Kalas—that are distributed among the five primary deities."

Ganaka replied, detailing the precise cosmic mathematics of divine manifestation. He explained that in Vedic philosophy, the absolute, complete manifestation of the Supreme is measured in sixteen Kalas (much like the sixteen phases of a full moon). The Fifth Supreme Brahman, Ganesha, is the perfect, unmitigated manifestation of the absolute Brahman known as Svanandesha. Lacking nothing in cosmic power or completeness, Ganesha shines brilliantly in His absolute fullness, endowed with all sixteen Kalas.

Descending from this absolute state, the power is distributed in a step-down hierarchy to the cosmic administrators. The unmanifest (Avyakrita) Chief Minister, Guneshana, operates with exactly half of that supreme potency, possessing eight Kalas. His two supreme consorts and forces of cosmic execution, Siddhi and Buddhi, are also each independently endowed with eight Kalas.

From there, the divine potencies are further distributed among the localized cosmic ministers. Shambhu (Shiva), who functions as the Lord conditioned by the veil of Maya, is endowed with four Kalas. Vishnu, who embodies the qualified, majestic, and preserving aspect of the Lord (Vishishtesha), is endowed with two Kalas.

Finally, Surya, who operates as the Kshetrajna (the animating soul or the "knower of the field" within the material universe), is endowed with one single Kala. In the exact same manner, Prakriti, manifesting as the dynamic cosmic energy known as Shakti, is also endowed with one Kala.

Through this perfectly scaled distribution of divine power, the infinite and absolute Brahman manages the diverse, finite operations of the manifested universes.

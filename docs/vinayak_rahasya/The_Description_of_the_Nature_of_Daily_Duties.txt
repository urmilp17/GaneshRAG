The Description of the Nature of Daily Duties

Sanaka and the other sages approached the Lord and said, "O Shankara, the Yogis who desire ultimate liberation constantly praise the performance of Nitya Karma (daily duties). Therefore, please gracefully explain to us the true nature and inner meaning of these daily duties."

Lord Shiva replied, revealing the profound esoteric truths hidden behind standard rituals. He explained that the action performed during the twilight junctures is called Sandhya. However, in the deepest vision of the Vedas, the true Sandhya Karma is not merely a physical ritual, but the cultivation of non-dual realization (abheda-bhavana). This supreme realization is achieved through the continuous contemplation of the Mahavakya (the Great Vedic Statement). The true form of Sandhya is the Mahavakya itself, which establishes the absolute identity between the Jivatma (individual soul) and the Paramatma (Supreme Soul).

Through the deep contemplation of this Mahavakya, a seeker becomes established in the supreme majesty of the Absolute. This realization ascends and firmly mounts itself upon the modifications of the inner instrument of the mind (antahkarana). This state, comprised of the five-fold modifications of consciousness, is declared to be Mahabuddhi.

Shiva offered a powerful analogy: just as fire resides invisibly within the Shami wood and only becomes manifest through the friction of churning, the all-pervading Brahman remains unmanifest within until it is awakened. Through the friction of contemplating the Mahavakya, that hidden Brahman flashes into manifestation within the Buddhi (intellect). The Buddhi acts as the cave of the heart, and within this heart, the absolute Brahman becomes a direct, visible reality. O best of sages, for those advanced Yogis whose minds have reached the state of absolute restriction (nirodha), this internal contemplation of the Mahavakya is the only essential daily action required.

When the Buddhi rests in that restricted, thoughtless ground of nirodha-chitta, it is considered Nirguna (attributeless). But when it takes the form of intense, one-pointed concentration (ekagra), it is called Saguna (with attributes).

Shiva then decoded the Mahavakya (Tat Tvam Asi - That Thou Art). The three-fold manifested universe is declared to be the meaning of the word Tvam (Thou). The unmanifest, causal reality (Avyakrita) is the meaning of the word Tat (That), and the absolute focal point or Bindu is remembered as the meaning of Asi (Art). The Saguna Brahman corresponds to Tvam. To purify and understand this, the wise analyze the nature of the Supreme Brahman immanent in the universe as four-fold. The sacred Omkara, as well as the Gayatri mantra, are considered to embody this universal Brahman.

By chanting these mantras with a one-pointed mind, the hidden divine presence becomes manifest. This one-pointed realization of Brahman contains four internal divisions located within the subtle body. The complete Brahman in the form of Omkara is established at the root, in the Muladhara chakra. The sphere of fire (Vahni-mandala) resides at this base. The sphere of the sun (Ravi-mandala) is located in the heart. And, O Brahmins, the sphere of the moon (Chandra-mandala) is situated in the Ajna chakra (the third eye) of embodied beings. The four-fold universal Brahman resides in the heart within the solar sphere, while the attributeless Nirguna reality, in the form of pure knowledge, resides in the lunar sphere. These inner structures were conceptualized by the lotus-born Brahma to assist seekers in meditation.

Through the sustained contemplation of the Mahavakya on the ground of a restricted mind, the Supreme reality resting in the Muladhara rises and becomes fully manifest in the heart. Ignited by the brilliance of its own root, this inner light grows and expands through the continuous Japa of the Omkara and the Gayatri mantra. Furthermore, the presiding deities of the six inner chakras (Shad-adhara) also attain vivid manifestation through the chanting of Vedic mantras. By merging the contemplation of the Mahavakya with mantra Japa, the exact meditational forms of these chakra deities arise and reveal themselves as sudden flashes of divine illumination (sphurti) within the heart.

Shiva then delineated the stages of meditative absorption. When the mind is completely devoid of all modifications and rests solely in the infinite form of Brahman, it is called Asamprajnata Samadhi. This supreme state can only occur on the foundation of a fully restricted mind (nirodha-chitta) and nowhere else. However, the Saguna and Nirguna aspects of the Divine are first to be meditated upon through Samprajnata Samadhi (cognitive absorption). The four-fold universal form is celebrated as Saguna, while the state established in pure knowledge is celebrated as Nirguna.

In Asamprajnata Samadhi, consciousness is first concentrated into the Bindu. Whatever worldly or universal attachments reside there are completely sublated (badhita) through the prior practice of Samprajnata Samadhi. Then, the Yogi takes absolute refuge in the Bindu. The perfection of meditating on the Saguna aspect naturally belongs to the energy residing in the Muladhara. When the Nirguna aspect is perfected, the Yogi, endowed with the ultimate success of meditation, beholds their own Self and the entire universe as Svananda—the Supreme Brahman—using the newly awakened eye of knowledge (Jnana-chakshu). This eye of knowledge is born directly from the perfection of Samprajnata Samadhi.

Describing the mechanics of this inner awakening, Shiva revealed the secret of Kundali, the Yogini-Shakti. Embodying the eight-fold Prakriti, she envelops the six Adharas (chakras) and lies dormant in the Muladhara. When scorched by the awakened fire of the root chakra, this excellent power uncoils, drops her tail, forces open the inner cosmic doors, and surges upwards to the Brahmarandhra at the crown of the head. When this occurs, the embodied soul undoubtedly beholds the radiant Brahman within the heart, standing like a blazing fire that simultaneously pervades the universe inside and out.

Finally, Shiva bridged the inner and outer worlds. He explained that by increasing the external physical fire during the Homa (fire-sacrifice), the internal fire within the heart is simultaneously nourished and amplified. Therefore, the performance of the external Homa is mandated among the daily duties (Nitya Karma). The practice of Japa alongside the twilight Sandhya prayers is an eternal law. He concluded by stating that the occasional duties (Naimittika) serve simply as the facilitators and instruments that perfect these daily foundational practices.

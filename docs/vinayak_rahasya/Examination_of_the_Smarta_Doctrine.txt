Examination of the Smarta Doctrine

Ganaka said:

The absolute, attributeless Brahman alone is the underlying substratum of Maya. Upon attaining the direct experiential realization of true Knowledge, that Maya is completely destroyed. Leaving behind all conditioned states, the individual soul becomes none other than Brahman itself. On the other hand, the reality characterized by the primal union of Consciousness and Maya is known as Saguna, which remains conjoined with Maya.

That supreme principle which is described as the unchangeable Witness of all, free from any loss of its true nature, manifests through its association with Maya as Ganesha, Shiva, Vishnu, Surya, and Shakti in the Vedas; these are the five conceptualized forms of Saguna Brahman. These are the five localized physical manifestations of the otherwise formless Brahman. To attain the complete, steady realization of the absolute Brahman, all these five forms must be structurally worshipped.

Those who are endowed with this supreme, well-balanced doctrine are celebrated as the Smartas. They are universally famous as the true followers of the immaculate doctrine of Non-dualism. Those literalists who are dedicated to the theoretical study of Vedanta but are devoid of the deep experiential science of these five forms, and who dogmatically profess only an abstract Nirguna Brahman, are considered the foremost among the foolish. They are called the Hairanyagarbhas who tread a censurable, incomplete path. True Smartas, however, possess the comprehensive scientific knowledge of these five forms while remaining deeply anchored in the ultimate Nirguna truth.

Thus, they are sung as Smartas devoted to pure non-dualism. If a person limits their knowledge to only one out of the five deities while asserting the Nirguna, they are famous merely as an ordinary Advaitin but are not recognized as a Shuddhadvaitin. All of this is fully supported by the supreme authority of the Vedas; there is no doubt about it.

The defining external emblem of the Smartas is the performance of the Panchayatana Puja. Whichever deity among the five a devotee feels the highest internal love and spiritual affinity for must be placed directly in the center as the principal deity, and the worship must be executed according to the sacred rules.

Sanaka and the other sages said:

Ganesha is proclaimed as the absolute Fifth Supreme Principle by the core Vedic doctrine. O Lord, how can He then be placed merely as part of the surrounding retinue of the other gods? Being the senior-most and supreme over the other four, He must without doubt be worshipped first!

Ganaka said:

Based on the operational distinction between the Empirical and the Absolute, the five deities are declared in the Vedas to possess two distinct, functional dimensions. The transcendent aspect of the Lord of Ganas as the Fifth ultimate Principle is His Absolute form, whereas His form residing in the Turiya state is His Empirical form. For deities like Shiva and the others, their form established in the unchanging Turiya state is celebrated as their Absolute form, while their specific expressions conjoined with individual Gunas are called their Empirical forms.

By virtue of His supremacy and senior status, Ganesha as the absolute Fifth Principle is worshipped first at the very beginning of all rites; however, in the shared companionship or assembly of the other deities, His Turiya form is worshipped.

Thus ends the Examination of the Smarta Doctrine.

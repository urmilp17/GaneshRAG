Description of Panchayatana Worship

Sanaka and the other sages said:

O Master, please describe to us the precise procedure for the Panchayatana Puja centered on Ganesha!

Ganaka said:

The Vedic followers of Ganesha in this world are celebrated as five-fold. Those dedicated to the Jnana-kanda, the Upanishadic path of knowledge, are remembered as the Ganapatya-Advaitins. Those devoted to ritual worship are called Vaidika-Ganapatyas, while those who tread the emotional path of devotion are known as Bhakta-Ganapatyas. Those Brahmins devoted to the five-fold worship are designated as Smarta-Ganapatyas. O lords of sages, for the Karma-Ganapatyas and Smarta-Ganapatyas, formal initiation is not mandatory. Others are remembered as two-fold based on the distinction between the initiated and uninitiated. Thus, through these eight sub-variations, the Vedic Ganapatyas are classified.

In the center of the Panchayatana layout sits Gunesh, the supreme principle, not merely the localized deity Ganesha. For a Smarta-Ganapatya, receiving formal Ganapatya initiation is considered supreme. O twice-born ones, if initiation is taken according to the path described in the Gargya Kalpa, then the five-fold worship must be performed as a separate daily duty, just like the Sandhya prayers and Japa. It must be performed either during late morning or noon; there is no doubt about this. In the five-fold worship, the setup and placement of Ganesha is of two kinds.

O lords of sages, regarding the placement of the Sun and Shakti, aligned with the direction of the entrance doorway: Shakti is to be placed in the South-West when aligned with the body of Gunesh, or Ravi is placed in the South-West; thus the structural variation is described. Bowing first to Gajanana, Siddhi, Buddhi, Ganaka, and the foremost among Ganapatyas in due order, one should perform the Japa and worship. One should bow to all deities sequentially using the specific meditation verses of their mantras.

One should bow to Brahma, Shankara, Vishnu, Ravi, Shakti, Guha, Bhrigu, Bhrushundi, Mudgala, Gargya, and Vyasa—all these revered lineages of Ganapatyas. Having bowed to these Ganapatya gurus and sequentially to the supreme Ganaka, one should gather the worship offerings, starting with fresh blades of Durva grass. Having performed elemental purification of the body and other preliminary rites, one should sit down for worship. To ensure completion without obstacles, one should first invoke Ganesha.

Worship should be performed in sandalwood paste using brief offerings. After stating the solemn resolve with the lunar day and day of the week, one should perform the entrance doorway worship. Gunesh, who represents the transcendental Fourth, is eternally endowed with a single tusk. Based on the distinction between two tusks and a single tusk, Ganeshashana is remembered as two-fold. The essential nature of Brahman in its absolute completeness is represented as possessing two tusks. On the other hand, the attributeless Lord of Ganas is endowed with a broken left tusk. O best of sages, in His Saguna form, He possesses a broken right tusk. Thus, two forms of Ganesha are described based on the division of time, state, and function. The Nirguna aspect resides within Buddhi, while the Saguna aspect resides within Siddhi. Gajanana, the Lord of Svananda, abides as the master of both Siddhi and Buddhi.

Shakti, Surya, Vishnu, and Shiva act as the door-guardians of Svanandesha. Gajanana, the Lord of Svananda, is the very Self of the Fifth Supreme Principle. In the doorway worship of the Fifth Principle, starting sequentially from the East: Marjari, Kamala-Vishnu, Viraja, Girija-Shiva, Ashraya-Shaktishvaras, Mukta, Surya, and Prabha. In the doorway worship of Gunesh, Rati and Manmatha are invoked in the West. Mahi and Varaha are declared to be placed sequentially in the North. Thus, the entrance doorway worship of Gunesh must be performed.

After performing the doorway worship, one should worship the divine mount. Offering prayers at the tip of the bell, one should ring the sacred bell. After chanting the root mantra of Ganesha, one should perform the worship of the sacred pot, followed by the conch worship and throne worship. Meditating on Ganesha, one invokes Him with the Vedic mantra "Gananam Tva...". With the mantra "Pra No Devi...", one invokes Goddess Sarasvati on the right. Next, with the verse "Gandhadvara...", one invokes Lakshmi on the left. In the North-East, one invokes Keshava with the mantra "Vishnor Aratam Asi...". In the South-East, one invokes Shiva with the Tryambaka mantra; in the South-West, the Sun God with the mantra "A Satyena..."; and in the North-West, the Supreme Goddess, invoking Her with the mantra "Tam Agni-varnam...", in due order.

Then one offers the seat, foot-washing water, respectful offering, water for sipping, and Madhuparka, giving these to the surrounding family of deities. Then one bathes the Lord with the hymns of all five deities, or with the Ganesha Sukta, or with the Purusha Sukta. Having ritualistically bathed Ganesha along with His surrounding family of deities, one lifts Him up, establishes Him on the throne, and offers garments and the sacred thread. One then sequentially offers vermilion, sandalwood paste, blades of Durva grass, flowers, and unbroken rice grains using Vedic mantras and the 21 sacred names.

One worships Ganesha, Siddhi, and Buddhi with 25 names, Vishnu with 12 names, and Shiva and the other deities with 10 names each, and similarly, Lord Bhaskara with 25 names, worshipping each deity with their most beloved sacred leaves and flowers. Then one worships Gananayaka with His 108 divine names, followed by incense, lamp, food offering, betel leaves, and monetary offering. Next, one performs wave-offering of lights, offering of flowers with mantras, prostrations, and circumambulation. Then one chants the root mantras of Ganesha, Siddhi, and Buddhi. After chanting the mantras of Vishnu, Shambhu, Surya, and Shakti according to one's capacity and making ritual libations, one meditates on the Lord and concludes the worship.

This is declared as the standard worship for the Smarta-Ganapatyas. O best of sages, for those who are formally initiated, the five-fold worship is not their primary duty; for them, the direct worship of Svanandesha is celebrated as primary.

Thus ends the Description of Panchayatana Worship.

Exposition on the Nature of Ishvara

Sanaka and the other sages said:

O exalted one, kindly explain to us the true nature of Ishvara.

Ganaka said:

From the Saguna Bindu, composed of the complete syllable Om, from the Supreme Lord, arises Mahamaya in the form of sound, possessing the nature of pure Sattva. Within that arises Brahman, associated with the three qualities of Maya. This is Ishvara, conditioned by Maya, while the Purusha is of the Turiya state. He is spoken of as the Unmanifest and is also called the Lord of the Gunas. Known as the Great Cause, He bears the body of the Turiya state. From Him arose Maya, predominated by Tamas, having the nature of causality. 

Thus Ishvara is described as endowed with the causal body. He alone is the cause of both the gross and the subtle universes. Thus, O great sages, Ishvara is described as twofold in the Vedas and allied texts. Endowed with the collective subtle body, He is known as Hiranyagarbha. Associated with the gross body, He is remembered in the Shruti as Virat.

Thus ends the Exposition on the Nature of Ishvara.

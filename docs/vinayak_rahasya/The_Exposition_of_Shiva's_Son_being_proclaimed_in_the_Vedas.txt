The Exposition of Shiva's Son Being Proclaimed in the Vedas

Narada and others said:

Is there any scriptural authority in the Vedas for Shiva's son, Ganesha?

Surya said:

It exists in the Shri Rudram hymn and in the Shivopanishad, in due order. The salutation "To the Ganas and to the Ganapatis" is stated in the Namakam. And "And to Vinayaka" is mentioned in the Shivopanishad, in due order. This form of Shiva is also proclaimed in the Jabala Upanishad. And in all four Vedas, there are hymns dedicated to Brahmanaspati.

O great souls! There are ten principal Upanishads and also the hundred Upanishads. The son of Shiva is to be worshipped with Rudra mantras and with Ganapati mantras. This Ganesha, who is Brahmanaspati, is to be worshipped with Ganapati mantras. If worshipped with Rudra mantras, he becomes a giver of obstacles. He becomes pleased by the recitation of the Vedas alone, without a doubt. The Purusha Sukta and other hymns are heard in the Vedas to praise the Lord of the Ganas. And with those hymns of the Lord of the Ganas, the worship of Ganapati is performed. This is approved by the Puranas, Vedas, and Shastras; there is no doubt here.

The direct Self of Ganesha is Brahmanaspati. The direct body of Ganesha is the supreme Turiya state of consciousness.

Thus concludes the Exposition of Shiva's Son Being Proclaimed in the Vedas.

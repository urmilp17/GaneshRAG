The Description of the Nature of Ganaka

Sanaka and the assembled sages addressed their spiritual guide, saying, "O Deshika (Master)! Please kindly reveal to us the true, esoteric nature of Ganaka."

Ganaka replied, explaining his own profound cosmic identity. He stated that the supreme consciousness conditioned by the aggregate of all individual souls (Vyashti-sanghata), which resides in the transcendent Fourth State (Turiya), is famously known as the Kutastha—the unshakeable, foundational consciousness. This very Kutastha is what is remembered and personified as Ganaka.

He further revealed his administrative and spiritual roles. He alone is Hamsa (the pure, discerning swan of consciousness), acting as the supreme Commander-in-Chief of the Lord of Svananda, Brahmanaspati. Across all the manifested worlds, he is celebrated as the primordial, original Guru. Because he serves as the preceptor of the Supreme Brahman itself, he is universally acclaimed as the Guru of Gurus.

Thus ends the Description of the Nature of Ganaka.

The Description of the Nature of Laksha and Labha

Sanaka and the sages then asked, "O Deshika! Please kindly explain to us the true nature of Laksha and Labha."

Ganaka replied, revealing the cosmic origin of these divine sons. He explained that when Lord Ganesha cast His divine, empowering glance upon His consort Buddhi (Wisdom) while she was absorbed in deep meditation (Samadhi), the mighty Laksha was born. In the exact same manner, from His divine glance upon Siddhi (Attainment) during her state of Samadhi, Labha was born.

Ganaka then decoded their deeper metaphysical identities. Labha, who is the bestower of all gains, prosperity, and profit, is actually the alternate manifestation (Apara-murti) of Avyakta (Gunesha, the unmanifest Chief Minister). On the other hand, Laksha, who grants the ultimate goal, target, and focus, is the alternate manifestation of the Kutastha (Ganaka himself).

Therefore, while they are worshipped throughout the world as the two beloved sons of the Fifth Supreme Self (Ganesha), they are fundamentally the active, operative forms of Ganaka and Avyakta (Gunesha) functioning within the universal Brahman.

Thus ends the Description of the Nature of Laksha and Labha.

The Refutation of Pseudo-Vedic Shaivism and Vaishnavism

Lord Brahma continued his narration, describing the journey of the great Guru Ganaka through the sacred lands.

Brahma said:
"At Madhyarjuna, the sacred site of the Mahalingam, the Guru worshipped Lord Shiva, the bestower of liberation. After completing the worship, he sat there with his disciples and the king who accompanied him.

Upon arriving at that holy place, the excellent Brahmins of the region came forward, bowed to the Guru with reverence, and spoke to him.

The Brahmins said:
'O Lord of Sages, we are devoted worshippers of Shambhu, following the Vedic path with steadfast faith. We were guided and instructed by a great Yogi, a knower of Brahman, who resides in the Heranda forest.

In this land, we have all been purified through the proper performance of penance and have become established in the conduct befitting Brahmins. We are now steadfast in the practices prescribed by the Vedas and Smritis, devoted to the scriptures, and we perform the worship of the Linga daily, just as we perform the Sandhya prayers.

Even during periods of ritual impurity, such as after a death in the family, just as the Sandhya is performed in a shortened manner, we perform Linga-worship through the abbreviated or mental method. In the occasional rites prescribed by the Vedas and Smritis, we also worship the specific deities designated for those times.

However, in our daily worship, we worship no other god but Shiva. O best of Sages, thus we remain firmly devoted to the Vedic path.'
The great Ganaka listened to their words with patience. When they had finished, he began to speak, his voice carrying the weight of profound wisdom.

Ganaka said:
"O Brahmins, the daily duty prescribed for your Varna is the chanting of the Vedas during Sandhya. Ritual worship of deities, such as Archana, is not strictly listed among the mandatory daily duties for all in the Vedic tradition. The great Yogis have not classified such rituals as compulsory for everyone.
Know this clearly: the status of being 'twice-born' arises from the acceptance and chanting of the Mother of Vedas, the Gayatri mantra. One does not become a Brahmin simply by performing Linga-worship. According to the Vedic injunctions, a Brahmin who does not worship Shiva does not thereby lose his Brahminhood. To establish one's status in a Varna, the chanting of the Vedas alone is prescribed; the ritual worship of a specific deity is not a requirement for that identity.

The ritual worship of deities is described in the sectarian traditions, the paths specific to each deity. Even in those traditions, the primary status of certain deities is mentioned in the Vedas themselves.

Mantra-initiation is the first step into a specific path; the ritual worship of the deity is the second. It is through receiving proper Mantra-initiation that one truly enters and becomes established in a particular Mata, a spiritual path. Four types of initiation are described to awaken the spirit of worship. A Brahmin should never abandon his Diksha for any reason.

Those who have received a special initiation must not abandon deity-worship. Even during times of impurity, they are eligible to perform it in a specific manner. For a disciple, external worship during impurity should be done through the abbreviated method, wearing wet clothes; this is stated in the scriptures on initiation.

The worship of Ganesha, the worship of the threshold, the worship of the Kalasha, the invocation of the deity, and the chanting of the root mantra along with the worship of the seat—these are the five limbs of worship stated in the ritual manuals. By which path do you perform your worship? Is it through the path of initiation, or without it?"

The Brahmins replied:
"Revered Guru, we know nothing of Vedic or Tantric initiation. We received the mantra and the method of Vedic worship directly from the Guru's mouth. Filled with devotion to Shiva, we accepted it with faith and perform daily Linga-worship according to the Vedic way."

Ganaka said:
"You are all uninitiated. Your primary focus should therefore be on your Varna-dharma. In the context of Varna-dharma, the ritual worship of other deities, without the foundation of the Gayatri, and the chanting of other mantras during times of impurity, is condemned.

Therefore, for you who are without initiation, Linga-worship should be performed daily only with the Panchakshara mantra, the five-syllable prayer to Shiva. During times of impurity, it must be completely avoided; this is the truth.

Furthermore, before worshipping the Lingam, Ganesha must be worshipped, even if only through a simple offering of sandalwood paste. Of this there is no doubt; otherwise, Lord Shiva Himself becomes displeased. The worship of Shambhu performed without first worshipping Ganesha is fruitless. Worship performed without the threshold worship is said to be only half-complete.

If you have true faith in the Vedic tradition and desire to perform daily Linga-worship, O Brahmins, then receive the divine Pashupata initiation through a proper Guru and perform Shiva-worship according to the path described in the Kalpa-shastras.

The great Sage Gautama extracted the essence of the Vedas and Upanishads and composed the Kalpa-shastra for the perfection of the path of worship. Those Shaivas who possess intense devotion and desire liberation must follow the rules of the Gautamiya Kalpa-shastra. One should accept the Pashupata initiation; through Diksha, one attains liberation."

Hearing this, some among the Shaivas, understanding the importance of proper initiation, received the sacred Diksha. Others, however, remained uninitiated but continued to worship the Supreme Lord in their own way.

At that moment, the great sage Heranda, the greatest among the knowers of Brahman, came to see the Guru. He bowed, worshipped him with devotion, and sat by his side. Seeing him, the eminent Guru Ganaka spoke:

"By my command, instruct the Ganapatyas and Shaivas here in their respective scriptures, including the Puranas and Upanishads. Explain to them the distinct meanings and essential truths of these texts."

Having given this command, Ganaka, accompanied by his followers, worshipped Shveta-Vighnesha, the White Ganesha. He then proceeded to the city of Halasya and from there continued his journey to Kerala. In due course, he reached the sacred Badari-vana with his followers.

There, he worshipped Ganesha, the bestower of all spiritual accomplishments. Honored and worshipped by the divine sages Nara and Narayana, he sat down in that holy place. The people living nearby, along with his own followers, approached him. Bowing with devotion, they all spoke to the Guru with joy.
The People said:
"O World Guru, we are all residents of Aryavarta. By the grace of the great sage Dattatreya, after performing the required penance, we were purified and re-established in our Varna-dharma by that great Yogi.

We worship Vishnu, the Lord and protector of all worlds. We wear the vertical Tilak, the Urdhva-pundra, we chant the eight-syllabled mantra, the Ashtakshara, and we worship Lord Keshava in the Salagrama stone with sacred Tulsi leaves.

We perform all Shrauta and Smarta rites according to the injunctions of the Vedas. In our daily worship, we worship no other god but Vishnu. At the beginning, we merely offer a prayer for the worship of Ganesha, but we do not perform any specific ritual for him."

The Ganaka said:
"You are uninitiated and are therefore considered Vedic Vaishnavas in the general sense. However, know this: after performing the worship of Ganesha in an image or symbol, only then should the worship of your chosen deity be performed. This is the command of the Vedas. Otherwise, the performer does not obtain the full merit of the worship.

First, perform the worship of Ganesha in a brief manner or through a simple offering in sandalwood paste. Only after that should you perform the worship of Hari in the Salagrama. If one skips the worship of Ganesha out of delusion or ignorance, his claim to be following the Vedic path is not established. Rather, such a man becomes like a heretic."

Hearing these words of wisdom, the Brahmins and others understood the importance of this principle. They replied with humility, "We shall do as you say, O Guru of the World." Saying this and bowing to him with deep reverence, they departed from his presence, their hearts filled with joy and their understanding clarified.

Thus ends the Refutation of Pseudo-Vedic Shaivism and Vaishnavism.

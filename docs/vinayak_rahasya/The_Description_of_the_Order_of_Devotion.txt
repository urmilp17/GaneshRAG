The Description of the Order of Devotion

Sanaka and the assembled sages approached Ganaka, recognizing him as the master of all cosmic principles. They asked, "O Lord, please kindly explain to us the precise, sequential path of worship for a soul seeking refuge among the five great deities."

Ganaka replied, explaining that an embodied being must sequentially take refuge in Shakti, Surya, Ramesha (Vishnu), Isha (Shiva), and finally Ganesha. However, the sequence depends entirely on the practitioner's spiritual orientation. Ganaka revealed three distinct pathways prescribed in the Vedas to reach the ultimate absolute.

The First Path: The Sequence of Spiritual Evolution (Karma-Mula)
For human beings, the spiritual journey inevitably begins with action (Karma). Because Surya (the Sun) is the presiding deity of Karma and the physical world, the seeker must first take refuge in the Saura path. Once action is perfected, the soul moves to the Vaishnava path to understand the distinctive, glorious attributes (Vaishishtya) of the Divine. From Vishnu, the soul progresses to the Shaiva path of asceticism and dissolution, and then to the Shakta path of cosmic energy. Finally, transcending all attributes, the soul arrives at the attributeless (Nirguna) Ganesha path. This is the path of highest upward progression (Urdhvagati).

The Second Path: The Sequence of Human Goals (Purushartha-Krama)
Alternatively, for a mortal deeply anchored in faith and the structured goals of human life (Purusharthas), the sequence changes. Such a person begins by establishing righteousness (Dharma). To perfect Dharma, they must take refuge in Vishnu and his consort Rama (Lakshmi). Next, to attain wealth and meaning (Artha), they must worship Shambhu (Shiva). To fulfill and master worldly desires (Kama), they take refuge in Goddess Shivaa (Shakti). To achieve liberation from the cycle of birth and death (Moksha), they worship Surya. Finally, to transcend even Moksha and attain the absolute state of becoming Brahman itself (Brahmabhuya), they must take refuge in the Fifth Supreme Principle, Gajanana. This sequence is known as the Sequence of Gateways (Dvara-krama).

The Third Path: The Sequence of Ritual Worship (Upasana-Krama)
For a seeker whose primary method is intense ritual worship and devotion (Upasana), the sequence is altered to maximize divine grace. This practitioner must first take refuge in the Goddess (Devi), because the Divine Mother is the quickest to melt with compassion and bestow her grace (Kshipra-prasadini). Having secured her grace, the seeker sequentially worships Surya, Vishnu, and Sadashiva. Ultimately, supported by the grace of all these deities, the seeker takes refuge in Gajanana and attains the supreme state of Brahman.

Ganaka concluded by stating that these three distinct pathways are clearly proclaimed in the Vedas. Regardless of which of the three paths a soul takes, Vishnu, Shiva, Surya, and Shakti act as the essential "Gateway Deities" (Dvara-devas). One must pass through their grace and domains to finally reach the supreme, non-dual destination of Ganesha.

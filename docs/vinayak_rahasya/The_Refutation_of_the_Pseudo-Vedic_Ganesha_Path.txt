The Refutation of the Pseudo-Vedic Ganesha Path

The sages, their minds still immersed in the grand narrative of the divine descents, felt a new curiosity stir within them. They pressed for further clarity, their voices filled with a seeker's earnestness.

The Sages Said:
“O Lotus-born One! In those eras dominated by deluded and misguided doctrines, established firmly upon the earth, please tell us when and in which sacred places their refutation took place. Out of your compassion, relate all of this to us, for we yearn to understand the complete triumph of the Truth.”

A gentle light of approval shone in Brahma’s eyes. He nodded, preparing to unveil the deeper philosophical battles that underpinned the Ganaka’s earthly missions.

Brahma Said:

“Listen, O wise ones, to the account of how the light of the Veda was restored. The knowers of the Veda understand that Gunesha, the Lord of Qualities, alone is the supreme Ganaka, the great being who is the master of the three Gunas and the comprehender of the fourth state, Turiya. From Him, as if from a gentle exhalation of divine breath, the eternal Vedas themselves emerged. It is this Deva, the very father of the Vedas, who, by His own will, assumed the form of Ganaka and descended to the earthly plane.

He is the Guru of all the worlds, the Guru of Gurus, whom even Shiva, the devout one, serves and beside whom He always remains.

For the establishment of the sacred sciences at the very dawn of the Krita Yuga, the great yogi Angirasa and the king of sages, Yogi Mudgala, arrived. Mudgala had a most excellent and dear disciple, who was among the five principal yogis. This disciple, along with his gurus, assumed a Siddha, or perfected, form and went to the court of King Somaraja to behold a divine child who was an incarnation of the prince of Ganas. ‘This is my son, Bhrigu, the disciple of Yogi Mudgala,’ it was declared. Yet, know that yogis like Sanaka and others existed even before Bhrigu and Mudgala.

In that era, people who followed the paths of the Daityas, the demons, had also been born. They too had come to King Somaraja's court to see the child. It was in that very assembly that the great sage Bhrigu explained the profound non-dualistic doctrine of Ganesha, the Ganeshadvaita Siddhanta. The Ganaka himself, as the Supreme Guru, along with Mudgala and the five great yogis, respectfully but firmly refuted the doctrines of those who preached non-Vedic views.

The sage Bhrigu later compiled that very doctrine in the form of sutras. At the end of the Dvapara Yuga, under his own authority, he composed this excellent scripture. Thus say the great yogis; this specific refutation did not exist in the Krita Yuga. This scripture was composed in that later age, O Vedic scholars. They studied the incomparable Ganesha doctrine from the path of the Guru, and the scriptures concerning Ganita and others were composed on earth once more.

By the command of the prince-Ganesha, for the refutation of false doctrines and the re-establishment of the Vedic path across the globe, a great mission was undertaken. Attended by his four disciples, served by crores of Siddhas, and accompanied by Shambhu, Somanatha, and various groups of attendants, the yogi Bhrigu set out. With joy, he went from Somanathapura to Trayuresha. He bathed in the sacred waters of the Kamandalu-bhava along with his companions, performed the worship of the Lord of Bhumisvana, and undertook the daily pilgrimage.

There, in the presence of Mayuresha, surrounded by all, the Guru of Gurus, Bhrigu, sat upon the divine Siddhasana, facing Ganesha. Brahmins, Kshatriyas, and others came to the assembly and approached the great sage. After bowing to him, they sat around him according to his command. Among them, a learned and eminent Brahmin named Mayureshvara Sharma, spoke to the Siddha Ganaka with deep devotion.

The Brahmin said: "O Guru, in this holy place, a strange practice has taken root. A statue of a Vaishya named Pramatta was installed. People, creating a form and worshipping it for a long time, became devoid of their proper duties. Then, the deluded people here, following a Tantric path, worshipped Ganapati with devotion, while some followed heretical paths. After the destruction of the deluded daitya Matta, my father, a noble Brahmin named Garga, well-versed in scriptures, prescribed an expiation on the authority of a Tantric text. After performing it, he authoritatively taught the Siddhanta of the Vedic path. Now, people from the three varnas, taking refuge in the Ganapa path, worship Gajanana. They are all followers of what they call Vaidika Ganesha. The Shudras and Sutas are followers of the Tantric Ganapatya path. My father became the Guru for the people here, and I, following his command, now remain in his position.

I will now speak the Siddhanta of our path, as told by my father. In the Vedic scriptures, the Brahman alone is described as the cause of all. Based on the reality of Sound and Meaning, Its names and forms are conceived. All the names and forms in the Vedas are indeed His. All rituals and meditations are for Him alone. Therefore, O Noble One, all Vedic mantras are His mantras, and the various hymns are His praises. Though there are many, for the satisfaction of the Supreme Self, one should chant a single mantra in His presence, a mantra that identifies Him with the names of Shambhu, Ganesha, and Surya. His worship should be performed with this understanding. ‘Except for the One, there is no second’—this is the view in our doctrine, O Guru!"

Having heard his well-established conclusion, the Guru, Ganaka, spoke to him.

Ganaka Said:

“O Best of Brahmins! The statement 'Brahman is one alone, without a second' is indeed an authoritative statement from the Upanishads. However, Brahman associated with names and forms becomes conditioned. The individual soul and the Lord are also conditioned by their respective limiting adjuncts. Ishvara is said to be five-fold based on different aggregates of these adjuncts, and the individual souls are infinite in their diversity. Inferiority and superiority among them arise due to the gradation of their limiting adjuncts.

Just as differences in water are perceived based on its source, so it is with Brahman. Through association with the subtle creative power, the state of having a subtle adjunct came into being. Through the collective Maya, the state of being the Lord manifested. Through individual Maya, the state of being an individual soul was born. Thus, multiplicity is proclaimed due to differences in the limiting adjuncts.

The consciousness present in divine bodies is not the same as in mortal bodies. The consciousness in the five principal deities is distinct and possesses abilities not seen in mortals. The deities mentioned in the ritualistic section of the Vedas are associated with specific spheres of authority, like various officials of a king.

Gunesha, who embodies the three Gunas, holds the unified form of the five deities. Endowed with all authority, He is proclaimed like a Crown Prince. The supreme Brahman with attributes, the excellent fifth one, is the Ganapa. Possessing independent power, He is the cause of even the fourth state, Turiya. He is the 'Eldest King,' the nature of witnessing consciousness. The Vedic scholars know that this very one is the King of Ganas.

Thus, the distinction among the deities stated in the Vedas is proclaimed. While all authority ultimately resides in the one king, it is delegated. O Brahmin! You speak of a single essence, but your application is not approved by the Veda. The names in the Vedas are like entities under the rule of a single sovereign. To chant the hymns of Shiva, Vishnu, etc., in the very presence of the King of Ganas, is like praising a minister in the king's own court—it is condemnable and will not please Him. Indeed, it may invoke His obstinate nature.

The path you have described is indeed the path of 'Ganesha-abhasa,' a false reflection of the true path. From the Emperor down to the servant, all are officials, but one must have the understanding of a king towards the king, and a servant towards the servant. To reverse this brings adverse results. If you go to a king and say, 'You are the best minister,' it would not be pleasing. Similarly, chanting the names of Shiva near Ganesha is not pleasing. However, chanting Ganesha's mantras in the presence of Shiva and others is considered pleasing to them, for all other deities are His attendants.

Therefore, O Brahmin! Abandoning your path—which is the 'Vaidikaganeshabhasa-marga,' the false appearance of a Vedic Ganesha path—you should take refuge in the true Vedic Ganapata path. Along with the Brahmins here, following the path of the Ganesha Shastra, worship the Lord Ganesha. Then you will surely attain Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, which is the state of being one with Brahman. This is the teaching of the Vedas.”

Having heard this, the excellent Brahmin bowed down to the Guru of Gurus and asked with humility for the authentic texts that establish the truth of Ganesha. The Guru, the knower of this truth, blessed them all and instructed his devoted disciple, Bhruśuṇḍi, son of Mudgala, to go to the middle country and properly instruct the people there in the true doctrine of Ganapata. Accepting the command, the excellent sage Bhruśuṇḍi went forth, purifying the paths of doctrine and establishing the true Vedic orthodoxy, his journey ultimately leading him to the sacred city of Kānchī, preceded by disciples like Somaraja, to spread the unadulterated light of Ganesha.

The Description of the Wearing of Sacred Marks

Sanaka and the assembled sages posed a highly practical, ritualistic question to their master, Ganaka. They asked, "O Lord, the various sects are easily identified by the markings on their foreheads. What is the proper procedure for the Smarta-Ganapatyas? How should they wear their sacred marks (Pundras)?"

Ganaka replied by categorizing the universally recognized Vedic marks. He explained that the Vedas and subsequent scriptures describe four primary geometric shapes for sacred forehead marks: the vertical upward lines (Urdhvapundra, typically associated with Vishnu), the three horizontal lines (Tripundra, typically associated with Shiva), the circular dot (Vartula), and the half-mark or crescent (Ardhapundra).

Different sects utilize different materials and shapes to signify their spiritual allegiance. Ganaka noted that the followers of the Goddess (Shaktas) are identified by their use of Kumkuma (bright red powder) for their Tilaka. The Sauras, who worship the Sun God, traditionally wear a perfectly circular mark (Mandala).

The Ganapatyas (exclusive worshippers of Ganesha), on the other hand, are distinctly identified by their use of divine sandalwood paste (Divya-gandha). Ganaka added that in terms of the actual physical substance used, there is no difference between the Sauras and the Ganapatyas—both predominantly utilize sandalwood paste.

However, Ganaka delivered a crucial, overriding instruction specifically for the Smarta-Ganapatyas (those who worship the five deities with Ganesha at the center). Because the Smarta tradition emphasizes harmony, social duty, and respect for ancestry, a Smarta-Ganapatya must never abandon the specific shape of the forehead mark inherited through their family lineage (Kula-krama). If a devotee's ancestors wore the Vaishnava vertical lines or the Shaiva horizontal lines, the devotee must not discard that ancestral shape.

Instead of changing the shape to denote their personal devotion to Ganesha, the Smarta-Ganapatya should honor their family tradition by maintaining the ancestral shape, but apply it joyfully using the materials sacred to Lord Ganesha: Sindura (the bright orange-red vermilion highly favored by Ganesha) or divine sandalwood paste. In this way, they perfectly balance their familial heritage with their personal spiritual devotion.

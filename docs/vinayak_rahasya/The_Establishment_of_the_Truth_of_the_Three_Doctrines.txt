The Establishment of the Truth of the Three Doctrines

Lord Brahma continued his narration, describing how the great Guru Ganaka resolved the apparent contradictions between different paths.

Brahma said:
"After the previous groups had departed, some other seekers approached. They bowed with reverence and spoke to the Guru of Gurus, Ganaka, their hearts filled with sincere inquiry.

The other men said:
'O Revered Guru, we are disciples of the great sage Dattatreya, purified and instructed by him. We worship Shambhu as the Fourth, the Turiya, the Supreme Lord.

According to our understanding, Brahma, Vishnu, and Mahesha—the Trinity—are born from His attributes. By the command of Mahadeva, they perform the functions of creation, maintenance, and destruction of the universe.

Through the power of penance, Rudra attained a form similar to that of the Turiya. This Rudra, who possesses the name and form of the Turiya, is associated with the Gunas, the qualities of nature.

The elephant-faced Lord, Ganesha, is the son of that transcendental Maheshwara. He holds the lordship over his own attendants and the supreme lordship over all obstacles. The Supreme Lord granted to his son, Gananatha, the state of being worshipped by all gods and the honor of being invoked first in all sacred rites.

Such is the Supreme Lord who is called the Fourth. However, some Vaishnavas here claim that Achyuta, Vishnu, is the Turiya. They question how Mahavishnu, who is a form of the Gunas, can be the transcendental Turiya. They further assert that Ganesha attained the status of first-worship only through the excellence of Shiva's boon, implying a dependent status.

On the other hand, the Ganapatyas here proclaim Ganesha to be the Fifth Brahman, the ultimate reality beyond even the Turiya. O Guru, we are confused by these conflicting claims. Please tell us what among these is the truth according to the Vedas and Vedanta."

The great Ganaka listened to their words with patience and compassion. A serene smile graced his lips as he prepared to unveil the wisdom that harmonizes all paths.

The Ganaka said:
"Know this, O sincere seekers: the doctrines of all three paths are true according to the testimony of the Vedas. There is no real contradiction when one understands the deeper truth.

Among the six beginningless entities spoken of in the scriptures, there is that which is the union of Consciousness and Maya, the primal creative power. The Ganapatyas speak of the Fifth, Ganesha, as that reality which is established like the Attributeless Brahman—the immutable Witness of all—based on the authority of the Vedic scriptures. This is one level of truth.

The collective Parameshwara, the Supreme Lord mentioned among these beginningless entities, is remembered in two ways in the Vedas: as the Empirical Lord and as the Absolute Lord. This distinction is crucial for understanding.

The Absolute Lord is the pure, unconditioned consciousness. However, for the purpose of creation and divine play, this Absolute becomes conditioned by the Maya of pure Sattva. That which is reflected in this pure Sattva-Maya is like the sun reflected in calm water. The knowers of the Veda understand this reflection as the Empirical Lord.

Shambhu, the auspicious one, is the Absolute Lord, the Paramartha-Ishvara. Hari, Vishnu, is the Empirical Lord, the Vyavahara-Ishvara, the reflection of the Absolute in pure Sattva. They speak of the Turiya Lord as the body that supports the three Gunas. Therefore, the Vaishnavas refer to the reflected Lord, the Abhasa-Isha, as the Turiya. The Shaivas, on the other hand, call the Absolute Lord the Turiya. Both are correct, speaking of different aspects of the one reality.

In the divine play of the three Gunas, this Rudra is of the nature of Shankara, the Absolute. Similarly, Vishnu is of the nature of Vasudeva, the all-pervading Lord who sustains the universe. There is no contradiction, no cause for rivalry or malice among these deities. They are different manifestations of the one supreme reality, revealing themselves according to the needs and capacities of devotees.

The Ganapatyas, in their worship, focus on the ultimate source, the Fifth, from which even the Turiya emerges. The Shaivas and Vaishnavas each worship the Turiya, but from different perspectives—one as the Absolute, the other as the Empirical Lord reflected in pure Sattva. All are valid paths leading to the same truth."

A profound understanding dawned upon the Shaiva disciples as they heard these words. Their confusion melted away, replaced by a vision of the harmonious unity underlying all paths. They bowed to the Guru with deep gratitude and departed, their hearts filled with joy.

At that moment, the great Yogi Dattatreya himself arrived to see the Guru of Gurus. He bowed with reverence, worshipped him according to the prescribed rules, and sat by his side. Seeing this eminent sage, Ganaka spoke:

"O high-minded Dattatreya, to the mortals residing in Aryavarta who follow the Vedic path and are devoted to the Six Paths, the Shanmata, you must teach their respective truths. Provide them also with the scriptures corresponding to each path, so that all may find their way to the supreme goal."

Hearing these words of the Guru, the great sage Dattatreya accepted the command with reverence, bowed once more, and departed joyfully to carry out this sacred mission.

Then, surrounded by his group of disciples led by Somaraja and others, the Yogi sage Ganaka, who resided on the banks of the Ganges, proceeded to the land of the Rishis to continue his divine work of establishing Dharma.

Thus ends the Establishment of the Truth of the Three Doctrines.

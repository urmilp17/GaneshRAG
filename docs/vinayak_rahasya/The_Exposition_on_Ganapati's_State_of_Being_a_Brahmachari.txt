The Exposition on Ganapati's State of Being a Brahmachari

Sages like Sanaka said:

Some also say that Lord Ganapati is a Brahmachari. How can the Lord, who is united with Siddhi and Buddhi, be a Brahmachari?

The Teacher said:

Due to the distinctions in Karma, Jnana, and Upasana, there are distinctions associated with the three sections of the Vedas. Because He is the bestower of the respective Siddhis in each, He is worshipped in each of them. The Pranava, known as Brahman, has the form of the four-fold universe. Because the syllables of Pranava move through the four states of consciousness, and because He, the Lord of Brahman, moves in the forms of the waking state, Brahman, and so on, He is called a Brahmachari, one who moves in Brahman. This is stated in the four Vedas as well.

In every stage designated as Jnana, Upasana, Karma, and Bhakti, which are embodied by various deities, due to His innate nature as the Eldest Lord, and because He moves with the inherent nature of being the first-worshipped, the Lord of Brahman, the son of Rudra, born from the elephant, is called a Brahmachari. He appears as one established in the Brahmacharya stage of life, devoid of taking a wife, yet endowed with forms like a child, a youth, and an old man. For the protection of Brahmacharya, He manifested with an elephant head. The son of Shambhu, born of the elephant, is undoubtedly a Brahmachari.

Though united with Siddhi and Buddhi, the Lord Himself, like Rudra and other gods, is called a Brahmachari because He is devoid of the act of sexual intercourse. When He was born from the body of Parvati for the destruction of the wicked, He assumed the state of being a son to Shiva, Parvati, and others. Even though associated with Siddhi and Buddhi at that time, He was born without sexual union. Therefore, the Lord of Brahman is declared to be a Brahmachari.

He is associated with all states of consciousness and yet free from all states. He is associated with all castes and stages of life and yet free from all castes and stages. He is united with all Maya and yet free from all Maya. The Remover of Obstacles ever abides as Brahman Himself.

Thus concludes the Exposition on Ganapati's State of Being a Brahmachari.

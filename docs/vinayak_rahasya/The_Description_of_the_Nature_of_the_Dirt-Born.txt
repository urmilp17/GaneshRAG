The Description of the Nature of the Dirt-Born

Sanaka and the other sages said:

The Shaiva Brahmins say that Sadashiva cut off the head of the Ganesha who was born from the dirt of Uma's body. By this account, Ganesha's inferiority to Shiva is established.

Ganaka said:

The history of the dirt-born is celebrated as two-fold. In this specific Kalpa, Ganeshana, the Fifth Supreme Principle, was born from dirt. O lords of sages, in this Kalpa, Ganeshana was born from dirt. Shambhu, the bearer of the Turiya state, resided in Kailasa with Uma for the protection of the universe. His power, Goddess Uma, undertook strict vows and pleased the elephant-faced Ganesha through penance to obtain a son. Thus, Lord Ganeshana granted her an excellent boon. Parvati, the daughter of the mountain, whose body had become covered with dust and dirt while she remained in penance for the boon, pleased Shankara.

Seeing Parvati in such a state, Girijapati said: "The dirt resting upon your body shall undoubtedly become worthy of worship by the greatest of gods, beginning with myself, and by the sages." Saying this, Shambhu ritualistically embraced Parvati. With a joyful heart, he reached Kailasa and engaged in divine play with the Mother of the Universe. Sometime later, while bathing, Parvati gathered the dirt born of her body and placed it on the ground. From that, a boy was born. O lord of sages, in his previous life, this soul was famously known as a devotee of Shakti.

Having abandoned the worship of Shambhu, he had joyfully pleased Para-Shakti. Though devoid of formal Shakta initiation, he was entirely dedicated to devotion. Purely through the glory of his devotion, he attained ultimate union with Shakti. Now, in this Kalpa, by the power of that same devotion, he was born as the son of Mahadeva and Parvati.

The Mother of the Universe placed that boy at the door of the house and told him, "No one is to enter the inner chambers without my command. You must guard it accordingly." Bearing her command upon his head, the boy did exactly as instructed. Sometime later, Shiva went to the house of Shakti to see Parvati. The son born of Shakti obstructed his entry. Rebuking him, Girijapati attempted to enter again. The boy grabbed Rudra by the hair and threw him outside. Angered by that son of Shiva, Girijapati, his mind filled with wrath, cut off the head of the dirt-born boy with his trident.

His head severed by the trident, the dirt-born boy died. Arriving there and seeing the dead child, the daughter of the mountain became enraged and withdrew all the energy permeating the universes. Rendered powerless, all the gods went to the house of Shakti. The lotus-born Brahma asked the cause of her anger. Weeping, Girija said, "Pleased by my penance, Ganesha became my son and the obedient follower of my commands. The foolish, Tamasic Hara has killed such a child! By the grace of Brahmanaspati, I shall revive my son, and with this child's very hand, I will effortlessly crush the five heads of Rudra; this I declare by oath!" Then, a voice from the sky manifested, audible to everyone.

The voice said: "The one killed by Shankara today is not the Supreme Lord of Ganas. He was a devotee of Shakti, a practitioner of Bhakti-yoga, who was deeply engrossed in enmity towards Shiva. He was devoid of the true knowledge of Shakti, devoid of the knowledge of Brahman, and devoid of Shakta initiation; he had taken refuge solely in the path of devotion. By the power of his Bhakti-yoga, at the end of his previous life, this excellent devotee merged into the body of Shakti by Her supreme grace. By the ripening of his past actions and through specific meritorious deeds, this virtuous soul was born from the dirt of Uma's body. However, because he still possessed enmity towards Shiva, he was killed by Shiva today. Now, to manifest the Supreme Ganesha within his body today: invoke the three-lettered mantra known as the Pranava into the corpse, perform the installation of life-breath, attach the head of Gajasura to the body, and diligently chant the single-syllable mantra of Brahmanaspati ten million times. Ganeshana will manifest; there is no doubt."

Hearing this voice from the sky, the gods joyfully did exactly as instructed. Ganeshana manifested within the body of the dirt-born. The Supreme Goddess then reminded Him of her oath. To honor His mother's words, the dirt-born Vighnesha threw the five-faced Shiva to the ground, sat upon his body, and cut off four of his heads. Then, Brahma created a coconut resembling the three-eyed head of Shiva. Giving it to Ganapa, he respectfully prayed.

Brahma said: "Do not kill Shambhu. Shiva is now one-faced. Strike this coconut instead, which resembles the head of Maheshvara. By this, your mother's vow will reach completion, O Vighnanayaka! If Shiva dies, your mother will become a widow. By striking the coconut, save Shankara from death."

Hearing Brahma's words, Girija also prayed to Gananayaka to do so. Striking the coconut, the dirt-born Ganeshvara released Gaurisha. Then Brahma declared to everyone: "Wherever in the universe, for the pleasure of the gods, let Ganeshana, the son of Shakti, be pleased by the striking of the coconut fruit. And let Shiva remain in the universe with a single face."

Then Ganeshana affectionately said to Shiva: "By my boon, O Gaurisha, become five-faced again. However, bear my instruction and remain one-faced as well. Your one-faced idol shall always be universally worshipped in temples and images, O Shankara! Your five-faced form shall not be suitable for primary worship in temples, as it caused distress to Para-Shakti. By my command and grace, O Sadashiva, it shall be suitable only for meditation, without a doubt."

Saying this, Gananatha pleased Shankara. He subsequently killed leading demons like Chanda and Prachanda in battle. Taking Tushti and Pushti, the daughters of Vishnu, as his consorts according to the rites, he delighted his parents with his wondrous childhood pastimes. Afterward, taking leave of his parents, he departed for Svananda-bhavana. This Ganesha, born from the dirt of Uma's body, embodies the three Gunas. The foremost among Ganapatyas call him Triguna-Ganapa.

Thus ends the Description of the Nature of the Dirt-Born.

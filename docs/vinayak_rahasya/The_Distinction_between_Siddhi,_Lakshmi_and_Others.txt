The Distinction between Siddhi, Lakshmi, and Others

Narada and the other sages said:

Knowers of the Vedic truth describe Siddhi using the terms Shakti, Maya, and Mahalakshmi. Yet, they also refer to Goddess Parvati by the word Shakti, and to Bhargavi, the daughter of Sage Bhrigu, by the word Lakshmi. O Lord, please explain the precise distinction between them!

Surya said:

Rama, Bhumi, Rati, Gauri, Prabha, Amba, and Yogarupini—the entire cosmic collective Maya itself is designated by the name Siddhi. She is the ultimate Maya of even Mayas, embodying the supreme bliss of divine union. Therefore, Siddhi is sung in the Vedas through the names and expressions of all these individual goddesses.

O twice-born ones, in the Vedas, the word Lakshmi is applied to both Rama and Siddhi. However, the specific title Siddhi belongs strictly to Mahasiddhi and is never used to denote Rama. Mahasiddhi is the direct, absolute self-nature behind Vishnu's consort. In the exact same manner, the word Shakti is applied globally to both the individual Goddess and to Siddhi. But the specific word Siddhi is never used in the Vedas to refer to Shakti, O best of the twice-born! The same rule applies to the names Prabha, Rati, Bhumi, Gauri, and the rest.

The presiding Goddess Siddhi is the perfect, auspicious synthesis and union of both Shakti and Lakshmi. Thus declare the Ganapatyas who advocate for the Turiya Brahman. That this is the absolute truth, without a single doubt, is realized by the masters of Yoga.

Thus ends the Distinction between Siddhi, Lakshmi, and Others.

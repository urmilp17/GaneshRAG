The Distinction between Buddhi, Vani, and Others

Narada and the other sages said:

Knowers of the Vedic truth describe Buddhi using the terms Gauri, Vani, and Mahamaya. Yet, they also refer to Girija by the word Gauri, and to Bharati by the word Vani. O Lord, please explain the precise distinction between them!

Surya said:

Uma, Rama, Rati, Prajna, and the very form of the union of speech—this entire cosmic collective Maya is without a doubt designated as Mahabuddhi. O twice-born ones, in the Vedas, Buddhi is praised through the names of all these individual goddesses. However, those individual Shaktis are never sequentially praised in the scriptures by the specific name of Buddhi.

The primordial Maya of Brahman is split into two functional aspects: Siddhi and Buddhi. Their supreme distinction is clearly understood through the nature of their Lord whom they accompany. In the Vedas, the macrocosmic realities of Mahasiddhi and Mahabuddhi are expressed through the names of all female deities. However, Siddhi is never described by the names or attributes unique to Buddhi. Similarly, Buddhi is never addressed by the names unique to Siddhi.

The Ganapatyas who advocate for the Turiya Brahman declare that Buddhi is Sarasvati, the Supreme Power, and the ultimate embodiment of cosmic union. She is the absolute Maya of all Mayas, whose very nature is pure Consciousness and Knowledge. She is the dynamic experiential power of Brahman, seated upon His right lap. Conversely, the supreme, illusion-weaving Siddhi acts as that which provides enchantment or manifestation-delusion even to Buddhi.

Siddhi acts as the moving force behind the industry and activity of Buddhi. By depending upon Buddhi, that beloved power of Brahman executes the creation, maintenance, and dissolution of the universes. Everyone intensely desires success and attainment, both within the worldly realms and in spiritual liberation. Yet, that Siddhi can never be obtained without the prior presence of Intellect and Wisdom, which supports both the cosmos and Brahman.

Therefore, Buddhi is the absolute universal cause behind everything, and Gajanana is its ultimate regulator. Siddhi is the actual executor of all actions and the giver of all outcomes, without a single doubt.

Thus ends the Distinction between Buddhi, Vani, and Others.

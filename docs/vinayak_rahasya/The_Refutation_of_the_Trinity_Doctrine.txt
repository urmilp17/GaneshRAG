The Refutation of the Trinity Doctrine

Lord Brahma continued his narration, describing the journey of the great Guru Ganaka to the sacred regions beyond the Himalayas.

Brahma said:
"The best of Yogis, accompanied by his devoted followers, crossed the majestic Himalayas and reached the region known as Kimpurusha-varsha, worshipping Lord Ganesha at the sacred Hemakuta mountain.
After performing worship there, he stayed on the banks of the divine Lake Manasa. All the people residing in that region, hearing of his arrival, approached the Guru of Gurus with reverence. They bowed, worshipped him with devotion, and spoke with great joy.

The men said:
'O Revered Guru, due to the wicked actions of intoxicated demons, we had fallen from our Varna, our sacred social and spiritual order. We had lost our way and forgotten our duties. But by the grace of the excellent disciples of the great Sage Pulastya, we were gradually purified and restored. Now, we are devoted to the duties of Varna and Ashrama and are fully engaged in the study of the Vedas.

We have become established in the Smarta path and worship Brahma, the creator. Our understanding is this: the Supreme Brahman, which is attributeless, manifested when united with Prakriti, the primordial nature. That same Brahman, when engaged in the play of the universe, is remembered as the Fifth.

The unmanifest Supreme Brahman, when joined with the Maya of pure Sattva, is called the Turiya, the Fourth. When joined with Tamas, it is called the Brahman of Deep Sleep. From that state, the five subtle elements were born. From those elements, further transformations gradually arose. The reflection of Brahman falling upon the mind manifests as the Dreamer. That which resides in the intellect is known as the Dream state. When united with identification with the gross body, it becomes the Waking state. From this Waking state, the Cosmic Being, Virat, was born.

From this Virat, Brahma, Vishnu, and Maheshwara—the Trinity—were born. And from Brahma himself, this entire universe of moving and non-moving beings was created. To destroy the obstacles of the worlds, Hari, the protector of the worlds, became the elephant-faced son of Rudra by a portion of himself. Brahma, the Grandfather, gave him lordship over all obstacles. Sadashiva gave him lordship over the Pramathas, his celestial attendants. In Vaikuntha, the boon-giving Keshava granted him a place near himself.
Surya, the Sun, is the son of Kashyapa, born by the grace of Brahma. He obtained lordship over all actions. These three—Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva—are remembered as equal. Ganesha is worshipped first in all Vedic and Smarta rites. Actions are always performed by embodied beings with the Sun as their basis. By such actions, Lord Brahma, the Grandfather of the worlds, is pleased. Shiva and Vishnu are also pleased by worship; of this there is no doubt.
We wear both the vertical mark of Vishnu and the horizontal lines of Shiva according to the prescribed rules. Having performed rituals correctly, we worship both Shiva and Hari. When pleased, the Lord of Parvati grants us the Knowledge of Brahman. Vasudeva, who is anchored in the quality of Sattva, grants liberation. By the grace of Vasudeva, we attain union with Brahman.

O Lord of Sages, we are the worshippers of Shiva and Vishnu, known as Smartas. We are all devoted to the Vedic path and always perform our Vedic duties with faith and dedication."

The great Ganaka listened to their words with patience. A compassionate smile played upon his lips as he prepared to correct their misunderstanding.

The Ganaka said:
"Hearing your words, I must tell you that the path you have described is not considered truly Vedic. What you follow is the Traimurta path, the Trinity-centric view, and it contains elements that show disregard for the true essence of the Vedas. O best of the twice-born, only six paths are described in the Vedas as the authentic spiritual traditions. The deity of the true Smarta path is the Supreme Attributeless Brahman, the Nirguna, beyond all forms and qualities.

That same Brahman, through association with its own Maya, is described as five-fold for the purpose of worship. These five are: Ganesha, Shiva, Govinda, Para-shakti, and Surya. These are the Panchayatana deities, and their worship is the heart of the Smarta tradition.

The Trinity you speak of—Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva in their cosmic functions—are described in the Vedas as the 'Forms of Brahma,' the functional roles that serve as causes of the world. Such deities bear the forms of the Gunas, but they are not recognized as being of the nature of Brahman itself in the highest sense. They are the performers of creation, preservation, and dissolution, the supports of the three Gunas.

In the Vedas, the worship of the Five is considered equal to the worship of Brahman. Those devoted to this five-fold worship are the true Smartas, followers of the middle path. The Trinity, however, are described in the Vedas like Indra and other gods—they possess forms and are subject to the cycles of manifestation and dissolution. They are functional deities, not the ultimate reality.

But the five deities of the Panchayatana are different. They are remembered as being in the state of Turiya, the transcendental. They are rich in the nature of Supreme Light and assume forms only by their own free will for the sake of devotees. Just as space is all-pervading, so are they described. The support of the Guna-Vishnu, the Vishnu who functions within the three Gunas, is the Lord Hari who resides in the Turiya state. His reflection, conditioned by the quality of Sattva, is the Vishnu of the Trinity. Similarly, the Rudra you speak of is Hara associated with the quality of Tamas. These Guna-based deities are described as being like the sun reflected in water—they are manifestations, not the source.

By abandoning the source and worshipping only the reflection, what can be gained, O best of Brahmins? This Trinity-only path, this exclusive focus on the functional deities, is not found in the authentic Vedas and Smritis. Like Indra and others who hold positions of authority in the celestial hierarchy, the Trinity is described for the purpose of cosmic creation. Paths like the way of Indra, the way of the Moon, and the way of Hiranyagarbha are mentioned in the Vedas, and your Trinity-centric view is like those—valid for certain purposes but not the highest truth.

You claim to be Smartas, but you are followers of the Traimurta path. You are falsely calling yourselves Smartas while following a different doctrine. I urge you to abandon this meaningless doctrine which is outside the true Vedic essence. Resort to the true Smarta path and perform the five-fold worship of the Panchayatana deities. The true nature of these five deities is established in the Puranas, Upanishads, and Vedas. Study them under the guidance of a proper Guru. Only then will the true knowledge of them arise in your hearts." A profound silence fell upon the assembly as the people absorbed the Guru's words. The scales of misunderstanding fell from their eyes, and they saw clearly for the first time.

Hearing this wisdom, all those who had resorted to the Trinity doctrine and had falsely proclaimed themselves as Smartas, bowed deeply to the Sage. By his compassionate command, they abandoned their mistaken view and, adopting the true Smarta path which is the very essence of Vedic doctrine, they departed with gratitude and joy. Then, the eminent Guru, accompanied by his group of disciples and served by the devoted Somaraja, continued his journey and reached the region known as Rasatala.

Thus ends the Refutation of the Trinity Doctrine.

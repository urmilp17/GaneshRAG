Chapter 1 : The Dialogue between Shaunaka and Suta

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Here begins the Shriman Mudgala Purana.

Meditate with a steady mind upon Ganesha, the bestower of all accomplishments, the illuminator of intellect, the complete one, who resides in the hearts of yogis. He who has conquered all, described by various Vedas, and who has created the multitude of diverse worlds—both are the work of Ganesha. For the protection of his devotees, he who is formless (Nirguna) takes on form (Saguna), and manifesting in various incarnations, he appears in diverse forms. Salutations to that Ganesha, who takes the form of a man and an elephant; he who is Saguna is in human form, and he who is Nirguna has the face of an elephant.

Salutations to Shiva the peaceful, to Vishnu the powerful, to Surya, and to Shakti—to Him who is divided into these four. I bow with devotion to the supreme primordial being, who is Brahma, Shesha-naga, and Nara-Narayana. I take refuge in the gods, the yogis, and the great-souled sages, seeking the knowledge of the Puranas.

In a previous age, the sages led by Shaunaka, being great practitioners, took refuge in the Naimisha Forest out of fear of the evils of the Kali Yuga. Knowing from the mouth of Brahma that the Kali Yuga had arrived, they sought to establish the duties of sages and the path of action and knowledge. Brahma had also spoken of the constant listening to the Puranas, which removes the sins of Kali and bestows merit; for this purpose, they gathered there.

At one time, the wise Suta Romaharshana, a knower of the Puranas, arrived to see the sages. Seeing the beauty of the Naimisha Forest, he was amazed and exclaimed, "Oh, how blessed is this great forest, free from the evils of Kali!" He saw the sages with their lotus-like faces, performing their daily duties, devoted to their own dharma, engaged in the five sacrifices, and studying the Vedas. They were filled with the radiance of Brahman, shining like fires. Like the sun without clouds, these sages led by Shaunaka were the guides for all beings.

The great-souled Suta Romaharshana approached them, bowed down with his whole body in great joy, and after greeting all the sages with devotion and folded hands, he spoke sweet words.

Suta said: "My lineage, my parents, my family, my age, my knowledge, my vows, my penance, and my wisdom are all blessed today, O great sages. Your lotus feet, which destroy all inauspiciousness and are always desired by the gods, have been seen by me due to my great merit. You are Brahman incarnate, the best of the knowers of Brahman, by whose mere sight beings become fulfilled. I am fulfilled, I am fulfilled, I am fulfilled—there is no doubt. By your sight alone, I am liberated from the ocean of existence."

Hearing this virtuous Suta Romaharshana speaking thus, all the sages, rich in penance, replied with devotion.

The Brahmanas said: "O Suta, great-souled one, please stay, O increaser of merit. We are truly satisfied by your mere sight."

Being told this, the highly intelligent and glorious Suta sat on the auspicious seat provided by the sages, with folded hands. Seeing Suta comfortably seated, the great sage Bhrigu-shreshtha, the leader of the sages, spoke to him.

Shaunaka said: "O Suta, great-souled one, all-knowing and giver of happiness, you have served the great Krishna Dvaipayana (Vyasa), who is an incarnation of Narayana and Narayana himself. He is the one who divided the Vedas and the Puranas. From him, you have learned the Puranas and the histories, and thus you have become all-knowing, O increaser of merit. We are afraid of the time characterized by the evils of Kali, and having taken refuge in the Naimisha Forest, we have been instructed by the Self-born (Brahma). You have arrived here as a giver of rest, a speaker of the Puranas, and a bringer of merit. Where the stories of the Puranas are told, Kali does not remain. Therefore, out of compassion, please tell us the Puranas, O Lord. Your arrival is due to great merit; you are blessed, O great soul, for you are the remover of doubts."

Asked thus by the great sage Shaunaka, the great-souled Suta was delighted and spoke these words, refined by dharma.

Suta said: "Today I am fulfilled by your grace. I will tell you everything that was told by Vyasa."

Having said this, he narrated the Puranas in their entirety, divided into various stories and endowed with various dharmas. They are the means to achieve all goals, destroyers of sorrow, bestowers of the state of Brahman, and contain various doctrines. He spoke of the Puranas, the Upapuranas, and the histories, which grant merit by bestowing dharma, wealth, desire, and liberation. Hearing them again and again, the sages were filled with joy and complete satisfaction.

However, after hearing all the Puranas and histories, with their various sects and doctrines, the sages became confused. They could not determine what was the supreme and universally accepted truth. For that reason, the great sage Shaunaka asked him again.

Shaunaka said: "O Suta, great-souled one, listen to my brief request. Having heard the Puranas, we are confused, O glorious one. You have described everything as 'this is supreme' and 'this is not.' Because they are different, how can we find certainty? Somewhere Shiva is supreme, somewhere Vishnu, elsewhere the Sun, Shakti, or Ganesha. Somewhere the cosmic form is described. Somewhere Brahman, yoga, knowledge, and action are emphasized. Somewhere the soul, the life-breath, or food is called supreme, and elsewhere the mind is called beyond the beyond. Somewhere intellect, Sankhya, or self-bliss is described. Due to such differences in opinion, no certainty is reached. Therefore, please tell us, out of compassion, what is accepted by the essence of the Vedas and Shastras, which is one, acceptable to all, and the destroyer of all doubts."

Asked thus, the highly radiant disciple of Vyasa began to tell the essence of the Shruti and Smriti.

Suta said: "Listen, all you sages, to the essence spoken by Mudgala, which is the essence of all Shruti and Smriti and is especially acceptable to all. Long ago, at the sacrifice of Daksha, a great destruction occurred. Rudra arrived suddenly and caused a great disturbance. All the gods were defeated by the fierce Rudras, and the head of Daksha was severed by Virabhadra. Brahma arrived there and consoled him with many words, and Shiva brought Daksha back to life through yoga. Daksha, suffering from the grief of the destruction of the sacrifice and deluded by the curse, could not reach any certainty due to the veil of ignorance. Then, the Lord arrived, shining like the risen sun—the great yogi Mudgala, the knower of the essence of the Vedas and Shastras. He was the best of Ganesha's devotees, worthy of worship by all, and of great fame. He was the eternal repository of penance and action. Seeing him, Daksha stood up, bowed to the great sage, worshipped him with offerings, and began to speak."

Daksha said: "O great sage, you are worthy of worship, O glorious descendant of Angiras. Protect me, who am paralyzed by the grief of the destruction of the sacrifice."

Mudgala said: "Do not worry, O Daksha, best of the protectors of subjects. Remember the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha); through him, nothing is unattainable."

Thus ends the first chapter of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Dialogue between Shaunaka and Suta," in the context of the story of Vakratunda.

Chapter 2 : The Second Chapter Named The Dispute Between Daksha and Nandi

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Suta said: O best of sages, Daksha having heard the words of Mudgala, bowed down to him and asked again for his own benefit.

Shaunaka said: We wish to hear the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala. O Suta, you who bring joy, please narrate it to us in detail.

Suta said: Listen, all you sages, to this story which purifies the world. Having heard it, a person finds no other duty left to perform in this world. I shall describe the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala in brief; it is the essence of everything, complete in itself, and a remedy for the disease of worldly existence.

Having heard the words of Mudgala, Daksha, who knew the essence of Dharma, spoke with folded hands, his body trembling with joy and his fame great.

Daksha said: Great is the merit of my past deeds, for it is only because of that I have attained your vision. Your presence grants liberation, bestows the fruits of the next world, provides worldly enjoyments, is righteous, and yields infinite merit. My entire wisdom was destroyed by a curse, and my status as Prajapati was lost. The destruction of the sacrifice and the various hardships I faced—it is truly wondrous to have your vision amidst all that. By your mere vision, some spark of awareness has returned to me. You are blessed among yogis; such purity resides in you.

Who is that Lord of Obstacles, the complete one? What is he like, and what is his nature? How many are his incarnations, whose portion is he, and what is his praise? By whom is he worshipped according to the scriptures, and what supreme fruit does he grant? What are his pastimes, and in which world does that Lord reside? Please tell me all this in detail as I ask with reverence. Having known that Lord of Lords, I shall become beneficial to the world.

As he was speaking thus, the sages, filled with wonder, respectfully asked Suta about all of Daksha’s actions.

Shaunaka and the others said: How did Daksha, the Prajapati himself, become foolish? How was the status of that great soul lost? How did the destruction of the sacrifice occur, and why was Shambhu (Shiva) enraged? O wise Suta, tell us all this in detail.

Having heard the beautiful words of the sages, the disciple of Krishna Dvaipayana (Vyasa) spoke sweet words, bringing great joy to them.

Suta said: Listen, Shaunaka, with effort to this story that purifies the world. I shall narrate it exactly as I heard it from the mouth of Vyasa. Among the ten sons of Brahma, Daksha was a great lord; therefore, the Creator made him a Prajapati. Having attained the kingdom, he became intoxicated and overwhelmed by pride, thinking, "I am the best of the Prajapatis, a king, and there is no one equal to me."

Once, the gods, sages, Siddhas, Kinnaras, Gandharvas, Nagas, Vidyadharas, and Apsaras went to the great mountain Kailasa to see Shankara. Brahma, Vishnu, and Mahendra were all well-received by him. They sat in the assembly and were honored by Shankara. Vishnu sat on the left side, and the Grandfather (Brahma) on the right. Gradually, all the gods took their appropriate places, and the attendants led by Nandi shone as they stood nearby.

The sages, led by Vashistha, were delighted and praised him. All the Gandharvas, led by Narada, sang beautifully. The Apsaras danced, and thus all worshipped Shankara, who was surrounded by his family and filled with bliss. They engaged in various conversations with deep devotion. Then Daksha, the lord of Prajapatis, arrived there himself. He was honored by Shiva and praised with words, but because of his pride, he did not bow down to the Lord.

Enraged by this, and blinded by pride, Daksha said: "Listen, you who are the best in the assembly and followers of truth. I am the father-in-law of Rudra, and he is like a son to me. Why was I not bowed to? Therefore, this Maheshwara should be abandoned. He is filled with the pride of power and has fallen from his own duty. How is he fit to be in the company of gods? He is the lord of ghosts (pishachas). He is devoid of Varna and Ashrama (caste and stage of life), naked, addicted to begging, adorned with snakes, and smeared with funeral ashes. He is wrapped in a tiger skin and always wears a garland of skulls. Because he is endowed with such faults, he is not to be touched by the great souls. He is 'Ashiva' (inauspicious), though named 'Shiva,' and is vainly proud of his lordship. Therefore, this fault-ridden lord of ghosts should not be spoken to."

As he spoke thus, the Self-born (Brahma) rejected him, but Daksha, in a fit of rage, did not heed his father's words. Seeing him full of such insults, the divine Nandikeshwara, with eyes reddened by anger, spoke in the presence of all.

Nandikeshwara said: "Shame on you, O protector of subjects, who are intent on insulting Shiva. You do not know the essence of Shiva, blinded by your pride, O evil-minded one. He is the one who plays with Maya and is devoid of those faults. He has attained the state of Shiva; why do you insult him? Those who insult Shiva are destined for hell and are devoid of knowledge. Therefore, because of your evil words, you shall become devoid of knowledge. By the very mouth with which you insulted the great Shambhu, you shall become goat-faced and lose your kingdom."

Hearing these words, Daksha replied to him: "You do not know, O evil-minded one, that Shankara is tainted by his actions. A person who remains devoid of Varna and Ashrama is a great sinner at every step and will go to hell upon death. Therefore, you are all great sinners; the Shaivas are never to be touched. May those on earth who are followers of Shankara always be devoid of knowledge. In the Kali Yuga, they will become heretics, wearing matted hair. Shudras will not read this; they will be naked and smeared with ashes. Addicted to women, meat, and wine, and engaging in various enjoyments, those who follow the path of Shambhu will fall into hell."

The Lord Brahma himself rebuked the Prajapati. Then Daksha, overwhelmed by anger, went to his own house. All the others went to their respective places, saddened by what he had done. Shiva also became filled with sorrow and fell into a state of deep gloom.

Thus ends the second chapter named "The Dispute Between Daksha and Nandi" in the Vakra-tunda-charita of the first section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 3 : The Abandonment of the Body by Parvati

Suta said: Listen, O sages, with a steady mind. Those who lack devotion to the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) are inevitably overcome by obstacles. Daksha, filled with anger and resentment, returned to his home. He found no peace, O Brahmins, suffering like a man pierced by a spear. He resolved in his heart to teach Shiva a lesson, but finding no immediate way, he remained deeply distressed.

At that moment, the divine sage Narada, possessing great radiance, arrived there and was well-received by Daksha. After offering him a seat, water for his feet, and the customary offerings, Daksha inquired about his well-being. Pleased, Narada spoke.

Narada said: O Daksha, why are you distressed? Your face appears withered. Tell me, O great lord of the subjects, what troubles you?

Daksha said: Listen, Narada, to the reason for my sorrow and gloom. I was disrespected by Shiva in the assembly, in the presence of everyone. Shiva is filled with pride; how can his pride be humbled? Tell me quickly, O wise one who knows all things.

Narada said: O Daksha, perform the Brihaspati-sava sacrifice. In that sacrifice, ensure that Shiva is excluded from receiving any share of the offerings. Deprived of his share, he will be excluded from the rank of gods and will become a lord of ghosts, his divinity stripped away. You are the lord of the subjects and respected by all; do as I say, and the outcome will be as you desire. This is the traditional path to manifest his true nature. Do as you wish.

Having said this, Narada departed for the celestial realms, absorbed in playing his veena and focused on the Supreme. Daksha, feeling joyful and believing this to be the right path, immediately began the preparations for the Brihaspati-sava. Lord Vishnu, the best of gods, arrived there, as did the gods and Gandharvas, adorned with various vehicles. All the learned Brahmins and sages, including Kashyapa and his sons-in-law with their wives, also arrived.

Daksha, filled with great joy, performed the sacrifice exactly as planned, but without Ganesha and without Shiva. Because of the pride of those involved, Ganesha was forgotten. Dadhichi and other devotees of Shiva were filled with anger. Dadhichi, overcome with rage, said: Listen, O wise Daksha, to these beneficial words. A sacrifice without Shiva does not shine; it is like a body without life.

Hearing this, Daksha, deluded by pride and cursed by his own arrogance, replied: Shiva is inauspicious and the lord of ghosts. Because he is outside the social and moral order (Varnashrama), he is not fit for sacrificial rites. Where the Lord of Lakshmi (Vishnu) himself is present, what could be lacking? Do not speak falsehoods.

Hearing his cruel words, Dadhichi replied: You are deluded by ignorance and are slandering Shiva. This sacrifice will be destroyed because of your hatred for Shankara. You will gain no success, and there is no doubt that your fortune is destroyed. All the Brahmins and others gathered here will become sinners and heretics in the Kali Yuga. They will lose their dharma and suffer in hell. Vishnu, who is pleased here, is the cause of this heresy. In the Kali Yuga, the divine will always support the heretics. Because you have excluded Shiva from the sacrifice, you are cursed. All the gods will soon be defeated. Having said this, the sage departed, filled with sorrow.

The other devotees of Shiva, such as Gautama, Galava, Vamadeva, and others, followed him. They declared that this sacrifice, being outside the Vedas, should not be witnessed. Vishnu and others remained, but the gods and sages were eager to see the sacrifice. The sacrifice began under the guidance of Bhrigu and other sages. Daksha, the foolish sacrificer, was pleased as the sages conducted the rites according to the Vedas.

Meanwhile, Narada went to Kailash and bowed to Shiva and Parvati. The mischief-loving Narada smiled and said to Sati: Devi, why did you not go to your father’s sacrifice? Everyone there is joyful, and your sisters are celebrating with their husbands. In the home of one's father or husband, a woman does not need an invitation. Having said this, the curious sage departed.

After the sage left, Sati was deeply distressed in her heart. Bowing to Shankara with a heavy heart, she said: Salutations to you, O Nilakantha, Shankara, and Shiva. Salutations to the one who is both with attributes and without. I wish to go to my father’s house to attend the sacrifice. Please, my Lord, grant me permission.

Hearing her words, Shiva, smiling, said: Why do you speak thus, my beloved? Your father, Daksha, hates me and has not invited me. You should not go; it will lead to a bad outcome. You are the Great Maya, and I am the illusioned one. You create the sages and gods; I shall perform the sacrifice here.

Hearing Shiva’s words, the goddess, with a heavy heart, asked for his permission. Shiva remained silent, but the goddess, thinking of her father’s home, went without his consent. Knowing this, Shiva, in his wonder, sent a divine chariot. Riding the chariot, the goddess arrived in the sky at her father’s house and was welcomed by her relatives. Her mother, father, and sisters received her with joy. Daksha said to his daughter: You have come, and this sacrifice is graced by your presence.

Hearing her father’s words, she replied with sorrow: How can I grace this sacrifice when I was not invited by you? I have come uninvited to your sacrificial hall.

Daksha, filled with anger, said: Listen, daughter. It was a great mistake on my part to give you to that lord of ghosts, who is outside the social order. Because of his faults, I did not invite you. Still, you are welcome; stay by my side.

Suta said: Hearing her father’s words, which were filled with the slander of Shiva, her body burned with rage. Seeing the sacrificial hall, she realized it lacked a share for Rudra. She said: What have you done, father? This is contrary to the Vedas. A sacrifice without a share for Rudra yields no fruit. You are slandering Shiva; you are undoubtedly inauspicious. You do not know that Shiva is the two-syllabled Brahman, both with and without attributes. Because of this, I am deeply distressed. My body was born from your body, but because of this slander of Shiva, I abandon it.

Having said this, she meditated on Lord Shankara in her heart, ignited the fire of yoga, and left her body. Later, by her own maya, she was reborn as the daughter of the Himalayas and, through devotion to Shiva, regained him as her husband. Through intense penance and self-control, she attained joy and was reunited with Shiva, filled with love.

Chapter 4 : The Story of Daksha

Suta said: On the beautiful peak of Kailash, Shiva became deeply contemplative, wondering what would happen to the Goddess who had gone to her father’s house uninvited. Sitting at the root of a banyan tree, surrounded by Nandi and other attendants, and served by sages, he engaged in various discourses. Suddenly, many terrifying omens appeared. Seeing these, the sages were filled with anxiety and began to offer prayers.

At that moment, the sage Narada arrived. The yogis bowed to him, and he stood before Shiva, the bestower of welfare. Finding an opportune moment, Narada spoke sternly: "Sati is blessed; there is no woman in the three worlds equal to her. The name 'Sati' is truly meaningful." Hearing of the insults directed at Shiva, Sati’s body had burned like straw. Hearing Narada’s words, which were as hard as thunderbolts, Shambhu asked, "O Sage, has the Goddess died?"

Distressed by the fear of Shiva’s anger, Narada remained silent. Shiva wondered if the fire of grief would consume him, and he realized the truth. He became submerged in an ocean of sorrow, thinking, "Why has Shiva left me? I am rendered powerless and incapable of any action." He sighed deeply, feeling as though half of his body had been lost. "What is the use of living? I shall abandon this body." Though consoled by the yogis, the Lord found no peace. Shiva, lost in thought, pondered in his heart: "Alas, I have been troubled by the creator of obstacles. I was proud of my supreme divinity, and now that pride has been shattered. The Mother of the Universe, who is my half, is my incomparable power. I am Ardhanarishvara; how can there be separation? Always empowered by her, I am the Lord. In my pride of eternal sovereignty, I forgot to remember the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha)."

Thus, Shambhu began to worship Ganesha in his mind with single-minded devotion, meditating on his supreme form: four-armed, large-bodied, elephant-faced, with a large belly, adorned with a crown, earrings, a necklace of gems, and anklets that tinkled. Meditating on him as the one accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, with the Chintamani jewel on his chest, he attained a state of deep meditation. He praised Ganesha, the embodiment of Brahman, the formless and the manifest, the Lord of Obstacles residing in his heart.

Shiva said: "Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, the remover of obstacles, the creator of obstacles, the Lord of all obstacles. Salutations to the one with the large belly, the curved trunk, the one who manifests as the three Gunas and the diverse forms of the world. Salutations to the one who is beyond the Gunas, the direct embodiment of Brahman. Salutations to the breaker of bonds and the protector of devotees. To those who are not devotees, you bring darkness and fear. Salutations to Heramba, the eternal one known through the Vedas. Salutations to you, who have infinite faces, forms, arms, feet, ears, and belly. Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, the one worshipped from the beginning, the one who takes all forms. Salutations to the great soul whose complete form performs eternal play.

In the beginning, you create Brahma, the embodiment of Rajas, and through him, you create the universe. Then, you create Vishnu, the embodiment of Sattva, and protect the world with your infinite power. In the end, you create the form of Tamas, and through your own power as Shambhu, you destroy it. The Vedas describe you in this way; I take refuge in that Ganesha. You created the Ganas and the Maya-filled world. You shine in the city of Svananda, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. I take refuge in that Ganesha, who always resides in the hearts of yogis. I meditate on that curved-trunk Lord, who is not known by the Vedas nor attained by the mind.

My state of Ardhanarishvara is now gone, O Lord. I have become powerless and feel as if I am destroyed. The Goddess, who possessed all divine powers, has left, and I have lost my status as the Lord of Lords. I am always Shambhu, the attributeless, yet I am also the one with attributes who knows all states. When I am united with Shakti, I am the Lord; what am I to do now? I am so powerless that I cannot even walk, O Lord of Ganas. Therefore, by your grace, make me capable of action."

Then, the Lord of Ganas appeared before him and spoke to Shankara with a gentle voice, gladdening him: "Look at me, Shambhu. Why do you grieve, O Maheshwara? By my words, you shall be reunited with your power; there is no doubt. I am the only Lord in the universe. You were filled with pride, and thus this obstacle was suddenly created. Now your delusion is gone, and my remembrance has returned to you. I am pleased by your meditation and praise. There is no doubt that the daughter of the Himalayas will become the Goddess. You shall marry that Sati, who will be Parvati, O Shiva. You will enjoy life with her, firmly imbued with devotion to me. By my grace, you shall always be the Lord united with Shakti. Whenever you remember me, I shall appear before you, O Sadashiva." Having given this boon, the Lord disappeared.

Then, Shankara, now restored and capable of action, shook his matted hair, remembering Ganesha in his mind. From this, the great-armed Virabhadra was born—three-headed, three-legged, holding a trident, the very image of death, and full of brilliance. He bowed to Shankara and stood before him with folded hands: "Command me, O Mahadeva; I am your servant, ready to obey." Hearing this, a delighted Shankara said: "Go and destroy the sacrifice of Daksha immediately."

Taking the command upon his head, Virabhadra let out a roar that made the three worlds tremble in fear. He departed with great speed, creating countless Rudras from his body. Surrounded by them, he went to the sacrifice. He was followed by other Ganas of Shambhu, such as Nandi and others, all filled with anger, resembling the fire of cosmic destruction.

At that time, all sorts of bad omens appeared in Daksha’s house. The sages and gods were terrified, wondering what would happen. In the meantime, the powerful and terrifying Virabhadra arrived at Daksha’s sacrifice, surrounded by the Rudras. Nandi, Bhringiriti, Bhringi, Pushpadanta, and others arrived, their faces glowing with rage, devouring everything like the god of death. Suddenly, they began to strike everyone, and Daksha’s followers fled in all directions. They reached the sacrificial pavilion and destroyed it in an instant, tormenting all the gods and sages. They seized thousands of sages' wives and gods' wives, causing them to cry out in distress. "How could you have chosen such unworthy husbands?" they asked. "You are the ones who insulted Shiva; now you are reaping the great fruits of that."

They ate the sacrificial offerings and, in their joy, defecated and urinated on the sacrificial altar. Then, the Lord Shankara himself arrived, filled with anger, holding his bow and arrows, rising like the fire of cosmic dissolution. A fierce battle ensued between the gods and the Rudras. Shambhu himself fought with weapons that pierced the heart. Shankara shattered the teeth of Pusha with his arrows and plucked out the eyes of Bhaga in the battle. Nandi, the great hero, held Dharma and said, "Tell me, you wretch, what kind of Dharma is this in a sacrifice devoid of Shiva?" Bhringi, the best of the brilliant, seized Kashyapa, and the powerful Pushpadanta struck Varuna. Bhairava seized Indra and rendered him unconscious. Virabhadra forcibly threw Daksha to the ground.

As the sacrifice was being destroyed, Vishnu, holding the conch, discus, and mace, fought with great strength. The Rudras and spirits were crushed by his many weapons and cried out in pain. Seeing this, Shambhu suddenly meditated on the Lord of Ganas in his heart and, piercing Vishnu’s heart with his arrows, threw him to the ground. Regaining consciousness, Govinda released his own weapon, the Narayana-astra, which is capable of destroying the world. Then Shambhu released his great Pashupata-astra. Both weapons fought in the sky, creating a great fire that burned the earth. The three worlds were terrified and seemed as if dead.

At that moment, Virabhadra cut off the head of the animal-formed Daksha and offered it into the fire. The Rudras created a great uproar. The gods were shattered and broken, finding no savior. The sages, terrified, fled in all ten directions, pursued by spirits who shrieked horribly. Vishnu, filled with anger, spun his discus, which approached Shankara like the god of death. Seeing the discus, Shankara bowed to it, and then, his body filled with rage, he took up his trident. As he raised the trident to kill Vishnu, a voice from the sky stopped him: "Do not kill Hari, O Shambhu; he is to be protected by you. Abandon your anger, O Mahadeva; salutations to Shiva always."

Brahma arrived suddenly and restrained him: "Do not commit this killing, O Shambhu, the destroyer of Vishnu. Salutations to the peaceful Shiva, the wielder of the trident, the auspicious one. Salutations to Mahadeva, the Rudra, the Lord of all beings. You are the embodiment of destruction; why are you acting out of season? Abandon your anger, O great-armed one; salutations to you, O Supreme Lord."

Praised by Brahma, the Lord became calm. At that time, Vishnu was informed by Brahma. He bowed to Shiva, feeling deeply ashamed. Brahma spoke sweet words to Shankara, the one with the bull-banner: "Be calm, O Lord. Whatever sacrificial offerings remain, let that be known as the Rudra-bhaga; accept it, O Sadashiva. Daksha and others were deluded by the Maya of the Lord of Obstacles, O Shiva. One should not look at the actions of those who are deluded. Therefore, O Lord, be pleased by your grace. Be the giver of happiness to all and restart this sacrifice. Restore the gods, sages, women, and servants to their original forms, O Shankara."

Bhaga was restored with his eyes, and Pusha was given the ability to eat. The gods, sages, women, and servants were all restored to their original forms, beauty, and brilliance. Free from sorrow, they all bowed to Shankara again. Daksha’s head had been burned in the fire. Learning this, the Lord Shankara said to Bhringi: "Go and bring the head of the first creature you see, and that shall become Daksha’s head." Bhringi saw a goat, killed it, and brought its head. Shankara joined it to Daksha’s body and brought him back to life. Daksha rose immediately, now with the face of a goat.

Prompted by Brahma, Daksha, his pride gone, praised Shankara with devotion: "I bow to Shankara, the very foundation of this world. He is the form of Time, which teaches everything in its season. By Time, fruits, roots, and grains are born. By Time, the balance of heat and cold is maintained. By Time, the sun shines, and the stars move. By Time, rain falls or drought occurs, O Lord. By Time, the creation is repeatedly created by Brahma. By Time, the eternal Vishnu performs the protection. By Time, Shambhu, by his own will, performs the destruction. Whatever is here is all subject to Time; there is no doubt. That very Time is the Lord, the Supreme Being, known as Brahman. He is Shiva himself, as declared by the knowers of the Vedas. Forgive my offense, O Lord, for I was proud due to your Maya. Salutations to you, O Supreme Lord, the ocean of mercy. Salutations to you, the one with attributes and the one without attributes. Salutations to the creator, the destroyer, and the one who takes many forms. You are an ocean of infinite qualities; what can be described? Even Brahma and others have not reached your end; what to speak of those like me? Salutations to the embodiment of Brahman, the primordial Lord, the auspicious one, the complete form. Salutations to the one without beginning, middle, or end, the infinite one, the remover of the fear of devotees."

As he praised him with a voice choked with emotion, he began to bleat like a goat. Delighted by this, the Lord Mahadeva restored his human head as it was before, O sages. Shiva spoke sweet words to him in the presence of all: "O Daksha, Lord of the Prajapatis, you shall be respected by all. The hymn you have composed to me shall be dear to me. Whoever praises me with this, I shall grant them their desires. After enjoying all pleasures here, they shall attain my abode. There is no doubt that they will be dear to me. Whoever, in my presence, makes the same sound you made like a goat, I shall be pleased and fulfill their wishes." Having given this boon, Shambhu disappeared.

Everyone returned to their respective places, some happy and some humbled. Daksha returned to his home, feeling sorrowful. Since then, those who are devotees of Shiva perform the sound of the goat in the temple of Shiva, and Shankara is pleased by it. Thus, everything regarding Daksha’s curse and the cause of the destruction of the sacrifice has been told. What else do you wish to hear?

Chapter 5 : The Inquiry of Parvati

Salutations to Lord Ganesha. Shaunaka said: I have heard the great account of Daksha, including the destruction of his sacrifice. O Sage, please narrate the dialogue between Mudgala and Daksha. Suta replied: Listen, Shaunaka, to the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala, which leads to the realization of Brahman and is deeply felt in the heart. Holding the profound questions of Daksha in his mind, the great sage Mudgala, who is dear to Ganapati and the best among yogis, replied with devotion.

Mudgala said: There is no doubt that Ganapati is the mother and father of all; therefore, He is the first to be worshipped by all and the Lord of all gods. The Vedic declaration that He is the Supreme Lord (Jyeshtharaja) is universally accepted because of His primordial nature. All parts and sub-parts of the universe are derived from Him, yet His complete form is nowhere to be seen in the world. He is called Vinayaka because He is the Lord of all, and specifically the leader of all groups (ganas). Being Vinayaka, He is self-willed, residing in the realm of Self-Bliss (Svananda-loka), blissful by His own nature. He is worshipped by Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, the Vedas, and the yogis for the attainment of righteousness, desire, wealth, and liberation.

When the ego of "I am Brahman" arises in beings like Shiva, it is because He is the foundation of all existence. To grant them the realization of true knowledge, He, the remover of pride, becomes the Lord of obstacles (Vighnapa) and the creator of obstacles (Vighnakarta). When gods and sages are displaced from their positions, they seek the foundation of all existence through deep meditation (samadhi). Through yoga, they perceive Him as the illuminator of the five states of the mind, and thus He is known as Chintamani. Realizing Him through the Vedas, they take refuge in Him, and He becomes the remover of obstacles and the granter of all desires. He is the essence of all existence and is known as the King of Obstacles. Therefore, there is nothing higher than Ganesha. You have become obstructed, so take refuge in Him—the Chintamani, the remover of obstacles, Ganesha, the Vinayaka.

Suta said: Hearing this from the Lord of creatures, Daksha was pleased and humbly asked for the story that destroys sins. Daksha asked: Who is this dweller of Self-Bliss, and how can I know Him? Tell me the path, O Lord, so that I may worship Him constantly. Mudgala replied: I will tell you an ancient history; listen to the dialogue between Parvati and Shiva as it occurred. On the great mountain Kailasa, Parvati, filled with joy, bowed to Maheshwara and asked: O Shiva, you are undoubtedly the worshipped Lord of all gods, serpents, demons, and humans. You are the giver of positions to them, the ancient Lord of all, and because you are devoid of the distortions of Maya, you are called Shiva. O Lord, why do you meditate in samadhi? I have neither seen nor heard of anyone greater than you.

Asked thus, Mahadeva replied with respect, speaking the essence that is honored by the Vedas and leads to Brahman. Lord Shiva said: Listen, Beloved, I will tell you whom I meditate upon day and night—Ganesha, who resides in my heart and dwells in the realm of Self-Bliss. In Him, there is no distinction between form and formless; He is beyond mind and speech, yet He is not devoid of them. Hearing the words of Shiva, Parvati, filled with love and knowing the essence of speech, spoke sweetly: Who is this Ganesha, and how does He reside in the heart? What is this realm of Self-Bliss where the Lord dwells? How is He known by people if He is neither with form nor without it? Even you, O Maheshwara, find it difficult to meditate upon Him; tell me the path by which you meditate. I am your servant and disciple; tell me the meditation of Ganesha so that I may realize Him and become fulfilled by your grace.

Mudgala said: Asked with such devotion and respect by the Goddess, Mahadeva, seeing her as a worthy recipient of the path of Ganesha, spoke with love: I will tell you the most secret of secrets, the essence of the Vedas and Shastras, than which there is nothing higher. Daksha said: O Mudgala, I am pleased by your nectar-like words; please tell me the dialogue between Uma and Maheshwara. Suta said: Hearing the heartfelt words of Daksha, Mudgala was pleased and began to speak. Mudgala said: Remembering Ganesha and meditating upon Him in his heart, the glorious Shiva replied to the Great Goddess: Know that everything born of the mind is Maya. That which is beyond the reach of the mind and beyond Maya is the Lord of Groups (Gananyaka). He who created the twofold world is the Lord Himself. He is the light of Self-realization. Yogis who have mastered the sacrifice perceive Him there; thus, the Vedic scholars call Him the dweller of Self-Bliss. He is known as Chintamani, the illuminator of the five states of the mind, who resides in the heart. He is the Lord of all groups, and He can always be meditated upon through the path of non-dual yoga.

Mudgala said: Hearing the beautiful words of Shiva, Parvati was astonished and asked: O Lord, you have spoken in riddles of yoga; I do not understand. Tell me in a way that is easy to grasp. What is this Brahman that is said to be full of Maya, and how is the Maya-less state to be known? Explain this to me in detail. Mudgala said: Hearing the question of the Goddess, Shiva, the knower of yoga, was filled with devotion, his eyes shedding tears and his hair standing on end. Praising the Primordial Maya, Maheshwara began to explain in detail for the sake of the path of yoga.

Chapter 6 : The Granting of Boons to Prakriti and Purusha

Shounaka said: O wise one, knower of all truths and disciple of Vyasa, please narrate the dialogue between Parvati and Shiva.

Suta said: I shall narrate to you the dialogue between Shiva and Parvati, which brings peace, as it was told by Mudgala to Daksha.

Shri Shiva said: O blessed Goddess, there is no one equal to you. Your intellect is filled with a great desire for the knowledge of Ganesha. You have inspired me, O Shakti; there is no doubt that you are a virtuous woman. It is my great fortune that the beloved of Ganesha resides in my home. Having bowed to Ganesha, I shall tell you this story. Know first that which is illusory (Maya), and then that which is beyond Maya.

The yogi must always know that everything is his own true self, which is realized in two ways through the path of yoga and spiritual practice. Initially, Brahman existed, devoid of oneness or multiplicity. From its own inherent arising, it appeared in two forms. In the initial arising, there was no distinction; but with the thought "I am," the reflection separated and emerged. That which is formless by nature is the eternal Brahman, known as "I am." Through this, it perceived its own self as the supreme soul. Due to delusion, the question "What am I?" arose instantly.

The four categories of existence, established in the form of yoga, were seen as non-different from the supreme soul. By Maya, this reflection took on a name and form. Maya is the second reflection, born from the persistence of delusion. By merely holding onto this delusion, the self forgot its true nature and, desiring the pleasures of Maya, immediately became that deity.

Maya, which is both formless and with form, became twofold. It is the intellect (Buddhi), O blessed one, through which everything is understood. People understand both the formless and the formed through the intellect. That which is understood by the intellect is the field (Kshetra), and it is also the form of the intellect itself.

Therein, delusion is with form, while the Lord himself is formless. The confusion of being "without delusion" arises from the two qualities (Gunas). This Siddhi (attainment) creates delusion and manifests various illusions. The intellect, though one, is thus divided into two. Parvati, the intellect became deluded by Siddhi, entangled in the delusion of form and bewildered by the formless.

The intellect that holds delusion is the Siddhi that bestows delusion. Ganesha himself became the one who provides the light to both. He remained there without taking a specific form, yet appeared as such due to the nature of the reflection and the reflected. The supreme, all-encompassing reflection was reflected there, and the formless soul attained union with it.

That Lord of the Ganas is the one who plays the game of Maya. By his will, these two move like puppets on a string. O Parvati, through delusion, the one became three, and the thought arose: "I am one, may I become many." Through this thought, the two forms of the body and the embodied soul were created. That which the knowers of the Vedas call "I am Brahman" is the embodied form, and the body is considered merely a point (Bindu) due to the removal of bodily delusion.

Know that the ancient Brahman is the form of the four types of bodies, and that which is called the singularity of the body is its manifold form. O Shiva, the "I am Brahman" form and the manifold form with the Bindu are powerless in their own functions due to the lack of existence.

They performed severe penance for their true nature, O beautiful one. At the end of a thousand divine years, Gajanana (Ganesha) was pleased. He appeared before them, adorned with his trunk, pot-bellied, large-eared, four-armed, and three-eyed. Upon seeing him, they were filled with inspiration. Recognizing him, they bowed and offered prayers.

Prakriti and Purusha said: Salutations to you, Lord of the Ganas, who is known by his own self. You are of formless nature, yet your trunk shines. Your body is described as having form from the neck downwards. Because you are the non-dual union of these two, you are Gajanana. The Vedas describe the fourfold nature from your own bliss: the first is the unmanifest, the second is the uniform, the third is the nature of existence, and the fourth is that which is always non-existent. Because you have four hands, you are called Chaturbhuja (four-armed). Everything resides in your belly, hence you are known as Lambodara. You are the two feet of Maya and the two forms of existence. All these Ganas are yours, O Lord; there is no doubt. You are the Lord of the Ganas, and thus you are always known by that name.

This true self of ours appeared in our meditation. You are the one who bestows grace upon devotees when meditated upon as the self-aware. Out of love for your devotees, you have taken a body. You are Ganesha, possessing these characteristics. You have made everything blessed, O Lord, by taking this form. Remain forever in our hearts, you who dwell in the bliss of the self. Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, salutations to the remover of obstacles. Salutations to you, who creates obstacles for both devotees and non-devotees. Salutations to you, the giver of the fruits of actions, the bestower of knowledge and Siddhi, the immeasurable power, and the foundation of all. O ocean of mercy, tell us what we must do. Grant us intense devotion to you, O leader of the Ganas.

Hearing this praise, the Lord of the Ganas was pleased and spoke with a voice deep as the clouds, bringing them joy.

Shri Ganesha said: Well done, O blessed ones. This excellent hymn you have spoken will be pleasing to me and will grant all desires. It bestows righteousness, prosperity, pleasure, and liberation, and increases growth. Reciting it a hundred thousand times frees those imprisoned. It destroys death and obstacles. Whoever recites this with devotion for twenty-one days, twenty-one times each day, will obtain their desired fruit. Reciting it without desire leads to the attainment of Brahman.

Satisfied by your penance and this hymn, I am pleased and will grant you whatever you desire. From the eternal Brahman, which exists as a mere point, the fourfold universe will manifest. Because everything is created through your excellence, your name will be "Prakriti" among people. After creating the fourfold universe, you will be free from bondage, and you will surely attain my unwavering devotion.

Those who worship you with desire, and those who worship you without desire, you shall grant them everything, O beautiful one. In the fourfold creation, the Purusha (Spirit) shall reside; let him be known everywhere by the name "So'ham" (I am He). Being devoid of all forms, he will be deluded there, always enchanted by Prakriti through the nature of reflection. He himself is without delusion and is always of the nature of "I am He." By my grace, he will always be the blissful Brahman.

My unwavering devotion will be yours as well. Through it, you will be free from bondage and will be honored. People will worship you with devotion, and you will undoubtedly grant them all their desires. You will bestow the state of Brahman through the service of yoga. You, in the form of Purusha, be the essence of all existence. Place your seed into Prakriti, O mighty one; there is no doubt that she will create this entire universe. After creating the fourfold world, the tasks of preservation and dissolution must be performed in accordance with time. Prakriti is called the Mother, and Purusha is called the Father. People will imagine various forms and names for you.

Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas disappeared. Prakriti and Purusha acted exactly as they were instructed.

Chapter 7 : Description of the Praise Offered by the Elements

Parvati said: Hearing this from your lotus-like mouth, my heart was filled with joy. O Lord, please tell me again the nectar-like story of the Lord of the Ganas. This is undoubtedly the mystery of all mysteries; when heard with devotion, it purifies the questioner, the listeners, and the speaker. How did Prakriti (Nature) and Purusha (the Cosmic Being) create the universe when Ganesha, the Lord who is bliss itself, had withdrawn into his own self by his own will?

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Parvati, Shiva was delighted and began to narrate the beautiful, essence-filled story, which is the purest of all purifiers.

Shiva said: Listen, O Goddess, I shall tell you the auspicious story of the Lord of the Ganas. You are blessed, for you have listened with reverence and love to the story that destroys all obstacles. When Ganesha withdrew, Prakriti and Purusha became endowed with power and followed one another. Through penance and the granting of boons, Prakriti—who reveals the meanings of various states of being—instantly deluded Purusha. Seeing her endowed with various splendors, the supreme Purusha was completely infatuated and became forever subject to her.

When the great Purusha gazed upon the Great Maya (Prakriti), she stood before him, filled with grace and devotion. The Purusha then cast his essence—which is the source of all existence and illumination—into her, and she immediately became pregnant. That embryo grew, enveloped by its own radiance, and after a long time, it became complete and powerful. In due course, she gave birth to that supreme, radiant son, whose name became famous in the world as "Mahan" (The Great).

O Goddess, know that he who dwells within as the indweller of all is the supreme 'Mahat-tattva' (Great Principle), which must be realized through deep meditation. He became the primordial soul and the Lord of all; he is the eldest of all categories of elements and resides in the hearts of all. From this Mahat-tattva, the principle of ego (Ahamkara) was created. It became threefold, possessing different forms and great power. Because I exist everywhere and this world is supported by me, it is called Ahamkara (the "I-maker"), for it always asserts the "I."

He stands everywhere in external form as the ruler of all, ready to execute whatever the Mahat-tattva desires. O Goddess, know that he is threefold according to the qualities (Gunas): Sattva, Rajas, and Tamas. Thus, the ego also possesses three powers: the Sattvic power of knowledge, by which consciousness is awakened; the Rajasic power of action, by which work is performed; and the Tamasic power of matter, which is described as the form of the body.

First, the Tamasic power, impelled by the source of the universe, created the essence of sound, from which the sky (Akasha) was formed. From the sky, touch was created for the sake of all accomplishments, and from touch, the powerful wind was created. From the wind, form was created; from form, light (Tejas) was created; from light, taste was produced; and from taste, water emerged. From water, smell was created, and from smell, the earth was born. This creation, consisting of the Tamas quality, is truly wonderful. These five subtle elements and the five gross elements constitute the body, which is seen everywhere.

Then, through the Rajasic power, the sense organs were created, which are the instruments of action. The five organs of knowledge are the tongue, eyes, skin, nose, and ears. The five organs of action are the hands, feet, the organ of excretion, the organ of speech, and the reproductive organ; through these, actions are performed. The five vital breaths (Pranas)—Prana, Vyana, Apana, Udana, and Samana—are also known as the five Rajasic winds. This creation, which is functional and meant for the division of the seven bodily tissues, is called the Rajasic creation.

Through the Sattvic Maya, the deities of the senses were created: the presiding deities of the organs of knowledge are the Directions, Wind, Varuna, the Ashvins, and the Sun. The presiding deities of the organs of action are Agni, Vishnu, Prajapati, Indra, and Mitra. This is called the Sattvic creation, consisting of the ten deities.

These primordial deities, who are the embodiments of the elements, were born according to the law of nature, but they were devoid of knowledge. Due to this lack of knowledge, they were unable to perform the work of creation. To grant them knowledge, Prakriti and Purusha gave them the supreme, single-syllable mantra of Ganesha, along with the method of practice. They all remained there and performed intense penance. Pleased by this, Ganesha, the beloved of the Ganas, appeared before them. Seeing him, all the gods were overjoyed and offered their praises.

The Elements said: Salutations to you, O Lord with the curved trunk, protector of your devotees! Salutations to the Lord of all, the All-encompassing, the Lord of the Ganas! Salutations to you, who are both manifest and unmanifest, truth and untruth! Salutations to the Lord of obstacles, who is both equanimous and uneven! Salutations to you, who are the Self and the non-Self, the one without qualities and the one with qualities, the one who possesses name and form, and yet is beyond both! Salutations to you, who dwell in the infinite womb, the giver of various enjoyments, yet yourself beyond enjoyment, the Lord of self-bliss! Salutations to you, the support of Maya, yet beyond Maya, the deluder of the deluded, the All-knowing! Salutations to you, the possessor of all perfections, the Lord of perfections, the one beyond perfection, the Perfect One, the Lord of the perfected! Salutations to you, who are the universe and yet beyond the universe, the giver of the fruits of actions, and the very form of action! Salutations to you, who are beyond action, the giver of knowledge to the ignorant, the creator of knowledge for the wise, and yet beyond knowledge! Salutations to you, who possess the four states, who are the four states, yet beyond the four states, and known only to yourself! Salutations to you, who hold the noose and the elephant goad, the tusk and the sign of fearlessness, who have four arms, elephant ears, and a large belly! Salutations to you, the Great One, the Single-tusked, the greatest of the great, the small, the subtle-formed, and the lightness of the light! Salutations to you, the elephant-faced God, the Brahman, the form of Brahman, the Lord of Brahman, and the giver of Brahman! What praise can we offer to you, who are an ocean of infinite virtues? Salutations, salutations to Ganesha; may you always be pleased with us.

Seeing the sages and gods standing before him with folded hands, the Lord of the Ganas was pleased and spoke to them.

Chapter 8 : The Bestowing of Boons to Gunesha

Lord Shiva said: O Devi, listen to the story of Ganesha, which destroys all sins. It grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, and bestows great success upon those who contemplate it. Gajanana, the blissful, the Supreme Soul, the greatest of the great, and the one who delights his devotees, spoke with a heart filled with devotion.

Lord Gajanana said: O all you elements, ask for the boon you desire in your hearts. I am pleased by your penance, devotion, and heartfelt hymns. This hymn composed by you is very dear to me. It grants all accomplishments to those who read or listen to it. Those desiring knowledge will gain knowledge; those desiring wealth will gain wealth. Those desiring a wife will obtain one, and those desiring sons will be blessed with virtuous children. Those seeking liberation will attain it, and those seeking victory will be victorious. By reading this hymn, one attains their highest desires.

Lord Shiva said: Hearing the words of Ganesha, the sages, with necks bowed in devotion, bowed to him and spoke with joyful hearts. The primordial sages said: O Lord, if you are pleased and wish to grant a great boon today, then grant us unwavering devotion to you, O Lord of the Ganas. May we possess the great power of creation through your grace, and may we be the bestowers of all accomplishments to those who worship us. O Lord, may whatever is desired in our hearts always be fulfilled. O destroyer of obstacles, grant us eternal freedom from bondage.

Hearing the words of the elements, Ganapati himself granted their desired boons, for he is the great bestower. Lord Gajanana said: O gods, I have granted the supreme boons you have requested. Having said this, Dhundhi (Ganesha) disappeared while the sages watched. When the Lord, the Supreme Soul Ganesha, departed to his own abode, the gods became distressed and looked at one another. Having attained marvelous knowledge through Ganesha’s boon, the great gods, endowed with wisdom, began the work of creation through his power. The sages then merged into one another, attaining a state of oneness, from which the cosmic egg (Anda) was born.

In that egg, the primordial Purusha, the one who is the power of all souls, was born as the Lord himself, Gunesha, the beloved of the Gunas. Over a long period, the waters emerged from his body, and the egg rested there, growing in size. In time, he developed limbs and a complete body. By the power of his radiance, that great egg split open. He emerged, shining like countless suns, possessing infinite glory, the origin of all, the creator of all. He appeared with hands, feet, noses, stomachs, eyes, and faces in all directions, infinite and hearing everything. He is the Lord with countless limbs, infinite and noble prowess, endowed with countless divine plays, and possessing the nature of the Supreme Soul.

He is without beginning, middle, or end, pervading everywhere. He is greater than the greatest, whose limit cannot be seen. He is smaller than the smallest, and in the pores of his skin, universes revolve like dust particles in the sky. He is known as the Great Cause (Mahakarana), the fourth state, the bearer of the body, the form of self-realization, and the one of great brilliance. Because he is the Lord of the Gunas, he is called Gunesha. He is the soul of all, the form of all, and the Lord who bears the body of sound (Nada).

The actions performed by Gunesha, being of various intentions, led to the names of that great soul. Because he slept in the waters that were connected to his body, he received the name Narayana. Since the waters were born from Nara (the Lord), they are called Nara, and because they were his abode, he is called by that name. Because he creates various things in three distinct ways, he attained the name Brahma through his power. He is declared in the Vedas and Upanishads as the eternal Brahmaloka, the highest and most excellent of all; know this for certain. He is the giver of effort to all and exists in the form of Time. Because he is the mover, he is called Mahadeva. He is the foundation of all actions and is remembered as Surya (the Sun). He is the deluder of all objects and is therefore the Primordial Power.

Thus, he attained various names through various states and actions. Gunesha is the fourth state (Turiya), the great Lord present everywhere. There is no doubt that he is the cause of all causes. This Great Cause is read about in the Puranas. He is the doer of the doer, the remover of the remover, the protector of the protector, and the light of light; he is Guneshwara. He possesses three bodies according to the Gunas: he is Rajasic on the left, Sattvic in the middle, and Tamasic on the right.

Such is the great soul, the primordial Lord Guneshwara. Because he was covered by ignorance, he remained in penance. He performed severe penance, meditating on Gajanana in his heart. After a long time, Gajanana was pleased. The Lord of gods arrived, like a cow towards its calf, and out of affection, the one who loves his devotees quickly enlightened Gunesha.

Lord Ganesha said: I am pleased by your chanting of the single-syllable mantra and your penance, O mighty-armed Gunesha; ask for your desire. Thus enlightened by the merciful Ganesha, Guneshwara praised him with a heart full of devotion.

Gunesha said: I bow to the Lord, the leader of the Ganas, who is always peaceful in the hearts of yogis. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, who possesses infinite yoga and is the firm master of yoga. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, who is beyond description, the essence of renunciation, established in samadhi, without root, and both non-dual and dual. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, who is always in samadhi, infinite, the Lord who is self-evident and shining, and attainable through the yoga of one's own soul. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the adorable King of Ganas, who is attained by people through disembodied yoga (Videha Yoga) and who is not attached to any attributes. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the primordial Lord who is well-established, full of all splendor, the essence of heart-knowledge, non-dual, and the form of ultimate truth. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the primordial Ganesha, the ancient Purusha, who is without beginning, middle, or end, infinite, and the eternal form of the soul. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the formless, the source of nature, the essence of the divine point (Bindu), immeasurable, beyond illusion, and the famous deluder. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, Ganesha, who shines in the Gunas, is infinite, beyond manifestation, devoid of Gunas, and the mover of Gunas. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the one Lord of the universe, the form of deep sleep, the illuminator of all, and the bearer of the delusion of beginningless Maya. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, Ganesha, who is the universe, the bearer of subtle majesty, golden, existing in dreams, and the ancient one who grants knowledge to people. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the Lord praised by gods and sages, famous as the deluder of the world, always supreme, and endowed with gross manifestation. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the form of non-existence, non-dual in various forms, the power of Ganesha’s Maya, whose greatness is beyond mind and speech. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, Ganesha, the form of the sun, immortal, ancient, the essence of non-duality, the form of the soul, and the embodiment of undivided bliss. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, Ganesha, who is always equal, Vishnu, of inconceivable nature, devoid of duality, and always full of happiness and bliss. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the inconceivable form, devoid of all, the primordial Sadashiva, free from delusion, of infinite appearance, and self-luminous. I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the form of matter, of various kinds, endowed with change, the supreme measurable, the essence of knowledge, and the form of the word "Thou" (Tvam). I bow to the peaceful Lord of Yoga, the one Ganesha, who is undivided, the form of the universe, without change, great, immeasurable, and established in the heart. O Ganesha, these various forms are your beautiful manifestations. How can your one peaceful form be contemplated, as it is not attainable by the mind? When I speak of the all-soul, I am speaking of the form of nature. I take refuge in the letter 'Ga', the one Lord of the universe. I bow to the letter 'Na', which is beyond mind and speech, the form of renunciation, the primordial and supreme. O Dhundhi, you cannot be described, and you cannot be left undescribed; therefore, I bow to you. O Ganesha, Lord of Obstacles, O great soul, be pleased, O Lord of Brahma, O great soul. My birth is blessed, my eyes, knowledge, and the fruit of my penance are blessed, for I have seen the Lord of Ganas; I am now fulfilled. If a boon is to be granted to me, then grant me firm devotion to you, O Supreme Lord, by which delusion is destroyed. O destroyer of obstacles, grant me the power to create and fulfill the desires of those who worship you, and keep all my works free of obstacles.

Hearing the words of Gunesha, Gajanana was very pleased and spoke with a voice as deep as the clouds. Lord Ganesha said: You shall have great devotion to me, and the creation of universes will continue. Everything will be done without obstacles. O sinless one, you will always be a bestower to others. When a great task arises, I will grant you my vision. The hymn you have composed will be respected by all. For those who read or listen to it, it will be a great means to attain the state of Brahman. This hymn, which is the essence of praise, will be the supreme grantor of all accomplishments. It will grant desires to those with worldly motives and liberation to my devotees who are selfless. It is dear to me, the essence of all, and spoken everywhere. By reading it at the three junctures of the day, I am easily attained; there is no doubt in this.

Lord Shiva said: Having said this, Ganesha, who loves his devotees, disappeared. Gunesha became distressed at the separation from the Lord.

Chapter 9 : Description of the Eightfold Nature

Shri Shiva said: By the power of the boon, Gunesha (the Lord of Gunas) became knowledgeable. Being capable and divine, he set his mind to creation. From his breath, the Vedas emerged, embodying all knowledge, shining as the true gurus of the virtuous. Thereafter, Gunesha created the world known as Sushupti (deep sleep), consisting of the Mahat-tattva and other elements. Because it is the basis of all worldly dealings, it is called Pradhana (the primary cause). It is known as the cause of all dualities. Because it is filled with Maya and darkness (Tamas), it is called Tamasa. Being uniform in its nature, this Pradhana appeared as a source of delusion. Because of its inert nature, it does not know itself, nor does it know what should or should not be done; thus, it is called Mahat.

It performed severe penance for a thousand divine years. Pleased by this, Ganesha, who is fond of his devotees, appeared before it. Being worshipped and praised by it, the glorious Ganesha granted it the power of Yoga. The Vedas also performed severe penance, and Ganesha, the remover of obstacles, appeared before them. The Vedas praised him with their own hymns; the Rig Veda praised the Lord with the Brihaspati Sukta, the Yajur Veda with the Ganesha Shanti, the Sama Veda with the Namashtaka, and the Atharva Veda with the Atharvashirsha. Thus, he was praised with various hymns. Pleased by their worship with the Mula-mantra, the remover of obstacles granted them a boon, saying, "You shall be the gurus of all." Thus, the Vedas became universally respected and worshipped, and they are considered the true gurus of all rituals.

Parvati asked: O Lord, tell me what Pradhana, which is composed of Tamas and is uniform, did after receiving the boon from Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Hearing these words, the great Lord Shiva, with a serene mind and a smile, replied to her.

Shri Shiva said: Listen, O beloved Parvati, I will tell you about the glory of that Lord, which increases the devotion of Ganesha. By the boon of Ganesha, he created the subtle, golden-wombed (Hiranyagarbha), Sattvic, dream-like state. Enveloped by the Mahat-tattva and subtle elements, it stood in the space between, being the root of all dualistic knowledge. Being devoid of consciousness, it performed difficult penance. Pleased by this, Ganesha granted it the desired boon. By the power of this boon, the divine Hiranyagarbha, the lord of dream-maya, attained various powers.

He then created the gross form, known as Rajas, which provides the experience of the waking state, filled with gross elements. Being devoid of knowledge, it performed severe penance. Pleased by this, the compassionate Ganesha appeared before it. Worshipped and praised by it, he granted a boon. By the power of this boon, the Lord of the waking state became capable of creating the many from the one. The three states of waking, dreaming, and deep sleep were created in millions of variations. From the pores of Gunesha, countless different cosmic eggs (Andas) emerged and stood everywhere. Gunesha pervades these eggs, and he is the all-pervading Lord. In their non-dual state, this is Prakriti and the Brahman-point.

From the waking state, the sky (Akasha) was created, which is the manifestation of Brahman, the primordial element consisting only of sound. From the sky, air (Vayu) was created, possessing the quality of touch. From air, fire (Tejas) was created, possessing the quality of form. From fire, water (Apah) was created, possessing the quality of taste. From water, the earth (Prithvi) was created, possessing the quality of smell and the nature of support. Through the process of quintuplication (Panchikarana), each element entered the others. Thus, Earth possesses five qualities, Water four, Fire three, Air two, and the Sky one. These five elements, arranged in this order, are called the eightfold Prakriti, which forms the beautiful cosmic shell.

Because Gunesha is reflected in this shell, Prakriti attained the power of the eightfold form. Naturally, the cosmic egg was created with eight coverings. Within it, the Virat Purusha was born. I have told you that the Earth is fifty crore yojanas in extent; Water is ten times greater, Fire is ten times greater than that, Air is ten times greater than that, and the Sky is ten times greater than that. The waking state is ten times greater than the sky, the dream state (Mahat-tattva) is ten times greater than that, and the deep sleep state (Maya) is ten times greater than that. Gunesha, the fourth, is the infinite and boundless Purusha.

The eternal Earth is covered by the shell of the Virat; Water covers the Earth, Fire covers Water, Air covers Fire, the Sky covers Air, the Ego (Ahankara) covers the Sky, the Mahat-tattva covers the Ego, and Maya covers the Mahat-tattva. This is the eight-fold covering of the cosmic egg that I have described to you. O beloved, countless such eggs exist, held by Gunesha above, below, and on all sides.

Chapter 10 : The Description of Various Universes: The Tenth Chapter

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Parvati said: O Lord of all, I wish to hear about the nature of the Virat Purusha. O auspicious one, please tell me what he is like.

Shiva said: The Virat Purusha is five crore yojanas in extent in all directions. Within his body reside all beings, both stationary and moving, and all the fourteen worlds. Listen with a focused mind as I describe this to you.

His feet are the Patala (netherworld), his ankles are Rasatala, his heels are Mahatala, and his calves are Talatala. His knees are Sutala, his thighs are Vitala, his hip joints are Atala, and the Earth resides at his waist. His navel is Bhuvarloka, his heart is Svarloka, his throat is Maharloka, and his mouth is Janaloka. His forehead is Tapoloka, and his head is Satyaloka. This is the body of the great Virat Purusha. He is adorned with a thousand heads, a thousand faces, a thousand feet, a thousand hands, a thousand ears, and a thousand stomachs.

This Supreme Lord exists pervading everywhere. He is the enjoyer of immortality among mortals and exists through his own self-illumination. Within him resides a form named Hiranyagarbha. Such is the dream-like universal form of the Virat. Externally, he is known as Vaishvanara, the waking state of the Virat, while the one residing within the Hiranyagarbha-kosha is the holder of the dream-state Virat. Through the equal state of both the external and internal, the Lord known as Virat became the holder of the deep-sleep state.

O Parvati, I have told you of the three-fold form of the Virat. The fourth, the Atman-form, exists within these three. Now, hear about the origin of the various beings and their diverse natures, which are full of moving and non-moving entities. Covered by the ignorance of the Virat, he performed penance. By the method of the single-syllable mantra, he meditated upon Ganesha in his heart. He performed severe penance for a thousand divine years. Through the power of this penance and meditation, the Lord of Ganas became manifest before him.

The Lord told him to ask for a boon. Seeing Ganesha suddenly, the Virat bowed at his feet. He said, "My birth, my penance, my knowledge, my body, and my vision are all blessed today. My wealth is blessed by the sight of your lotus feet." Having said this, he began to praise Ganesha, the Lord of the Vedas. The Lord, the sustainer of the universe, was pleased by his clear words.

The Virat said: Salutations to you, O Lord of the Ganas, Lord of all groups. Salutations to the remover of obstacles, the Supreme Lord. Salutations to the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the giver of various attainments, the bestower of diverse knowledge, the Brahman, and the one whose form is Brahman. You are the essence of mind, speech, and the elements; you dwell in the hearts of yogis. You are the creator of various incarnations and the protector of your devotees. Salutations, salutations to the great Lord, the auspicious one, the giver of bliss. You are the same everywhere, the all-pervading Vishnu. Salutations to you, the Lord of the mass of brilliance, the Sun. Salutations to you, who are the power behind the various manifestations of Maya, and who are the form of power itself. You are the creator, the sustainer, and the destroyer; you are the essence of all moving and non-moving things. Salutations to the pure one, the giver of knowledge to all. Salutations to you, who are distinct from all, devoid of Maya, and the one who bestows delusion upon those caught in Maya. Salutations to you, whose limits even the various Brahmas could not reach. Therefore, O Lord of Ganas, please show grace upon me.

Having praised Ganesha in this way, the Virat Purusha stood silent with folded hands. The Lord Ganesha, filled with devotion, said to him, "Ask for a boon, I will grant it; do not worry, O Lord of the universe. This hymn composed by you will be pleasing to me. Whoever recites it with devotion will attain success. Whatever desire they contemplate, I will grant it. Its recitation and hearing will bestow both worldly enjoyment and liberation."

Having been told this, the Virat deity bowed to the primordial Lord of Ganas. Filled with emotion, he spoke to the remover of obstacles. The Virat said: "If you are pleased, O Lord of gods, and if a great boon is to be granted, then grant me firm devotion to you, by which all delusion is destroyed. By your grace, O giver of enjoyment and liberation, I shall perform the unparalleled creation, sustenance, and destruction of the universe. Grant me incomparable majesty, O elephant-faced Lord. May everything I desire be accomplished through your grace."

Shiva said: Saying "So be it," Ganesha disappeared. The Virat, filled with joy, meditated upon Ganesha in his heart. Thus, O Devi, in all the various universes, the Virat beings performed this excellent penance to Ganesha. By his grace, all these great souls remained established in their respective duties, free from obstacles. Know that the universes are infinite; no one except Ganesha can describe them accurately. Even the Lord of Ganas does not attempt to count them. The number of universes exceeds all counts known in the scriptures. Therefore, no one versed in the Vedas has ever counted them. Know that these infinite universes are all filled with the presence of the Lord. Such is the power of Ganesha, who dwells in my heart, who is fond of meditation, the Lord of devotees, and who loves his devotees.

Thus ends the tenth chapter titled "The Description of Various Universes" in the first section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 11 : The Granting of Boons to the Five Deities

Parvati said: O Lord, Lord of Lords, please be gracious and tell me how the Vairata (the Cosmic Form) created the moving and non-moving universe. Also, O Lord, tell me the ancient path of meditation by which I may realize Ganesha directly, without any doubt.

Mudgala said: Being asked thus by the Great Goddess, the Great Lord (Shiva) was very pleased and narrated the most sacred story; listen to it.

Daksha said: Hearing the story of Ganesha brings joy to the heart. O Great One, please tell me what was narrated by Shiva.

Mudgala said: You are blessed, Daksha, in this world, for you have such devotion to the story of Ganesha, which is like a stream of nectar; there is no doubt about it.

Seeing the delighted Girija (Parvati), Shiva spoke these words: You are blessed, O Great Lady, listen from the beginning with devotion.

Shiva said: Meditating on Ganesha in his mind, Vairata began the creation. From his navel, Brahma, the grandfather of the worlds, was born. From his mouth, Vishnu, the protector of the world, was born, and from his eye, I, Hara (Shiva), was born. We are the foundations of creation, maintenance, and dissolution, possessing the three qualities (Gunas). From his left side, you, O Parvati, the enchantress, were born as Shakti. From his right side, the Sun was born, who became the foundation of all actions.

The five deities were born, distinct by nature. O beautiful one, they did not know themselves, nor did they know the Vairata. They wandered for a long time in total darkness. They found nothing despite their efforts and remained in great distress. Thinking, "What should be done? Where should we go?" they were deeply covered in ignorance. "From whom have we been born? We do not know the Great Lord."

Then, as they pondered, their intellect became focused. They began to perform penance, saying, "Salutations to whoever it may be." Contemplating the source of all, they performed severe penance. Through this penance, the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) became pleased and resided within them. Then, vision dawned upon those whose minds were purified. They saw the wonderful seed-form pulsating in their hearts. Having received the single-syllable great mantra, they were filled with joy.

By chanting that mantra from then on, O Mother of the World, they attained pure knowledge through the power of that mantra. After a thousand divine years, Ganesha became manifest and stood before them. He possessed the radiance of ten million suns, adorned with his trunk, four-armed, pot-bellied, and directly decorated. Adorned with ornaments and weapons, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, he is the giver of joy to devotees, the glorious one, riding a mouse, and of great splendor.

Seeing him, the gods, led by Shambhu (Shiva), were struck with fear. "What is this great radiance coming, like the fire of dissolution? Will this deity burn us, O Lord of Obstacles, Ocean of Compassion? We are your devotees, O Lord; why are we being afflicted by obstacles?"

Hearing their words, he assumed a gentle form. Seeing him, all were delighted and bowed down with devotion. By the very sight of Ganesha, a wonderful inspiration was attained. By that, all five of them knew everything as it truly was. Then, all of them praised him with necks bowed in devotion, their hair standing on end, and eyes filled with tears.

The Five Deities said: Salutations to you, O King of Obstacles, the remover of obstacles for your devotees, and the creator of obstacles for the non-devotees. Salutations to Ganesha. Salutations to you, Heramba; salutations to you, Dhundhiraja. Salutations to the Lord Vinayaka, the leader of the Brahmas. Salutations to the pot-bellied one, the Lord of Siddhis, the elephant-faced one. Salutations to the one with winnowing-basket ears, the hidden one, the four-armed one. Salutations to the one with the hanging lip, the single-tusked one, the Lord of all, the leader of the Ganas. Salutations to you, the foundation of infinite glory, the supporter of the earth.

Salutations to you, the one who is Maya-like and the one who is devoid of Maya. Salutations to the giver of delusion, and salutations to the destroyer of delusion. Salutations to the one who is the five elements and the supporter of the five elements. Salutations to the Lord of the senses and the one who provides knowledge of the senses. Salutations to you, the Adhyatma (Self), Adhibhuta (elements), and Adhidaiva (divine). Salutations to you, the food, the Lord of food, and the eater of food. Salutations to the life-breath, the Lord of life, and the form of life. Salutations to the mind, the one beyond the mind, and the giver of consciousness to the mind. Salutations to the knowledge, the Lord of knowledge, and the holder of dualities. Salutations to the giver of self-knowledge to the wise.

Salutations to you, the bliss, the Lord of bliss, the giver of bliss, and the cause of all. Salutations to the consciousness, the effort, and the supporter of sentience. Salutations to the one who gives self-consciousness to the conscious, the form of sound. Salutations to the point (Bindu), the Lord of points, and the natural one. Salutations to you, who are both distinct and non-distinct, the form of light. Salutations to you, the 'I am He' (So'ham), the void, the supporter of the void, the embodied one. Salutations to the person (Purusha) who is the void of voids. Salutations to the knowledge, the Lord of wisdom, and the creator of understanding. Salutations to you, who are beyond speech and mind, the soul of all.

Salutations to you, the bodiless one, the supporter of the bodiless. Salutations to the one who is beyond the bodiless, the form of Sankhya. Salutations to you, who take many forms and the one-and-many forms. Salutations to you, the form of bliss in the unreal, the form of Shakti. Salutations to you, the immortal, the eternal, devoid of distinction and non-distinction. Salutations to you, the form of the eternal Self, the supporter of the sun-form. Salutations to you, who are beyond truth and untruth, the form of equal bliss. Salutations to you, Vishnu, the root of bliss. Salutations to you, the unmanifest, the Supreme Lord, the one described as 'not this, not this'. Salutations to you, the auspicious, the eternal, the one devoid of delusion.

Salutations to you, the giver of the state of silence, who takes form in union and meditation. Salutations to you, the one beyond union, the form of non-dependence. Salutations to you, the god devoid of Maya, the one beyond meditation. Salutations to you, the giver of peace, the giver of complete peace. Salutations to you, the Lord of yogas and the form of yoga. Salutations to you, Ganesha, the Supreme Lord, whose virtues are infinite. Salutations to you, the giver of yoga and peace, the great yoga.

Whose end of virtues even the Vedas and the creators of the Vedas could not reach, how can he be praised? He is praised according to one's capacity. May that Lord, the Chintamani elephant-faced one, be pleased and be our savior; you are our ultimate goal.

Having said this, the Lords of the gods became silent. Ganesha, with a pleased heart, spoke to them.

Shri Ganesha said: O Great Deities, I am pleased with your praises, penance, and devotion. Ask for the boon you desire. This hymn composed by you is the increaser of supreme joy. It will be pleasing to me and will grant everything through devotion. Whoever reads it with devotion will attain Dharma, wealth, desires, and liberation. He will be prosperous with children and grandchildren and will attain supreme bliss in the end. If one reads it daily for seven times or for twenty-one days, even one who is in prison will be freed from bondage. Whoever reads it once, twice, or three times a day will undoubtedly be worshipped by the gods and others. By reading it twenty-one times for twenty-one days, one will have no fear of death or removal. Everything, including wealth, grain, health, and cattle, and whatever desire one contemplates, one will certainly attain.

Shiva said: Hearing the words of Ganesha, the gods led by Vishnu were delighted. Bowing to the Lord of the Ganas with folded hands, they said:

The Five Deities said: We have become fulfilled, O Lord, now that you have come. Grant us a boon, O Lord of the Ganas, and grant us firm devotion to you. By that, O Lord, may Maya not bind humans or even gods. Tell us what we should always do. Give us various powers to fulfill the desires of those who worship us. Give us a place named Sadananda (Eternal Bliss), O Lord, salutations to you.

Ganesha said: O four-faced one, O mighty-armed one, born of the Rajas quality, you shall be the creator of the world, named Brahma, O radiant one. Reside in Satyaloka, have the swan as your vehicle, be endowed with the knowledge of the Vedas, and be the grandfather of all. You, O Lord of gods, are four-armed, born of the Sattva quality. You shall be the protector of all worlds, named Vishnu. Your residence shall be Vaikuntha, your vehicle shall be Garuda, and you shall be of great strength with many incarnations. O five-faced one, of great radiance, born of the Tamas quality, you shall be the destroyer, named Hara. Your residence shall be Kailasa, your vehicle shall be the bull, and with your third eye, you shall turn everything to ashes.

O thousand-rayed one, you shall be named Surya, the mover of actions, the revealer of the six seasons' duties. Because of the ego of the three qualities, you have become the revealer. You shall have a chariot adorned with horses as your vehicle. Your residence shall be the solar world, and you shall be the king of planets. You are the root of rain, a mass of radiance, and powerful.

O four-armed one, O great power, you have been born from the three bodies. You shall be the enchantress, named Shakti, the Great Maya, for the sake of various worldly enjoyments. You shall be the giver of delusion in two ways, the embodiment of worldly enjoyment and liberation. Riding a lion, residing in the world of Shakti, you shall delude the world with various enjoyments. When a great task arises, I shall become manifest. There is no doubt that I have made the gods equal to me in all respects.

The Lord of the Ganas gave them weapons, excellent ornaments, various wonderful powers, and the fulfillment of their resolutions.

Shiva said: Having granted these boons, the Lord Ganesha disappeared. They were also delighted and considered themselves fulfilled. "We have been made fulfillers of desires for those who worship with devotion. We have attained the status of Supreme Lords by the grace of Ganapa."

Thus ends the eleventh chapter named "The Granting of Boons to the Five Deities" in the first section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 12 : The Dispute Between Brahma and Vishnu

Shounaka said: O Suta, O highly wise one, bestower of supreme bliss, please narrate to me the nectar-like story, O radiant one. We have attained satisfaction and feel fulfilled, as if we are drenched in the joy of drinking the nectar of your stories. Please narrate the divine story of what the five deities did, in detail and according to the scriptures, so that we may attain liberation. Hearing the devotion-filled words of the sages, the joyful Suta spoke the sin-destroying story again.

Suta said: O great sage, just as the great Lord of Yoga, Mudgala, was asked by Daksha, I shall narrate the same to you. Mudgala said: Listen, O fortunate Daksha, I shall narrate in detail all that was asked by Parvati and heard in the presence of Shiva.

Parvati said: O Lord, O Lord of gods, I have been delivered by you. By drinking the nectar of your stories, I am now fulfilled. The five deities, empowered by boons, became mighty. What did those great souls do? Please tell me according to the tradition. Narrate to me the story filled with devotion to Ganesha, or the life of the Ganapatyas which bestows Brahman. One should not waste life in useless chatter; blessed are those saintly people who are devoted to the Lord of the Ganas.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of his beloved, the highly glorious Maheshwara was very pleased. Smiling, he embraced the Goddess and spoke. Shiva said: You are blessed, O Parvati, and your life is meaningful. Because you have held Ganesha in your heart with devotion, O beautiful one. Those who are devoted to Ganesha are themselves of the nature of Brahman; there is no doubt that they are the givers of liberation just by their sight. He who desires eternal success while abandoning Ganesha is a fool and is destined for hell, without doubt. Those who are devotees of other gods but do not worship Ganesha will not attain success and will fall into hell. Just as a foolish person consumes dry food while abandoning nectar, so too is the servant of another god who abandons Ganesha. He is the father and mother of us all, there is no doubt. Being known as the eldest lord, he is the first to be worshipped. The foolish soul who abandons Ganesha to worship other gods is cast into hell by us for transgressing against Ganesha. A person who criticizes Ganesha is an enemy of our devotee; he is a hater of our Guru and will immediately go to hell. Therefore, you are blessed, for you worship Ganesha, the root seed of all, with devotion. O Goddess, what you have said is true; the glorification of Ganesha makes one's birth meaningful.

I pray to Ganesha: O Lord Chintamani, O dweller of the heart, O elephant-faced one with a large belly, may I always have the company of the fortunate Ganapatyas. O elephant-faced one, grant me a dwelling among them. May those Ganapatyas be before me, behind me, to my left, and to my right; may my dwelling be everywhere with those Ganapatyas. Those upon whose lotus-like faces the story of Ganesha shines, which destroys the bonds of the world and grants happiness here and hereafter, are blessed. The life of the Ganapatyas, which is dedicated to the service of the Lord of the Ganas, day and night, fulfills all desires and grants devotion to Dhundhi. O Goddess, I have been delivered by you, yet you ask me again about the story of Ganesha, which is pure with devotion. I shall tell you that story of Ganesha which grants the state of Brahman to the speaker, the listener, and the one who asks.

How the five deities were born and how Ganesha himself, the bestower of boons, spoke to them as commanded by the Lord, I shall tell you. Five worlds were created by the power of the boons, and they lived in them separately, unsupported and eternal. Shiva lived in Kailasa, Vishnu in Vaikuntha, the Sun in the Solar world, the Goddess in the world of power, and Brahma lived in Satyaloka, filled with joy. They were enjoyers of wealth and pleasure, and were devoted to each other's welfare. They were firm in their resolutions and considered themselves to be the Lords of Brahman. After a long time, jealousy arose among them. Thinking in their hearts, "I am the greatest and the Lord of all," they immediately began to argue.

Water was created by Vishnu from his own body, and the Lord slept in it for a long time in comfort. Gradually, moss and creepers grew in the water, and after a long time, the Earth was naturally formed within it. For the sake of play, Vishnu created a brilliant lotus from his navel, a hundred yojanas wide and adorned with petals. Seeing it full of fragrance, Janardana delighted in it. At that same time, the grandfather Brahma went there. He saw the lotus and was greatly astonished in his heart, wondering, "Who created this divine and beautiful lotus?" Seeing Narayana sleeping there in the great ocean, Brahma woke the Lord out of affection. At the words of Brahma, Janardana arose and honored Brahma, saying, "Welcome, O Grandfather."

Brahma, filled with jealousy, said to the great Vishnu, "Why did you create this beautiful lotus to delude me? I am Brahma, the creator of the world, the beginning of all, and the source of all. Creation is done by me, and you will protect it later. If I do not create it, what will you protect, O Janardana? Everyone is under my control, therefore I am the Lord." Hearing the words of Brahma, Janardana was astonished and spoke sweet words, as if to console him. Vishnu said, "I did not create the lotus to delude you, O Grandfather. See that this lotus is only for the sake of play; there is no doubt about it. You have spoken these words out of jealousy, O Grandfather. I am the one who protects you, and that is why you are the creator. There is no other protector besides me, as I am the protector of all. I am the great Vishnu, and there is none equal to me."

Thus ends the twelfth chapter titled "The Dispute Between Brahma and Vishnu" in the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 13 : The Dispute of the Five Deities

Shiva said: Listen, O Goddess, to this sacred story that increases the devotion to Ganesha. I shall tell you what happened in the beginning of time. Hearing the words of Vishnu, which were filled with pride due to his power, the Lord Brahma, the creator of the Vedas, spoke to him.

Brahma said: O Vishnu, if you are my protector and the great Lord of all, then behold my power by entering into my belly, O teacher of the world. Hearing the words of Brahma, Janardana (Vishnu) entered into the belly of the Creator by the power of his Yoga-maya. There, he saw the creation of the universe in various supreme forms, and he also saw Vishnu himself, the Supreme Person, residing within a lotus arising from the navel. Astonished and deluded in his heart, he wondered what this great wonder was. He kept looking but could not find the end, so he emerged from the body.

Having emerged, the Lord bowed down and said to the Creator: O Brahma, I truly do not know your end, there is no doubt. I resided in your belly, therefore you are my Guru; you are the Lord of all, there is no doubt, for you are the creator by your own power. O Lord, you also behold the supreme power within my belly and see what it is like.

Hearing the words of Narayana, Brahma was astonished in his mind. By the power of Yoga, he went there as he pleased. Inside the belly of Vishnu, Brahma saw the universe filled with worlds, and he was deluded to see another Brahma residing there. Brahma could not find the end as he kept looking at the Great Soul. He decided to go out, but the path was blocked by Vishnu. The doors were closed by his own Maya through the power of Yoga. Wandering and wandering, Brahma himself could not find the excellent path. Then, by the power of Yoga, Brahma suddenly pierced the door from the lotus stalk and went outside.

Having come out, he spoke to Vishnu in anger: Why have you brought me under your control and become independent, O Lord? I emerged by my own power through Yoga-maya; why were the doors closed, tell me, O Madhava? Hearing the words of the God, the glorious Vishnu smiled and spoke to him with affection and composure.

Vishnu said: O Creator, I do not wish to bring you under my control, O teacher of the world. The doors were closed by me only for the sake of play. O Great Grandfather, whatever was done for the sake of sport, please forgive my offense, as I only wished to know your power.

Being thus appeased, Brahma was pleased and said to him again: O Vishnu, I did not know that what you did was for the sake of play. I became filled with anger due to ignorance, O Lord; please forgive the unpleasant words I spoke. I do not know your end, as I resided in your belly. You are my superior and my direct Guru, there is no doubt, O Hari.

Since you and I are both wise, superior, and supreme Lords, Shiva, Shakti, and Surya also became filled with pride. The two Lords remembered that their authority should be known. By their mere remembrance, those three arrived there. First, the Lord Shankara, the wielder of the trident, arrived and asked them why they had remembered him. Hari and Brahma said: Listen to our words, O Shankara. Show us the extent of your majesty, O Sadashiva.

Hearing their words, the prideful Sadashiva spoke with anger, as if in astonishment. Shiva said: I am the destroyer of all, and there is no doubt that I am superior to both of you. There is nothing beyond me that needs to be known in any way. Having said this, the Lord Shambhu himself showed them his form: a great Linga of light, supreme and existing everywhere.

Seeing this great wonder, the Creator and Madhava were astonished. They looked below, above, in front, behind, to the left, and to the right. Then Brahma said sweet words to Vishnu: O Lord of Gods, you go below, and I shall go above. Let us see the end of Shiva and see what kind of Great Lord he is. Therefore, be quick and do this immediately.

Being told this by Brahma, Vishnu replied to him: O Brahma, give me a boon, and then I shall strive to go behind. Brahma, being deluded, said: Ask for the boon. I will give it, there is no doubt; be pleased and ask quickly. Vishnu said: O Brahma, become my son; this is the boon I desire. Hearing those words, the astonished Creator said: I have surely been deceived by you in this confusion. Since I have given the boon, I shall become your son. When the time of creation arrives, I shall create a body from your navel-lotus by Yoga, and as the lotus-born, I shall be your son.

Having been told this, Hari bowed to him and said "So be it," and entered below to see the end of the Great Soul Shiva. Brahma also quickly went above to find the end, but the Grandfather could not find the end of the Lord there. Vishnu also could not see the end of Shankara below. After a long time, they both returned, distressed in mind. Through mutual deliberation, they prepared to praise Shankara. With folded hands, they performed an excellent hymn.

Brahma and Vishnu said: Salutations to Shankara, salutations to the wielder of the trident. Salutations again and again to Rudra, the form of time, and the three-eyed one. Salutations to you, O Lord of Uma, whose vehicle is the bull. Salutations to you, O Primeval God, O God, O Great God. Be pleased, O Lord Shambhu; we cannot see your end, O Lord. We do not know your form or your nature in any way. Show us your own form, O Great Lord, salutations to you, so that people may worship you, O God, without fear.

Hearing their words, Shambhu manifested himself. Then Hari and the Lord Creator saw him. They saw Vishnu on the left side and the Grandfather on the right side, and the entire moving and non-moving universe residing within that Linga. Seeing his infinite glory, they bowed with respect: Withdraw that form, for you are the Supreme Lord. Hearing their words, the happy Lord Shankara, the bull-bannered one, stood in his previous form. The Great God told them to ask for their desired boon, as he was pleased by their hymn. Whoever reads this hymn will obtain all their desires. He will become dear to me and will go to my abode at the end.

Chapter 14 : the Manifestation of Ganesha

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Shiva said: Listen, O Devi, I shall tell you what happened thereafter, which is wondrous, and describes the character of those deluded by the illusion of Ganesha. Hearing the words of Shiva, Vishnu said to Brahma, "O Creator, ask for a boon that you may become my son." Brahma replied, "You have become the son of Shiva, and through him, you have become my grandson. Thus, the desired and wondrous wishes of both have been fulfilled." Hearing the words of Vishnu, Brahma said to Shankara, "Become my son; this is the boon I have chosen." Vishnu also said the same, and Shankara replied, "O Creator, I shall be born from your forehead as Rudra, a portion of myself." Hearing the words of Shiva, Brahma and Vishnu were pleased and spoke to Shankara with great affection. They said, "O Shiva, behold our divine power separately, so that we may be satisfied, O Great God, there is no doubt." Shiva, using his eye of wisdom, looked at their divine nature, but could not find its end, and he spoke to them again. "I see your glory as infinite and eternal. We three are declared to be the supreme Lords in every way."

In the meantime, the Sun God arrived there, a mass of radiance and a soul of truth, and asked with focus, "Why have you gods remembered me with such respect? Tell me today, for I have come out of great affection." Hearing the words of the Sun, the three were very pleased and said with a cheerful heart, "O Lord, show us your divine power." Hearing their gentle words, he showed them his form, which was radiant, infinite, and encompassing all moving and non-moving things. By the radiance of the Sun, all three were rendered lusterless; their vision was lost, and they could not see clearly. Seeing him with the eye of wisdom as the Lord of the moving and non-moving, possessing an infinite and brilliant form, they bowed to him with affection. Having shown his true self, he assumed a gentle form. The three gods said to him, "O Lord, we could not see the end of your form. Now, please behold our divine power with affection." Hearing their words, the Sun was pleased. The Sun saw their supreme divine power through his eye of wisdom and spoke, bowing down, his mind filled with great astonishment. "Even you, O great souls, I cannot see the end of your nature from any side. I have seen your wondrous divine power. We are all supreme Lords, eternal and divine; there is no doubt that there is no difference of more or less even by a fraction."

At that moment, the supreme Shakti, Mohini, arrived herself and said, "Why have you remembered me, for I am pleased?" The four gods, filled with affection, said to her, "O Great Goddess, show us what your divine power is like." Being thus addressed, she showed her beautiful form, which was full of all moving and non-moving things, endowed with both the gross and the subtle. Seeing her form, they were bewildered and sought refuge, for they could not see the end of that great form. She then assumed a gentle form, and they said, "O Devi, behold our divine power with affection, and tell us what we are." Hearing their words, she looked with the eye of wisdom, but could not find the end of them, and she bowed to them specifically. She said with affection, "I cannot see the end anywhere. You are also great souls, Lords in your fullness. We five are equal lords; no difference is seen anywhere. Which god is the most capable? No certainty is seen."

Deluded by Ganesha's illusion, they said to each other, "The wise read in the Vedas that there is only one Lord everywhere. The existence of five has not been mentioned anywhere in the accepted scriptures. Therefore, there must be some other Lord, and where does he reside?" As this dispute arose, they forgot their original knowledge. Due to the restlessness of their minds caused by Ganesha, they went upwards, but saw nothing there. Then they went downwards, and thus wandered in the ten directions, looking at each other. They saw nothing there and stood together once again. Through the path of yoga, they meditated in their minds in sequence. They delighted in the form of Brahma, which is non-dual, through their yogic state. They became as if one soul and attained peace by nature. Then, the Lord of the Ganas revealed to them the supreme form, which is the object of meditation for yogis, residing in the heart. He was four-armed, three-eyed, adorned with a trunk, one-tusked, with large ears, a curved trunk, and a large belly. He was red in color, of a great body, smeared with vermilion, with a navel, and adorned with various ornaments like a crown. He was complete, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, worshipped with divine powers, and held the auspicious Chintamani jewel in his heart. His face was pleasant, and he was the granter of grace to his devotees. O beloved, the gods were astonished to see him in their hearts. He first appeared in the heart of Shankara himself. Shankara asked him, "Who are you, O Lord? Tell me. Have you been attained through the yoga of peace, or are you the supreme Lord of Brahma? Tell me, O great one, I do not know you in truth."

Chapter 15: The Grace of Ganesha

Suta said: Hearing the words of Shiva, who was filled with devotion, the Lord of Obstacles, Ganesha, spoke sweet words to delight him. Vakratunda said: Listen, O Shiva, O great soul, you who are deluded by my Maya. I am that same Ganesha whom you have seen again through your purified soul and penance. In the past, I was the supreme boon-giver to you, and pleased by your intense penance, I granted you wisdom. My Maya constantly deludes humans, and there is no doubt that because of it, even you forgot. Having said this, he dispelled the web of illusion from Shiva, and Shiva regained all his memory. Remembering everything, Shiva began to praise him, but at that moment, the auspicious form of Ganapati disappeared.

Shiva remained in meditation, contemplating him, but when he could not see that form in his heart, he became distressed. Vishnu arrived nearby, and Shiva told him everything. He also described that he could not see that form. Thus, all five gods were greatly astonished at not seeing him. They gathered together and, having shed their pride, began to praise him.

The five gods said: Salutations to you, O Vakratunda, who bears three eyes. Salutations to the four-armed God who carries the noose and the goad. Salutations to you, O Lambodara, salutations to you whose navel is the universe. Salutations to the great one, the single-tusked one, and the one with winnowing-basket ears. Salutations again and again to Ganesha, whose body is red like vermilion, who wears red garments, and who is both massive and subtle. Salutations to you, who are accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, who possess divine glory, who carry the Chintamani, and who are the Chintamani yourself. Salutations to the unmanifest, the supreme Lord, the supreme of the supreme, who is beyond mind and speech, and the Lord of Obstacles. Salutations to the bearer of various illusions, the deluder of the deluded, O Heramba, salutations to you, the Lord of the Vedas. Reveal your form that brings joy to your devotees. We are protected by you; salutations to you, the Lord of all.

As they praised him, the elephant-faced God appeared before them. Seeing him, they all bowed down like a staff with devotion. Lifting them up, Dhundhi (Ganesha) himself spoke these words: O gods, I am pleased by your praise, there is no doubt. Whoever recites this hymn shall be honored by me. I will fulfill their desires, and in the end, they shall attain union with me. Listen to my beautiful words and do not be proud, my dear ones. You are all under my command and are parts of my essence, there is no doubt. You were all created for the sake of play, competing with one another. Why have you become confused in your minds, even though you are complete in your essence? By hearing this secret knowledge of mine, you shall be purified, forever freed from delusion, and become like me.

Thus ends the fifteenth chapter entitled The Grace of Ganesha, in the Vakratunda-charita of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 16: The Essence of Ganesha Gita

Shiva said: Having heard the words of Ganesha, the sages, filled with devotion and humility, bowed down and asked him again, O Elephant-faced one, please instruct us in the ultimate knowledge.

Ganesha said: The body is said to be of four types, and the word 'Thou' refers to the differentiation of Brahman. The 'I' (the indweller) is also fourfold, and the word 'That' refers to Brahman, which is eternally one. The union of these two is what is called the word 'Brahman'. Arising from oneself, it becomes twofold due to the creation of duality. Brahman, which is always established in its own bliss, is called Sankhya. That which arises from another is supreme because it is devoid of play. That Brahman which is said to be devoid of arising from oneself or another is the Self-Bliss, which is the form of all non-duality and the cause of union.

Know that this has become fivefold, O gods; understand this. The arising of Brahman from oneself and from another is remembered as twofold. In the Vedas, the name of Brahman is spoken of due to these different states. The experience of these two arises in the hearts of the yogis. That very form should be known as the 'non-existent' (asat) spoken of in the Vedas; this is called Shakti, which is the form of Brahman and consists of the non-existent.

In that, the sun is called the soul, the support of nectar. Vishnu is the sun of Shakti, and bliss arises from their union. In the three states, his form is that which is devoid of action. Shiva is the fourth, beyond the three, as he is the negation of them. The three states are merely delusion, while Sadashiva is the one without delusion. In their non-duality, the Brahman that exists is Self-Bliss, attained through all yoga.

The reflection of the five Brahmans is remembered as being made of Maya. He alone should be known as Brahman, the beginning of all and the creator of all. By this reflection, everything is created, and thus he is the Great Grandfather. We are remembered as the form of Self-Bliss, transcending both the existent and the non-existent. That which is beyond Self-Bliss is the supreme Brahman, known as Ayoga (non-union) among the Brahmans; no one can enter there, nor can he himself go anywhere.

My vision does not exist for yogis in that state of Ayoga. The vision attained in Self-Bliss is of the nature of self-awareness. Therefore, in the Vedas, they call me the dweller of Self-Bliss. The enjoyments of the four Brahmans are through the union of non-duality. Union and non-union are considered beyond those. Yoga, which provides complete peace, is the restraint of the fluctuations of the mind.

The mind is fivefold: scattered, dull, distracted, one-pointed, and restrained. The wish-fulfilling gem resides there. By restraining these five states, yogis attain it in their hearts. Through the yoga of the form of peace, peace becomes my own nature. This knowledge of Ganesha, which is of the nature of yoga, has been told by me. Always remain united with yoga, and you will never fall into delusion.

The intellect is itself the form of the mind, and success is remembered as being full of delusion. The Lord of that plays with both through the differentiation of various Brahmans. By abandoning anxiety and ego, and through Samadhi, you too will become like me, free from delusion, O Ganesha.

Shiva said: Having said this, Ganesha, who is fond of his devotees, became silent. They too, abandoning all duality, attained peace at that very moment. This essence of the Gita, proclaimed by Ganesha in twenty-one excellent verses, grants peace to those who are calm and practice yoga. Whoever reads this essence of Ganesha Gita with devotion, or listens to it with faith, becomes equal to Brahman. Having enjoyed all pleasures here, one becomes full of yoga at the end. By the mere sight of such a person, all the sins of the world are dissolved.

Chapter 17 : The Conclusion of the Dialogue Between Shiva and Parvati

Shiva said: Having heard the essence of the Ganesha Gita from you, the five lords of the gods, being astonished, bowed to the leader of the Ganas and spoke.

The five gods said: O Lord, everything you have said is indeed the truth. By that, we are satisfied, O Master, and you have made us free from delusion. By your mere vision, our previous memories have been restored. O Dhundhi (Ganesha), tell us, how are we, who are of the nature of qualities (Gunas), also of the nature of Brahman?

Hearing their words, Gajanana (Ganesha), the great Lord, who is pleased by the devotion of his followers, spoke to them.

Ganesha said: In their own state, the lords of the world are indeed of the nature of Brahman. I have created you as beings of qualities (Gunas) in a differentiated state. I play in four types of states; the reflection that fell through this play is why I became fivefold. O lords of the gods, in those various pastimes and states, you are undoubtedly dependent on me. In these various manifestations, I have created Brahma, Hari, and Shambhu, differentiated by Maya, consisting of Rajas, Sattva, and Tamas. The ego of the three gods, the sun, and the delusion-filled power (Shakti) for the fulfillment of the three qualities—these five have been created by me.

I am present here in a partial aspect and am known as Vakratunda (the curved-trunk one). I am the direct manifestation of the Bindu-Brahman, the revealer of the differentiated state. O gods, the Turiya (fourth) form of mine, which I have created as the abode of qualities, should be known as the form of intellect; it is called my right side. The left side, which is the play of the five gods, is the form of Siddhi (perfection). I, endowed with Siddhi and Buddhi, became the essence of the Bindu.

The obstacles of the devotees are called 'Vakra' (crooked) by the gods; I shall destroy them with my trunk, hence I am known as Vakratunda. Because my Maya is from my own self, and the curved (Vakra) is the face of Brahman, I am Vakratunda, attainable by the one whose mind is turned inward. For the sake of your work and for the sake of play, I have assumed the form of the Bindu through Maya and remain here with intent.

If you forget to remember me, then, O great lords, you will become confused by the delusion of Maya. Therefore, you must always keep the remembrance of me in your hearts. I shall be worshipped first in all divine and ancestral rites. By that, you will all undoubtedly be endowed with success. Having said this, the Lord Ganesha, the protector of the Ganas, disappeared.

Shiva said: Thus, O Devi, the ancient path of meditation has been told to you. I meditate upon that Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, who resides in my heart. O Shiva, the entire universe, moving and non-moving, is created by Brahma. Vishnu, possessing various incarnations, protects it by the command of the Lord of the Ganas. I am the destroyer, and you, O Shiva, are the giver of joy. The sun is undoubtedly the mover of actions through time.

You are the daughter of the Himalayas, and therefore you had forgotten. You have all the knowledge that awakens the Ganesha in your heart. I have reminded you, so remember everything, O one with a beautiful smile. You are the Mahamaya; behold the Lord of the Ganas in your heart. I shall briefly tell you of his eight incarnations. By worshipping him through that path, Yoga is not difficult to attain.

The first is Vakratunda, called the Brahman of the 'Twam' (Thou) word. The second is Ekadanta, the revealer of the Brahman of the 'Tat' (That) word. The third is Mahodara, the holder of the 'Asi' (Art) word of Brahman. The fourth is Gajanana, who is the form of Sankhya, O great Goddess. Lambodara is famous; this Brahman resides in you. The sixth is named Vikata, the supreme sun of the self-Brahman. Vighnaraja is Vishnu himself, the seventh, who has attained equality. The eighth is Dhumravarna, who is Shambhu, said to be the unmanifest.

These eight are known as the holders of the eight-fold Brahman, the manifestations of the Lord of the Ganas. They are declared as the incarnations of the Ganaraja. O beloved, they are held by the Brahman which is self-aware. Therefore, O Devi, he is known as the player of all Brahmans. For the one who is not in Yoga, there is no play. Know the one who dwells in his own bliss as the player, O beloved. The Lord of the Ganas is complete Yoga, devoid of the Yoga of self-bliss. Peace is attained by Yogis there through the service of Yoga.

Thus, everything that you asked, O beautiful Devi, has been told. By this method, always worship Ganesha daily. There is no doubt that Ganesha is our family deity. I worship him, the Chintamani Vinayaka, daily. He remains as the illuminator of the five functions of the mind; therefore, that Gajanana is called Chintamani. Behold the elephant-faced image, O Devi, situated in the house. I worship that jewel-like form with great devotion.

Mudgala said: Having said this, Sadashiva showed the place of meditation. Seeing it in private, the Devi was greatly astonished. Having said this, Shankara, the ocean of compassion, became silent. The great Goddess, the mother of the world, spoke to him again.

Shakti said: O Sadashiva, how did I, the one who creates delusion for all, become your wife, the wife of the one who is in the state of Tamas and who exists in a single part? You should now remove this doubt of mine, O Lord. There is no one else anywhere who is omniscient and capable of removing doubts like you.

Hearing the words of Parvati, Shiva was filled with joy. He spoke sweet words, explaining the reason to her.

Shiva said: Listen, O Devi, to the ancient story of how you became my beloved. I shall tell you the essence of the story, which gives happiness. The dispute that arose among the five gods due to rivalry—in that, I saw your great and wonderful majesty. When Ganesha, the Lord who awakens the five, disappeared after instructing us according to the law, being the Lord of Brahman, I performed great penance to attain you, O beloved. Satisfied by my devotion, you appeared after a hundred years. You said, "Come near me and choose the boon you desire; I am pleased, O Mahadeva, I shall grant it."

I bowed to you and praised you with various gestures of your own form. By that, you were also greatly pleased, O Goddess of the world. I requested, "O Devi, be my wife, O beloved. This is the only boon you must grant me, O Goddess of the world." Hearing my words, you smiled with intent, granted me the boon, and disappeared. Therefore, you became my beloved due to the power of the boon. Lakshmi, the beloved of Narayana, was born from your left side. Savitri, the beloved of Brahma, arose from your right side. Sanjna, the wife of the Sun, was born from your heart. Thus, everything has been told; remember everything, O one with a beautiful smile. Practice again the Ganesha Yoga, which gives supreme peace.

Mudgala said: Hearing everything told by Shiva, the daughter of the mountain practiced the supreme Yoga of Ganapati in private. Due to previous impressions, in a short time, she, the auspicious Shiva, attained peace and went to the presence of Shiva.

Mudgala said: Thus, O Daksha, the deeds of Ganapati have been told to you. Worship Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, with all your heart. By that, you will undoubtedly be free from obstacles, respected by all, a great Yogi, free from sorrow, and a teacher of the world.

Thus ends the seventeenth chapter entitled the conclusion of the dialogue between Shiva and Parvati in the Vakratunda-charita of the first section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 18 : the Dialogue Between Mudgala and the Divine Messenger

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O noble Suta, I have heard the highly wonderful deeds of the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) as narrated by you, and I am satisfied, O Lord. Having heard the great discourse between Shiva and Shakti, my thirst for more has increased. O Suta, please tell me more about the activities of Ganesha.

Suta said: Listen, Shaunaka, to what the great sage (Mudgala) said when asked again by the wise Daksha, who had already partaken of the nectar of the sacred story. Having heard the dialogue between Shakti and Shankara, which grants the state of Brahman, the noble Daksha, best of sages, asked further.

Daksha said: Some ancient merit of mine has surely borne great fruit, for I have drunk this nectar of the story from your lips, O Mudgala. You are blessed and have accomplished your purpose, for you are filled with devotion to Ganesha. I have learned from your words that there is nothing greater than Ganesha. O Mudgala, please tell me more of the auspicious greatness of the Lord of the Ganas. I am not satisfied, O great yogi, even after drinking this great nectar of Brahman. I have heard of the eight incarnations of the Lord of the Ganas from the discourse between Shiva and the daughter of the mountain (Parvati). I wish to hear of their deeds and their great majesty; please be kind and tell me. Why does the Lord of the Ganas take incarnations, even though He is the embodiment of the state of Brahman? O great sage, tell me this. Noble sages like you wander the earth for the sake of helping others, bestowing happiness upon the fallen. By their mere sight, touch, and especially their speech, such equanimous saints purify all people.

Suta said: Hearing these beautiful words of Daksha, Mudgala, pleased with the high-souled and devoted Daksha, replied.

Mudgala said: Listen, noble Daksha, you are truly blessed, there is no doubt, for you ask me about the story of the Lord of the Ganas. The desire to drink the nectar of sacred stories never arises in the hearts of sinners, atheists, or the arrogant. Those who understand the nature of duality, who are patient, who are equanimous towards all beings, and who are intent on crossing the ocean of worldly existence—their minds are engaged in acts of benevolence. Such noble souls are devoted to Ganesha. They listen to and read the story of the Lord of the Ganas daily, and filled with devotion, they narrate it to others. Therefore, you listen with reverence to this story which destroys sins, and by your questioning, you increase the auspicious story of the Lord of the Ganas.

Listen to my own past experience, which I will tell you with devotion, so that your doubts may be destroyed. You will truly understand the story of the incarnations, and being devoted, you will attain the state of the Lord of the Ganas. I was engaged in penance, living on grains gleaned from the fields, eating only on the twelfth day after fifteen days of fasting. I would collect only a drona (measure) of grain while fasting, and with that food, I would perform five sacrifices according to the rules. As much time passed while I was engaged in this penance, all the gods became anxious, wondering what I was trying to achieve. "Does this great sage, engaged in such penance, desire the position of Indra, Brahma, Vishnu, or Shiva?" Sent by the gods, the sage Durvasa, who is well-versed in all meanings and prone to anger, came to my hermitage to test me.

He arrived at the time of my breaking the fast, naked and looking like a madman, saying, "O sage, give me alms so that I may be satisfied." I gave the food I had to that high-souled one, not knowing that it was the great sage Durvasa who had come to my house. Having eaten all the food, the great sage smeared the remnants on his body and left with a disturbed mind. I felt great joy, O Daksha Prajapati, thinking my penance was successful because a person who eats all kinds of food was satisfied. I remained in penance, observing my fast, and again on the next twelfth day, he came for the breaking of the fast. Seeing Durvasa return in the same guise, I worshipped the great sage with great joy. He ate all the food immediately, and he came six times in this manner to test my disposition and disrupt my penance. Yet, due to the power of my past merits, I was not shaken in the slightest. I remained in penance for three months with a devoted heart.

Satisfied by my penance and purity of heart, the great sage revealed his true, wonderful yogic form. He said, "Mudgala, you have attained purity, there is no doubt. Go to heaven, O noble one, you deserve to enjoy eternal bliss. You have conquered all the heavens that shine with eternal light; go to them now, Mudgala, through your penance and pure heart." Having said this, Durvasa went to his own hermitage. At that very moment, a celestial chariot arrived from heaven. A divine being, adorned with celestial ornaments, descended from the chariot and approached me, and I worshipped him. He spoke sweet words, "Come with me, O Mudgala, know that I have come from heaven. The powerful Indra, led by Brihaspati, is waiting for you, O master; I have been sent by him, O sage. Board this chariot, which is endowed with various enjoyments, and come with me to heaven, which grants great pleasures."

Suta said: Hearing the beautiful words of the divine messenger, the sage Mudgala, bowing with humility, asked him with joy.

Mudgala said: Tell me what the qualities of heaven and all the worlds are, and what the faults are that reside there, so that I may go after hearing them.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Mudgala, the divine messenger, pleased by his humility, spoke to him accurately and in detail.

The Divine Messenger said: This is the land of action, O Brahmin, while that is considered the land of enjoyment. Heaven is the place that grants the fruits of actions to humans and others. It is impossible to describe the qualities of that place, which grants various pleasures; one gets everything one desires, and there is eternal youth. There is no fatigue, perspiration, cold, or heat there. One can roam with celestial nymphs and move according to one's will. One must enjoy the fruits corresponding to one's own actions; whatever enjoyments beings desire are all present there. It is called the land of enjoyment, O great sage. Tell me, what is lacking there that you ask me, O Lord? You have performed various actions through penance, O sage, and all the heavens are yours to enjoy.

Listen, O great sage, to the three faults of heaven. In this world, people perform actions and must enjoy the auspicious fruits. But in heaven, the actions performed by people there are fruitless. Because of this, the person is unhappy, wondering what will happen when the merit is exhausted. "I have performed such great meritorious deeds, and for this long, my stay in heaven will last." Know this as one great fault, O best of sages. Listen to the second fault, which I will explain in detail. There are many friends and sources of joy in heaven, and the gods enjoy themselves with them, O noble one. But when the merit of their good deeds is exhausted, they are cast down. For a single moment, no one looks at them with affection; the fear of falling is a fault that dwells in the mind. Listen to the third fault with a focused heart, which I will tell you with affection so that you may attain supreme knowledge. There are others in heaven who have performed great meritorious deeds and enjoy great pleasures, O sage. Seeing them, one feels tormented, thinking, "Shame on me, I am here with little enjoyment due to my small merit, while others who have performed great merits are enjoying more." This constant burning in the heart is the nature of heaven. This is the third fault I have described to you. Know heaven to be endowed with infinite qualities, O ascetic, but also accompanied by these three faults despite the power of great merit. You are blessed, O tiger among sages, for you have conquered heaven. Enjoy the delightful pleasures, and come with me quickly.

Thus ends the eighteenth chapter in the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which deals with the deeds of Vakratunda, titled "The Dialogue Between Mudgala and the Divine Messenger."

Chapter 19 : The Dialogue between Angira and Mudgala

Suta said: Hearing the words of the divine messenger, Mudgala spoke to him. O Shaunaka, listen to the greatness of that great yogi.

Mudgala said: Divine messenger, I have listened to everything you said with full understanding. I ask you again, please tell me, O wise one. Tell me, O Lord, which world is free from the three types of defects, where humans dwell without mental agony?

Suta said: Hearing Mudgala’s words, the divine messenger spoke. I shall describe that world to you in detail.

The divine messenger said: Through desireless action, O Brahmin, one attains Brahmaloka, Vaikuntha, Shivaloka, or the realms of the Sun or Shakti. However, even there, O best of sages, the presence of defects remains. That which is called the state of Brahman is described as free from all defects.

Hearing this from the mouth of the divine messenger, the virtuous Mudgala, with his neck bowed in devotion, asked him again.

Mudgala said: Where is that attained, O Lord? Tell me everything completely. You are intent on doing good, therefore I ask you this in truth.

The divine messenger said: This is the land of action (Karma-bhumi), O Brahmin. Here, humans attain Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha, and indeed the state of Brahman, without a doubt.

Hearing his beautiful words, Mudgala said to him: Go, messenger, I bow to you. I shall take myself toward heaven. But I do not desire heaven, O wise one, which is filled with mental agony and the fear of falling again. I shall strive for Brahman.

The divine messenger said to the great sage Mudgala: You are surely devoid of fortune, as you do not welcome heaven. Tell me, O sage, what man does not desire heaven? Heaven is attained through severe penance, charity, and sacrifices. Having performed various meritorious deeds and good actions, humans desire heaven, which is the giver of supreme happiness. That heaven has been attained by you through severe penance and good means; you do not desire it because you are a great fool and devoid of knowledge, in my opinion.

To the messenger who was speaking thus, Mudgala, filled with affection, replied as if smiling.

Mudgala said: Why do you delude me, O wise one? This body is a source of bondage. Heaven, which provides bodily pleasure, is not considered by the wise to be the giver of liberation. Even there, O best of messengers, there is mental agony. The wise man never desires that which is associated with the three types of suffering. Go, O fortunate one, to Indra, led by Brihaspati, and tell him that the sage has not come.

Having said this and bowed to the divine messenger, O Lord, I went to the hermitage of Agni, and the messenger went as he had come. I also stayed in my hermitage, holding this in my mind: the Vedas describe Brahman in various forms. What is the proof of them, and how should I attain it? Without knowing the complete truth, I was surely deluded. Therefore, to destroy my doubts, I shall go to the great sage Angira, the best of yogis, who is worthy of worship and the best knower of the Vedas. He is well-versed in the Word-Brahman, absorbed in the Supreme Brahman, our original ancestor, and is Brahman incarnate.

Having decided this, I went to the presence of the sage. I saw his hermitage, which grants fearlessness to all beings. There was no heat or cold, no anger born of desire, no jealousy; it shone with equanimity. There was no drought, no excessive rain, no untimely death, no suffering caused by planets, and no fear of demons. It was full of peace and beneficial to the ascetics. Being astonished and virtuous, I entered by the grace of fortune.

There, I saw the beautiful calves of various sages, O fortunate one, resounding with the chanting of the Vedas. It was filled with Agnihotra sacrifices, echoed with the sounds of various birds, adorned with trees and creepers, and abounded in various flowers and fruits. Various lakes shone with swans and ducks, filled with various birds and nectar-like water. The waters were pure, containing aquatic creatures, and shone with lotuses and lilies, visited by bees. Lions, tigers, deer, snakes, elephants, cows, and mice lived there without enmity, O Prajapati.

Seeing such an hermitage, O fortunate one, I was filled with joy. Looking again, I was astonished in my own mind. I saw the sage Angira sitting on a Kusa-grass mat, shining like another sun, brilliant with radiance. He was surrounded by various perfected beings, yogis, and sages, teaching them the knowledge of Brahman in its true sense. I bowed to him like a stick (Dandavat) with devotion, and stood before the sage with folded hands.

I said: O Lord, know me to be Mudgala, born in your lineage, who has come to see you, O giver of Brahman. Hearing my words, the sage was pleased and said to me: Son, you have come; stay here in my presence. You have performed penance with an undisturbed mind, my son; that is why you have attained my vision. You are truly a good son.

Hearing the sage’s words, I attained supreme joy. I stayed there, O Daksha, in the presence of that wise one. On one day, having found a good opportunity, I asked the great yogi while the other sages were listening. I wish to know, O Lord, tell me, O ocean of compassion. You are our direct refuge, and we are all your devotees.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Mudgala, the divine yogi Angira spoke again, smiling and delighting him.

Angira said: Why do you ask me, my son? Tell me, O radiant one. You are a mantra-knower and a teacher of the Puranas yourself.

Mudgala said: It is said in the Vedas that Brahman is associated with various states. What should be considered the proof for those desiring the state of Brahman, O Lord?

Angira said: Listen, son, I shall tell you what has been declared by the Vedanta regarding the gradual realization of Brahman and the description of the various forms of Brahman. That which is called "Food is Brahman" has an adjunct; it is of the form of food, full of great radiance, and then the sheath of food. That which is called "Prana is Brahman" is the illuminator of the Prana-sheath. Know that "Mind is Brahman" is the mind-sheath. "Knowledge is Brahman" is the sustainer of the knowledge-sheath. "Bliss is Brahman" is the illuminator of the bliss-sheath. "Consciousness is Brahman" is the sustainer of the great cause. "Bindu-Brahman" is described as the body of knowledge, O sage. "Chid-Brahman" is known as the "I am" essence. "Bodha-Brahman" is known in the Vedas as the self-arising knowledge. "Sankhya-Brahman" is the sustainer of the bodiless state, my son. Karma and Jnana are described in the Vedas as being of that nature. Know that "Sama-Brahman" is described as the essence of joy. The unmanifest, natural Brahman should be known as the "Not this, not this" (Neti-Neti) form. "Shakti-Brahman" is the illuminator of the non-existent form in the Vedas. "Surya-Brahman" is described as the form of the Self. "Vishnu-Brahman" is the sustainer of both forms. "Shiva-Brahman" is the illuminator of the one free from delusion in the scriptures. "Svananda-Brahman" is the supreme union in the Vedas. "Nirvritti-Brahman" is the supreme sustainer of non-attachment in the scriptures.

Thus, it is described in the Vedas and other texts in various ways by sages who know the doctrine of Brahman, all of which is true and authoritative. That which expands and causes to expand is Brahman, according to the Shruti; that state of being Brahman should always be known by the sages. That which is described as Brahman, associated with the beginningless Self and consciousness, is indeed considered by yogis as being associated with adjuncts. Brahman is to be known as Brahman, and that is attained through yoga. For that purpose, all the churning of Karma, Jnana, and the like is performed. That very Brahman is manifest and is the speaker of the Lord of Brahman (Brahmanaspati). It is famous as the Brahman of Brahmins, as stated in the Vedas and Smritis. The word "Gana" is described as expanding and causing to expand; poets have called it a group. A group of external and internal things is called "Gana." Through non-dual yoga, the Supreme is attained by yogis. There are many divisions of food, based on external and internal distinctions; that which is denoted by "Gana" should always be known by the wise. Such are the infinite "Ganas" (groups) that speak of Brahman. Their Lord is Ganesha, and he is the Lord of Brahman (Brahmanaspati). Worship Him, O fortunate one, if you desire the state of Brahman. His incarnate bodies are known as Jnana and other forms of Brahman.

Thus ends the nineteenth chapter, titled "The Dialogue between Angira and Mudgala," in the first section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 20 : The Meeting of Ganesha and Mudgala

Suta said: Hearing these words, Mudgala, with his hair standing on end due to joy, bowed down humbly and spoke to him as if in astonishment.

Mudgala said: O Great Sage, please tell me the greatness of His incarnations. I shall worship Him in that order for the attainment of Brahman.

Angira said: The incarnations of the great Ganesha are infinite. It is not possible for me to describe them even in hundreds of years. I shall briefly tell you about the eight prominent incarnations that are known to be the upholders of Brahman.

The Vakratunda incarnation is the upholder of Brahman for all beings; he is the destroyer of the demon Matsarasura and is known to ride a lion. The Ekadanta incarnation is the upholder of Brahman for all beings; he is the destroyer of the demon Madasura and is known to ride a mouse. The one known as Mahodara is the revealer of the knowledge of Brahman; he is the enemy of the demon Mohasura and is known to ride a mouse. Gajanana is to be known as the bestower of perfection to those who follow the Sankhya philosophy; he is the destroyer of the demon Lomasura and is known to ride a mouse. The Lambodara incarnation is the destroyer of the demon Krodhasura; he is the one who upholds the power of Brahman and is said to ride a mouse. The one known as Vikata is the burner of the demon Kamasura; he rides a peacock and is known as the upholder of the solar Brahman. The Vighnaraja incarnation is said to ride the serpent Shesha; he is the destroyer of the demon Mamatasura and is known as the Vishnu-Brahman. The Dhumravarna incarnation is the destroyer of the demon Abhimanasura; he rides a mouse and is known as the embodiment of Shiva.

These eight Vinayakas, who are parts of Ganesha, have been described by me to you. By merely worshipping them, they grant one's own Brahman. The one who is called Ganesha, who dwells in Svananda, is seen by yogis in Svananda as Brahman itself, there is no doubt. His eight incarnations are known as the removers of obstacles; by worshipping them in Svananda, one becomes absorbed in Him. O good son, Maya herself will become absorbed there. Through this union, people attain silence and Samadhi. By worshipping the Lord of Ganas, the non-yogi's ignorance is destroyed, the illusion of Maya is broken, and the supreme liberation is attained. The yoga-natured Ganesha is the speaker of the Brahmanaspati; there, peace is declared, and people perform yoga-like peace. Various types of peace are spoken of in different places, but among all peaces, this Yoga-Shanti is the supreme one. O good son, the yoga-nature of yoga is seen in all Brahman. There is nothing higher than yoga, and Brahman is attained by becoming Brahman.

This supreme secret has been told to you in its entirety, my son. Worship Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, with all your heart. I have performed complete penance for the great Ganesha, and He, being pleased by my devotion, granted me the boon I desired. In your lineage, there will be special devotees of Ganesha. Among them, Mudgala will be the greatest devotee of Ganesha. No devotee of Ganesha like Mudgala has been seen, nor will there be one; he is the king of devotees, the one and only. By Mudgala's salutation, touch, speech, and sight, and by the touch of his breath, living beings will be liberated. O son, having given me this boon, Ganesha disappeared. You are that Mudgala, the ornament of my lineage. My lineage is blessed today, for in it, the great devotees of Ganesha have been born, who are considered the saviors of the beings of hell. You are the embodiment of that powerful devotee of Ganesha; you will surely become a great yogi.

Having said this to Mudgala, Angira instructed him in the one-syllable great mantra of Ganesha according to the rules. Go, my son, this very day to the best forest for penance. Worship Ganesha, and He will grant you His vision. Bowing to Angira, Mudgala went to the forest. With single-mindedness, he performed a wonderful and complete penance. Being satisfied with the one-syllable mantra performed with all its parts, Ganesha arrived after a thousand divine years. He, the lover of devotees, arrived riding a mouse, four-armed, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, and surrounded by Pramoda and Amoda. Adorned with various ornaments, elephant-faced, large-bellied, single-tusked, three-eyed, and decorated with four weapons, He wore the Chintamani gem on His chest, which radiated light. He spoke to His great devotee, filling him with joy in every way.

Ganesha said: Choose a boon, O great soul Mudgala, that you desire in your heart. I shall grant it, being pleased by your one-syllable mantra.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Ganesha, Mudgala bowed to Him. With folded hands, he worshipped Him with devotion. Having worshipped Him according to the rules, the sage's heart was filled with joy; his entire body was thrilled, and his eyes were filled with tears. Then, bowing again and again with great devotion, he spoke with a choked voice, just as the sage Angira had done.

Mudgala said: My lineage is blessed today; my birth, my age, my knowledge, my penance, and my pilgrimage are all blessed by your sight, O Lord. This earth is blessed today, the trees are filled with flowers and fruits, and the animals, birds, and creepers are blessed because the Great Lord has come here. Having said this, he praised Ganesha, the bestower of all perfections, with a hymn, being established in his own self and filled with supreme devotion.

Chapter 21 : The Description of the Excellent Hymn to Ganesha

Shaunaka said: How did he praise the great Ganesha? Tell me that, O great soul, for you are considered omniscient by the wise.

Suta said: I shall tell you, O sage, with a heart filled with emotion, the hymn composed by Mudgala to Ganesha.

Mudgala said: How can I praise you, O Lord of the Ganas? You cannot be praised even by the Vedas. I speak according to my limited understanding, O Lord. By the power of your vision, I am capable; there is no doubt. By your grace, everyone becomes capable of praising you. Salutations to you, O Ganesha, the beginning of all, the Lord of lords, the one who transcends the Vedas. Salutations to you, the supreme illusionist, the giver of delusion to the deluded, the uncountable, and the countable. Salutations to you, who are always praised by the Puranas and other scriptures, and by Shiva and the king of gods. Salutations to you, who are devoid of the distinctions of beginning, middle, and end, who are of many forms, and who are the Lord of all. Salutations to you, who are the form of the Vedas and the source of the eight types of creation. Salutations to you, the yogi, the food, the source of all, the form of all arts, and the giver of nourishment. Salutations to you, the form of the earth, the bearer of various forms, the eternal. Salutations to you, the form of water, the giver of satisfaction, the formless, the essence, the abode. Salutations to you, the form of light, the illuminator, the enjoyer, the one who resides in all. Salutations to you, the form of air, the mover, the one who is different from that. Salutations to you, your own form, the radiant, the full light, the giver of Brahman to the elements. Salutations to you, the form of gross enjoyment, the waking state, the Rajasic. Salutations to you, the form of dream-illusion, the subtle form, the one who resides in all. Salutations to you, the cause, the giver of both types of Brahman, the one who enjoys the deep sleep state. Salutations to you, the base of consciousness, the giver of effort, the giver of three types of enjoyment. Salutations to you, the Lord of qualities, the stream of consciousness, the sound, the time. Salutations to you, the point (Bindu), the elephant-faced, the one who plays with the illusion of the body. Salutations to you, the consciousness, the giver of "I am He," the formless. Salutations to you, the eternal Ekadanta, the one who transcends birth, the supreme, the void, the destroyer of demons. Salutations to you, the enemy of Mada, the one with the mouse-banner, the beginningless, the holder of Maya. Salutations to you, the form of knowledge, the giver of various plays, the complete, the all-pervading. Salutations to you, the one who creates the distinction between Maya and the illusionist, the form of wisdom. Salutations to you, the form of Sankhya, the supreme for all beings, the one devoid of self-enjoyment. Salutations to you, the one who is always the pure Brahman, the one who is devoid of knowledge, the embodiment of intellect. Salutations to you, your own form, the giver of bliss, the form of Samadhi, the embodiment of silence. Salutations to you, who are attainable by self-realized yoga, the Lord of all without self-realization. Salutations to you, the creator of the five-fold Maya, the one divided into four parts. Salutations to you, the non-existent, the power-natured, the one who is both different and non-different. Salutations to you, the form of truth, the form of the sun, the one who awakens the self, the holder of Maya. Salutations to you, the eternal nectar, the non-dual, the destroyer of time, the giver of liberation. Salutations to you, the form of Vishnu, the giver of bliss, the creator of both Maya and the illusionist. Salutations to you, the infinite, the devoid of distinctions, the all-pervading, the form of all. Salutations to you, the form of Shambhu, the one who says "not this, not this," the one devoid of delusion, the three-eyed. Salutations to you, the Dhumravarna, the supreme Brahman who shines through the three-fold Maya. Salutations to you, the non-different, the one who brings about union, the Lord of obstacles, the one who always stands by the side of His devotees, the destroyer of great demons. Salutations to you, the form of action, the doer of actions, the giver of fruits of actions to people. Salutations to you, the form of knowledge, the giver of detachment, the one devoid of action, the pure. Salutations to you, the giver of equality, the one who is both, the one who gives bliss through yoga. Salutations to you, the giver of natural Brahman, the three-aboded, the one devoid of the three, the giver of satisfaction. Salutations to you, the protector of the four states, your own form, the one who brings about union. Salutations to you, the infinite, the Lord of the four groups, O Heramba, salutations to you. Salutations to you, the one devoid of yoga and Maya, the eternal, the non-blissful. Salutations to you, the form of cessation, the different, the one established in the Samadhi of yoga. Salutations to you, the form of Yoga-Shanti, the giver of peace, the Ganesha, the yoga. Salutations to you, the giver of Brahman to the Brahmins, the Lord of all, the Lord of Ganas, the eldest. Salutations to you, the Brahman, the creator, the one who holds Rajas, the creator of Vedas and scriptures. Salutations to you, the one devoid of creation, the equal, the abode, the illusionist, the one who resides in the middle. Salutations to you, the form of Sattva, the protector of the world, the holder of Maya. Salutations to you, the one devoid of Sattva, the one who transcends all, the Lord of poets, the destroyer of obstacles. Salutations to you, the Tamasic, the destroyer, the one who shines with three eyes, the holder of power. Salutations to you, the eternal time, the giver of Kailasa, the one devoid of darkness, the Lambodara. Salutations to you, the seed of actions, the form of the sun, the radiant. Salutations to you, the knower of the three times, the sun, the all-pervading, the one devoid of light, O Heramba. Salutations to you, the form of power, the giver of delusion, the base of action, the various illusions. Salutations to you, the holder of infinite forms and plays, the one devoid of power, O Lord of perfections. Salutations to you, the form of the moon, the giver of nourishment, the one who resides in the three worlds. Salutations to you, the holder of nectar, the illuminator of stars, the one devoid of food, O Dhundhi. Salutations to you, the form of desire, the enemy of Kama, the elephant-faced. Salutations to you, who are praised by Indra and other gods, and by sages like Shuka. Salutations to you, the non-eternal and the eternal, the Lord of gods, the giver of desire and desirelessness. Salutations to you, the one worshipped by all, the Lord of all, praised by the Lord of water and Shesha. Salutations to you, the son of Kashyapa and Kapila, the son of Varenya and Parashara. Salutations to you, the son of Shambhu and Parvati, the enjoyer of devotion, O Lord of Ganas. Salutations to you, the son of Madhava's wife, the mother of all, the father of all. Salutations to you, the son of all, the soul of all, the all-pervading, the many-formed. Salutations to you, the holder of the noose and the tusk, the bearer of the goad, the giver of eternal bliss. Salutations to you, the giver of sons, grandsons, and kingdoms, the giver of enjoyment and liberation. Salutations to you, the infinite power, the Lord of qualities, the Lord of peacocks, the Lord of yoga, the Lord of Maya. Salutations to you, the unmanifest and manifest, the elephant-faced, the one who deludes through form. Salutations to you, the one-formed and many-formed, the protector of obstacles, the holder of the trunk. Salutations to you, the Lord of the helpless, the abode of all. Salutations to you, the body of "Tvam" (Thou), the trunk of "Tat" (That). Salutations to you, the embodiment of the union of both. Salutations to you, the one who holds Siddhi on the left side, the mother of those deluded by Maya. Salutations to you, the one who holds Buddhi on the right side, the one who is both male and female. Salutations to you, the Bhramari, the power of "Tvam," the giver of desires of "Tat," the non-different. Salutations to you, the one who dwells in the head of those who know the union of both. Salutations to you, the one who dwells in the ocean of nectar and sugarcane juice. Salutations to you, the one who plays without being male or female. Salutations to you, the giver of bliss to those who hear the three-syllable "Ganesha." For those who chant it even once, there is no birth or death, O Dhundhiraja. Your devotees become Brahman; it is no wonder that by their touch, they grant Brahman. May those who are worshipped by Indra and others, and who are the form of Ganesha, be praised. Salutations to you, O Ganesha.

Suta said: Having praised Ganesha thus, Mudgala remained silent, held Ganesha's feet, and fell to the ground like a stick. Gajanana, with a pleased heart, lifted His devotee, embraced him with great respect, and spoke to the great sage.

Ganesha said: The hymn you have composed in my presence will become famous as the best of hymns; there is no doubt. To the one who praises me with this best of hymns, I grant liberation along with enjoyment, and I grant firm devotion to me; there is no doubt. That best of men will not encounter trouble or disease. I grant him sons, grandsons, wealth, and grain. By reading this, death, destruction, and other such things are destroyed. By reading it twenty-one times for twenty-one days, troubles arising from imprisonment and other such things are destroyed. The man who is most dear to me attains his heart's desire. You, ask for boons from me; I shall grant them, bound by your devotion. There is no one who knows my heart as you do; I am always under your control.

Chapter 22 : The Granting of Boons to Mudgala

Suta said: Hearing the words of Ganesha, Mudgala, filled with devotion and joy, bowed down and spoke.

Mudgala said: Why do you tempt me, O Dhundhi? I desire nothing. However, if you insist that I must ask for something, then I shall ask. Grant me your firm, unbroken devotion, O Lord. I desire complete devotion to Ganesha, wherever I may be.

Hearing the words of Mudgala, Ganesha himself was astonished and said: Oh, that you should choose devotion in its complete form! My unwavering and complete devotion shall be yours. There is no fortune in the three worlds equal to yours. O Brahmin, this devotion is ninefold and brings joy to the heart; there is no tenth state for the mind to experience this essence.

Listening, chanting, remembering, serving the feet, worshiping, bowing, servitude, friendship, and self-surrender—I shall describe these nine marks of supreme devotion. Through these, people attain me; there is no doubt.

When one listens to my virtues and contemplates them, an experience arises. When that experience brings forth the essence of joy, one should always listen to my virtues. When, due to this absorption in me, one finds no joy in listening to anything else, then my devotion in the form of listening is complete. I dwell in the ears of such a person, longing for their devotion.

When the chanting of my virtues becomes the source of joy and one finds no pleasure in other chants, then my devotion in the form of chanting is complete. I dwell on the tip of the tongue of such a person.

When one is diligent in vows and worship, never forgetting my work, then devotion in the form of remembrance is attained. I dwell in that remembrance to enjoy that devotion.

Knowing that only my lotus feet are true and all else is false, one who engages in service without laziness—for such a person, devotion in the form of serving the feet arises. I shall dwell motionlessly in the body of one who is free from such laziness.

When one performs my work with complete devotion, including all its rituals, then devotion in the form of worship is attained. I dwell there, moved by that devotion.

When one realizes that there is no one equal to me in the Vedas and that I am the supreme, then devotion in the form of bowing is attained. In that state of recognizing my supremacy, I dwell everywhere, becoming the enjoyer of devotion and destroying the sense of superiority in others.

When one worships me with the natural pride of being a Ganesha devotee, as prescribed in the scriptures, then devotion in the form of servitude is attained. I dwell in such a person naturally, manifesting as the one who enjoys this servitude.

Just as there is no secrecy between friends and a friend knows everything about the other, both inside and out, so too, when a person worships me without hypocrisy or duality, they attain devotion in the form of friendship.

Knowing that I, the indweller of all, know everything that is held in the mind, one who worships me with a fearful yet devoted heart, with an undivided mind—in that friendship, I always dwell, captivated by their actions.

Having abandoned the fivefold nature of the mind, one who declares "I am Ganesha" without doubt—in that state of egolessness, I dwell with devotion. Know, O Sage, that I am not different from such a person.

Whoever practices this ninefold devotion filled with essence, whose mind cannot bear to be without my essence even for a moment—they are my complete devotee; there is no doubt. I remain joined with them through affection, always. I am unable to leave them even for a moment, bound by their devotion. I shall always protect them through this ninefold state of mind.

There is no state equal to devotion that pleases me. Bound by it, I dwell in the heart of such a wise person. Among those who worship me in these nine ways, if one excels in a particular type of devotion, that becomes the primary path for them.

I shall now tell you, O fortunate one, who the kings among devotees are in these nine paths. In listening, Kartikeya is the king of devotees. In chanting, the Sun is the king. In remembrance, Ramachandra is known to be the king. Parvati is the queen of devotees in serving the feet. Lord Vishnu himself is the king in the worship of me. In bowing, Shankara is the king of devotees. In servitude, Rama, the son of Jamadagni (Parashurama), is the chief. In friendship, the four-faced Brahma is the chief. In self-surrender, Shuka, the son of Vyasa, is the king.

These are the chiefs of devotees, free from obstacles. They worship me and have become one with Brahman. I have made you the king of all these, without a doubt. You shall worship me with complete devotion. You will excel in these nine forms of devotion. I have made you a complete devotee and the lord of devotees. No devotee of mine has been or will be equal to you. O Brahmin, I am truly under your command. Those who are touched by the air around you, or who see you, will undoubtedly become purified and attain the wisdom of Brahman.

Suta said: Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, disappeared. From that day on, the sage Mudgala became worthy of worship by all. Whoever hears, reads, or narrates the story of Mudgala to the devout will attain the fulfillment of all desires. Having enjoyed all pleasures here, they will become one with Brahman in the end and attain the status of the king of devotees of Ganesha.

Chapter 23 : The Description of Matsarasura’s Penance

Daksha said: You are blessed, you are the king of yogis, and you possess the highest devotion. By the grace of my fortune, I have attained your audience, O Lord. Please tell me in detail, O great sage, about the eight incarnations of the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) that have been proclaimed. Tell me specifically about the demons associated with them, their births, and all the details of their battles. I have never heard of such great demons before, O Lord; this story, which dispels sins, fills me with wonder.

Mudgala said: O Prajapati, controlled by your sentiments, I shall narrate to you in brief the story that delights the Lord of the Ganas. First, listen to the virtuous character of Vakratunda and the wondrous battle between him and Matsarasura.

On the day Indra attained the position of the King of Gods, a great annual festival was held, O sage. Once, in Amaravati, Indra, worshipped by all, sat on a beautiful throne, absorbed in the festivities. At that time, the gods, Gandharvas, Apsaras, Nagas, Yakshas, great sages, demons, and celestial women arrived. Lord Shiva arrived there, surrounded by all beings, accompanied by Skanda, Nandishvara, and Parvati. Lord Vishnu arrived with Lakshmi, Vishvaksena, and the Ganas, and Brahma arrived surrounded by the Vedas and the Prajapatis. The Sun arrived with all the planets, as did Agni, the Lord of Dharma (Yama), Kubera, and Varuna. The Adityas, Manus, Vasus, Rudras, Sadhyas, Vidyadharas, and Vishvedevas also arrived, O Lord. The Ashvins, Sheshanaga, and many others came and sat down with great joy at the festival of the hundred-sacrificed one (Indra).

There, the sages, filled with divine sentiment, engaged in various stories of ancient times. The Apsaras danced, and the Gandharvas, including Haha, Huhu, Tumburu, and Narada, sang melodiously. They played instruments, and the Charanas sang their praises. The people were happy and prosperous, and the women were filled with great joy. Rambha performed various dances, using subtle gestures and expressions, O Prajapati. She moved here and there in the assembly, engaged in playing with a ball. Seeing her, Indra became afflicted by desire. Infatuated by her sweet voice and the art of her dance, the Lord of the Gods became so absorbed that he forgot his duties. He brought the festival to an early end, and everyone, honored and happy, departed to their respective places.

Indra invited Rambha and went alone to a forest where trees were filled with various fragrances and blossoms. There were many clear streams, adorned with golden lotuses and buzzing bees. Indra, infatuated by desire, enjoyed himself in those forests, which were filled with various objects that stimulated passion. Even while playing intensely with Rambha, the wielder of the thunderbolt (Indra) found no satisfaction, just as fire is never satisfied with ghee.

The sage Durvasa, wandering at will, arrived there. Seeing him, the King of Gods felt ashamed and resorted to humility. Leaving Rambha, he bowed down and stood with folded hands. The great sage left him, ignoring him. Because Indra’s body was trembling with agitation, his seed fell upon the earth, and from that, a demon was born. Indra returned to his home, left Rambha, and spent his time lamenting his own actions. He thought, "Alas, shame on my kingdom and my status as a god, for I have been conquered by a woman. My mind is as restless as that of a demon. Shame on me, who is infatuated by a loose woman and condemned by the virtuous. Because of this sinful act, fear will surely come in the future." Thus, the King of Gods, having returned to his duty, reproached himself.

In time, from the potency of Indra, a great demon was born on earth. The Earth protected him, nourishing him with milk and other things. He grew up there like a second Himavan. He had long arms, large eyes, a broad chest, and great strength. He was red in color and performed cruel deeds, shining like a blazing fire. By nature, the Earth named him "Matsara" (Envy). When he reached the age of five, the Earth, out of affection, had the Brahmins perform his sacred thread ceremony. He stayed at the teacher's house to learn the Vedas and other scriptures, possessing a sincere and receptive mind.

With great intelligence, he mastered the Vedas in a short time. He married a beautiful and charming maiden named Madakara, who was beautiful in every limb, clever in all tasks, and a stimulant of desire. One day, the evil-minded demon decided to conquer the universe with his power by performing great penance. He approached Shukracharya and learned the five-syllable mantra. With that penance, he pleased Shiva with devotion. He performed penance in a lonely, secluded forest, adorned with flowers and leaves, rich in roots, fruits, and pure water. He remained without food for a thousand divine years, chanting the five-syllable mantra and meditating on Sadashiva in his heart. By observing the Pashupata vow, he pleased Shiva.

The gods were troubled by his intense penance. The sun did not shine, the fire lost its brilliance, and all beings became distressed by the power of his penance. The gods and sages went to Shiva for refuge, saying, "O Lord of the Universe, protect us from the one scorched by the penance of Matsara." Shiva, accompanied by them, Parvati, and his Ganas, happily went to that place to see his great devotee. He saw him reduced to skin and bone, with only his breath remaining, his limbs withered, and covered by an anthill on all sides. All the gods and the noble sages were astonished to see such a great ascetic.

Thus ends the twenty-third chapter, titled "The Description of Matsarasura’s Penance," in the first section of the Vakratunda character of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 24 : The Meeting of Matsarasura

Mudgala said: Lord Shiva awakened the fierce and powerful demon Matsarasura, who was performing penance as a devotee of Shambhu.

Shankara said: O Matsara, arise! Choose your boons, O observer of vows. I am pleased with your intense penance and devotion, and I shall grant them to you. Oh, what patience! Oh, what patience! You are blessed, O best of ascetics. Having transcended bodily consciousness, you have remained firm in your devotion. Abandon your suffering; your penance has borne fruit, and you have attained the goal of your asceticism. Whatever you desire, even that which is difficult to obtain, I will grant you.

Hearing the words of Shiva, Matsarasura awakened. Opening his eyes, he saw Shankara standing before him. By the mere sight of Shiva, he became filled with vitality. He arose, bowed down, and stood humble and overwhelmed with love. He addressed Shiva, the benefactor, the wish-fulfilling tree for devotees, and the Lord accompanied by Parvati.

Matsarasura said: Blessed is my lineage today; my birth, my wealth, and my knowledge of the scriptures are all fruitful. My father, my mother, and my penance are blessed, O great Lord. I am beholding the Sadashiva whom even the sages and meditative yogis do not fully know, and whom the Vedas and Smritis describe. Having said this, he bowed down and worshipped Shambhu, who was accompanied by the sages and gods, with a heart full of devotion. Having worshipped Shankara according to the rites, he prostrated like a staff on the ground, rose, and with folded hands, offered a hymn.

Matsarasura said: Salutations to you, O Lord of the gods, O Shankara, O Shiva. Salutations to you who are attributeless, yet the Lord of all attributes, and the embodiment of all virtues. Salutations to you, O Mahadeva, the wielder of the Pinaka bow, who are infinite. Salutations, salutations to you, O Maheshwara, the Lord of all. Salutations to you, O consort of Uma, the protector of devotees. Salutations to you, O God who loves devotion, who wears a serpent as an ornament. Salutations to you, who have the bull as your banner, Nandi as your vehicle, and who carry the trident. Salutations to you, who are smeared with ash and wear a tiger skin. Salutations to you, O moon-crested one, O five-faced Shambhu, who possess ten arms, three eyes, and carry a skull. Salutations to you, who wear matted locks and carry the Ganga. Salutations to you, O dweller of the mountain, who wear elephant skin. Salutations to you, who are always the embodiment of Brahman and the bestower of knowledge. Salutations to you, O supportless, eternal Lord of the great yogis. Salutations, salutations to you, O Rudra, the ocean of infinite virtues. How can I praise you, O Mahadeva, when even the Vedas are silenced before you?

Having praised Maheshwara in this way, Matsara bowed to him. As he stood up, Shiva said: Choose a boon, O observer of vows. This hymn you have composed will increase my delight; whoever reads or hears it will have all their desires fulfilled. I shall grant knowledge, longevity, happiness, kingdom, sons, grandsons, worldly enjoyment, and liberation to the one who praises me with this hymn, O demon.

Hearing the beautiful words of Shiva, Matsara was overjoyed. Becoming humble, he spoke to Shiva, the bestower of boons to his devotees.

Matsara said: O Lord of the gods, if you are pleased and if you will grant me boons, then grant me fearlessness everywhere and grant me devotion to you, O Sadashiva. Everything is composed of the five elements and adorned by the three qualities; therefore, O Shambhu, may I never face death here. Grant me eternal prosperity along with health, and grant me the kingdom of the entire universe, O Sadashiva.

Mudgala said: Hearing these words of his, Shiva was astonished, and all the gods and sages were also amazed. They wondered what Matsara had asked for and what his fate would be. Pleased by his intense penance, Shiva replied to him.

Shiva said: O demon, whatever you have requested will be yours. Because of your intense penance, I have granted you that which is very difficult to obtain.

Mudgala said: Having received these rare boons, Matsara returned home, overjoyed, and bringing joy to all his friends, his mother, and his wives. He lived there in constant happiness, honored by the respected, enjoying various pleasures, and providing happiness to his friends. Matsara fathered two sons with Madakara, who were as radiant, equal to their father in prowess and brilliance. He named the eldest Sundarapriya, and the younger one, who was a source of great joy, was named Vishayapriya.

One day, the demon went to his father-in-law's house. There, various demons gathered and incited him.

The demons said: Why do you remain idle, O powerful one? We are at your command. With your help, O Matsara, we shall conquer all the gods. There is no warrior like you anywhere in the universe, O Lord. You are powerful due to the boons you received, and you are fearless, O great demon. Give us the command, and we shall go to conquer the gods without doubt. By piercing them with weapons, we shall become the enjoyers of heaven.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of the demons, Matsara said to them: Everything you have said is beneficial and excellent. This thought also dwells in my heart daily, O demons. But what can I do? My mother always forbids me. She says that Indra is your father, the gods are to be worshipped along with Vasava, so act righteously to attain this world and the next.

Hearing the words of Matsara, the demons spoke to him, attempting to obstruct his sense of duty.

The demons said: Listen, Matsara, to the story of the past regarding your own duty, which is worthy of our respect and will destroy your doubts. Jalandhara was born from the energy of Shiva and was immensely powerful. He defeated Shambhu in battle and conquered all the gods. He even attempted to take Parvati as his wife. Many demons like this have existed, O Matsarasura. Wherever the gods are honored, the demons are oppressed; when the demons are honored, the gods are oppressed. The lords of the gods are always the enemies of the demons, O Lord. Therefore, abandon your doubt and conquer the gods, O powerful one. Abandon your mother, the earth, who supports the sages and gods. Dwell here with us always, O fortunate one.

Hearing their harmful words, Matsara did exactly that; he abandoned the earth and dwelt there.

Chapter 25: Description of the Army of Matsarasura

Mudgala said: O Daksha, listen to this sin-destroying story, which reveals the purpose of the incarnation of Vakratunda. All the demons gathered and went to the great sage Shukra; filled with jealousy, they bowed down to him like a staff. Shukra honored them and asked, "Why have you come? I will strive to accomplish whatever task you desire." Hearing the words of Shukra, which were like nectar, the sages were delighted and, after bowing, spoke words that were beneficial. They said, "O Master, by your grace, Matsara has become the sustainer of our lineage and the fruit of all our efforts. Having obtained boons, this mighty-armed one has taken refuge in our welfare; it is certain that he is the king of all demons and Danavas. O best of sages, you who honor everyone, go and perform his coronation. You are the essence of our abode."

Hearing the words of the demons, Shukra agreed and went to the wicked Matsara for the sake of his coronation. All the demons, Danavas, and Rakshasas gathered there, filled with great joy and intent on a grand festival. O Prajapati, listen to the names of their leaders; I will mention the most prominent ones briefly. Namuchi, Shambara, Shumbha, Nishumbha, Kalabhairava, Virochana, Viprachitti, Prahlada, Mahishasura, Andhakasura, and the powerful Ruru were among the many demons who gathered for his festival.

According to the prescribed rites, Matsara was anointed by Brahmins well-versed in the Vedas, using hymns from the Rig, Yajur, and Sama Vedas. Shukra, the great sage and devotee of Ganesha, acted as the chief priest. Matsara, the slayer of enemy heroes, became the king of the demons and shone with brilliance, like the midday sun. The demons, filled with joy, said to each other, "We have been made secure by Shukra," and returned to their respective homes.

After some time, Matsara conceived the idea of conquering the universe and summoned all the demons. They arrived in haste and asked, "O honor-giver, what is the task?" Desiring victory, he told them, "Bring the three worlds under my control." Hearing his words, all the great demons were delighted and roared like the clouds of the end-time. Equipped with infallible weapons and riding their vehicles, they stood ready with their four-fold armies. Countless infantry soldiers, carrying various weapons, stones, and trees, marched forth. With foreheads reddened by vermilion, disheveled hair, and immense strength, they flew and ran, skilled in the art of war. They were experts in sword fighting, wrestling, mace fighting, and archery.

The dust raised by them obscured the sun. Thousands of elephants, adorned with various colors and ornaments, marched in front. Their foreheads were smeared with vermilion, they carried shining tusks, and their bells rang loudly. They were ridden by great warriors carrying spears and other weapons, appearing like moving mountains. They were capable of breaking mountains with their tusks and felling trees with their trunks. Their foreheads were wet with rut, surrounded by buzzing bees, and they were ready to destroy thousands of enemies in battle.

Then, a massive army of horses marched out, swift as the wind and fully adorned. They were fanned by chowries and ran through the sky like the wind. Sparks flew from their hooves, and the demons riding them appeared like dark clouds. All were terrifying, skilled in various forms of warfare, and determined to commit sins. Some great demons rode on donkeys, goats, or bulls. Others rode on tigers, lions, leopards, or birds. Adorned with such diverse vehicles, all the demons set out as if they were messengers of Yama.

Then, the chariot-warriors emerged, riding on chariots that were terrifying, with eyes reddened by great anger. The chariots were decorated with jewels and pearls, filled with various weapons, and roared like thunder. They were adorned with flags, drawn by swift horses, and driven by skilled charioteers. Some chariots were drawn by donkeys, others by wolves, lions, bulls, elephants, or bears. Matsara himself rode a chariot adorned with a hundred donkeys as strong as thunderbolts. His two heroic sons rode on horseback in front of him, and Virochana and other demons followed on all sides. On the earth, his army appeared like an ocean, decorated with the ornaments of great chariots and mighty warriors.

Thus ends the twenty-fifth chapter of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "Description of the Army of Matsarasura," in the story of Vakratunda.

Chapter 26 : The Victory Over Patala

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Then, O Daksha, that great army, ready to conquer the earth, began to destroy the Kshatriyas like the wheel of time. Where the demons like Virochana, who were equal to Yama, stood, what man on earth could remain standing while fighting them? The demons, crushing heaps of mountains with the kicks of their feet, made the earth tremble under the weight, even bowing the head of Shesha. Seeing them like clouds standing in the shape of mountains, those Kshatriyas, who were like Yama, lost their lives. Some fled, while others sought refuge. The great warriors who fought were considered to have attained heaven. The sons of those who died were established as tax-payers, and the kings who sought refuge were reinstated in their positions. Wherever there was no king, demons were made the rulers, and kings in all regions were made to share the tribute. Thus, in every country, demons were placed near the Kshatriyas to ensure the realization of this intent; this was done everywhere.

Having conquered the entire earth with its islands, the demon Matsara rejoiced. Seeing the earth under his absolute control, O Prajapati, the demon, accompanied by a host of demons, went to Patala, for the powerful Matsara desired to conquer the Nagas and serpents. Having reached the gardens of the Nagas, the sons of Diti destroyed them. Seeing them, the Naga kings came forward to fight. Vasuki, Takshaka, Kambala, Ashvatara, and many other Naga kings arrived. Equipped, full of anger, possessing great hoods and immense strength, they appeared mounted on chariots and various other vehicles. They struck the powerful demons with a multitude of weapons and arrows. The demons, with great force, struck them down with their own weapons.

The hosts of demons were killed there by the powerful Nagas. With their pride broken, they fled in all ten directions, completely bewildered. Seeing the slaughter of the demons, Virochana and others arrived. The angry Asuras crushed the Nagas. The demons killed them with various weapons and great force. The heads, feet, hands, and bellies of some were broken. Great rivers of blood flowed in all ten directions. With their pride broken, the Nagas fled in fear. Some sought refuge with the fierce demons. The Nagas, filled with terror, cried out in distress. Seeing the prowess of the demons, Vasuki and others arrived. They tried to stop them with great force, burning with anger. The great Nagas, led by Takshaka, released their poison in agitation. O Prajapati, the entire space was filled with that poison. The flames born of the great poison burned the foremost Asuras. Many demons died, tormented by the venom.

The demons could not stand on the battlefield; all their weapons were burned at that time. Elephants, horses, chariots, and infantry were all killed and burned. Virochana and others fell to the ground unconscious. The Nagas rejoiced, causing the ten directions to resound; filled with great joy, the others danced. Meanwhile, the valiant Virochana, who was proud of his martial prowess, rose up, free from pain and possessing great strength. By the knowledge imparted by Shukra, the demons were made immune to poison. All of them, led by Andhaka, rose up. The angry Asuras released the Garuda-astra. From it, Garudas were born, who devoured all the Nagas. Then, Vasuki unleashed flames of poison. The Garudas were burned by it, and the Garuda-astra was neutralized. Seeing this great wonder and the immense prowess of Vasuki, Virochana and all the others transformed themselves into Garudas through illusion. They devoured the entire army of the Nagas in all ten directions. The great Nagas, terrified and cowardly, fled. The king of serpents, Takshaka, and others, severely tormented by the Garudas, became unconscious and appeared as if dead in the fierce battle.

Shesha, having learned of the entire situation which was terrifying to his kin, arrived to seek refuge with the demons through diplomacy. "O powerful demons, why are you filled with anger? Tell me. You are my brothers, the Asuras; there is no doubt about this. Why has this war begun? You must speak, O sons of Diti. You are all inhabitants of Patala, and so are we."

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Shesha, the valiant Virochana spoke to him in a pleasant and naturally radiant manner. Virochana said: "O Shesha, O great one, you are worthy of respect and highly intelligent. You speak with affection, O wise one; hear my truth. Our king Matsara wants the entire universe under his control. His command must be accepted by you with respect. If not, the destruction of both Nagas and Asuras will follow. You are the best in every way; do what is beneficial for all."

Hearing the words of Virochana, Shesha, who was well-versed in all matters, smiled and said this to him with diplomacy and great wisdom: "I will do whatever the great Matsara desires." Having said this, he prepared to pay the annual tribute. Giving various jewels, he established peace with the Asuras. Taking the Nagas, led by Vasuki, he returned to his own realm. The Asuras were all delighted, having conquered the powerful Nagas. Having become the conquerors of Patala, they set out to conquer Varuna.

Thus ends the twenty-sixth chapter named The Victory Over Patala in the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 27: The Victory of Matsarasura and the Defeat of Indra

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: The demons, filled with the glory of victory, arrived at the abode of Varuna and sent a messenger to his assembly. The messenger went to Varuna’s court and said, "O Lord of the Waters, hear my words sent by Matsara. O mighty one, submit to my master and remain in your place; otherwise, I shall slay you and all the gods on the battlefield." Hearing the messenger’s words, Varuna was overcome with rage and prepared for a terrifying battle. Mounted on a makara and wielding his noose, he emerged with his aquatic forces.

The messenger reported everything to Matsarasura, who then prepared for war with his demon army. The formidable armies of the gods and demons, both well-prepared, marched to the battlefield. Seeing the fierce divine army, Virochana, Mahishasura, Shumbha, Andhakasura, and many others rushed to the front lines, roaring and striking the gods with great courage. The two sides clashed, striking each other with weapons, trees, and stones. A tumultuous battle ensued for three days, causing great destruction, until the demons were defeated. The gods roared fiercely, while the demon kings, led by Virochana, lay unconscious. Shukracharya then restored them with his power, and the demons rose to fight again.

Filled with great fury, they struck the powerful gods, leading to another terrifying battle. The aquatic gods fought day and night, but their forces were killed, broken, and crushed. The gods fled in all directions, and the powerful Virochana captured Varuna. Hearing that Varuna was captured, the aquatic beings fled; some were killed, some wounded, and others sought refuge. The jubilant demons then marched toward the North, toward the Lord of Wealth (Kubera), via the water route.

Hearing of the events from Varuna’s realm, Kubera fled to Mount Kailash to seek refuge with Shankara. Upon learning of his arrival, the fierce demons entered Alaka and emerged victorious. Having defeated Kubera, the demons, accompanied by Varuna, proceeded toward the realm of Dharmaraja (Yama). Hearing the news from his messenger, Yama, surrounded by various spirits and diseases, prepared for battle. Mounted on a buffalo and wielding his staff, he arrived at the battlefield with the force of death itself.

Seeing Yama arrive with death and disease, the demons fought back with sudden force. The heroes of both sides fought intensely, striking each other with various weapons. For five days, a wondrous and terrible battle raged, destroying many. The demons were crushed by Yama’s forces, and many fled in terror. At that moment, Vishayapriya, the son of Matsarasura, arrived and fought against Death. Prompted by Matsara, he struck at the hearts of men, and the diseases were severely afflicted by his weapons. The forces of Yama fled. Yama fought fiercely, rendering the buffalo unconscious and felling the demon, but the son of Matsara rose again and struck Yama with his staff. Seeing the demon son’s great feat, the demon lords praised him.

Having captured Yama, the demons marched toward Amaravati. Indra, along with the gods, prepared for war. Agni and Vayu arrived with their forces, and all the gods came from their respective realms. The Vishvedevas, Sadhyas, Rudras, Vasus, Gandharvas, Yakshas, Vidyadharas, planets, the Moon, the Sun, and the Adityas all gathered and went to the wielder of the thunderbolt (Indra). Indra arrived at the battlefield mounted on Airavata, holding his Vajra. Seeing the divine army, the demons attacked with weapons, and the gods retaliated with great fury.

The battle raged for seven nights, destroying both sides, until the demons fled. Virochana and others lay unconscious on the ground, and the gods rejoiced in their victory. Seeing this, Sundarapriya, the eldest son of Matsara, arrived and fought all the gods. He crushed the divine army with weapons and wrestling, causing them to flee in terror. Rivers of blood flowed on the battlefield. Indra fought the wise demon son, but when he struck him with the Vajra, the demon did not move, which astonished Indra. The demon son grabbed Airavata, rendered him unconscious, and seized Indra by the feet. Having subdued the King of Gods, he took him to his own abode.

The demons worshipped the son of Matsara for his great strength and valor. Some gods were captured, some were unconscious, some fled, and others were crushed. Thus, the mighty Matsarasura subdued all the gods and became the Lord of Heaven.

Thus ends the twenty-seventh chapter of the first section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Victory of Matsarasura and the Defeat of Indra."

Chapter 28: The Narrative of Matsarasura's Deeds

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Some of the gods approached Kashyapa and others. Distressed and terrified, they reported the actions of the demon.

The gods said: O noble one, the gods, including Indra, have been defeated by the wicked Matsara. Indra has been captured, and we have come seeking refuge. O best of sages, protect us, for you are like a father to those overwhelmed by fear. Save Indra from Matsara, whose actions are akin to the pain of death.

Hearing the words of the gods, Kashyapa, accompanied by Vashistha and others, went to Brahma for the sake of the lineage. Arriving at Satyaloka, he saw him seated, bowed down, and after praising him with various words, spoke with folded hands.

Kashyapa said: O Lord, Indra, along with the best of the gods, has been captured by Matsara. That extremely cruel demon has become the master of the gods' abodes. O Lord of the world, act in such a way that the gods and demons may dwell in their respective rightful places, for you are the creator and our only refuge.

Hearing the words of Kashyapa, the grandfather and lord of creation spoke to the gods and sages with a pained heart.

Brahma said: Listen, O sages and gods, this demon has obtained boons from Shiva and is invincible to all beings, especially the gods. By his very nature, I am unable to do what is appropriate. Let us go and seek refuge in Vishnu, the protector, along with you.

Brahma went to Vaikuntha with the gods and sages. Upon arriving, he bowed to Vishnu, praised him, and spoke.

Brahma said: O Lord, Indra, the best of the gods, has been captured in battle by Matsara. O Great Vishnu, why do you remain silent, you who are known as the protector?

Hearing the words of Brahma, Vishnu replied: O Creator, it is not possible for me to bring that great demon under control. That foremost of demons is intoxicated by the boon granted by Shiva. Therefore, there is no doubt that we must seek refuge in Shankara.

Accompanied by Brahma and others, Vishnu himself went to Kailasa. Bowing to Shankara, he spoke along with all the hosts of sages.

Vishnu said: Salutations to you, O Lord Shambhu, salutations to you, the wielder of the trident. Salutations to you, O Lord of the worlds, the witness of all. Salutations, salutations to you, O Great God, the creator in the form of Rajas, the sustainer in the form of Vishnu, and the destroyer in the form of Rudra. Salutations to you, the five-faced one, the yogi, the giver of yoga, the formless, the one without attributes, who has become Brahman. O Great God, the gods and sages are tormented by Matsara. He has captured Indra, the lord of the gods, after crushing them. Terrified by him, we have come to you for refuge. Protect us, O Lord of gods, you are the greatest and the protector of the gods.

Hearing the words of Vishnu, Shiva himself became anxious. He spoke to the gods and sages, led by Vishnu.

Shiva said: O Lord of gods, through severe penance, I have empowered this demon with very rare boons. What can I do, O Janardana? O Vishnu, it is impossible to destroy this demon at any time. O Lord of gods, you are endowed with wisdom, so consider what should be done.

Hearing the words of Shiva, Vishnu was greatly shaken and spoke to the Lord of gods with a pained heart.

Vishnu said: You are the creator, the one who can change the course of events, and the one who can make the impossible possible; there is no doubt about this. If you speak in such a contradictory way, what are we to do? O Sadashiva, we dwell in your presence. It is my opinion that we should not live under the control of a demon. We have left our abodes like Vaikuntha and come to your presence. Now, that demon Matsara will come here as well. He will take Vaikuntha and Satyaloka and live happily. Because of his devotion, he will even seize your abode, Kailasa.

Hearing the words of Vishnu, Shiva sighed deeply and remained there. O Daksha, listen to the deeds of Matsara.

Thus ends the twenty-eighth chapter of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which narrates the deeds of Matsarasura in the story of Vakratunda.

Chapter 29: The Meeting of Shiva and Matsarasura

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Having captured Indra, the great demon Matsara, filled with joy, mounted Airavata and proceeded to Amaravati. Surrounded by heroic demons led by Virochana, the radiant demon chief shone with his power and strength. As he sat upon the throne of Indra, the groups of Apsaras, Gandharvas, and Charanas approached him, trembling with fear on all sides. They served the glorious and powerful Matsara with all their hearts, employing their various arts to please him. The great demon wandered through all the celestial gardens, enjoying the divine pleasures as if he were an expert in them. He himself divided all the divine abodes and distributed them among the prominent demons, accompanied by his might. The great demons, having been given these positions, resided there and enjoyed various divine pleasures. Serving Matsara, they were filled with extreme arrogance. One day, while seated on the throne of Indra, Matsara, full of joy, spoke proudly to the demons.

Matsarasura said: O Virochana and other demons, listen to my words. Why have the glorious Brahma and the other gods not come here? Since the lords of the gods do not bow to me out of jealousy, I shall surely take away all their pride.

Hearing the words of Matsara, all the demons and Danavas praised him. Filled with anger, Matsara sent a messenger. He said: Go, messenger, to the abodes of the three gods, Brahma and the others. Speak my words to the lords of the gods with an attentive mind. Let the gods come to see Matsara along with me; otherwise, the demon Matsara will surely strike them down.

Hearing the words of Matsara, the messenger, a powerful demon named Andhaka, possessed of great speed, departed. In Satyaloka, the mighty demon did not see the Creator. Seeing the city empty, he went to Vaikuntha with respect. Seeing the same situation there, he went to Kailasa. There, he saw all the gods and sages filled with joy. The demon was brought into the assembly by the attendants of Shiva and was seated on a seat by Shiva himself. Seeing the splendor of Kailasa, he was amazed in his mind, considering it a place of eternal bliss and blessedness. As Shankara asked him questions, the demon, proud of his strength, spoke while belittling Maheshwara and all the gods.

Andhaka said: Listen to the words of Matsarasura, O best of gods. Having conquered the lord of the gods, Indra, I have become the lord of the world. Therefore, for the sake of seeing me, you all, along with your followers—Brahma, Vishnu, and Rudra—must come to my presence. Therefore, you lords of the gods must come with me today, quickly, to see Matsara.

Hearing the words of Andhaka, Shiva, filled with anger, spoke to the great demon while emitting fire from his mouth.

Shiva said: Why do you speak to me, you sinner, belittling me with your words? It is by the power of my boon that Matsara is victorious. When I am filled with anger, I will surely turn that wicked one, along with his demons, into ashes. Am I under the command of Matsara, you lowest of demons? Considering you a mere messenger, I let you go; there is no doubt about it. Go and tell that demon Matsara the words I have spoken; otherwise, I will reduce you to ashes, for I am enraged, O best of the wicked.

Hearing the words of Shiva, Andhaka went to Matsara and narrated the entire incident to him and all the demons. He described the splendor of Kailasa as it was, saying that there is nothing in the universe equal to Kailasa, O great demons. Hearing the cruel words of Andhaka, the powerful demons, led by Virochana, spoke cruelly to Matsara. They said: Why do you wait, O mighty one? By your grace, we shall conquer the three gods; there is no doubt. Give the command, O lord of the demons.

Hearing the words of the demons, the radiant Matsara spoke, filled with anger, as if to delight the demons.

Matsara said: By the power of my penance, I have attained wonderful radiance. Shiva is vainly proud, considering himself the bestower of boons. If he grants the kingdom of the universe to one devoid of penance, then he considers himself the lord of the world, but I do not think so. Therefore, we shall conquer Shiva with the power of our penance. I will take away the pride of him who vainly considers himself Brahma. In the Vedanta and the Brahma Vedas, it is said that the Absolute is without attributes; that alone is worthy of being honored as the Lord of the world. Through desire-driven actions, a person attains everything; if actions are performed as an offering to the Divine, liberation is attained. Everything is created, sustained, and destroyed by action. What, then, can those three gods, who are themselves born of action, do? By action, I create, sustain, and destroy. The entire world, including gods, demons, and humans, is subject to action.

Having said this, the great demon, surrounded by Virochana and others, arrived at the abode of Shankara, filled with intense passion. The attendants of Shiva who were outside were struck by various weapons and fled, being tormented. With their bodies cut and broken, they went to the presence of Shankara and said that Matsara had arrived with all the demons.

Thus ends the twenty-ninth chapter, titled "The Meeting of Shiva and Matsarasura," in the first section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 30 : The Defeat of Shiva and Others

Mudgala said: Hearing the harsh and grave words of the Ganas and Pishachas, Shiva himself became prepared for battle. Brahma, Vishnu, Kamadeva, the Vasus, Kubera, Chandra, Surya, Agni, Vayu, and others also armed themselves. Kalagnirudra, Bhairava, and the leaders of the Bhutas, having assumed a form suitable for war, stood in the presence of Shankara. The eight Bhairavas, Virabhadra, Nandi, Bhringi, Pushpadanta, and Mahakala also stood ready. Mahakali and other goddesses arrived, wielding weapons. Surrounded by such diverse armies, Lord Maheshwara, mounted on his bull and filled with the desire for battle, arrived where the Daityas were stationed, accompanied by Kartikeya and others.

Seeing Shankara with his army, skilled in various forms of warfare and bearing many weapons, the Daitya lords showered arrows upon them. Seeing this, the enraged gods struck them with their own weapons and missiles. Thus, a fierce and terrible battle ensued between the gods and the Daityas, both desiring victory over the other. The sky became dark with the multitude of arrows and weapons. The atmosphere was filled with the dust raised by the armies, making it impossible to distinguish friend from foe. The air was deafening with the sounds of bells, conches, war drums, the shouts of warriors, the rumbling of chariots, and the cries of elephants and horses.

The gods struck the Daityas, and the Daityas struck the gods; a chaotic, massive battle raged between them. They countered weapons with weapons and missiles with missiles, and the mighty warriors, filled with pride, engaged in hand-to-hand combat. In that fierce and terrifying battlefield, some were killed, some were dying, and others were torn apart. From the bodies of various beings, a river of blood flowed, appearing as a terrifying stream of flesh and mud. It was adorned with bones like cranes, hair like moss, serpents as waves, lotus-like heads, and headless trunks as trees. It was filled with logs of clubs, adorned with chowries and blades of grass, difficult to cross due to the whirlpools of shields, and infested with the leeches of daggers. It struck fear into the cowardly but brought joy to the brave.

Seeing this, the Daitya kings, filled with rage, advanced. The army of the gods was quickly broken on all sides, and the directions were covered with nets of arrows, leaving no path to escape. The gods, intent on fleeing, ran in all ten directions. Seeing this, the kings of the gods stopped them with great vigor. A duel ensued, leading to mutual destruction. I shall tell you of some of the duels between the main gods and the Daityas: Namuchi fought with Kamadeva, Shambara with Vayu, Shumbha with the Goddess, Nishumbha with Chandra, Virochana with Guha, Kalabhairava-Danava with Kalabhairava-Deva, Mahishasura with Kalagni-Rudra, Prahlada with Vishnu, Viprachitti with the Sun, Andhakasura with Virabhadra, and the foolish one fought with Agni. He who was fond of worldly objects fought fiercely with Brahma, and he who was fond of beauty fought with Shambhu. Matsarasura stood by, watching the battle.

Thus, hundreds and thousands of such duels took place, O Prajapati, which are impossible to count due to their vastness. The Asuras struck the gods with weapons, and the gods struck them back. Filled with rage, they released fire, wind, and other celestial weapons, causing the three worlds to tremble in that terrible conflict. A great destruction of gods and Asuras took place, and rivers of blood flowed, which were extremely terrifying.

Seven days and nights passed as they fought, and neither the Asuras nor the gods ceased their efforts. Then, the gods, led by Shankara, became extremely enraged. They countered the Daityas' weapons with their own and struck them with various types of missiles, leaving many unconscious, fallen, dead, or crushed. The Daityas, filled with fear, fled in all ten directions, even as the gods shone with their tridents and other great weapons. Sundarapriya and others fell to the ground, unconscious and near death, at the hands of Vishnu and the others, O Sage.

Seeing this, Matsara, filled with rage, arrived himself. He let out a roar so terrifying that it seemed to shake the entire universe. He drew his bow, causing the gods to tremble, and unleashed a shower of arrows that destroyed his enemies. Struck by his arrows, all the gods were severely battered; some died, some were wounded, and others fell unconscious on the battlefield. He struck the commander (Kartikeya) in the chest with five arrows, causing him to fall to the ground, unconscious and overwhelmed. He struck Agni with seven arrows, Nandi with ten, and the Daitya king caused them to fall on the battlefield. He struck Virabhadra with ten arrows, Savita (the Sun) with eight, and Chandra with seven, felling them all. He struck Anila (Vayu) with nine, Kalagni-Rudra with eight, and Mahakali with ten. With five arrows each, he felled the Gana leaders like Bhringi, shining like the fire of dissolution. He struck Kubera in the chest with ten arrows and felled Brahma to the ground with a hundred arrows.

Seeing this miraculous feat, Vishnu arrived and showered arrows like a rain cloud. The Daitya, warding off those arrows with his own, struck Vishnu with a hundred arrows, causing him to fall to the ground. Then, an enraged Shankara struck him hard with his trident, but the trident proved ineffective and fell to the ground. The Daitya king then suddenly cast a noose at Shankara, caught him by the neck like an animal, and brought him near. Holding the mighty-armed Shambhu, he said, "O Sadashiva, tell me, what incomparable thing have you given me today? You are capable through your actions, and so am I. I have attained the lordship of the world; your pride is in vain."

In this manner, the mighty Daitya captured the gods, led by Vishnu, and enjoyed himself on the beautiful peak of Kailasa, surrounded by the Daityas. Having become the most worshipped, the Daitya was filled with great joy and indulged in various pleasures and enjoyments.

Chapter 31 : The Meeting with Dattatreya

Mudgala said: O Daksha, listen to this great wonder and the activities of the demon. Through his own actions, Matsara became the lord of the universe. He appointed his son Sundarapriya as the lord of Kailasa, his son Vijayapriya as the lord of Vaikuntha, Prahlada as the lord of Satyaloka, and Virochana as the lord of Mahendra. In this way, the king of demons appointed his sons as the rulers of the heavens.

He himself descended to the earth and built a city named Matsaravasaka, where he resided with joy. By his own power, he brought the three worlds under his control. Even Shiva and the other gods took residence in his house. The lord of demons, with a joyful heart, summoned them and spoke these sweet, diplomatic words: "O Shiva, O lord of gods, listen. Go wherever you wish, but always obey my command." Having said this, the demon king released Shankara. The great-souled Matsara was filled with joy along with the gods.

Shiva, accompanied by the host of gods, took refuge in a mountain valley. Filled with anxiety, the great Lord Shiva spent his time watching the situation. Wherever they stayed—in caves or elsewhere—the gods, led by Vishnu, lived in fear and passed their days. On earth, the demon king destroyed Dharma everywhere, causing a great outcry. Under his orders, the demons broke the idols of the gods and threw them into the water, causing the collapse of righteousness. Everywhere—in countries, cities, and villages—the demons promptly installed idols of Matsara. They broke the sacred idols of the gods in every home and threw them into the water. They replaced them with idols of Matsara and forced the people to worship them, terrorizing the world.

Matsara summoned the Brahmins and struck them, saying, "O Brahmins, perform sacrifices and other rituals using only my mantras and hymns. Chant the Gayatri dedicated to me without delay. Perform the Agnihotra and other rites according to my will. I alone am the Lord and God of all people, including the Brahmins. What will be unattainable for those who worship my feet? I will grant you all wealth, grain, and heavenly enjoyments. There is no doubt that the world is always under my control. I will surely fulfill the desires of those who please me by honoring the demons with food."

In this way, the foolish and jealous Matsara caused the destruction of all righteousness everywhere. O Daksha, some groups of sages went to forests filled with tigers and other beasts, where they performed their duties, devoted to Dharma. O Prajapati, the people became degraded, losing their status and neglecting the protection of their bodies. Fearing the demon, they ceased performing sacrifices, rituals, and their own inherent duties. There was no Swaha, no Swadha, and no Vashatkara. People lived in fear, and the mixing of castes occurred.

Thus, a terrifying increase in sin took place. The gods, suffering from hunger, deliberated on how to destroy Matsara, but being overwhelmed by fear, they were unable to find a way to defeat him. At that moment, Dattatreya arrived—a yogi who was the embodiment of Brahman, having abandoned the illusion of "self" and "other." He wandered the world like a ghost, appearing dirty and dull. Naked and acting like a child, he moved about, indifferent to the dualities of the world. The great-souled one acted like a madman and felt no fear. He was a devotee of the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha).

Seeing him, the gods stood up with great respect. To achieve their goal, they bowed to him and worshipped him. Some affectionately massaged his feet, and others respectfully offered him fruits and food. After he had eaten, the gods, led by Shiva and Vishnu, approached the great sage with humility and fear.

Shiva said: "O Master, you are free from duality, eternally blissful, and supreme. You have abandoned the sense of 'self' and 'other,' yet you are compassionate toward all beings. You are a great devotee of Ganesha, indeed Ganesha himself. You are a yogi endowed with the state of Yoga, the best among those who know Yoga. Yet, seeing the suffering of others, your heart becomes moved, and through your selfless nature, you destroy their sorrow. We are suffering greatly because of Matsara; we have lost our positions and wander in the forest like wild animals. We are suffering intensely due to the destruction of Dharma and are dying of hunger. By the power of our past merits, we have obtained your vision. We have no doubt that we will now be freed from our suffering. Therefore, O Lord, tell us the way to destroy the demon Matsara, who has afflicted the entire world. If many are suffering due to the fault of one, a yogi, through his selfless nature, can destroy that fault. If you are capable, please bring happiness to all by your nature. O King of Yogis, please protect the entire world."

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Shiva and seeing the gods in such a miserable state, the compassionate Dattatreya replied, bringing them joy.

Dattatreya said: "Listen, O Shiva, Lord of the world, you are the Supreme Lord himself. Everything is under your control; what can I tell you, O wise one? Nevertheless, urged by your command, I speak so that you, along with the gods, may find eternal happiness. O Shiva, Matsara is currently residing in your heart. The thought that 'I am the Lord, and all this exists under my control,' and 'There is no one equal to me in the three worlds'—that is the demon Matsara. Destroy that great demon residing in your heart through the path of Yoga. The great jealousy that arises in you when you see someone superior—destroy that through penance and by serving Ganesha, O Shiva. I worshipped Him in the past using the single-syllable mantra. Because of that, the Lord of the Ganas and I are not different; He resides within me. Therefore, you should also worship Ganesha, the giver of all success, through the path of Yoga and the single-syllable mantra with effort. By destroying the Matsara within your heart, you will then be able to destroy the demon outside. There is no doubt that you will become capable through Yoga."

Having said this to the great Lord Shiva, the excellent sage departed, wandering at will and serving the Lord of the Ganas.

Chapter 32 : The Manifestation of Vakratunda

Mudgala said: O Daksha, wise one, listen to the supreme and great deeds performed by all the gods, which I shall narrate to you in brief. When the noble sage had departed, Shiva, filled with joy, meditated upon Ganesha in his heart and spoke to Brahma and the others. O Vishnu, O Brahma, O gods, O sages, listen to these words that are beneficial for all who dwell here. That which was told by Atreya is the destroyer of sorrow; therefore, propitiate the Lord of Obstacles using only the single-syllable mantra. Hearing the words of Shambhu, the sages and the leaders of the gods said, "Excellent, excellent," and were greatly pleased.

Then, O sage, all the gods and sages began to serve the Lord of the Ganas with great devotion using only the single-syllable mantra. Shiva and the others remained there, engaged in intense penance, worshipping the Lord of Gods, Ganesha, the leader of all. Some sat in the lotus posture, others in the hero's posture, while others sat in the svastika posture, and thus they remained in various positions. Some remained without food, O Prajapati, while others subsisted only on leaves or air, devoted to breath control. Some consumed only water, standing on the tips of their toes in firm postures, while others worshipped Him through mental offerings. Some worshipped Him with great effort using flowers and other gifts, while others performed circumambulations in various ways. By performing penance for the Lord of the Ganas according to the prescribed rites and with focused minds, they easily pleased Him.

Thus, after a thousand divine years of penance, the highly radiant and powerful Vakratunda became pleased. He appeared before the gods and sages, shining like a mass of light, appearing as the embodiment of Brahman itself. He was mounted on a lion, with the face of an elephant, large ears, and a large belly. He had four arms, holding a noose, a goad, a boon-granting gesture, and a gesture of fearlessness. On his left side, he held Siddhi, and on his right side, the Lord held Buddhi. He wore a crown, armlets, and bracelets, his chest shone with the Chintamani jewel, and he wore a serpent as his sacred thread. Adorned with various ornaments, he glowed with an amazing brilliance, holding a lotus and spinning it as he approached.

Seeing him, the gods and sages were filled with fear, wondering what this radiance was, resembling the fire of universal dissolution. They pondered whether this was the Lord of the Ganas, or if their master had arrived, or if an untimely dissolution caused by fate was at hand. To the gods and sages filled with such doubts, Vakratunda spoke with a voice as deep as the clouds.

Vakratunda said: O gods and sages, I am the very one who has arrived. Know that I am the Lord of the Ganas, whom you noble ones meditate upon. Pleased by your penance, the supreme mantra, and your constant meditation on me, I have come to grant you a boon, without a doubt.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Vakratunda, the gods and sages were overjoyed; they bowed and prayed, overwhelmed with a gentle emotion. Hearing their prayers, the Lord assumed a gentle form, such that the sages and gods could behold him with their own eyes. The gods and sages, seeing Vakratunda in his gentle form, bowed to him and said, "We are blessed, we are blessed, O Lord." They worshipped him with effort, devotion, and affection, using the sixteen offerings according to the prescribed rites. After bowing according to the rules, they praised him, all with folded hands and necks bowed in devotion. With tearful eyes and hair standing on end, they celebrated the festival of his vision, praising the highly radiant Vakratunda according to their knowledge and the scriptures.

The gods and sages said: Salutations to you, O Vakratunda, salutations to you, O elephant-faced one. Salutations to the one-tusked God, the Lord of all. Salutations to you, who are without attributes, without form, and possessor of four arms. You are the rider of the lion, the one who holds the earth on the navel of the Lord of Serpents. Salutations to you, the infinite, the boundless, the difficult to perceive. Salutations to the holder of the noose and goad, the one who wears a serpent as a sacred thread. Salutations to the one whose hands grant boons and fearlessness, the bestower of Siddhi and Buddhi. You are the embodiment of Brahman, the one who leads your devotees to the state of Brahman.

Salutations to you, who are in the form of Shambhu, salutations to you, who are in the form of Vishnu. Salutations to the form of the Sun, the Creator, and salutations to the one who is the embodiment of energy. Salutations to you, who take the form of demons and giants, and who take the form of gods. Salutations to you, who take the form of birds and the form of a boar. Salutations to you, who are in the form of planets and stars, creepers and trees. Salutations to you, O mountain, salutations to you, who are in the form of rivers and oceans. Salutations to you, who are in the form of aquatic creatures and serpents. Salutations to you, who dwell in the eighty-four lakh species of life.

Salutations to you, who are the embodiment of all moving and non-moving things, and who take many forms. Salutations to you, who are the embodiment of action, inaction, and forbidden action. Salutations to the God of knowledge and yoga, who possess the form of the Self. Salutations to you, who are the embodiment of self-realization and always the embodiment of yoga. Salutations to you, who are the support of various streams and the embodiment of the sound-Brahman. Salutations to you, who are the form of Maya, who are Maya itself, and who are beyond Maya. Salutations to you, who are the giver of delusion to the deluded and the cause of all. Salutations to you, who are the distinction of gross and subtle, and who are devoid of all distinctions. Salutations to you, who are the embodiment of both distinction and non-distinction, and who are infinite. Salutations to you, the giver of boons to your devotees and the protector of your devotees. Salutations, salutations to the Supreme Lord, salutations to the imperishable one. Salutations to you, O Ganesha, salutations to you, the Lord of Yoga.

How can we praise you, O Ganesha, in the form of Vakratunda, whom even the Vedas with their limbs and the yogis are unable to praise? The delusion of Maya in the minds of all is the creator of knowledge; therefore, Brahman is known by the wise as "Vakra" (curved/indirect) when devoid of knowledge. Maya is the body, and Brahman is the "Tunda" (face/mouth); the union of the two is the supreme state. The peace of yoga resides in the hearts of all yogis. Your form is the body of Maya, and your face is known as the Brahman-Tunda. Because of the union of both, you are called Vakratunda. We behold you, Vakratunda, the supreme embodiment of yoga. We are blessed to have praised and bowed to the Lord of the Ganas with these hymns. Blessed is this birth, blessed are these eyes, blessed is this wealth, and blessed is the knowledge and penance by which the elephant-faced one has been seen.

If you are pleased, O Lord of Gods, and if a boon is to be granted, then may we always have devotion to your lotus feet. We pray for this boon, as you are the granter of all desired objects. O Vakratunda, destroy the demon Matsarasura, O Lord. We and the entire world are tormented by him, O Lord of Obstacles. For the benefit of all, slay this mighty demon.

Having praised and prayed to him in this way, the gods and sages bowed down. Raising them up, Vakratunda himself spoke.

Vakratunda said: This hymn composed by you will be supreme and will grant all accomplishments. O noble ones, it will be great and will grant all desired objects. O gods and sages, I will destroy Matsarasura. Pleased by your devotion, I have attained completeness. The entire world is said to be composed of the five elements, the three qualities, and the eight coverings, O gods and sages. All gods, humans, serpents, and the entire moving and non-moving world are bound by these eight coverings; there is no fear for those who are beyond them. Therefore, the destruction of the demon Matsara is difficult and great. Nevertheless, O gods and sages, I will do what is dear to you.

Thus ends the thirty-second chapter titled "The Manifestation of Vakratunda" in the first section of the Vakratunda-charita in the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 33 : Consideration of Matsarasura

Mudgala said: Having said this to the gods and sages, he remembered his powerful forces. By the mere act of his remembrance, the attendants (Ganas) of the Lord manifested. They were named Pramoda, Amoda, and others, possessing curved trunks and elephant faces. All were four-armed, holding various weapons and missiles in their hands. Some were mounted on lions, some on mice, some on peacocks, and others on the serpent Shesha. Still others were mounted on horses, adorned with crowns, bracelets, earrings, and various other ornaments. They were terrifying to their enemies and givers of happiness to their devotees. They were great souls with loud voices, capable of shattering the universe. Engaged in various sports, they were always absorbed in their own bliss, speaking to each other about the greatness of Ganesha’s name. After bowing to Vakratunda, they stood with folded hands and said, "O Lord of the Ganas, give us your command; we shall do what you desire. Why have you remembered us, your servants, with such respect? We shall make all people the servants of your feet."

Hearing their words, the powerful Vakratunda spoke to those great beings with a voice as deep as the clouds. Vakratunda said: "Listen, O Amoda and others, I shall slay the demon king Matsarasura. He is of great might, and a battle will take place with him. Now, there is a terrible situation there, and I need you, O mighty ones. For this reason, I have specifically remembered you." Hearing the words of Vakratunda, all the Ganas were filled with joy. They bowed to him with folded hands and said: "O Lord, everything you say is full of wonder. Is the destruction of Matsarasura to be done by you alone? Through your grace, everything is easy for us, O Lord of the Ganas. Give the command, O great one, and watch the prowess of even one of us."

Hearing the pleasant words of the Ganas, the Lord of the Ganas, Vakratunda, spoke with a pleased heart, delighting them all. Vakratunda said: "Everything you have said is true, not otherwise. I created this demon for the sake of sport through the power of my Maya. If I were to destroy that demon in an instant by my power, then how would my divine sport be played? Therefore, O great ones, fight with him in my presence. I shall slay him in an instant in a form devoid of the eight coverings." Hearing his words, the Ganas were filled with joy and roared, causing the directions and sub-directions to resound. Seeing this great wonder, the gods and sages were astonished, and filled with great joy, they considered Matsara already slain.

Then, taking all the Ganas, gods, and sages with him, Vakratunda went to that supreme city named Irshya (Envy), where the powerful Matsara ruled. He was a great hero of the universe, endowed with various enjoyments. The demon king always remained surrounded by celestial damsels, indulging in wine and other drinks, protected by various warriors. Whatever he thought of in his mind always became successful due to his penance and the boons he had received. He did not realize that time was passing, as if cheated by time itself, thinking, "I am blessed; there is no one else in my mind equal to me."

One day, he was sitting in his assembly, surrounded by heroes, watching a dance for entertainment. At that moment, a terrifying voice came from the sky: "The mighty Vakratunda has arrived to slay Matsara." Hearing the voice from the sky, Matsarasura fainted, fell down with a dry throat, and trembled in agitation. Seeing him fallen, all the demons were terrified, wondering who had spoken such harsh words and swearing to kill that person today. Some wandered in the sky here and there, but seeing no one, they returned to the assembly. They revived the demon king, who sat in the assembly with a heavy heart.

Matsara, trembling with fear, spoke to himself: "Will this word be true or false? I am the death of time itself; how can my death occur? This voice from the sky is neither known nor heard of before. The universe is endowed with eight coverings; my death cannot occur from those who are within these eight coverings. Who is this creature with a curved face, and where is he? If I see him, I will kill him in an instant, without doubt." Hearing these words of the evil-minded Matsara, Prahlad and others spoke to him with humility.

Prahlad and others said: "Do not worry, O great one, O destroyer of enemies. How can your death happen? How can there be a sunrise after the night? The gods and their lords are our enemies, and even though they have been defeated, they are always looking for our weaknesses. O best of demons, they must have created such a Maya. Otherwise, how could your great death occur, O giver of honor? This is not a voice from the sky. Therefore, O demon king, we shall kill the gods and their lords today, and then we shall be free of enemies." Hearing their words, Matsara was pleased and said: "You have spoken truly; we must go to slay the enemies." Having received Matsara's command, the demon chiefs went out, searching for the gods everywhere with great effort.

Thus ends the thirty-third chapter named "Consideration of Matsarasura" in the Vakratunda-charita of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 34 : The Episode of the War between Devas and Asuras

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: The demons, full of wandering, arrived near Dhundhi, where the powerful Vakratunda was present, accompanied by the host of gods. Seeing that Vakratunda, who possessed a massive body, immense strength, and the form of a human and an elephant, they became terrified. He was surrounded by various heroes who were like the fire of dissolution, and he was being praised by the gods who were filled with joy and devotion. All the demon leaders mentally accepted their own death, recalling the voice from the sky which said, "He shall be Vakratunda." Because the gods kept saying, "O Vakratunda, victory to you, victory to you, victory always," he came to be known as Vakratunda.

The demons thought, "If we wage a great and wondrous war with him, and if we die in the battle, who will report the news?" Therefore, they decided to go to the demon king Matsara with haste. Having made this decision, they went to Matsara with respect. Lord Vakratunda, having left behind the jealousy and having held a meeting, stayed five yojanas away. The demons went to Matsara and reported the news, "O King, the words from the sky are indeed true. The Lord with the crooked face has been seen in the form of a human-elephant, surrounded by gods. O King, consider this in every way."

Hearing their words, Matsara became filled with anxiety. Knowing his enemy to be immensely powerful, his face became pale. Then, filled with anger, he made up his mind to kill him and ordered the demon leaders, "Be prepared! I will go to kill the enemy, the immensely powerful Vakratunda, along with his gods and his retinue, and then there will be happiness in my heart." At his mere command, the demon leaders, eager to crush the gods, arrived fully armed with great joy. A massive four-fold army emerged from the city, and in the midst of it, the hero Matsara shone, seated in his chariot.

They arrived quickly at the place where Vakratunda was stationed. Seeing them, the host of gods praised the leader of the Ganas, "O Lord, the demon has come himself, O Great Lord. Whatever you are going to do, O Lord, do it quickly. Otherwise, the immensely powerful demons will kill us. We have reached the point of death; there is no place where we can stand." Hearing the words of the gods with respect, Vakratunda was surprised and said, "Do not fear, my dear ones. I will surely kill the demon Matsara, O gods. Watch my most wondrous spectacle in the battle."

Having said this, the Lord Vakratunda mounted a lion, holding a noose and a goad in his hands, roaring with a sound like the thunder of clouds. At his sound, the three worlds, including the moving and non-moving, were terrified, and the demon hosts became fearful, O Prajapati. Matsara, terrified in his heart, spoke these words, "A powerful army is seen; what will happen, O demon leaders?" Hearing his fearful words, the demon leaders said, "We will fight, O Lord; watch the power born of war."

Having said this, all the heroes, led by Prahlada, went toward Vakratunda, making the heavens resound. Filled with anger, they unleashed a fierce rain of arrows; they showered weapons and missiles like clouds during the rainy season. By those weapons, the demon kings struck the army of the gods. The gods, led by Vishnu, fought those who had arrived. A terrible, cruel, and indescribable war between the gods and demons ensued, which caused their destruction.

The gods and demons fought, weapons in hand, having decided on death and desiring victory over each other. Some unleashed a rain of arrows, while others threw shaktis (spears). Others cut with swords, while some bound with nooses or pierced with lances. The best of heroes engaged in a terrible mace-fight. The great heroes killed with clubs with great force. Others engaged in wrestling, making the ten directions resound. The entire horizon was covered with the dust of the army; there was no awareness of friend or foe, and they struck those who were in front.

Blocking weapons with weapons and missiles with missiles, they cut each other's arrows with their own. When their weapons were broken, the heroes engaged in a fierce wrestling match, filled with anger, moving in circles. Pressing hand against hand, grabbing feet with feet, they crushed each other's elbows with elbows with great force. They struck head against head, knee against knee, and forehead against forehead.

As the battle progressed, some died, some fainted, some were broken, and others were wounded by the strikes of various weapons. A terrible river of blood flowed, which was difficult to cross, and the dust was submerged in blood everywhere. By that, the directions became illuminated, revealing friend and foe. Then, a tumultuous battle ensued as appropriate, O Prajapati. Charioteers fought with charioteers, elephant-riders with elephant-riders, horsemen with horsemen, and infantry with infantry. The best fought with the best, endowed with great strength. Some heroes, skilled in war, killed after challenging each other. They pierced each other with heart-piercing weapons and missiles, and all shone like blossoming Kimshuka flowers.

Sometimes the gods were victorious, and sometimes the demons. Filled with anger, they did not rest even at night. Thus, a terrible war took place there for five days. Neither the gods nor the demons attained victory, O Prajapati.

Thus ends the thirty-fourth chapter of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Episode of the War between Devas and Asuras," in the story of Vakratunda.

Chapter 35 : Shiva's Victory

Mudgala said: In this manner, the powerful warriors fought a terrible battle against each other, yet neither side could achieve victory, as their actions were equally matched. Seeing this great wonder, Prahlada became enraged; he readied his formidable bow and took up his arrows. Invoking the fire-weapon, he released many arrows, from which a great fire emerged, resembling the fire of universal destruction. This fire burned the army of the gods, and the terrified deities began to burn on all sides. Crying out in distress, they were scorched by the fire and fled, abandoning the battlefield. The great fire, urged on by Prahlada, burned the gods along with their vehicles on all sides. The hosts of demons were filled with joy and praised Prahlada, saying, "Well done, well done!"

Seeing all the gods fleeing, Indra, filled with anger, fitted the cloud-weapon to his bow. At that moment, Prahlada arrived and skillfully severed Indra's bow with his sword. Then Indra, in a rage, hurled his thunderbolt at him. Struck by the thunderbolt, Prahlada fell to the ground. Seeing him fallen, Mahishasura arrived and struck the King of the Gods on the head with his mace. Struck by the mace, Indra also fell on the battlefield, unconscious and vomiting blood. Then Agni, filled with great fury, blazed toward the demon. Mahisha took the form of water and instantly flooded him. Deprived of his brilliance and filled with anxiety, Agni hurled his weapon, but the great demon struck it down to the ground with his sword. Then, in the form of a buffalo, Mahisha again attacked the fire-god with his horns, rendering him unconscious, and the lord of light fell.

Then Yama arrived and struck the terrifying demon Mahisha with his staff, rendering him unconscious. Meanwhile, the valiant Virochana arrived and quickly struck Yama, the terror of all, with his sword. Yama, the lord of those who dispense justice, fell from the sword blow. Varuna then bound Virochana with his noose, but the powerful demon cut the noose and freed himself. Andhaka then struck Varuna with his sword and mace, rendering him unconscious. Virochana rose again, and then Vayu and Kubera, both enraged, attacked. Vayu was struck by the sword of the one named Andhakasura and fell to the ground. Shambara, a great warrior, struck Vayu with a mace, and the god, despite being the supporter of all, fell to the earth. The guardian of wealth (Kubera) was struck by a shower of arrows from Virochana; pierced in his vital parts, the powerful god fell.

At that moment, Vishnu himself arrived and struck his enemy Virochana with his mace. He shattered the chest of the demon king and left him as if dead. Keshava (Vishnu) then felled Andhaka with his discus and brought Shambara to the ground with a volley of arrows. Enraged, Madhava (Vishnu) then hurled his terrible discus. Thousands of demons were killed by the sharp-edged discus, and the main leaders who stood there were rendered unconscious by his mace. At that point in the battle, the hero Prahlada appeared. Alert and filled with rage, he approached. He unleashed a fierce shower of arrows that destroyed the gods, leaving them wounded and scattered on the battlefield. He fought the Lord of the Gods, Keshava, fiercely with his mace and rendered Garuda unconscious with his sword.

Then Vishnu, enraged, took up his discus to slay him. He whirled it many times and released that supreme weapon. The discus severed the heads of the demons on all sides and cut their arms and torsos into pieces. It suddenly moved to slay the demon king Prahlada, shining like fire. Prahlada quickly fitted a weapon to his great bow, remembering Janardana (Vishnu) in his heart as the embodiment of Narayana. He released that great, infallible, and radiant weapon. It was irresistible and capable of turning the world to ash. By the force of the Narayana weapon, the discus was repelled, and the great weapon burned all the gods on all sides. It suddenly struck the heart of Keshava, rendering him unconscious and as if dead.

Then the jubilant demons roared with joy and praised the radiant and mighty Prahlada. Seeing the gods and sages scorched by the Narayana weapon, Shankara (Shiva), disturbed for the protection of the gods, took up his Pinaka bow in his hand. Filled with anger, he fitted the great Pashupata weapon. Shankara acted with haste and released it with great force. The Pashupata weapon and the Narayana weapon clashed in the sky. The gods and sages were filled with joy and praised the Great God, the God of Gods, Shankara. Then Pashupati, enraged, fitted his trident and released it upon the demon army, causing supreme destruction. The trident killed thousands of demons and suddenly struck the heart of Prahlada, resembling Yama himself. Struck by it, he fell to the ground like a tree felled by a storm. A great uproar arose in the demon army. Then, by the force of the sharp trident, the demons were suddenly rendered unconscious, and some were killed. All the hosts of demons fell on the battlefield. Then two sons of Matsara, Sundarapriya and Vishayapriya, approached Shankara, the Lord, and spoke to him in great anger.

Chapter 36 : The Killing of Sundarapriya and Vishayapriya

Daksha said: O Brahman, please narrate to me in detail the story that brings joy, which arose from the influence of Vakratunda.

Mudgala said: It is not possible for Agniputra, Pinakin (Shiva), or Shesha to narrate it in full detail, so I shall tell it to you briefly.

Daksha said: Tell me what the sons of the Daitya said, what Shiva heard, and what the Great God did after hearing it. What did the two Daityas do there? Tell me specifically, for I am never satisfied hearing the story from your lotus-like mouth.

Suta said: Understanding the eagerness of the humble-minded Daksha, Mudgala, filled with joy, spoke to him, O Shaunaka.

Mudgala said: Listen, O Prajapati Daksha, to what the two mighty sons of Matsara, filled with anger, said to Shankara.

The sons of Matsara said: Stay, stay, O Mahadeva! Witness our prowess. We know your strength, and you shall not escape. Our great army has been slaughtered, O Sadashiva, and all the Daitya lords have been felled by your radiance. You have displayed your full prowess, which surpasses all the gods. Now, we shall kill you while Vakratunda watches. After killing you, we shall undoubtedly kill Vakratunda, and then all the gods and the hosts of sages.

Having said this to Mahadeva, they prepared their bows. Mahadeva smiled and spoke these words:

Shiva said: Everything you have said is cruel, yet it is true. Now, this mighty Vakratunda has arisen to remove the burden of the earth and to destroy you. The time has come for the opposite to happen. By the grace of Vakratunda, I shall undoubtedly slay the sinful and excessively powerful Asura, Matsara.

Having said this, Lord Shankara unleashed a rain of arrows, fierce as death and manifesting the dissolution of the world. The two Daityas cut them down with their own sharp arrows, creating a pavilion of arrows in an instant. O Sage, even the gods were struck and wounded by those arrows. Shankara, astonished, released his fierce trident. Seeing the trident coming like death itself, the two great Asuras released the Pashupata weapon. It was irresistible, fierce, and capable of burning everything to ash; the entire universe began to tremble with its radiance. In an instant, the trident was rendered useless by that great weapon, and it struck Shiva in the chest, causing terror. Shankara fell to the ground, unconscious, and a cry of distress arose everywhere among the divine army.

Seeing Shankara fallen, the mighty Vakratunda urged his own army forward, eager to crush the Daityas. Eight of the chief messengers of Ganesha arrived there, bringing their own armies—they were great in form and strength. O Sage, they were Pramoda, Amoda, Brahmapriya, Yogapriya, Svanandabhokta, and Jnanamaya. They arrived filled with rage, being all-pervading and all-knowing. Upon seeing them, all the gods recovered from their unconsciousness. They became free from fatigue and wounds, and through their influence, they became twice as radiant, healthy, and strong, O Sage.

Meanwhile, the Daityas, who had been revived by Shukra, all rose up, healthy and powerful. The heroes, led by Prahlada, raised a roar on all sides, and the entire universe became filled with that sound. Then, all the hosts of gods blew their conch shells, and the Daitya hosts, terrified, stood as if deafened. The sky was filled with the sounds of conches, drums, and kettledrums, the shouts of heroes, the neighing of horses, the trumpeting of elephants, and the rumbling of chariot wheels, O Sage. Seeing each other's strength, they were astonished.

At that very moment, the Ganas, led by Pramoda, arrived. Filled with anger, they unleashed a rain of weapons that bewildered the Daityas. All their weapons were infallible, O great sage; they severed the heads and arms of the Daitya kings. Brahmapriya, the mighty-armed, seized Shukra and, before everyone's eyes, threw the sage far away into a cave. Seeing Shukra gone, all the Daityas were filled with fear. They cried out in distress, "What will happen?" and became distraught. "Our fierce Guru, who protected us, has been taken away, O great Asuras! Is it not better that we die?"

"Where shall we go now? Let us fight. We shall either die or release him with our own strength." Saying this to one another, the Daityas, determined to die, took up their weapons and fought with the divine soldiers. The gods were maimed and fell on the battlefield, and a cry of distress arose everywhere in the divine army. Then, the Ganas, led by Brahmapriya, filled with anger, struck the Daityas with weapons like axes, tridents, goads, and nooses, just as the Kala Rudras destroy the people. The Daitya army was instantly slaughtered by those weapons. Many Daityas died, and others fell to the ground unconscious, O Sage, including the heroes led by Prahlada.

Then, the two Daitya princes arrived on the battlefield and spoke to the Ganas, led by Pramoda.

The sons of the Daitya said: O Pramoda, Amoda, and others, you have quickly destroyed the strength of the Daityas, and the best of the Daityas have fainted. Now, see that your own death is near. We shall kill you villains, along with Vakratunda, with our sharp weapons.

Having said this, the two Asuras showered their arrows. Seeing their strength, two heroes, Sarvaga and Sarvajna, arrived, holding bows. They blocked the rain of arrows and released their own. All their arrows were infallible, resembling Death and Yama, and they cut the Daityas to pieces, O Sage. Some died, some were dying, and others fell to the ground. Suddenly, a river of blood began to flow there.

Seeing their strength, the two Daitya princes trembled. Filled with anger, they placed the best fire-weapon on their bows. As they released them, two axes resembling Yama arrived and instantly severed the heads of the two Daityas. Seeing the sons of Matsara dead, everyone was filled with fear. They went to Matsara for refuge and quickly told him the news.

The Daityas said: Listen, O Lord of the Daityas, to the news from the battlefield. It is so terrifying and horrible that it cannot be described. All the heroes, led by Prahlada, have been slaughtered on the battlefield. We do not know if they are dead or as good as dead. Your two mighty sons have died on the battlefield after fighting a fierce battle with axes, O wise one. Suddenly, two Ganas of Vakratunda arrived, and they killed the heroes in a short time, O Lord of the Daityas. There is no one like Vakratunda, nor will there ever be. Realizing this in your heart, take refuge in that God. Otherwise, all the Daitya hosts will certainly meet their destruction. Do not be proud, O Lord of the Daityas; protect the Daityas, O wise one.

Thus ends the thirty-sixth chapter of the first section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Killing of Sundarapriya and Vishayapriya."

Chapter 37 : The Meeting with Matsarasura

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of the demons, the demon Matsarasura suddenly fainted and fell to the ground, overwhelmed by fear. The king of the demons, not breathing, was quickly held by them, and they then made great efforts to revive him. After two muhurtas, Matsara regained consciousness and lamented: "What has happened, or what is to come, that fate has rendered my efforts fruitless? Through my penance, I attained immortality and the kingdom of the earth with ease; my incomparable power has been rendered useless. Alas, fate is powerful, and effort is considered futile. My attainment of fearlessness was inconceivable, like a sunrise after darkness. Countless demons have died for my sake, there is no doubt. My sons, Mahakala and Kalasya, how have they died today? Now, consumed by grief, I will surely die. My sons are dead; what is the use of my life? How could the sons of the lord of the universe meet their end? How could the great boon granted to me become false? Alas, my heroic sons, how did they meet death, they who conquered Vishnu and the other gods with the strength of their own arms? Alas, my sons, having left me in this deep ocean of sorrow, where have you gone? Speak, why have you become so cruel? Leaving your friends and your aged father, you who were fond of beauty and pleasure, how did you meet death? Why did you leave my strength?"

The guardian of the demon king lamented in many ways. All the noble courtiers consoled him with respect. The elders said: "O leader of the demons, you should not weep. Your heroic sons died in battle after achieving great glory. The death of the body is constant, as is birth, O Lord. Why are you surprised by what has happened, that you grieve, O demon king? In the scriptures of birth, it is said that the soul never dies. Abandoning delusion, become composed, O wise one. The final rites for a creature must be performed by their own kin. Therefore, O king of demons, perform the meaningful rites for them. Or, leaving them, destroy Vakratunda, O Lord. Or seek refuge, by which you may find well-being. Brave men do not weep anywhere, do not know this, O demon king. Do not make your enemies joyful now."

Thus advised, the demon Matsara, filled with anger, took his entire army and went to the battlefield. Seeing Matsarasura approaching, Shambhu and the other gods, terrified, bowed to Vakratunda and said: "Lord, the powerful demon Matsara has arrived, before whom the gods are like mosquitoes and humans like blades of grass. Before him, wealth-giving goddesses are submissive; the universe trembles in fear of him. In his kingdom, fortunate people are free from sorrow; trees yield fruit in all seasons, and clouds rain at the right time. His command is obeyed by the sun, which shines accordingly; there is no extreme cold where Matsara rules. Diseases do not spread here, terrified by him; the world is not troubled by thieves or others while he rules. He is worshipped everywhere in the world by people; all sacrifices are consumed by this great soul. Even Time fears him, and he has become the one who enforces his own commands. Even the performance of rituals has been stopped by this enemy of the gods. Where Matsara rules, there is no Svaha, no Svadha, no Vashatkara, no study of the Vedas, and no tradition. There is no bathing or ritual, no natural duty, and no caste-based dharma where Matsara rules. The gods wander like wild animals in the forests, deprived of food and clothing by this demon king. Sages are stripped of their penance and the power of their curses by the demon Matsara; he is the one who has arrived, O Lord. Divine maidens, his daughters, and Naga maidens have been enjoyed by this great soul in various ways. Human maidens, animal maidens, and sage maidens have been forcibly taken by him; there is no one equal to his strength in the universe, and he has arrived. He, for whom death is impossible due to his eight-fold protection, has arrived; look, look, O Lord of the Ganas."

To the gods speaking thus, the mighty Vakratunda said: "Do not fear, O wise ones, I will destroy him." Mounted on a lion, with long arms and a single tusk, holding a noose and a goad, he went to the battlefield. Seeing him surrounded by Pramoda and Amoda, eager for battle, Matsara, filled with anger, spoke mockingly: "Why have you come before me in battle, O Vakratunda? I will kill you this very day; do not show me your face. You are a fool to have come to fight. My death cannot occur due to the eight-fold protection of embodied beings. You are incited by Shambhu, Vishnu, and other gods, and you do not understand. You have come in delusion, wanting to fight Matsara. I will kill you and your army with a single arrow, there is no doubt. With the strike of my foot, the earth is shattered, O wicked one. Mountains have been turned to dust by the strike of my palm. Why have you come before him, or have you been sent by Time? The universe trembles at the mere sound of his roar. Tell me, what will you do in battle before him? By whose power the entire universe was conquered in war, where were you hiding then, filled with fear? This is a good thing you have done now, O fool, O wicked one. Having come within my sight, you will be killed by me. Seek my refuge, O wicked one, if you wish to live. Become subject to my command and stay in the forest as you please. I will not kill you, who have come in delusion and are weak. I will certainly kill those sages and gods who incited you."

Thus ends the thirty-seventh chapter, titled "The Meeting with Matsarasura," in the Vakratunda-charita of the first section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 38 : The Victory of Vakratunda

Mudgala said: Vakratunda, seeing the arrogant and angry Matsara speaking in such a manner, smiled and addressed him. Vakratunda said: Do not be proud, O Lord of Daityas; I am neither a god nor a human, neither an asura nor a naga, nor a gandharva, nor a mountain. I am not a creature born of various wombs, nor am I born of the eight coverings. I am the form of Brahman, the source of bliss and the giver of joy. I have come here to destroy you and to establish the sages and gods. O wicked leader of the Daityas, I will surely destroy you. Your merit is currently waning due to your accumulation of sins, and I have arrived as the dispenser of the fruits of your various sins. Know that I am the Lord who can make the impossible possible and change the unchangeable; tell me, Matsara, what will you do now? If you wish to live, O wise one, then take refuge in me. Abandon your hatred of dharma and live happily in your own domain. If you abandon your hatred for the gods and others, I will not kill you; know this to be my resolve.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Vakratunda, Matsara was astonished. With a pleased heart and restrained by devotion, he spoke to him. Matsara said: O Vakratunda, salutations to you. O Lord of the Ganas, please tell me everything and remove the doubt that resides in my heart. If you are not with attributes (saguna) and not without attributes (nirguna), and you are the form of Brahman, then how do you possess this form? Furthermore, O noble one, you are established in the state of Brahman; how have you taken refuge in a single form that treats gods and asuras differently? You are the destroyer of asuras and the protector of gods; O Lord of Obstacles, please remove this doubt of mine.

Hearing the words of Matsara, the glorious Vakratunda, seeing the state of his heart, spoke to the great warrior. Vakratunda said: Listen, Matsara, to what you have asked, O wise one. I will tell you everything to dispel your doubts. The entire universe, moving and non-moving, has been created by me in the form of Brahman for the sake of play, each endowed with its own dharma. It is sustained and withdrawn by me through Purusha and Prakriti, while I reside in their hearts as the Chintamani. I am the mover of all mental faculties, without a doubt. Everything exists under my command, O best of Daityas. When the gods abandon their own dharma and strive to destroy the Daityas, they become the destroyers of their own roots. At that time, I reside in the hearts of the Daityas and inspire them to perform penance. The Daityas perform penance with the desire to defeat the gods. By the power of their penance and the boons they receive, they defeat the gods, and the gods become powerless. Then, I become the opposite of the gods and become the giver of success to the asuras, O great asura. If the wicked Daityas abandon their dharma and wish to destroy the gods, then I reside in their hearts in the form of a god and reveal their weaknesses to facilitate their destruction. Through that path, the gods defeat the enemies of the gods. Matsara, I have told you my entire character. If the gods decide in their hearts that they must create obstacles in the penance of the Daityas, then I become the giver of boons to the Daityas to humble the pride of the gods, O leader of the asuras. If the Daityas become difficult to overcome by the gods, then I take a form for the destruction of the Daityas. When both Daityas and gods are devoted to their own dharma, then I reside in their hearts in the form of Brahman. You have oppressed the gods by abandoning your natural dharma; therefore, I have come to kill you, O wise King of Daityas.

Having said this, the Lord of Obstacles revealed his true form, which was beautiful and established in the singular point of Brahman. By the grace of Vakratunda and the result of that vision, the King of Daityas attained divine sight. With that sight, the Daitya saw the wonderful form of Ganesha, the form of Brahman. It was neither less nor more, neither equal to the self nor different. It was the Brahman that is four-footed yet naturally footless. There is no body and no embodied soul here, neither with attributes nor without. The elephant-faced Vakratunda appeared neither as maya nor as illusory. The body was with attributes, but the head was without attributes; the elephant face was seen as distinct due to its power. Vakratunda, the glorious one, is the bridge between the two, and the Daitya king recognized him as being endowed with limbs. Then, with a heart filled with devotion, he bowed down and praised him with folded hands.

Matsara said: I bow to Vakratunda, who is four-footed and beyond the four, who is without the four bodies and established in a mere point. I bow to the Lord who is both with form and without form, who wears the form of maya, and who is the master of maya yet devoid of it. I bow to the Lord of the Ganas, who is the universe yet beyond it, the fulfiller of all desires, and the giver of the state of Brahman. I bow to the destroyer of obstacles, who is mounted on a lion, four-armed, the lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, and the giver of Siddhi and Buddhi. I bow to him who is endowed with infinite play yet is beyond play, who is unmanifest yet manifest, and who resides in my own heart. I bow to the elephant-faced one, who is known by Vedanta, who is the light and the illuminator of lights, and who is the embodiment of Truth, Consciousness, and Bliss. I bow to the God who resides in the hearts of yogis, who gives yoga to yogis, who is the embodiment of existence and non-existence, and who is devoid of worldly existence. I bow to the Lord named Vakratunda, the Brahman and the Lord of Brahman, who is without sides yet holds all sides. Oh, what good fortune, what good fortune, that I have seen the elephant-faced one! This Vakratunda is beyond all forms and formlessness. I have taken refuge in you; protect me, O lover of devotees. I am always devoted to your feet, seeking liberation from this world.

Having praised Vakratunda in this way, he fell at his feet. Again and again, he bowed and stood with folded hands. Seeing the Daitya filled with such devotion, the elephant-faced Lord, pleased by his sentiment, spoke to the noble one. Vakratunda said: Matsara, I was indeed angry to destroy you, but I do not wish to destroy those who have taken refuge in me. Choose your boon. This hymn composed by you will increase my pleasure and will grant great prosperity, longevity, health, wealth, and grain. It will grant sons, grandsons, wives, and various other riches, and will be the giver of dharma, wealth, desire, and liberation.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Vakratunda, Matsara, filled with devotion, spoke to Vakratunda with folded hands. Matsara said: If you are pleased, O Lord of all, and if a boon is to be granted to me, then grant me firm devotion to you, O Lord of Obstacles. Grant me another boon: may your devotees be dear to me. Also, grant me a place and a livelihood that provides for my well-being, O Lord. Hearing the words of Matsara, Vakratunda said to him: From today, you will have unwavering devotion to me. Protect those creatures who are devoted to their own dharma, and carefully protect my excellent devotees. Wherever my image is worshipped or my name is remembered, do not create obstacles in those beginnings, O King of Daityas. In other matters, in the performance of work, knowledge, and other things, act according to your asuric nature, and you shall receive the fruits. Reside in their hearts and rule, O wise one, but in matters of devotion to me, always remain like a servant. Being told this, the great Daitya said "So be it" to him, and bowed to Ganesha with a joyful heart.

Chapter 39 : The Disappearance of Vakratunda

Mudgala said: Seeing Matsara (the demon of jealousy) having attained peace, the gods and sages were filled with joy and praised the Elephant-faced Lord.

The gods and sages said: We bow to and always worship the One who is always in the state of Brahman, devoid of changes, the source of all changes, and worthy of being worshipped by the great gods like Shiva. We bow to and always worship the One who is of infinite form, known only to the self, and the unique Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is unborn, without alternative, devoid of time and arts, residing in the heart, the eternal witness, and the Supreme Lord. We bow to and always worship the One who is the cause of knowledge for the people, devoid of external light, and the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is of infinite form, the root of eternal bliss, the embodiment of light, and omnipresent. We bow to and always worship the One who is beginningless, the source of qualities, and the foundation of all qualities, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the essence of earth, air, fire, water, and space, residing in the great elements. We bow to and always worship the One who is the holder of ego, residing in the quality of darkness, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the light of the sun, life-force, Vishnu, Varuna, Yama, Shiva, Brahma, the Ashvins, and Agni. We bow to and always worship the One who is the guide of the directions and the supreme form of all gods, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the light in the organs of generation, skin, speech, and eyes, the form of hands and feet, and the creator of the nose and tongue. We bow to and always worship the One who resides in the anus and the ears, the light of the great void, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is Brahma in the creation of the form of passion, Vishnu in the preservation of the form of goodness, and Shiva in the destruction of the form of darkness, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the lord of the directions, the form of all hopes, the light of the planets, the pole star, and the dweller in the sky. We bow to and always worship the One who is the infinite stars, yet formless, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the form of the great principle (Mahat), the primordial nature, the holder of ego, and the cause of the knowledge of the three Vedas. We bow to and always worship the One who is beginningless and endless, the base and the tail of Maya, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the holder of actions, the giver of heaven through fruits, and the giver of liberation through the light of non-action. We bow to and always worship the One who is the foundation of suffering through bad actions, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is devoid of greed, the holder of greed, the cause of knowledge for people, and the protector of the lords of men. We bow to and always worship the One who gives success to men, who is human and resides in humans, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the form of creepers and trees, the form of birds, the light of wealth, and the form of grain. We bow to and always worship the One who is the various forms of progeny, sons, and grandsons, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the lord of birds, residing in the bull and other vehicles, the knower of lions and the form of the lion. We bow to and always worship the One who is the support of the earth, the Golden Mountain (Meru), the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who resides in gold and other metals, the essence of colors, the form of oceans and clouds, and the dweller in water. We bow to and always worship the One who is the various forms of aquatic creatures like fish, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is the form of Shesha and other serpents, adorned with serpents, and performing divine play through them. We bow to and always worship the One who is the form of the enemies of the gods and the demons, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who holds the boon and the noose, the protector of devotees, the great person, the master of Maya, and the one who resides on a lion. We bow to and always worship the One who has four arms and is the destroyer of obstacles, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who is Ganesha, worshipped by Ganesha and others, the lord of gods, the supreme, worshipped by all, and attainable through true knowledge. We bow to and always worship the One who is known through the great Vedantic sentences and is the Supreme Lord, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One who protects through infinite incarnations, makes people established in their own duties, and is the one worshipped by gods and demons alike, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One by whom this great demon king was destroyed, who established great peace in the world, and who is filled with unbroken joy, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One whom the Vedas and those who know the Vedas do not find, whom the scriptures and those who know the scriptures do not find, and whom the yogis and masters of yoga do not find, the Vakratunda. We bow to and always worship the One whom even the Vedas and the king of gods do not know, and whom we, the sages, cannot praise through yoga. Still, we praise the Vakratunda with our own intellect, the Vakratunda.

Mudgala said: Having praised Vakratunda in this way, the gods and sages stood with folded hands in silence, O giver of honor. Hearing the praise of the gods and sages, the Elephant-faced Lord, being pleased, spoke these great words to them, bringing them joy.

Vakratunda said: Listen, all you sages and gods, to my great words. This hymn composed by you will increase my pleasure. Whoever praises me daily with this, I will fulfill their desires, including various types of enjoyments and the wealth of sons and grandsons. Whatever they desire, I will grant them; there is no doubt in this. This hymn grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, and leads to the state of Brahman. It grants various successes and illuminates the intellect in the heart. Whoever praises me with this attains the status of being worshipped by the lords of gods. By reciting this a thousand times, a man who is in prison will undoubtedly be released, and he will always be dear to me. A person who is full of hundreds of sins will have their sins forgiven by the recitation of this hymn. Whoever reads this twenty-one times for twenty-one days, I will grant them what they have contemplated. You have composed twenty-one verses with devotion to me; by those, I am praised by all the gods and sages. Through this, you will become free from obstacles, fond of your own duties, and will become great souls who grant worldly enjoyment and liberation. Ask for a boon, O great souls, whatever is in your minds. I will grant it without doubt, as I am pleased by this hymn.

Mudgala said: Having been spoken to in this way, all the gods and sages bowed to Him and, with joyful hearts, spoke to the mighty Vakratunda.

The gods and sages said: If you are pleased and wish to grant a boon, O Vakratunda, then grant us your unwavering devotion, O Lord, O ocean of mercy. If a creature attains your devotion, O Lord, then what is difficult for them? All successes are in their hands. Therefore, O great one, grant us devotion, O Lord of obstacles. By that, we will be satisfied, being devoted to your feet.

Hearing their words, the Lord of gods, the Elephant-faced one, who is dear to the wise, spoke with deep meaning.

Vakratunda said: My unwavering devotion will be yours, there is no doubt, O gods and sages who are filled with devotion. Having said this, the Lord of Ganas, Vakratunda, disappeared, and while the sages and gods watched, he remained in his own bliss. Then all the gods and sages, feeling sad, went to their respective abodes, meditating on that very Vakratunda, the giver of success. Matsara, surrounded by all the demons, also went to his own abode, quickly protecting it, and became peaceful forever. Leaving him, the groups of demons went to their own homes, O sage. Thus, the greatness of Matsara was brought about by Vakratunda. That same Vakratunda creates, preserves, and destroys, being devoid of feelings, and he himself is the beginning, middle, and end. Thus, through various incarnations, Vakratunda makes the gods, demons, and humans devoted to their own duties. The incarnations of Vakratunda are infinite, O Lord; it is not possible to describe all their stories. Even Brahma, the six-faced Kartikeya, Vishnu, Shankara, Shesha, the sages, and the Vedas cannot do so, so what can I say? For the sake of Divodasa's peace, that same Vakratunda became the son of Shiva, known by the name Dhundhiraja. To grant a boon to Vamana, that same Lord of Ganas, the protector of devotees, appeared before him with great brilliance. Thus, through various incarnations, he is eager to protect his devotees. Know this, O creator, that he performs the protection of his devotees. When Brahma, troubled by the work of creation, did not remember him, the demon Matsara entered him. Troubled by that demonic nature, Brahma became filled with obstacles and was unable to perform creation. Remembering the ancient words, he again took refuge in Him. Brahma, becoming free from obstacles, created the entire world.

Thus ends the thirty-ninth chapter named "The Disappearance of Vakratunda" in the first section of the Vakratunda-charita in the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 40 : Brahma's Performance of Penance

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: I am blessed and favored that you have narrated to me the great story of Vakratunda, which leads to the realization of Brahman. I never feel satisfied hearing it, just as one never tires of nectar. I wish to hear again how He was worshipped according to the scriptures. Please tell me in detail this story that destroys sins and grants all accomplishments to the listener, the inquirer, and the narrator.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Daksha, Mudgala, the lord of the Ganas, spoke to him with joyful, wide-open eyes.

Mudgala said: Listen, O fortunate Daksha, you are truly blessed, for you have such wonderful love for the story of the Lord of the Ganas. Those with little merit never develop such love for hearing or understanding the story of Dhundhi. Therefore, pleased by your devotion, I shall briefly tell you the divine story of the great-souled Ganesha.

At the beginning of the dissolution, Ganesha created five lords of the gods. Pleased by their penance, He became the bestower of boons. By the power of those boons, they became capable and engaged in their respective duties with focused minds. When Brahma began the work of creation, he forgot to worship Ganesha at the start. Consequently, he became immediately deluded and filled with pride in his mind, thinking, "There is no creator equal to me in the three worlds; there is no doubt about this. I create the entire universe, Vishnu protects it, Rudra destroys it, and Shakti creates illusion. The Sun god, the lord, reveals the actions in the world. If I do not create the world, then divinity itself is meaningless. Since Ganesha performed creation at my command in the beginning, and seeing my capability, there is no one equal to me."

Deluded by this pride, the Grandfather (Brahma) was soon beset by numerous obstacles. These fierce, multi-formed, and powerful beings attacked Brahma as he was creating the world. Some had three eyes, five eyes, or eyes on their backs; others were immensely strong, possessing ten faces or even a thousand. Thus, these powerful beings, assuming various forms, tormented Brahma, treating him like a child's toy.

Helpless, Brahma abandoned all his pride and took refuge in the Lord, Ganesha, the remover of obstacles. Having lost his arrogance, he meditated upon the Lord of the Ganas in his heart and praised Him. Upon this, all the obstacles vanished. Freed from them, Brahma performed supreme penance. He pleased Vakratunda with the six-syllable mantra. Brahma lived on air, standing on the tips of his toes, and focused his mind steadily on Ganesha through his spiritual power.

For a thousand divine years, he performed this severe penance, causing all beings to become distressed. Then, pleased by his intense penance, Vakratunda appeared before his great devotee, satisfied by his devotion. He was the Lord, possessing a radiant form, a great body, four arms, riding a lion, three-eyed, and holding a noose and a goad. He had a broad chest, a large belly, was accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, adorned with the Chintamani jewel, decorated with various ornaments, having a serpent as his navel, an elephant face, a body red like vermilion, and wearing various garments.

Seeing Him, the Creator was filled with awe and praised the Supreme Lord Ganesha, meditating upon Him in his heart with various hymns. Vakratunda, hearing his praise, spoke with a voice like thunder, "Ask for a boon, O Creator; I shall grant it, there is no doubt." Hearing these words, Brahma bowed to Him and said with a gentle demeanor, "O Elephant-faced Lord, please grant me a boon."

Hearing Brahma's request, the gentle and radiant Elephant-faced Lord, satisfied by his devotion, spoke to the fortunate one.

Vakratunda said: I reside in the city of Swananda in this very form. How will you, living in the mortal world, see me, O giver of honor? O Creator, I am pleased by your penance and devotion; that is why I have appeared before you in this gentle form. You may ask for the boons you desire from me, and I shall grant them. When I am pleased, nothing is unattainable.

Hearing the words of Ganesha, Brahma bowed to Him again, and with folded hands, began to praise Him.

Thus ends the fortieth chapter of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "Brahma's Performance of Penance" in the story of Vakratunda.

Chapter 41 : The Coronation of Dambhasura

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Brahma said: Salutations to You, O Vakratunda, the great-souled Ganesha. Salutations again and again to You, who are infinite, boundless, and the remover of obstacles. Salutations to You, who are fond of devotion, the protector of devotees, the destroyer of the desires of the non-devout, and the eternal giver of all. Salutations to You, who are attributeless, formless, pure, the embodiment of virtues, the possessor of infinite faces, and the rider of the lion. Salutations to You, the beginningless one, the beloved of all, whose body is known through Vedanta, and who possesses the face of an elephant. Salutations to You, who reside in the infinite universe, who grant various enjoyments, who are the support of Maya, and who bewilder Maya itself. Salutations to You, who possess immeasurable power, who are the supportless one, the tusked one, the four-armed one, and the Lord of self-bliss. Salutations to You, the giver of success and wisdom, the possessor of success and wisdom, the divine form of Brahman, and the destroyer of obstacles.

O sinless Vakratunda, be pleased and grant me Your unwavering devotion, through which there is no bondage. By Your command, I have begun the work of creation; grant me the wonderful strength to perform it, and may it always be free from obstacles. O Lord of the Ganas, since You have told me that I am the creator of all, please reside in my home in the form of my son through Your Maya. If You, who are the direct embodiment of Brahman, become my son, then I shall be liberated from the bondage of Rajas. If You, O Lord, were to be bound by the roles of mother and father, then the Vedas and everything else would become false. By merely uttering Your name, You grant the state of Brahman; what more if You reside in my home as a son? I shall shower You with affection, serve You, and worship You; by constantly beholding You, I shall become incomparable.

Hearing these words of Brahma, Ganapati, who knows the essence of all things, spoke to the great-souled one, bringing him joy. Vakratunda said: O Brahmin, whatever you have prayed for, let it all be so. Your wonderful power to create will be effective. By remembering Me, all your tasks will be accomplished without obstacles. Whatever you desire, you will always attain. I shall become your son, a savior through a portion of My Maya, and I will fulfill your desires, pleased by your devotion. This hymn you have composed will be all-granting, pleasing to Me, and will bestow the state of Brahman.

Having said this, the powerful Vakratunda vanished. Brahma, feeling somewhat dejected, remained there. By the grace of Vakratunda, he created the entire world, both animate and inanimate, as was appropriate. Having created the three worlds, he felt accomplished and remained absorbed in his own bliss, constantly remembering Vakratunda. Once, when the Creator was shaken by the wind, a being named Dambha manifested from him in human form. Before him stood a fierce, four-armed man, holding a mace, discus, trident, bow, and arrows. He was full of radiance and vigor, incomparable, massive, and stood like a mountain.

Seeing him, the astonished Brahma asked, "Who are you? Where have you come from, and what is your purpose here? Tell me, O mighty one, for you are blessed and beautiful." Hearing Brahma’s words, the man smiled and spoke with a voice as deep as a cloud. The man said: O Creator, I know you, and you should know me as having been born from the trembling of your body. Give me a suitable name, for I am your son. Grant me a place and a means of livelihood.

Hearing his words, the lotus-born Brahma smiled and spoke with respect. Brahma said: "Why do you pretend, my son? You speak with arrogance (Dambha). Therefore, you shall be known as Dambha, the great wicked one. Wherever you go, O great demon, you shall enjoy various pleasures as you please." Having been told this, the mighty one bowed to the Creator, circumambulated him, and went to the earth. There, the wicked one thought, "I have been insulted by Brahma; I shall humble his pride."

Having decided this, Dambha sought refuge in Shukracharya. Following the mantra taught by him, he pleased Brahma. Standing on one leg and abstaining from food, he performed severe penance for ten thousand years. Due to his penance, the entire world became agitated and fearful. Seeing this, the astonished Brahma, along with the gods, came to grant him a boon. Seeing him standing like a log, the lotus-seated one spoke to him, wishing to please him.

Brahma said: "Ask for any boon you desire, O great one. I will fulfill it; have no doubt." Hearing Brahma, Dambha was overjoyed. He bowed and praised him with folded hands. Dambha said: "Salutations to You, the creator, sustainer, and destroyer of the world, the three-fold form of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva. Everything is pervaded by You; You are the support of all. If You sleep, the world will surely perish. You play as the animate and inanimate; You exist as the one and the many. Who can praise You, who exist in the form of Brahma? It is my great fortune to have Your vision today, which destroys all sins. If You are pleased, grant me my desires: give me the kingdom of the three worlds, and may I never die from the five elements. In battle, let no one be my equal. Grant me unbroken prosperity and health."

Hearing his words, Brahma, pleased by his penance, replied: "Whatever you have asked for, Dambha, shall come to pass. Whatever else you have thought of will also be accomplished." Having said this, Brahma vanished. Dambha was overjoyed and returned to his home. He built a beautiful city, the most magnificent on earth, and made it his residence. Then, all the demons, urged by Shukra, arrived at Dambha's city, filled with joy. Shukra also arrived with the sages, ready to perform the coronation of Dambha as the king of the demons. At an auspicious time, the Brahmins performed the coronation, and Dambha became the king of the demons. Everyone celebrated with great joy, and the city, filled with happy and prosperous people, shone brilliantly. Then, all returned to their homes, while the chief demons remained there.

Chapter 42 : The Victory of Dambhasura

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: O Prajapati, for some time, all the people in the city of the demon Dambha lived in great prosperity and festivity. Once, all the powerful demon leaders, including Suhundu, Hundu, the mighty Rukmaketu, the drunkard and wrathful Krodhasura, the powerful Kamasura, and Rasabhasura, along with many others, approached the magnificent Dambha. After bowing to him, they expressed their eagerness to destroy the gods.

The demon lords said: Why do you remain idle, O Great King? We are your servants and have arrived to conquer the three worlds, as we are eager to crush the gods. All demons and ogres are under your command, and there is no one in the universe equal to you in strength, glory, or brilliance, O Lord of demons. Command us, O mighty-armed one, and we shall conquer the entire world.

Hearing the words of the demon lords, Dambhasura was delighted and spoke to them with great enthusiasm. Dambhasura said: You have spoken well, O noble ones, and this is also what is in my heart. Together with you, I shall conquer the entire world, both animate and inanimate.

Having said this, the demon king went to Shukracharya, sought his counsel, and then departed from the city. Accompanied by an infinite army consisting of four divisions, the demons defeated all the kings on earth. After conquering all the Nagas in the netherworlds, they ascended to heaven. There, Indra, along with the leaders of the gods, fought against the demons. The demon hosts were terrified by Indra’s fierce thunderbolts, which crushed and scorched them. Fleeing the battlefield, the demons retreated, but then Dambhasura, proud of his strength, entered the fray. With a strike of his discus, he rendered Indra unconscious and caused all the gods to be wounded by his arrows.

Regaining consciousness, Indra fled to save his life, followed by the terrified gods. Dambhasura, riding on Airavata, sat upon Indra’s throne. He entered the city of the immortals, rejoicing and bringing joy to his own followers. Indra, along with all the gods, sought refuge with Brahma. Together with Brahma, they went to Vishnu, and then all of them proceeded to Shiva.

Shiva, filled with rage, arrived at the battlefield and challenged the mighty demon king to combat. Dambhasura arrived, his eyes reddened with anger, fully armed and accompanied by his forces. A fierce and hair-raising battle ensued, with mutual destruction between the demons and the gods. For forty days, the battle raged with intense ferocity, creating great rivers of blood filled with ghosts.

Then, Dambha, in a fit of rage, struck the gods with his arrows, rendering the king of the gods unconscious and casting him to the ground. Shiva struck the mighty demon with his trident, causing him to faint, but the demon rose again in an instant. The powerful Dambhasura then hurled his discus at Shiva, leaving him unconscious for half a watch. Seeing the extraordinary prowess of the demon king, Shiva, upon regaining his senses, fled to the forest along with the gods.

Seeing the gods fleeing, the demon leaders laughed and joyfully entered Amaravati. Dambha then carefully installed his demon leaders in Satyaloka, Vaikuntha, Kailasa, and Amaravati. He placed the demons in all the beautiful cities of the gods and returned to his own magnificent city, accompanied by his forces.

The mighty and intoxicated demon king ruled over the three worlds. He then ordered his demon commanders to put an end to all religious duties. Following his command, the fierce demons bound the Brahmins and struck them with great force. These villains destroyed the sacrificial altars and sacred trees, and they desecrated the holy pilgrimage sites and temples. In this way, through various efforts, they caused the cessation of all religious rites. Some Brahmins were ruined, while others died. Some fled to forests filled with lions and tigers, and there, the Brahmins lived without performing their daily sandhya prayers.

With Dambha ruling, there was no Swaha, no Swadha, no Vashatkara, and no observance of the Varna and Ashrama duties. The people on earth became of mixed caste, and all the gods were filled with terror. O Shaunaka, since the gods subsist on the offerings from these rites, with the cessation of rituals, they became like the dead and spoke to Brahma.

The gods said: O Lotus-born, to whom shall we go for refuge except you? You are the creator, the sustainer, and the life-giver of all. You are the destination of all the gods, and you are the embodiment of Brahman. Therefore, O Lord of gods, protect us from this supreme fear of Dambhasura. He has made the three worlds devoid of religious duty. O wise one, you created the gods to subsist on these rites. Without these rituals, we shall surely perish. Therefore, O Great Grandfather, protect all the gods. You have given us protection and sustained us; now, afflicted by hunger, we shall perish before your very eyes.

Chapter 43 : The Dialogue between Dambhasura and the Messenger

Mudgala said: Hearing the cruel words of the King of Gods, the four-faced Brahma became anxious and began to contemplate a noble solution. The master of wisdom could not find a way to protect the gods, and thus the lotus-seated creator fell into deep distress. With fear, he spoke to the leaders of the gods, saying, "I and all of you must pray to Gajanana. He is the knower of all solutions and will surely bring about great auspiciousness. Therefore, through the skill of meditation, let us meditate upon Vinayaka. By the method of the single-syllable mantra, let us worship Vakratunda; there is no doubt that he will become the refuge of all the gods."

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Brahma, the gods, along with the sages, worshipped Vakratunda according to the prescribed rites without distraction. Brahma, too, absorbed in meditation, pleased the remover of obstacles. O sage, he saw him in his heart, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, as he had seen him in the past. After a hundred years had passed, Brahma perceived him in his heart, endowed with form and qualities, speaking in sweet tones. Astonished, as he was about to ask the Lord, the Lord himself appeared before him, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. The mighty Vakratunda enlightened Brahma, having become highly pleased by his meditative devotion.

Vakratunda said: "O Grandfather, look upon me as your son, the Lord of the Ganas. Because I was born from your meditation, they call me 'Dhyanaja' (born of meditation). In the past, you respectfully asked me to be your son through your penance, and I have now fulfilled that request." Hearing the words of Vakratunda, Brahma was filled with joy. Seeing the Lord of Ganesha before him, adorned with Siddhi and Buddhi, he bowed down with his whole body to Vakratunda Gajanana and worshipped Siddhi and Buddhi with great effort. Seeing this, all the gods and noble sages arrived and bowed with devotion. Seeing his mental son and his daughters-in-law, Siddhi and Buddhi, they stood humbly with folded hands and praised him. Brahma, along with all the others, prepared to praise Vakratunda and his consorts.

The gods and sages said: Salutations to you, who are the embodiment of Brahman, the witness Vakratunda. Salutations again and again to Ganesha, who is accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. Salutations to you, the Lord of Obstacles, who are both without qualities and the embodiment of qualities. Salutations to you, the beginningless, the all-knowing protector. Salutations to you, the embodiment of all, the overseer of all, the wise one, the one without beginning, middle, or end, the manifest God. Salutations to you, who possess immeasurable power, who bestow delusion upon those caught in illusion, and who are the support of Maya, yet untouched by it. Salutations to you, who are Truth, the embodiment of Truth, the protector of Truth, the giver of knowledge, and the one attained through knowledge. Salutations to the pot-bellied God, the Lord of the Ganas, the foundation of qualities, and Heramba. Even the Vedas and scriptures are unable to praise you; what am I, O Gajanana? O Lord of the Ganas, I have praised you to the best of my ability, and through this, my life has become fulfilled. You are undoubtedly the creator of infinite universes; how is it that you have become my son, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi? You, whom I meditated upon in my heart, have appeared before me to save me, there is no doubt.

Having said this, Brahma himself, filled with supreme joy, performed the birth rites and other ceremonies for him along with the Brahmins. On the eleventh day, the Grandfather, along with the Brahmins, established his name as the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi. Vinayaka played like a child, and Savitri lovingly breastfed him. Day by day, the child grew like the waxing moon, bringing joy to his mother and father with his deeds. When he was two years old, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, he asked the Creator with humility: "Father, why do you live in the forest with the sages? Why do you seem so distressed by the gods, O Lord?"

Hearing his words, Brahma was filled with joy and told the Lord of the Ganas the entire story of the demon's origin. Hearing this, the wrathful Vakratunda spoke, his voice deep as a cloud, gladdening the gods and sages: "I will surely slay the mighty Dambhasura. I will restore the positions of the gods and the Brahmins." Having said this, the Lord, armed with weapons and accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, mounted a lion and went to the demon's abode. The Lord of the world stood at the edge of the city, and all the gods and sages followed him with joy. Ganesha quickly sent Indra as a messenger, saying, "Abide by your own duty, O King of Demons, or else I will surely slay you."

Indra went to the great demon and spoke these words of conciliation, knowing the nature of the enemy: "Know that the eternal Brahman has taken the form of Brahma's son, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, born from meditation. He has arrived with the sages and gods near your city and has sent me as a messenger to seek peace. Abide by your duty, let the gods receive their offerings, and let the people of the four castes live without fear." Hearing Indra's words, the demon became extremely angry and spoke as if burning Indra with his gaze: "Why have you come to my house in the form of a messenger? If you do not leave, I will bind you and throw you into prison. Go, Indra, and tell that leader of the Ganas that I will fight you on the fifth day, there is no doubt." Indra returned to the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi and recounted the entire event in detail. Hearing this, the pleased Ganesha said to the wise Indra, "You have done well, O King of Gods."

Chapter 44 : The Deliberations of Dambhasura

Mudgala said: When Devendra departed from the presence of Ganesha, Dambhasura summoned the sage Shukra to his side. Upon the arrival of the highly intelligent Shukra, the lord of the demons bowed down with his entire body and stood before him with folded hands. Shukra seated the demon on a golden throne and asked him why he had been summoned. Hearing the words of Shukra, the demon, filled with both fear and excitement, spoke to the great sage with a heart full of devotion.

Dambhasura said: Master, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the mind-born son of Brahma, has arrived to wage war, having been created through penance. I ask you, O best of sages and knower of all, so that I may truly understand him in every aspect. Who are Siddhi and Buddhi, and who is their Lord? What power does he possess? Please tell me everything.

Hearing the words of Dambha, Shukra spoke again, explaining the truth by which one gains wisdom regarding Ganesha.

Shukra said: Listen, O King of Demons, to his wondrous glory, by which you shall become proficient in the knowledge of Ganesha. Siddhi is the deluding Maya seen everywhere, for which all people in the world strive. In this world, the demon desires Siddhi for the sake of enjoyment, and people are anxious to attain it for the sake of the next world. One desires the attainment of the state of Brahman through the form of Siddhi; thus, the attainment of Brahman in this world is threefold, known as delusion. This Maya is called the Ganesha's, O wise one, and it is in the form of illusion. The elephant-faced Lord plays with the Goddess who resides on his left side, deluding Brahma, Vishnu, and the sages. Being the embodiment of Brahman, he is considered ever independent.

The second Maya of that great soul is in the form of Buddhi. Listen to this, O great demon, so that you may become all-knowing. Whatever is seen in this world is composed of mind and speech; that which is devoid of mind and speech is the manifestation of Buddhi. The mind is said to be fivefold—scattered, foolish, distracted, focused, and restrained—and it is the same as Buddhi. These five have many divisions, and it is impossible to describe them all. Thus, though one, this Buddhi is seen everywhere. Know that this Buddhi, which is the form of knowledge residing in the heart, is the very form of Brahman. By a person endowed with such Buddhi, one attains the beyond and the here; through Buddhi, the wise are united with the yoga of Brahman.

O demon, such is the auspicious Goddess who resides on the right side of that great soul, the Lord of Yoga, Ganesha. Know that this Maya was created by him from his own body for the sake of play, and through yoga, a man attains that state of Brahman. The supreme Brahman, known as Svananda, is considered the city, and he who is endowed with Siddhi and Buddhi dwells there eternally. That Ganesha, the son of Brahma, has come here because he was worshipped by Brahma for the sake of having a son. Because he removes the bonds of mother, father, and others, he was prayed to by Brahma to take the form of a son. He will certainly kill you to protect Dharma; therefore, if you wish to live, seek his refuge.

Mudgala said: Having heard the words of the great sage Shukra, which were full of the essence of Vedanta and bestowed wisdom, Dambha spoke.

Dambhasura said: Master, I have heard your words, which are filled with yoga. However, please dispel the doubt that resides in my heart. If Ganesha is indeed the supreme Brahman, then there is no doubt that both Dharma and Adharma have originated from him. If Ganesha exists everywhere in the form of yoga, why does he act with bias to protect Dharma and destroy Adharma? Please dispel this doubt of mine.

Shukra said: Listen, Dambha, to the supreme deeds of Ganesha, by which all your doubts will be destroyed instantly. Ganesha created this world, which is full of various forms, and established all beings in their respective positions. He created the system of Dharma and Adharma. When Adharma overcomes Dharma due to greed, the power of the gods diminishes, and the power of the demons grows day by day. When this Lord of the Ganas, who promotes the side of the gods, prepares himself to destroy the demons, he kills the great strength of the demons along with the gods and restores Dharma to its place with all effort. When the power of Adharma is destroyed by Dharma, the root of the demons will be severed. If the power of the gods becomes wondrously great, then the demon becomes a bestower of boons and takes many forms. When the demons, endowed with boons, cause Dharma to decline, he restores the state of Adharma as it was before. If the gods and demons become filled with greed, Ganesha strives for their destruction. In his view, there is no "self" or "other"; he merely places people in their roles for the sake of his own play.

Mudgala said: Having heard the beautiful words of Shukra, the powerful Dambha remained in his city, meditating on Ganesha in his mind. In solitude, the highly radiant demon pondered: "This Ganesha is the complete, eternal Brahman. By seeking his refuge, one becomes successful. There is no doubt that he exists equally in all. If a foolish person desires Siddhi while abandoning the Lord of Siddhi, he will surely face failure. If one desires well-being while abandoning Ganesha, the Lord of Buddhi, he will face misfortune due to his evil nature. I see nothing devoid of Siddhi and Buddhi. Therefore, I shall seek the refuge of that Lord who is equal to both gods and demons." Having made this firm resolution, Dambha slept fearlessly, having placed Ganesha in his heart with a spirit of devotion.

Chapter 45 : The Pacification of Dambhasura, the Forty-Fifth Chapter

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Listen, O Daksha, you who are highly wise, to the great skill of Dambha. Having abandoned his demonic nature, he became free from all distress. Rising early in the morning, he summoned all the demons himself and, in the presence of Shukracharya, spoke these words with great brilliance.

Dambhasura said: Listen, all you demons, I have been enlightened by our Guru. I am taking refuge in Vakratunda; there is no doubt about this. He is not merely the Lord of the gods, nor is he merely the Lord of the demons. He exists everywhere in the form of Brahman, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi. Hearing these words of his, the wicked demons became angry and left for their respective homes, abandoning Dambhasura.

Dambhasura, with a joyful heart and accompanied by Shukracharya, went to take refuge in the Lord of the Ganas, filled with delight. Emerging from the city, he approached the presence of the Lord. He fell prostrate on the ground like a staff and stood before Him with folded hands. He praised the Lord of the Ganas, celebrating the festival of His vision, and after worshipping Him according to the rites, he stood with his palms joined.

Dambhasura said: Salutations to You, who are in the form of Brahman, whose body is the manifestation of Brahman, who are Brahman Yourself, and the bestower of Brahman; salutations again and again to Ganesha. Salutations to You, the Lord of the thirty-three gods, the protector of the Daityas and Danavas. Salutations to You, who are the form of Yoga everywhere, and who are beyond all emotional states. Salutations to You, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the one with the lion on Your banner. Salutations again and again to You, the Lord of the Ganas, the Heramba. Salutations to You, the one-tusked God, the one of infinite glory, the Lord of obstacles, the destroyer of great obstacles. Salutations to You, the ocean of infinite virtues, the crusher of Daityas and Danavas, who are the embodiment of mind and speech, and who are the form of all. Salutations to You, who are beyond mind and speech, the bestower of Yoga upon the Yogis, the Lord of Yoga, and the protector of the universe. Salutations to You, the holder of Maya and the mover of Maya. Salutations to You, who are devoid of Maya and who maintain equanimity everywhere. Salutations to You, the unmanifest, the root cause of all manifestation. Salutations to You, O Lambodara, who are beyond delusion and the cause of delusion. How can I, who am insignificant, praise You, the Supreme beyond the Supreme, whom even the Vedas with their limbs, the great sages, and the great Yogis like Brahma and Vishnu are unable to praise?

Having said this, Dambha fell to the ground, filled with devotion. The Lord of the Ganas raised him up and spoke, moved by his devotion.

Ganesha said: O Dambha, the hymn you have composed has increased My satisfaction. Whoever reads it or causes it to be read shall attain all happiness. It shall grant sons, grandsons, wives, wealth, and grain. It destroys physical and mental ailments and all calamities. The person who reads it once, twice, or three times daily shall become extremely dear to Me. I had come with anger to destroy you, there is no doubt, but now that you have taken refuge in Me, I shall certainly not destroy you. Ask for a boon from Me, whatever is in your heart; I am satisfied by your hymn and devotion, and I grant it to you.

Hearing the words of Ganesha, Dambha was filled with joy and spoke to the Lord of the Ganas, his neck bowed with devotion.

Dambhasura said: O Lord of gods, if You are pleased and if a boon is to be granted to me, then grant me unwavering devotion to You; salutations to You, O Lord. Grant me Your command, O Lord, regarding my livelihood from now on. Give me a place, O Lord of the Ganas, where I may remain steadfast.

Hearing the words of Dambha, Ganesha, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, spoke to him with great pleasure and a deep, resonant voice.

The Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi said: Your devotion to Me shall be firm and unwavering. Remain in your own place, O Dambha, filled with joy. You are not inferior to Me in any way, nor are the gods considered superior. All who remain in their own Dharma are those who increase My joy. I am angry and destroy those who, through the fault of abandoning their own Dharma, stray. Therefore, O great demon, remain fearless for a long time. Wherever I am not remembered at the beginning of any task, O best of demons, there you shall always cause the failure of the work through the nature of 'Dambha' (pride/hypocrisy). Where there is no worship of Me at the beginning of a task, O demon, there you shall always make it fruitless by causing its downfall. Protect with effort those who are always dear to Me and filled with My devotion, O Dambha, by abandoning your base nature. Wherever I am not remembered or worshipped, O wise one, there I have assigned you a share in the form of the task.

Having said this, Ganesha vanished. The gods and sages, shouting "Victory," departed. They remained in their respective places, radiant and free from fear. The demons remained in the depths of Patala, free from anxiety, O Lord. Thus, the elephant-faced Lord pacified Dambhasura. That he attained the status of being a son of Brahma has also been stated by me. Whoever listens to this daily or causes others to listen with a focused mind becomes free from the fear of Dambha and attains the eternal Brahman. A human being attains whatever desire he contemplates and remains endowed with all prosperity, free from obstacles. Now, I shall briefly tell you the highly wonderful story of the Lord of the Ganas, which is filled with devotion, even regarding Vamana.

Thus ends the forty-fifth chapter, titled "The Pacification of Dambhasura," in the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 46 : The Granting of Boons to Vamana

Daksha said: Hearing the story of Ganesha fills my heart with joy. Therefore, O all-knowing one, please narrate the story of Vamana.

Mudgala said: Kashyapa, the best of sages, was like a Prajapati. Aditi was his eldest and most virtuous wife. From her, the gods, including Indra, were born to Kashyapa. The Daityas, who were also sons of the sage, stood in opposition to them. Proud of their strength and boons, they prepared to destroy the gods, but were destroyed by Vishnu, who assumed various forms. When they could not attain the kingdom of heaven, which is honored by all, they sought refuge with Shukracharya, the knower of all policies. Knowing their entire situation, Shukracharya, the best of the Veda-knowers, said to the Daityas:

Shukracharya said: O Bali and other leading demons, listen to my beneficial advice. The gods were born from yajnas (sacrifices), and thus the gods are sustained by yajnas. Therefore, perform one hundred Ashvamedha sacrifices. There is no doubt that you, Bali, will become the mighty Indra. By carefully concealing your enmity toward the gods, you will become the enjoyers of heaven and be honored everywhere. You will become highly fortunate, so follow my words. All of them were pleased and said, "Well said, well said."

Then, making King Bali their leader, the mighty demons respectfully and quickly initiated him into the Ashvamedha sacrifices. Bali, born of Virochana, was devoted to Vishnu and never had any interest in unrighteousness. Initially, the wicked demon, out of his devotion to Vishnu, began the first sacrifice while remembering Vishnu and ignoring Ganesha. Knowing his actions, Vighna (Ganesha) was greatly delighted and said, "In this assembly, Ganesha, the lord of the Ganas, has not been worshipped first. He who is the giver of worldly enjoyment and liberation, the lord who grants all success—those who abandon him and desire success will never succeed. What kind of success can the demon attain through the power of Vishnu? I will consume all the rituals performed in that sacrifice."

Having said this, Vighna himself, filled with anger, entered the heart of the king of the gods (Indra). The Brahmins had planned one hundred sacrifices for Bali. When the first sacrifice began, Indra became anxious. "Through the power of a hundred sacrifices, Bali will become Indra." Then, the lord of the gods went to seek refuge with Vishnu. Bowing and praising him, the radiant Indra respectfully prayed to Vishnu, the best of gods, for the destruction of Bali's sacrifice. Meanwhile, Vighna, assuming the form of Time, entered the heart of the powerful Vishnu. Knowing everything about Bali's actions from Indra, Vishnu quickly said:

Vishnu said: O Lord of the gods, Aditi and Kashyapa performed severe penance for my sake for a hundred years. At that time, I was pleased with them and became a boon-giver. You should go to them and ask to be born as their son. I have already promised them this, and I will now fulfill that boon by destroying Bali's sacrifice.

Having forgotten Bali's great devotion, Vishnu himself became ready to subdue him. That Lord Vishnu was born as Vamana to Aditi and Kashyapa. Seeing Bali's strength, he could not move him. Disturbed in his mind, Vishnu asked his father, Kashyapa, for a way to destroy Bali. He gave him the six-syllable mantra of Vakratunda. Then, Vamana went to Vidarbha and engaged in penance. For ten years, he performed severe, intense penance without food, meditating on Ganesha in his heart.

Due to his intense penance, the lion-mounted Ganesha, who is fond of his devotees, appeared before him, influenced by the six-syllable mantra. He was four-armed, complete, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, one-tusked, elephant-faced, holding a noose and a goad. His body was red like vermilion, he had winnowing-basket ears, a large belly, and shone with the brilliance of a crore of suns. Seeing him, Vamana stood up suddenly, bowed, and with folded hands, began to praise him.

Vamana said: Salutations to you, O Lord of obstacles, the destroyer of the obstacles of your devotees and the creator of obstacles for those who are not. Salutations to Ganesha, the Vakratunda, the protector of all, the indweller of all who assumes various forms. O Lord, all the moving and non-moving world is under your command; you perform everything effortlessly through your powers. You are the Brahma of the Prajapatis, the Vishnu of the sacrifices, the Shiva of the lords, and the Indra of the gods. You are the Sun among the luminous, the Moon among the food, Yama among those who have restraint, and Varuna among the lords of the waters. You are Vayu among the strong, the Lord of wealth among the treasures, Agni among the burners, and Nirriti among the demons. You are Shesha among the serpents, Shukra among the yogis, and undoubtedly Sanatkumara among the youths. You are Guha among the commanders and the lion among the beasts. Thus, you are intent on protecting the world in various forms. Who is capable of praising you, the eternal form of yoga? Even the Vedas, Shesha, and the Creator are unable to praise you. Who am I, with my dull intellect, to reach the end of your praise? Still, I have praised you as per my limited intelligence, O Lord of obstacles. By your mere sight, I am fulfilled. My birth is blessed, for I have seen the elephant-faced one. My mother and father are blessed, this place is blessed, and this penance is blessed. This six-syllable mantra is blessed, through which you have come.

Having said this, he danced, overwhelmed with devotion, his body covered in goosebumps, shedding tears of joy repeatedly. Abandoning body-consciousness, the highly fortunate Vamana became filled with joy and attained single-minded devotion. Seeing him thus overwhelmed with love, the Supreme Lord, the lord of the Ganas, who is fond of his devotees, said to Vamana:

Ganesha said: Listen to my words, Vamana, and ask for the boon you desire in your heart. I am pleased with your devotion, penance, and praise. I will grant you everything, even if it is difficult to obtain. You are blessed; even in your childhood, your devotion to me is supreme. This hymn composed by you will be all-granting. Whoever reads or hears it will attain success. Whatever desire they contemplate, I will grant that difficult-to-attain wish. In the end, I will grant them liberation and my own bliss.

Hearing these beautiful words of Vakratunda, Vamana bowed and said to Ganesha: "O Lord of gods, if you are pleased, grant me firm devotion to you. Make the demon king Bali, the enemy of the gods, easy for me to conquer. Whenever I remember you, show yourself to me. Grant me the boon, O Ganesha, that no obstacles may come to me." Saying "So be it," Ganesha disappeared. Vamana joyfully established the Lord of gods there. That excellent place in Vidarbha, known as Adosha, became the place of Vakratunda, granting all success to mankind.

Chapter 47 : The Story of Vamana

Mudgala said: Having obtained the boon and worshipped Vakratunda, Vamana bowed to Kashyapa and proceeded to the sacrifice of Bali. Seeing him radiant like the sun, learned in the Vedas, and short in stature, the sages were astonished. Bali rose, bowed to him, worshipped him with devotion, and asked, "O Great Sage, tell me what I may give you."

Then Shukra realized the purpose of Vamana’s arrival, noting that Bali, in his arrogance, had not performed the worship of Ganesha first. Because of this, the obstacle-creating energy of the Lord had manifested in the form of Vishnu. Shukra thought, "Whatever is destined to happen now, let it happen."

Hearing Bali's words, Vamana said, "O Lord of the Daityas, give me three paces of land." Bali, with great devotion and respect, granted the land. At that moment, Lord Vamana meditated upon Ganesha in his mind. Assuming his cosmic form, the Lord placed his foot; with one step, he covered the entire heaven. With the second, he covered the entire earth, and then asked the demon king, "O Lord, provide the space for the third step."

Bali was filled with wonder, confusion, and grief. Distraught, he could not speak, wondering what he could possibly give. Then Vishnu, in anger, bound the great demon and said, "O Daitya, you have become a debtor to a Brahmana; go to hell." Hearing Vishnu's words, Bali was deeply saddened, thinking it strange that Vishnu would send his own devotee to hell.

"Alas, great was my delusion that I performed this ritual without worshipping Ganesha, the Lord of Obstacles, who is beyond the Vedas. This is the fruit I have received from Vishnu, even though I am his devotee. Therefore, I shall take refuge in Ganesha; he alone will protect me." Thinking thus, the demon king meditated with all his heart and praised Dhundhi (Ganesha).

Bali said: "Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, the remover of hurdles for his devotees, and the creator of obstacles for those who are not. Salutations to you, the Lord of all forms, the one who is both the path and the destination, the Lord of Svananda, O Heramba. Salutations to the Lord of the Vedas, the leader of the Ganas, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the sustainer of the universe. Salutations to the Infinite, the refuge of various illusions, the destroyer of delusion, O Ganesha. Salutations to the indweller of all, the one who takes the form of Chintamani, the eternal Brahman, the beginning of the world. Salutations to you who reside in the beginning, middle, and end, the one who is beyond all, the rider of the lion. O Lord of the universe, forgive my countless offenses and save me as I fall into hell. I was undoubtedly deluded by your Maya, and in my arrogance, I abandoned you and sought refuge in Vishnu. This entire world, moving and stationary, along with Shiva, Vishnu, and others, is deluded by your Maya. If Shiva, Vishnu, and others take refuge in you, O Dhundhi, they become free from delusion and obstacles. Otherwise, they lose their status and fall. Therefore, you are the sustainer of all gods, without a doubt. You are the master of all great obstacles, the one who establishes and elevates all. Now, by your grace, I have gained wisdom, O Gajanana. I am poor and helpless, and I seek your refuge. Save me from hell, O compassionate one. By remembering you, a person attains the fulfillment of all desires."

Having praised him with various hymns, the demon king heard a wonderful voice from the sky: "Do not fear, O King of Daityas. I protect those who seek refuge. You shall have no fear of hell by remembering me. This hymn you have composed will destroy the fear of hell; whoever reads or hears it shall be free from such suffering. It will be a means to attain Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, and it will surely increase devotion to me."

Hearing the voice from the sky, Bali was joyful and remained there, meditating on Ganesha in his heart. At that moment, the obstacle left Vishnu, and Vishnu’s wisdom was instantly restored. Vamana meditated in his mind, "My devotee Bali is complete, yet he has fallen into great fear. Because of the debt to the Brahmana, he is destined for hell. Alas, I, in my ignorance, have oppressed my devotee. Bali performed the sacrifice without worshipping the Lord of Ganas, which made the ritual fruitless. Now, how shall I protect my devotee? I shall seek refuge in Ganesha, and then all will be well."

Thinking thus, the Lord of gods meditated on Ganesha in his heart. Upon being remembered, Gajanana appeared before him. Seeing him, the Lord bowed and worshipped him with devotion, as did all the gods and sages present. Meanwhile, Bali said to Vamana, having gained wisdom through the vision of Ganesha, "O Brahmana, place your third step upon my head. I have given this body as a gift, without doubt."

Hearing this, Vishnu was pleased and said, "So be it." He stood there, having freed his devotee from the debt of the Brahmana. Then, accompanied by the sages and gods, Janardana (Vishnu) praised Ganesha, the giver of all success, with folded hands.

Vamana said: "Salutations to the Lord of Ganas, the Lord of all. Salutations to the creator of the world. Salutations to the Lord of obstacles, the wearer of the crescent moon, the three-eyed one, the protector of devotees. O Lord, this world is created by your immeasurable Maya. Protect those who are deluded by your illusion. Who can know the power of your Maya, O Gajanana? Neither we nor the hosts of yogis can ever know it. Therefore, please forgive my offense, O Lord of honor. We are your devotees, O Lord of gods. This Bali is my devotee, full of devotion; due to my superiority, he became filled with jealousy. Because of that, he performed a ritual contrary to the Vedas and started the sacrifice without you, making the ritual fruitless. Now, O Gajanana, accompanied by the sages and gods, I pray to you: make this sacrifice fruitful. You are known as Vinayaka; whatever is done with respect to you is accepted by the Vedas and others, without doubt. When we, without your guidance, perform acts outside the Vedas, we are made to fall from our positions by you. Therefore, O Lord, make this devotee fruitful with the results of the sacrifice."

Having said this, he fell at the feet of Ganesha. The Lord of Ganas raised him and said, "What shall I do? I had already granted you a boon long ago. You were filled with anger, thinking, 'I will cast this demon into hell,' and now that he has remembered me, he has sought my refuge with body, mind, and speech. Since you pray to me, O Vishnu, I will grant your wish. Now, with me at the forefront, complete this sacrifice. By this, he shall enjoy the fruits of all sacrifices. Let Keshava see the greatness of the offense. After Indra's term ends, this Bali shall become Indra. Establish him in Patala; have no doubt about this. I have made the sacrifice fruitless until now."

Hearing Ganesha's words, everyone was filled with joy and praised him. Then, with Ganesha at the forefront, Bali completed the sacrifice and attained fulfillment. Ganesha then disappeared, and the gods returned to their respective abodes. Thus, the greatness of Vamana has been told to you, O Prajapati. Whoever hears this with devotion will surely attain his desires; for one who hears or causes others to hear this, nothing is unattainable.

Chapter 48 : The Description of Richika's Householder Life

Shaunaka said: O Suta, you have narrated the story of the destruction of Matsarasura by the wise Vakratunda, which grants all accomplishments. Tell me, O great soul, where is that sacred place located? Also, tell me the date of that incarnation which enhances the joy of festivals.

Suta said: That place was established in the eastern region by the great sages, along with the gods and the hosts of Ganas who are devoted to great festivities. It was on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Bhadrapada that the Lord of the gods, Vakratunda, manifested with a pleased heart; that date is considered supreme. Worshiping Him at midday is joy-giving; it is said to grant infinite accomplishments, while at other times it grants smaller ones.

In every island and every continent, in their respective eastern regions, images of Vakratunda were established by the sages in ancient times. That sacred field is known to be ten yojanas in extent, square in shape, highly auspicious, and the ultimate grantor of worldly enjoyment and liberation. In the center resides the Lord, Vakratunda, the Lord of the Ganas. On His left side is Siddhi, and on His right side is Buddhi. In front is His beautiful vehicle, the lion. O Shaunaka, the eight Siddhis reside in the eight directions. Similarly, the eight Ganas are known to be in the four directions in order. Shiva, Vishnu, the Sun, and Shakti—all these reside there, filled with devotion.

All the gods, starting with Brahma, are situated behind and to the east. All the rivers, starting with the Ganga, are situated in the south, O Lord. The sacred places, starting with Kashi, are situated in the west. The best of sages, starting with Vashistha, are situated in the north. The serpent kings, starting with Shesha, the Gandharvas, the mountains, the Siddhas, the Apsaras, the Vidyadharas, and the Kinnara Ganas—those who are the best among their various species—all reside there. O Lord, it is impossible for me to describe them even in hundreds of years.

For the service of the Lord Vakratunda, who possesses longing, they reside there, joyful and delighted, engaged in each other's welfare. They narrate the stories of Vakratunda to one another, their hair standing on end with emotion. Those who bathe in the ocean there have created sacred pilgrimage spots marked by their own names, even though they possess small bodies, O sage.

The Ganesha Tirtha is there, which brings joy to Vakratunda; creatures become fulfilled just by bathing there. By merely traveling there, a person attains their desired goals. A creature who dies in that field is born as one who has attained Brahman. Even a person who is far away, if they remember Him, attains eternal liberation through the bright path. O sage, those who are devotees of Vakratunda, wherever they may be, are undoubtedly one with Brahman and purify others by their very sight.

I have told you about His place in brief, O sage; even Shesha, Brahma, Vishnu, and Maheshwara cannot describe it in detail. The Vedas, all the Smritis, Skanda, and the Yogis themselves are unable to describe it even in tens of thousands of years. I have narrated to you what was told by Mudgala to Daksha, filled with devotion. What else do you wish to hear?

Shaunaka said: O Suta, O fortunate one, we have heard the glorious account of the origin of the sacred field, which grants union with Brahman. Now, O knower of Dharma, tell me what was heard by Daksha, what was told to him by Mudgala, and what it was like.

Suta said: Having heard the account of the field, Daksha, filled with joy, spoke to the sage Mudgala with his head bowed in devotion. Daksha said: Tell me in detail the story of the incarnation of Dhundhiraja. How did Vakratunda himself, who is the son of Shankara's body, manifest?

Mudgala said: Listen, O fortunate Daksha, I will tell you the ancient history of Ganesha, which is full of virtues and highly miraculous. I will narrate what was told by the great sage Yajnavalkya to Kaushika, which reveals the nature of Brahmanhood.

The foremost of the Bhrigu sages, Richika, was engaged in penance. After a long time had passed, he became aged due to his austerities. That great soul attained complete old age. Seeing herds of deer in the forest, he was amazed. A deer, attached to its mate and having attained independence, wandered in the forest with its offspring and grandchildren. They lived with their minds focused on one another, playing together. Seeing this, the best of the Bhrigus became filled with desire. He said to himself, "The householder life is indeed blessed." Thinking thus, the Brahmana went to King Gadhi.

Seeing him, Gadhi rose suddenly and worshiped the sage. Having eaten, the sage asked the best of kings for his daughter's hand in marriage. The King, seeing the aged sage, said to the great sage, "Give me a thousand horses with black ears. I have made a firm vow to accept this as the bridal price, O sage. Therefore, I request this of you, O Brahmana, and then I will give you my daughter."

Hearing this, the sage immediately went to the house of Varuna, took the thousand horses, and gave them to him. The King gave his daughter to that great soul, the Bhrigu descendant. Fearing the consequences, he took her and went to the forest. The Queen said to her daughter, "Such is your fate. What can we do? Live in the forest, my daughter." After lamenting and giving her daughter wealth and clothes, she went to her own home, having left her in the forest with the sage.

The princess, who was highly fortunate, stayed with Richika. She served him without laziness, devoted to him. Seeing her endowed with good character, the best of sages was pleased. For her sake, through his own penance, he created a city in the forest. It was endowed with the four castes, decorated with gold and jewels, filled with servants and maids, and provided with various kinds of prosperity. Through the power of his penance, he himself attained youth and beauty. He enjoyed life with her according to the rules, like the Lord of the gods. Being indifferent to the pleasures of the senses and seeing the smallness of worldly happiness, he held in his mind that the happiness of being a husband was in vain.

Thus ends the forty-eighth chapter in the Vakratunda Charita of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the final Upanishad of the Puranas.

Chapter 49 : The Meeting of Yajnavalkya and Vishvamitra

Mudgala said: Once, the queen, overwhelmed with grief, went to the forest to see her daughter, carrying clothes and wealth with her. Upon reaching Richika’s hermitage, she did not see her there, but was astonished to see a city shining like gold. She wondered whose divine city this was and whether it had been built by the gods. She pondered where her son-in-law, the great sage Bhargava, had gone. Filled with sorrow, she saw a man and, trembling with fear, asked him whose city it was. The man replied, "O Queen, this is the city of Richika, and we are his servants."

The queen, still astonished, asked him to inform her daughter, the sage's wife, that her mother had arrived in the city. The man went and conveyed the message. The daughter came to meet her mother and brought her to her home. Seeing her son-in-law endowed with such splendor, the queen was amazed and spoke to her daughter with affection regarding the desire for a son. She asked her daughter to request her capable husband, the best of sages, for a son for both of them. The sage's wife requested her husband accordingly. Wishing for sons, the sage prepared two offerings in the sacrificial fire. He invoked incomparable power into these two offerings—one representing the Brahmanical essence and the other the Kshatriya essence—and gave them to his wife.

The great sage told her, "You must consume this one, and your mother must consume the other, O lady of beautiful smiles. Then you both shall be blessed with sons." Having said this, the sage went to the river for his midday prayers. The daughter told her mother what had happened, and the mother said to her daughter, "You consume the one meant for me, and I shall consume yours." The daughter did as suggested, and the mother returned to her own home. Both the queen and the Brahmani became pregnant.

One day, the sage saw his wife radiating intense energy and asked her why she was displaying Kshatriya-like qualities. Terrified, she confessed the mix-up regarding the offerings. The sage was astonished and said, "Your brother will be a great ascetic, and your son will be a fierce wielder of the bow." The wife, trembling, begged him to grant that her son would be a Brahmana, or else she would end her life. The sage then declared that her grandson would be as she wished. Through his penance, he invoked Lord Vishnu and ensured the outcome. Thus, the great ascetic Jamadagni was born to her, and Rama (Parashurama), the best of warriors, was born as his son.

In the ninth month, the queen, overwhelmed with love, gave birth to the highly fortunate and ascetic Vishvamitra. After Vishvamitra reached youth, Gadhi entrusted him with the kingdom and retired to the forest to perform penance. The valiant Vishvamitra ruled the kingdom. Once, he went into the forests for hunting. After hunting, the king became hungry and thirsty, and while wandering, he arrived at the excellent hermitage of Vashistha. The king entered and bowed with devotion. He and his army were fed by the power of the Kamadhenu (the wish-fulfilling cow). After eating, the king asked the sage to give him the cow, Nandini. The great sage Vishvamitra refused.

The wicked king then ordered his soldiers to take her by force. The cow destroyed them all, and then the king himself approached. They struck the cow with whips, but she created a massive army. A fierce battle ensued between the two armies, and Vishvamitra’s entire army was destroyed in an instant. Defeated, he returned home, renounced his kingdom, and went to the forest. He performed severe penance for the great Lord Shankara. After a hundred years, Shiva was pleased and granted him all the knowledge of weapons. Armed with these, he returned home.

Vishvamitra attacked Vashistha’s hermitage with his weapons, but the glorious Vashistha, wielding the Brahma-danda (the staff of Brahma), neutralized all his weapons. Vishvamitra was defeated by the Brahma-danda and fled in terror. Vashistha was comforted by other Brahmanas and returned to his hermitage, while Vishvamitra went home, realizing that the power of a Brahmana is supreme and the power of a Kshatriya is insignificant. Having decided this, he renounced his kingdom and went to the forest to perform supreme penance to attain the status of a Brahmana.

The royal sage heated the world with his penance, chanting the Gayatri mantra and observing strict fasting. Ten thousand years passed in this manner. Then Brahma appeared and asked him to seek a boon. He asked for the status of a Brahmana. Brahma told him to continue his penance. Even after Brahma appeared ten times, the wise Vishvamitra did not accept any other boon. Through the power of his penance, the directions were filled with his radiance, and the gods became subject to his command. By his own power, the royal sage declared to the Brahmanas that he had become a great Brahmarshi through the power of his penance. Terrified, they all bowed and agreed. The great sage Vashistha, however, did not acknowledge him as such. Vishvamitra, agitated, tried to kill Vashistha, but failed in all his attempts.

The great sage Vishvamitra, radiant with the power of his penance, remained in his hermitage, deeply saddened. Then, the highly radiant and valiant Yajnavalkya, born from the body of Brahma and a great master of Yoga, arrived there. Vishvamitra bowed to him and worshipped him with devotion. The great Vishvamitra said with folded hands, "Today my birth and my studies have become fruitful, as I have received the vision of you, who are one with Brahman. Why have you come, O great sage? Tell me; I am your servant and wish to follow your command."

Chapter 50 : The Description of the Glory of Divodasa

Mudgala said: The great sage Yajnavalkya, being satisfied with the state of Vishwamitra, spoke to him. Yajnavalkya said: O best of sages, knowing that you are distressed, I have come to you. You should know that there is nothing else to be done, O wise one. Tell me, O Brahmin, what is the cause of your sorrow? I will destroy it through wisdom and reasoning.

Vishwamitra said: I performed terrible penance, yet Brahma did not grant me the boon I sought. Vashistha called me a Rajarshi (royal sage) in the presence of the people. By what means, O king of yogis, can I become a Brahmin? I will strive to become one who is respected by all.

Then the great sage Yajnavalkya said to him: Do not worry, O Rajarshi, you will become a Brahmarshi. Abandon your terrible pride and your wicked envy, and then you will find peace. Listen to a beautiful story related to the Lord of Kashi, by which you will become pure and attain the status of a Brahmin.

Even during the dissolution of the universe, Kashi is never abandoned by the Lord of Kashi (Shiva). There is never a moment of separation between Kashi and Shambhu. Because both were filled with ego, a great obstacle arose that caused their separation.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Yajnavalkya, the sage Kaushika (Vishwamitra) was delighted and spoke to him with a heart full of love. Vishwamitra asked: O great one, what was the nature of the obstacle that arose for Shiva in Avimukta (Kashi)? Please tell me in detail.

Yajnavalkya said: For sixty years, the clouds did not rain upon the earth. Because of this, the entire world, with all its moving and non-moving beings, was destroyed. Seeing the destruction of the people, Brahma, the grandfather of the world, went to the royal sage Divodasa, who was engaged in penance. Having abandoned his kingdom, the king, born of the solar dynasty, was performing supreme penance for the sake of liberation with great devotion. Through his penance and yogic power, he shone like another sun. Brahma himself spoke these words to him:

Brahma said: O great Divodasa, listen to my beneficial words. Accept the kingdom of Kashi, which I have given to you, O wise one.

Divodasa replied: I will not rule a kingdom. I am seeking liberation and am free from worldly desires; O Lord of creatures, grant me liberation.

Brahma replied again for the welfare of the world: Rule the kingdom I have given you, O wise one. If you rule, there will be miraculous rain. Otherwise, the world will be destroyed; therefore, follow my command.

For the sake of protecting the world, the wise and compassionate king accepted the excellent kingdom of Kashi, which was devoid of gods. The kingdom of the gods is always in heaven, that of humans on earth, and that of the Nagas and demons in the netherworld. Therefore, I will worship Shambhu as a deity, and everyone must remain in their own duty while I rule the kingdom.

Brahma agreed and gave the kingdom of Kashi to the wise Divodasa. Then Brahma went to Kashi, bowed to Shankara, and said: O Shankara, go to Mount Mandara. Marichi has performed penance; grant him a boon, O Shiva. If you do not, he will die; therefore, go, O teacher of the world.

Hearing the words of Brahma, Shiva went with him, accompanied by the gods and sages, to grant the boon to the Brahmin. Seeing him, Shiva asked what boon he desired. The sage asked Shiva for liberation and, having said this, left his body due to the pain of his penance. The sage attained the form of Shiva and went to Kailasa.

Meanwhile, Brahma said to Shambhu with folded hands: O Shambhu, dwell on Mandara and leave the great city of Kashi. For the sake of ending the suffering caused by the drought, I have given the kingdom to Divodasa, O Shankara. He has said that if the kingdom of the gods does not exist on earth, he will not rule; otherwise, he will never rule. I gave him the kingdom for the protection of the world. Forgive my offense, O Mahadeva, I bow to you.

Saying "So be it," the supreme Lord sighed and went to dwell on Mandara with Parvati and his attendants. Shiva lamented, "My great field of Avimukta has been taken away from me by fate. By what action will I be liberated now? The words of Brahma can never be false. What shall I do now, distressed by the separation from Kashi? I am Vishweshwara (Lord of the Universe), yet nothing is under my control. The name Vishwanatha is undoubtedly meaningless. I will seek refuge in the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha), who has made me equal to all living beings."

Having decided this, Shiva performed penance on the mountain, meditating on the Lord of Obstacles to regain Kashi. Kashi, too, was distressed upon hearing of Shambhu's departure and performed penance, meditating on Vighnesha (Ganesha) with an unwavering mind.

Divodasa ruled the kingdom righteously. Then, rain fell, bringing happiness to all. Due to the rain, the entire world, moving and stationary, became joyful under his rule. Everyone was engaged in their own duties according to their caste and stage of life, and there was not even a trace of sin while he ruled. The people were healthy and prosperous, the trees were laden with flowers and fruits, and the cows gave plenty of milk. The gods were satisfied with offerings, the ancestors were pleased, and sacrifices were performed.

Thus, the people enjoyed various pleasures, and King Divodasa himself performed daily pilgrimages. First, he worshipped Dhundhi (Ganesha), then Vishweshwara, the Mother of the World (Goddess), Kashi, and then Manikarnika. He worshipped the Bhagirathi (Ganga), Madhava, the Jaigishavya cave, Kalabhairava, and the powerful Dandapani. He made the people follow these rituals, and he stood like Dandapani to punish those who were devoid of righteousness.

Eighty thousand years passed, and not even a small sin could enter the kingdom. The gods in heaven were amazed and thought: "The kingdom of the gods on earth has been destroyed by him. Why should we stay on earth? That royal sage is competing with us; let us deceive him."

Indra, filled with anger, called Agni and said: "Do not let there be a place for you in Divodasa's kingdom." Hearing Indra's words, Agni disappeared. The people on earth, devoid of fire, followed the king. Seeing them, Divodasa, empowered by his penance, manifested himself in the form of fire wherever needed. In this way, the gods, one by one, went there, and the king himself assumed their forms. The king, knowing the duties of the gods, assumed the forms of Vayu and others, and the people on earth did not recognize him.

After a long time, the gods were astonished, as the king performed all the duties of the gods miraculously. Then all the gods went to Brahma for refuge, as they had abandoned the earth to delude Divodasa. But he, through the power of his penance, performed all the duties of the gods himself.

The gods asked, "O Lord, we are without our domains, what shall we do?" Hearing their words, Brahma said: "You must go there and pray to the king. He will surely return your domains to you."

Then the gods, led by Indra, went to Divodasa. Seeing them, he stood up and bowed with humility. The gods praised the king, and he, in turn, praised the gods and returned their domains to them. He remained in his own form, and the gods, including Agni, were re-established in their own places.

Chapter 51 : The Bestowing of the Boon of Kashi by Shiva

Yajnavalkya said: O Sage, thereafter, Lord Shambhu became deeply anxious in his mind. The gods had lost their enthusiasm due to the penance of that wise king (Divodasa). He is devoted to his own duty and endowed with penance; it is impossible to displace him. What will happen then? When will I attain Kashi? I am burnt by the fire of separation and will surely die. Thus, Maheshwara lamented. Even though he was reduced to mere bones and skin, Shiva grieved in many ways. All the gods and sages consoled him with respect.

The divine sages said: O Maheshwara, do not grieve in vain, for you are powerful. By your grace, we shall go to Kashi. By tempting the people there with various supernatural powers (siddhis), we shall make King Divodasa a partaker of sin. Then, O Sadashiva, you shall specifically reclaim Kashi. Having said this, all the gods went there with great joy.

Upon reaching Kashi, the gods assumed various forms and, by manifesting supernatural powers, they deluded the people. However, the virtuous people there did not pay heed to their words. After trying for a year, they became peaceful and desisted. Then, Sadashiva sent the Bhairavas there, but they too, despite various efforts, were unable to succeed. In this manner, the Adityas, Vasus, great sages, Yoginis, and all the Ganas became discouraged.

Then, Shankara summoned Brahma and sent him. He performed ten Ashvamedha sacrifices, but he too returned in peace (without success). Then, Shankara became absorbed in meditation at the feet of the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha), who is the fulfiller of all desires. He prayed: O Lord of the Ganas, I am troubled by obstacles; always make me free of obstacles. I am your servant; protect me from the separation of Avimukta, O Lord.

With a focused mind, Shankara meditated upon the Elephant-faced Lord for one year to achieve his purpose. Seeing Shankara devoid of ego, the Lord revealed his supreme form, which is meditated upon by Yogis. He appeared mounted on a lion, four-armed, holding a noose and a goad, with hands in the gesture of granting boons and dispelling fear, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. He was red-complexioned, beautiful, elephant-faced, with a large belly, adorned with various ornaments, wearing the Chintamani jewel, full of radiance, and highly effulgent. Seeing the Lord, Shankara was filled with joy and bowed down. Then, he worshipped him and praised the Lord of the Ganas, the curved-trunk one, according to his intellect and scriptural rites.

Shiva said: Salutations to the curved-trunk one, the bestower of all success. Salutations to the formless God, and salutations again to the one with form. Salutations to the one who awakens all, salutations to the one who is dear to all. Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, salutations to Ganesha. You are Brahma the creator, Vishnu the protector, and Hara the destroyer; salutations to you, the Lord of Gunas. Salutations to you who are of the form of Brahma, and salutations to you who are the embodiment of Brahma. Salutations to you who are the form of the universe and the mover of the universe. Salutations to you who possess infinite qualities and infinite majesty. Salutations to Heramba, who has an infinite belly. Salutations to the cause of all causes, and salutations to you who are the cause. Salutations to you who are without cause, and salutations to you who are mounted on a lion.

I have become like a living being, there is no doubt about this. O merciful Lord, my Avimukta (Kashi) has become 'vimukta' (released/lost) from me. Therefore, I have taken refuge in you; make me free of obstacles, O Lord. O Lord of the Ganas, grant me Kashi along with its 'Avimukta' status. You had previously given a boon that upon being remembered, you would stand before me as a son; O Elephant-faced one, fulfill that promise. Having said this, Maheshwara bowed at his feet. The Lord of the Ganas lifted him up and spoke as if smiling.

Vakratunda said: This hymn composed by you will be the bestower of all success. O Shiva, whoever reads or hears it will be granted worldly enjoyment and liberation. O Sadashiva, I shall fulfill everything else you have requested. I shall grant you Avimukta, and it will now be meaningful for you.

Having said this, he created from his body a very beautiful Brahmin, who was all-knowing, radiant, and powerful. The Lord commanded him: Go for the success of Shiva. Quickly create delusion in the minds of the people in Kashi. By my command, delude Divodasa and give the kingdom of Kashi to Shiva; you will be worshipped by all. People and Brahmins will call you Dhundhi, the knower of astrology. There is no doubt that all your descendants will be successful.

Hearing this, the Brahmin Dhundhi bowed with joy. He circumambulated and went to Kashi, remembering the Lord of the Ganas. Then, the Elephant-faced Lord summoned Vishnu and said: O Vishnu, go to Kashi in the form of a Buddhist by my command. The entire city will be deluded by the Brahmin Dhundhi. O Janardana, by my word, make the city fall from its path now. Bowing to Ganesha, Vishnu, in the form of a Buddhist, went to Kashi, remembering the curved-trunk Lord in his heart.

They did exactly as the Lord of the Ganas had instructed. The entire city was quickly deluded and fell from its path. O Vishvamitra, you who are rich in penance, hear another story. Kashi, who was devoted to the meditation of Ganesha, remained there. A thousand years passed, O great sage. Pleased by her penance, the Elephant-faced Lord came to grant a boon to Kashi. He appeared in his divine form, mounted on a lion, with a pleased mind, adorned with various ornaments. Seeing him, the most holy Kashi stood up suddenly and bowed at his feet, deeply moved and overwhelmed. Rising again, she worshipped the Lord with devotion and, standing steady with folded hands, she praised him.

Kashi said: Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, salutations to the one with infinite forms. Salutations to the giver of all, salutations to the one who is the support of Maya. Salutations to the elephant-faced one, salutations to the one with winnowing-basket ears. Salutations to the one adorned with all, salutations to the curved-trunk one. Salutations to the formless, eternal, quality-less, yet embodiment of qualities. Salutations to the one known by the Vedas, salutations to Brahma. Salutations to the giver of Brahma-knowledge, salutations to the one mounted on a lion. Salutations to the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, salutations to the one who removes all fear. Salutations to the Lord of the orphans, the protector of all. Salutations to the giver of all to his devotees, salutations to the remover of obstacles. Salutations to the one who creates obstacles for the non-devotees, the bestower of enjoyment and liberation. Salutations to the one who resides in the hearts of Yogis and is attained through Yoga. Salutations to the one beyond mind and speech, the embodiment of peace. Salutations to the giver of peace to the peaceful, salutations to Ganesha.

O Lord of the Ganas, how can I praise you, when even the Vedas and scriptures have attained peace through you? Therefore, I bow to you. Protect, protect your servant who is tormented by separation. My name 'Avimukta' has become meaningless because of Shiva's absence. O Lord, it is a great wonder of fate that I have been made devoid of Shiva. Therefore, I have taken refuge in you; show me Shankara.

Having said this, she fell at his feet, O great sage. The Lord of the Ganas lifted her up and spoke in a sweet voice.

Ganesha said: Do not grieve, O auspicious one; I will show you Shankara. This name 'Avimukta' will become meaningful. This hymn composed by you will be the remover of separation. O beautiful one, whoever reads or hears it with devotion, I, who am present everywhere, will grant them whatever they desire with a pure heart. There is no doubt that this hymn will lead to the attainment of Brahma.

Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas disappeared right there. She remained there, meditating upon him, waiting for the time.

Thus ends the fifty-first chapter of the first section of the Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Bestowing of the Boon of Kashi by Shiva."

Chapter 52 : The Description of the Questioning of Narayana

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Yajnavalkya said: O sinless one, at that moment, the best of astrologers, the Brahmana named Dhundhi, whom I mentioned earlier, arrived there. O sage, that entire city was immediately enchanted by him. Later, when the Buddha (Vishnu) arrived, he caused that enchantment to vanish. Divodasa was awakened by Dhundhi, and having renounced his kingdom, he remained there awaiting the vision of Shiva.

Summoned by them, Shambhu (Shiva) went ahead with the leader of the Ganas (Ganesha). Having worshipped him, he first established him as a resident of Kashi. Later, accompanied by the gods and sages, he himself entered the city. He bowed at the feet of the remover of obstacles (Ganesha) and Shiva in Kashi. All the gods and groups of sages settled in their respective places happily. Then, Shankara granted liberation to Divodasa.

After some time, the Lord of the gods spoke to Girisha (Shiva): "I have given Kashi to you, and everything has been done appropriately. Now, O Mahadeva, I shall go to my beautiful abode. Whenever you remember me, O Shiva, you shall see me." Having said this, that Gajanan (Ganesha), along with the Brahmana Dhundhi, disappeared while the gods were watching, O sage.

Then, Vishwanatha himself, having fashioned an image of him, installed it with great effort, accompanied by the gods and Brahmanas. He worshipped him according to the rites with delightful offerings, and after bowing to him, he circumambulated him. The Lord of the universe (Vishwanatha) said to all: "Listen to my beneficial words. Those who will truly please me after first worshipping Dhundhi, to them I shall grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation, and not otherwise. This Vakratunda (Ganesha) is the bestower of the fruits of living in Kashi. The secret of the Vedas and other scriptures, which I sought for so long, is this Lord, the master of the Vedas, who has been attained in the heart. Therefore, I have named him Dhundhiraja. There is no doubt that he is the bestower of righteousness, wealth, desire, and liberation in this region."

Having said this, Mahadeva, devoted to daily worship, became diligent in the service of Dhundhiraja. "In the same way, you too, O Lord, worship the Lord of obstacles (Ganesha). By abandoning the sense of ego, you will become a Brahmana."

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Yajnavalkya, Vishwamitra, who possessed great penance, spoke to that highly fortunate one with humility.

Vishwamitra said: O highly fortunate Yajnavalkya, I have indeed been awakened by you. Now, tell me the knowledge, as you are all-knowing, O great sage. Tell me by what path I should abandon ego. Tell me how it resides in my body at present. Tell me by what method I should worship Ganesha, and what is his true form, as you yourself are a master of Yogis.

Yajnavalkya said: Listen, O Kaushika Brahmana, I will tell you about ego. You will surely attain Brahmanahood through penance; there is no doubt. Understand this very thing as ego, O wise one. I will tell you the method for its abandonment; listen to it properly. To attain Brahmanahood, perform excellent penance. When the Lord grants it, then Brahmanahood will be attained. When it is not granted by the Lord, then let that state of being a royal sage be mine always. Who am I, and what is my penance? I am always dependent and embodied, there is no doubt. I will make great effort, and if destiny favors, it will happen. By this method, O great sage, perform penance. Then, having moved past the state of ego, everything will be accomplished.

Listen to another word of mine: abandon jealousy, O Kaushika. Take refuge in the Lord of the Ganas; through him, there will be happiness. Do not compete with Brahmanas, thinking no one’s penance is equal to mine. Filled with jealousy, how can you become a Brahmana? Due to your jealous nature, you destroyed the hundred sons of the great-souled Vashistha, just as a demon would. You started a different creation for the sake of Trishanku in the past, and you are making various efforts to kill Vashistha. Abandon that jealousy and listen to the truth: I am undoubtedly lower than all Brahmanas and ascetics. What penance should I perform with pride, thinking I am superior to them? The power of their penance cannot even be imagined by the mind. Knowing this, perform penance, and you will become a Brahmana. Otherwise, you will die after performing penance for thousands of ages. O wise one, you must always bow to everyone with a heart full of devotion, like a royal sage.

Having said this, the great Yogi, of great fame, narrated the story born of the power of Vakratunda that occurred in the past. Hearing the story, the highly fortunate Vishwamitra was filled with wonder and spoke to the ascetic Yajnavalkya after bowing to him.

Vishwamitra said: O master of Yogis, I have heard the story of Vakratunda. Now, O Brahmana, tell me about his experiential form.

Yajnavalkya said: Listen with effort to the ancient story of his greatness. I will tell you with affection what gives me knowledge. Once, O Brahmana, I went to Vaikuntha, the excellent heaven. There, after bowing to Narayana, I asked the Lord of the universe: "What is the essence among all beings, and upon whose support does it exist? Tell me, O highly fortunate one, as you are the Lord of the universe himself. All this appears to be supported by you in the heart always. Still, I wish to ask for the sake of hearing it properly."

Chapter 53 : The Dialogue between Narayana and Yajnavalkya

Narayana said: O best of Brahmins, know that this entire universe is supported by Ganesha. He is the giver of wisdom within the heart and the bestower of success in the external world. Therefore, O great sage, he should always be worshipped by us first. He exists at the beginning, in the middle, and at the end; there is no doubt about this. We are all manifestations of qualities, but Ganesha is the embodiment of Brahman. He is the Lord of success and wisdom, and he is worthy of worship by all. His abode is Svananda, which is devoid of existence and non-existence, beyond the reach of sages and gods, free from duality, and supreme. Knowing Ganesha in this way, one is liberated from all bonds. O wise one, this entire world is supported by him.

Once, all the elements, filled with ego, argued among themselves, each claiming, "I am the greatest." Each said, "I support this entire world; I create, I sustain, and I destroy it through my Maya; I am the one who acts according to my own will." While these powerful elements were thus deluded by their own strength and engaged in mutual dispute, the Lord of Gunas appeared there in the guise of a Brahmin to end the argument and bestow knowledge. Seeing him, the gods were astonished and wondered who he was, as they believed themselves to be the essence of all things. They asked him who he was and how he had entered the path of Brahman.

The Brahmin, smiling, replied through his Yogic Maya, "I have created this path as a wise Brahmin. Why are you arguing among yourselves? Tell me, and I will do what is best for you." The elements asked, "O wise one, tell us who among us is the true Lord of all elements." The Brahmin replied, "There is no doubt that this entire world is supported by a single essence. You may leave the world and watch; the one by whom the world is abandoned and yet remains is the true Lord."

Hearing this, the powerful elements, one by one, began to withdraw themselves from the world. First, the Earth withdrew, yet the world did not perish. Then Water, Fire, Air, and Ether withdrew, but the world remained as it was. Even when the subtle elements, the sense organs, the organs of action, and the presiding deities of the senses withdrew, the world remained unchanged. The gods, who are the illuminators of the senses, were astonished to see that even after they withdrew, the world continued to exist. They became powerless and dejected.

Then, the elements of food, life-force, mind, intellect, bliss, sound, and the ego-principle withdrew, but the world still shone. Even when the states of existence, non-existence, and the transcendental states were withdrawn, the world remained. Finally, when the state of Svananda (Self-Bliss) was withdrawn, the entire world dissolved.

Astonished, the gods praised Svananda and prayed, "O Lord, since everything is supported by you, and you are the leader of all elements, be our Vinayaka (the Supreme Leader). O Vinayaka, salutations to you! Please recreate the world and restore it as it was before." Thus prayed to, Ganesha recreated the entire world as it was. The gods then re-entered their respective positions and rejoiced.

O Yajnavalkya, this is how the Lord of the world is known. He is called Vinayaka because he is the leader of all, and he is known as the embodiment of Yoga. Because he destroys the crookedness of Maya, he is called Vakratunda (the one with the curved trunk). Take refuge in him, and you will undoubtedly become a great Yogi. Know that the syllable 'Ga' represents the mind and speech, and 'Na' represents that which is beyond mind and speech. Ganesha is the master of both, the embodiment of Yoga. By attaining him, beings become one with Brahman. This secret, born of the Vedanta, has been told to you; keep it hidden, and you will attain success. Having said this, Narayana fell silent, repeatedly bowing to Ganesha.

Chapter 54 : The Conclusion of the Story of Vakratunda

Yajnavalkya said: Then, being extremely pleased, I bowed to Janardana (Lord Vishnu/Ganesha) and went to the forest to begin my practice of Yoga. Gradually, by conquering the five functions of the mind through various means, I attained the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) through the path of peace and by His grace. Therefore, you should take refuge in Vakratunda, and by doing so, you will undoubtedly become worthy of being honored by Brahmins. O Great Sage, attain the supreme benefit born of the chanting of the Gayatri mantra; by dedicating it to Gajanana, you will become free from all obstacles. Always maintain a friendly attitude toward the entire world; only then will your name, Vishwamitra (friend of the universe), become meaningful.

Mudgala said: Having said this, Yajnavalkya departed, having been worshipped by him, while constantly chanting the mantra "Namah Ganeshaya." From that moment on, Vishwamitra, the knower of Dharma, abandoned his ego and jealousy and chanted the Gayatri. He recited the Gayatri with a spirit of dedication to Ganesha and performed penance while meditating on Gajanana in his heart. The Gayatri is the mother of the Vedas, and the Pranava (Om) is called the father. That very Lord of the Ganas is the one whose head is in the form of Om. He, the Lord of the Ganas, is the Saguna (with attributes) form of the Omkara. The Yogi attained Yoga by offering the Gayatri to Him.

Soon after, Brahma, the grandfather of the world, came to Vishwamitra and bestowed upon him the supreme status of a Brahmin. Honored by Vashistha Muni and other Brahmins, the highly radiant Vishwamitra was declared a Brahmin. He was proclaimed by the great sages to be among the Seven Sages (Saptarishis), and he became the foremost among the devotees of Ganesha. The sage worshipped Him daily according to the prescribed rituals and, with a steady mind, praised Gajanana according to his ability.

Vishwamitra said: Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, salutations again and again to the Lord of Obstacles. Salutations to You, who are Nirguna (without attributes) and yet the Lord of all attributes. Salutations to the best of the all-knowing, salutations to the one who brings joy to all. Salutations to the elephant-faced God, Ganesha. Salutations to you, O Elephant-faced one, salutations to the Lord of the Vedas. Salutations to You, who are beginningless and always worthy of the first worship. Salutations to the giver of all accomplishments, the bearer of various illusions, and the one who bestows delusion upon the deluded; salutations to You, who are beyond all these. Salutations to the Lord of Intellect, the bearer of the five types of intelligence. Salutations to You, who are the embodiment of mind and speech, and yet beyond them. Salutations to the God who rides a lion, the Lord of the Gods. Salutations to You, who are served by the Lord of Gods and are the beginningless reality. Salutations to the four-armed one, the bearer of the mouse-emblem. Salutations to the rider of the mouse, salutations to Heramba. Salutations to the pot-bellied one, salutations to the one with winnowing-basket ears. Salutations to the family deity of the Yogis and the Lord of the Yogis.

As he was praising Him, Gajanana appeared, radiant as a thousand suns, riding a lion, with a large belly. Seeing Him, the great sage fell to the ground, his eyes filled with tears, his body covered in goosebumps, overwhelmed by devotion. Seeing him, the Lord of the Ganas said to the great sage: "This hymn composed by you will be pleasing to me. Whoever reads it or hears it will become dear to me. I will grant them worldly enjoyment, liberation, and whatever else they desire, without doubt. Ask for boons from me; I will grant you all that is difficult to obtain. I am satisfied with your devotion, O King of Yogis."

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Ganesha, the sage became attentive and, bowing with great devotion, spoke with joy.

Vishwamitra said: My lineage is blessed today, my birth, penance, and fame are blessed. My parents are blessed because I have seen Your lotus feet. What should I desire, O Lord of the Ganas? Everything is illusory, O Lord. Therefore, grant me Your devotion, O Lord, salutations to You. Grant me a place among the Ganas, O Gajanana. Grant me this boon, O Brahman, which destroys the delusion of Maya.

Saying "So be it," the Lord disappeared. From that time on, Vishwamitra became a Gana (a devotee of Ganesha). Thus, the various incarnations of the wise Vakratunda are the givers of success to devotees and the destroyers of demons. Their entire history cannot be told; therefore, know that this story has been told in brief. O Prajapati, this story of Vakratunda, which destroys sins and grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, has been told to you in full. The person who hears it with devotion, or makes others hear it, or reads it, will surely become accomplished. This story grants sons, grandsons, wealth, grain, knowledge, and long life. The merit gained from hearing the story of Vakratunda is equal to bathing in Prayaga for a thousand months of Magha. It is equal to the merit gained from living in Kashi from birth until death, or performing rituals according to the scriptures. It is equal to performing a hundred thousand sacrifices. By merely hearing this, O Prajapati, all is fulfilled. What more need be said? Know, O wise one, that hearing this grants the state of Brahman.

Suta said: Having said this, the great Yogi Mudgala became silent. O Shaunaka, I have told you this entire story of the greatness of Vakratunda in brief, understanding your devotion. Do you wish to hear anything else? I am satisfied by your grace. This greatness of Vakratunda is a secret, the mystery of the Vedas. It should be kept hidden, for by doing so, one attains success. It should not be told to the wicked, those who hate Ganesha, or those who are sinful and worldly. If it is told in front of them, the anger of Vakratunda will surely arise. By mere disrespect, the narrator causes ten generations of his ancestors and ten generations of his descendants to fall into hell. Therefore, one should carefully tell this to the virtuous and the devotees of Ganesha, and by doing so, one can liberate a crore of one's own kin. Thus, the seeker and the speaker will undoubtedly liberate their entire lineage. The story of Vakratunda destroys the sins of the Kali Yuga; it is no wonder that it burns away heaps of sins in the Krita and other Yugas. In this Kali Yuga, the stories of all Gods, especially the story of the Ganas, are the providers of the ultimate path. O Sage, I have told you the story of Vakratunda with devotion. I am now fulfilled. I am blessed by your company, through which I have remembered the Supreme Lord Ganesha, and my life has become successful.

Thus ends the fifty-fourth chapter of the first section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which concludes the story of Vakratunda.

Chapter 1 : The Description of the Questions of the Sun and the Valakhilyas

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O Suta, you who know the essence of all things, you have enlightened me, O twice-born one. You have made me hear the holy and most wonderful story of Ganesha. I have heard the greatness of Ekadanta from you, O best of the twice-born, and I am as satisfied as a man who has drunk nectar. Now, please tell me in detail about the greatness of Mahodara. What is this Ganesha like, and how many are his incarnations? Why did he come to the earth at Kurukshetra? Tell me all this and his complete greatness.

Suta said: Listen, O Shaunaka, best of sages; you are blessed, there is no doubt. You are increasing the most wonderful mystery of Dhundhi (Ganesha). Therefore, I am delighted and will tell you everything in detail, just as I heard the greatness of Mahodara from Vyasa. Daksha also asked the same questions as you are asking me now, and I shall tell you exactly what Mudgala said to him.

Daksha said: O Mudgala, best of yogis, you have enlightened me greatly. You speak of the story that is the abode of auspiciousness and complete in every way, O giver of respect. Now, O Brahman, O great soul, tell me in detail the story of Mahodara, which liberates one from the bonds of worldly existence.

Mudgala said: You are blessed, O Daksha, for you drink the nectar-like story of Ganesha with devotion. I will tell you briefly. Who in the worlds is capable of describing the greatness of Mahodara, the giver of all success, in full detail? In the past, the great-souled Valakhilyas asked the Sun god the same thing, and you should listen carefully to what he said to them.

Daksha said: How did the glorious Sun god come to be asked by the Valakhilyas? O Mudgala, you should tell me all that in detail.

Mudgala said: The great sages, the Valakhilyas, always worshiped the Sun god as he traveled in his chariot, with hearts full of devotion. Seeing the Sun god joyfully engaged in the praise of Ganesha, the astonished Valakhilyas asked him, "O Sun, you are described in the Vedas as the form of Brahman. You are undoubtedly the one, the secondless, the essence of nectar. By worshiping you, great yogis and lords of gods like Vishnu become various beings. You shine as the one with attributes (Saguna) in the form of actions, and as the one without attributes (Nirguna) everywhere in the form of knowledge. There is nothing higher than you, especially in the Vedas. Yet, you specifically worship Ganesha. Who is this Ganesha? Tell us why you worship him. We have become deluded seeing your actions. Therefore, O Lord, tell us the truth by your radiance. Why do you lead everyone astray by praising Ganesha?"

Mudgala said: Being asked thus, the Sun, who is the soul of all embodied beings, told them everything. Listen to that, O Prajapati.

The Sun said: O great Valakhilyas, listen, all of you, to the story of the Lord of Ganas, and you shall become yogis. I am the soul as described in the Vedas, there is no doubt. I will tell you the knowledge that destroys delusion. The Brahman that is spoken of as the Truth, I am the sustainer of life in that. I am the one, the secondless, the form of Truth, O sages. It is established in the form of the soul, distinct from the states of the Vedas and others. The duality of truth and untruth should be understood by the wise. That which is the form of equality in the non-dual state of both is the Supreme of the Supreme. That which is called Vishnu is the Brahman of both joys. The fourth state, which is the unmanifest form of Brahman, is called Shankara, the one who does not act for anyone. He is the creator of the four yogas and is known as the bliss of the self. That which is the form of all union is called Brahman in the Vedas. There is no yoga higher than one's own state; it is called the form of union. He himself is called Ganesha, endowed with Maya. Because of Maya, Brahman itself resides in all states through the non-difference of union; therefore, it is endowed with Maya. The state of non-union is also called the Supreme, beyond union, there is no doubt. This Brahman is said to be without Maya in the Vedas. In that, for whom is there no union, and for whom does it not exist? Therefore, it is called 'Ayoga' (non-union), though it is non-different, O sages. In the union of union and non-union, Ganesha is the indicator of yoga. He is called Brahmanaspati, the giver of peace in the states of Brahman. O Brahmins, he is called the Lord of all Brahmins (Brahmanas), and I always worship him with devotion. 'Gana' is said by the wise to be the form of the group and the creator. O twice-born, the group is formed by the union of the external and the internal. Therefore, he became the King of Ganas, and the Ganas are considered to be the form of Brahman. There is no doubt that I worship their Lord specifically. That Brahman which is in the form of the soul is itself the form of the single Gana. I worship that Lord with a heart overflowing with devotion.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of the Sun, all the astonished Valakhilya sages spoke to him with folded hands.

The Valakhilyas said: O Sun, tell us in detail the greatness of Ganesha, which gives yoga, so that we may become skilled in the knowledge of the truth.

The Sun said: Listen, all you sages, to the story that gives all success, which was told to me by Brahma and which destroys all sins.

Thus ends the first chapter named "The Description of the Questions of the Sun and the Valakhilyas" in the Mahodara-charita of the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 2 : The Restoration of the Sun

The Valakhilyas asked: O Lord, please narrate to us the great story of Ganesha as told by Brahma, for we have a deep longing to hear it.

The Sun said: In the past, I existed in my own form, pervading all worlds, devoted to the meditation of Ganesha, and acting as the teacher of the greatest yogis. At that time, Brahma had created the three worlds, both animate and inanimate, and Kashyapa was the chief among them, equal to a Prajapati. I performed penance for ten thousand years, O sages, remaining absorbed in meditation while chanting the mantra beginning with "Akrsnena."

Pleased with his devotion, I went to his hermitage. Seeing me, he bowed down and worshipped me with great devotion. With folded hands and a humble heart, the sage praised me, the giver of boons. Kashyapa said: Salutations to you, the foundation of the world, the Supreme Self. Salutations to you, the Sun, who exists in three forms and knows the three times. Salutations to the formless, eternal God who is beyond distinctions yet assumes many forms. Salutations to you, the immeasurable power, the foundation of all actions, the Sun, who is the embodiment of all deeds and exists in three forms. Salutations to you, the immortal, the one established in Brahman, the inner self, the radiant one, and the lord of the horses. Salutations to you, who are without beginning, middle, or end, the support of all, the ocean of brilliance. Salutations to the protector of the poor, the lord of the days, the destroyer of obstacles, and the guardian of your devotees. Salutations to the Lord of Lords, the bestower of worldly enjoyment and liberation, the one who plays in many ways. Salutations to you, the unique, the non-dual, the master of Maya, the husband of Sanjna; protect me, for I have sought refuge in you. How can I praise you, the Sun, who is the form of the Self everywhere? Even the Vedas could not fully comprehend you; therefore, I bow to you. O Supreme Lord, grant me devotion to your lotus feet. I ask for one more boon: be my son, O Lord.

The Sun continued: O sages, hearing the joyful words of the great sage, I was pleased and replied to him. I will become your son, appearing in the form of the twelve Adityas. You will have intense devotion to me, O great sage. Whatever you desire, O best of sages, shall be fulfilled. By merely remembering me, I will appear before you. This hymn composed by you will grant all desires; pleased by this hymn, I will grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation. After saying this, I returned to the solar realm, and the sage was filled with joy.

Aditi also performed great penance, and I granted her request as well. I entered the womb of Aditi as the son of Kashyapa, and in due time, she gave birth to me. The great sage performed all the birth rituals and, in time, the initiation ceremony and Vedic studies. Meanwhile, Vishwakarma also performed penance to have a daughter, and Sanjna was born, who was the embodiment of action and the bestower of success. She was given to me by Vishwakarma. Brahma then anointed me as the king of the luminaries and planets, which brought me great joy. I became the foundation of the three worlds, providing rain and sustenance at the right time. I am the giver of the fruits of actions, the foundation of all deeds, and the inner self of all beings in the Vedas.

However, after a long time, pride arose in my mind, thinking I was independent and that the world existed because of me. I, the son of Kashyapa, failed to worship Ganesha. As time passed, a terrible obstacle arose. The demons Mali and Sumali performed intense penance for thousands of divine years, pleasing Shiva. Shiva granted them a boon, and they obtained a divine chariot as brilliant as the sun. Riding this chariot, they conquered the three worlds. After a long time, they began to harass me, traveling through the sky in their chariot like the sun. The brilliance of their chariot covered the entire universe, and they caused the disappearance of night everywhere. When I set, they remained in the sky, and when I rose, they were still there, creating a perpetual day. They mocked me with their sun-like chariot.

I became extremely agitated, O sages, and unable to bear their power, I struck their chariot. For seven days, this continued, and the world was bewildered. All actions ceased, and the earth and its people were scorched by the heat. Filled with pride and anger, I set the chariot on fire, and the demons fainted. Because they were scorched by my brilliance, Shiva arrived in a rage. With his trident, he severed my head, and I fell in Kashi and died.

The entire world was distressed by the absence of the Sun. Seeing this, the gods, led by Brahma and Vishnu, went to Shiva and pacified him. They asked, "O Lord, why have you killed the Sun, who sustains the world? Why are you destroying the world at the wrong time?" Hearing their words, Shiva was filled with sorrow and said, "This was destined. I was merely an instrument. This terrible obstacle was created by the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha). In the past, Ganesha was the one who granted me boons. I shall seek his refuge and restore the Sun with the power of Ganesha."

Saying this, Shiva meditated upon Ganesha in his heart and prayed for the life of the Sun. "O Lord of Obstacles, the Sun, who gives life to the world, has been killed by me. Restore him to life, or I shall abandon my body." Knowing Shiva's prayer, Ganesha, who is the Self, restored the Sun to life. Seeing the son of Kashyapa risen, the gods were overjoyed and praised Ganesha with shouts of victory.

Chapter 3 : The Granting of Boons to the Sun

The Sun said: O great sages, after I was born in Kashi as Lolarka, I was deeply astonished in my mind, wondering what the basis of this existence might be. I am the soul of all, so how can the soul die? If I were to die, who would sustain the world with life? By whom am I kept alive? I am not the soul of embodied beings, yet due to delusion, I have become vain and arrogant. Therefore, I shall leave everything and go to the forest; I will constantly worship that which is the foundation of the universe.

As I left my home, the Creator (Brahma), the knower of the supreme truth, met me and comforted me with the words of the Vedas. He said: O Sun, listen to this secret which brings peace to all; why do you struggle in vain? You will soon be at peace. You are the soul of all beings, and this world is known to be supported by the soul. In the Brahma-Veda, it is said that the soul never dies. The Lord of the Brahmas, Ganesha, resides as the master; from Him, various types of Brahmas emerge. O son of Kashyapa, He exists everywhere through Yoga. When He is present, how can the world be destroyed? Tell me. You attained the status of Kashyapa’s son through a boon, but due to being covered by ignorance, you did not worship the Lord of the Ganas. Because of that, a great and strange obstacle arose. See the death of the soul as your own; worship Him, and through Him, everything will become auspicious and complete again, O Sun. You will become a follower of Ganesha, possessing the peace of Yoga.

Aryama said: Hearing these words of the Creator, I was astonished and spoke to Him for the sake of knowledge regarding the Lord of the Ganas. Listen to my actions. O Lord Brahma, you have enlightened me; please dispel the doubt that has arisen in me. Ganesha is described as the embodiment of Yoga and peace; how can He, who is full of attributes, create obstacles?

Brahma said: Listen, O wise Sun, to these words that destroy doubt, through which you will become a devotee of Ganesha. It is certain that Ganesha is the embodiment of Yoga and peace. O respectful one, the diverse universe was created by Him through Brahma. Within it, the delusion of Maya creates confusion everywhere. Due to this confusion, the world and Brahma seem to be one. To destroy that delusion, the Elephant-faced Lord created the path of Yoga, which provides peace to all who seek it. Those who are His partial manifestations, such as Shiva and others, became established in their own ego, thinking they were the ultimate Brahma. To grant them peace, the Elephant-faced Lord, the creator of obstacles, humbles them so that they become devoid of pride. With a pride-free nature, they desire the eternal Yoga, which is the root of all worlds and Brahmas. By practicing great Yoga, they become peaceful. Dhundhi (Ganesha) is always the remover of obstacles for them. Therefore, those who seek peace worship the Lord of Obstacles, who is full of Yoga and peace, with a heart full of devotion. For this reason, the Lord of Ganesha creates supreme obstacles for gods, sages, and the virtuous, but not for the wicked.

Hearing the words of Brahma, the Sun, filled with devotion, became pleased and spoke to the all-knowing Creator for the sake of Yoga. The Sun said: O Brahma, tell me the supreme Yoga to attain Ganesha. By practicing it, I shall become a devotee of Ganesha.

Brahma said: You have asked well, my son. You know everything, yet having been born from Kashyapa, you have forgotten it all. Now listen to the supreme knowledge of Ganesha; by practicing it, you will eventually become a devotee of Ganesha. Know that Maya-shakti is of the nature of non-existence and diverse forms, O wise one, while you are the soul of all and are called the nature of existence. The creator of both is the ever-blissful Vishnu, the sustainer of balance. Know that Shiva is the manifest form, devoid of the three, and is united with the three. The union of the four is called Svananda (self-bliss). Beyond Svananda, there is no supreme Brahma. He is the Lord of the Ganas, the player of Maya, who resides in the world and in Brahma through the union of non-duality. In the state of non-union, devoid of Maya, Ganesha is the indicator of Brahma, devoid of all unions, and the giver of the happiness of Samadhi. In the union of union and non-union, Ganesha is the indicator of peace; He is the giver of Yoga-peace and is called the peace of all peaces. Know that everything consisting of mind and speech is indicated by the letter 'Ga', and that which is beyond mind and speech is born from the letter 'Na'. Their master is Ganesha, who is called the Brahma of Brahmas. See Him in the Sama Veda, O Sun, and worship Him according to the scriptures. His Maya is twofold, known as Siddhi and Buddhi. Accompanied by them, the Lord of the Ganas plays in various ways. The mind is fivefold, O son, and there is no doubt it is the form of intellect. Siddhi is called the power of prosperity, and it is for the sake of enjoyment in the five. By practicing Yoga, abandon the reflection of these two, and you will undoubtedly become a holder of the peace of Yoga, my son.

Having said this, the Grandfather (Brahma) gave the six-syllabled mantra of Ganesha to the Sun according to the rites. Bowing to the Creator, the Sun went to a beautiful forest and performed severe penance, remaining without food. He chanted the supreme mantra of Ganesha in his heart. After a thousand years, the Elephant-faced Lord was pleased. He came to the Sun with great respect and devotion, served by His Ganas, and enlightened him.

Ganesha said: O Sun, choose various supreme boons from me. I will grant them to you, as I am pleased by your penance. Hearing the words of Ganesha, the Sun, filled with wisdom and seeing the Ganapati, stood up with devotion and confusion. He bowed and worshipped the Elephant-faced Lord according to the rites. The Sun, with a pure mind, prayed with folded hands.

The Sun said: Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, the creator of obstacles for the wicked, and the remover of obstacles for the devotees. Salutations to Ganesha. Salutations to the four-armed one, the rider of the mouse, the one with the serpent as a sacred thread, and the one whose navel is the universe. Salutations to the great-bellied god, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, the one with winnowing-basket ears, the complete one, and the one with a single tusk. Salutations to the elephant-faced one, the one with the moon on his forehead, and the one who holds the Chintamani in his heart. Salutations to the red-robed, red-colored one, adorned with various ornaments, the immeasurable one, and the one who performs various divine plays. Salutations to the one who is the support of all, yet has no support, the dweller of Svananda, and the one who plays in the ocean of nectar. Salutations to Heramba, salutations to the big-bellied one, salutations to the vessel of the worlds and Brahmas, and the Lord of Brahmas. Salutations to the formless one, the protector of all, and salutations to the one with form, O Dhundhi, and salutations to the Yoga of both. Salutations to the giver of Yoga-peace, the embodiment of Yoga-peace, and the Lord of Yoga who grants Yoga to the yogis.

As the Sun prayed with the essence of devotion, he suddenly became choked with emotion and his hair stood on end. Seeing him in such a state, the Lord of the Ganas spoke to the Sun, who was drenched in the nectar of devotion.

Ganesha said: This hymn you have composed will be all-fulfilling. It grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, O Sun, and increases my love. Whatever you desire will be fulfilled. For those who read or hear it, it will increase eternal bliss. Ask for boons from me; I will grant them, as I am highly pleased by your penance and this hymn.

Thus addressed by Ganesha, the Sun, filled with devotion, spoke to the Lord of the Ganas with a heart full of joy. The Sun said: O Lord of the Ganas, if you are pleased and will grant me a boon, then grant me firm devotion to you. When I remember you, show me your form. May whatever I desire be fulfilled, O Lord.

Saying "Om" (so be it), Ganesha disappeared right there. The Sun remained there, feeling sad, along with the Valakhilya sages. Then, the Sun, with respect, installed an image of Ganesha and worshipped it with devotion along with the Brahmins. Since then, having returned home, I have made an image and always worship the Lord Ganesha. O Brahmins, I have told you all my actions. O noble Valakhilyas, what more do you wish to hear?

Chapter 4 : The Story of Kama

Mudgala said: Having heard the story of the Sun, which is filled with devotion to Ganesha, the Valakhilya sages were delighted and asked again with great respect.

The Valakhilya sages said: O Lord of the Sun, you have narrated a wonderful story. We are extremely satisfied by the nectar of your words. Now, O Lord of gods, please tell us about the story of Ganesha, which destroys the bonds of delusion, so that one may become free from all illusions. Oh, the greatness of delusion! Even the great Ganesha was covered by it, and because of that, people were bewildered and could not recognize the Elephant-faced Lord.

Mudgala said: Being asked thus by the great sages, the glorious Sun, who is a devotee of Ganesha, spoke with a joyful heart.

The Sun said: I shall narrate to you an ancient history, which contains the knowledge of Ganesha and the story of the demon Mohasura. Once, there was a terrible and wicked demon named Taraka, who, by the boon of Brahma, became the master of the three worlds. All the gods and sages were oppressed by him and lived in forests, filled with fear and sorrow. After a long time, the gods and sages, absorbed in the meditation of Shiva, sought refuge in Goddess Parvati. They knew that a son born from the energy of Shiva would be the commander of the army and would slay Taraka; thus, they praised the Supreme Goddess.

Lord Shiva went to the forest and began penance. O Brahmanas, Parvati, accompanied by the gods, went to him. Seeing Shiva absorbed in meditation, and to create delusion, the Goddess took the form of a Bhilla (tribal) woman and went to his hermitage. She wandered here and there, beautiful and graceful, engaged in gathering flowers, increasing the great delusion. Meanwhile, Shiva broke his meditation and saw the radiant and youthful woman. Seeing her, Shiva was suddenly overcome by delusion and, with a heart agitated by desire, followed her. Seeing the Bhilla woman full of charm, he was enchanted and tried to catch her, but she ran away. Shiva, the Lord himself, followed her, deeply agitated. She wandered near him wherever she went.

O sages, for a moment, the naked Shiva wandered, trying to catch her, but she did not come into his hands. Then, his divine energy fell, and the Bhilla woman vanished. Shiva became calm. From that energy of Shiva, a great, fierce, and four-armed being was born, who was the embodiment of delusion. He lived alone in the forest, handsome and proud. He ate roots and fruits and wandered through various forests. Shiva, sitting there, became very sorrowful. Knowing through meditation that this was the play of the Goddess, he became angry and immediately burned Kama (the god of desire). Shiva then returned to his abode and saw Parvati.

Requested by Parvati for the sake of the gods, the Lord remembered Kama, and he saw him standing before him without a body (Ananga). Bowing to the great Lord Shiva and praising him, Kama said, "O Maheshwara, please give me back my body." Hearing the words of Kama, Sadashiva gave him the single-syllable mantra of Ganesha and told him to worship the Lord with it, promising that his desires would be fulfilled.

Kama went to Nashik and performed penance with his wife, meditating on Ganapati and chanting the mantra. After a thousand years, Ganesha appeared and told him to ask for a boon. Seeing Ganesha, Kama stood up, bowed, and praised him with devotion.

Kama said: Salutations to Ganesha, the holder of the noose and the goad, the one who holds the tusk and grants fearlessness, the one with the mouse as his banner. Salutations to the Elephant-faced God, the one with winnowing-basket ears, the large-bellied one, the perfect one, the embodiment of complete bliss. Salutations to the one who is the form of all, the all-pervading, the one who differentiates between the gross and the subtle. Salutations to the Lord of Gunas. Salutations to the one who is the body and the soul, the form of nature, the formless one, the Lord of self-realization. Salutations to the one who creates through Rajas, sustains through Sattva, and destroys through Tamas. Salutations to the one who is the form of the world, both moving and stationary, the formless Heramba, the Lord of Brahman. O Lord of the Ganas, you are beyond the reach of the Vedas and the giver of yoga. I am a person of little knowledge; how can I praise you? Salutations to you. Now, O Elephant-faced Lord, grant me the boon of firm devotion to you and give me a beautiful body, O Large-bellied One.

O Brahmanas, having said this, Kama bowed with his wife. Then, Ganesha, who is fond of his devotees, replied.

Ganesha said: O Lord of Rati, my devotion will be intense for you. Your body was burned by Shiva, and it is not possible to restore it as it was. By my boon, Shiva has achieved all his purposes; therefore, I shall give you a different body for your benefit. I have created various forms for you: youth, woman, fragrant flowers, sweet music, the soft sounds of birds, gardens, spring, sandalwood, the company of those attached to worldly objects, the soft and fragrant breeze, new clothes, and ornaments. With these, you will conquer Shiva and others as before. Let your names be Manobhu (born of the mind) and Smritibhu (born of memory). This hymn composed by you will grant all desires and will be the cause of growth for those whose roots have been burned.

Hearing the words of Ganesha, Kama said: O Lord, you have done everything for my benefit. Still, O Dhundhi, please give me my previous body. I have been burned by Shiva; O Lord of obstacles, please protect me, the bodiless one.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Kama, Ganesha, moved by his devotion, spoke to him, as he was highly pleased by his penance.

Ganesha said: You have asked for the impossible, yet because of your great devotion, I am pleased and will do everything you desire. In the Yadu dynasty, Lord Vishnu himself will be born as Krishna. You will be born as the son of Rukmini, who is an aspect of Lakshmi. You will be famous as Pradyumna, possessing a beautiful body and respected by all. This wife of yours will be your spouse there.

Having said this, Ganesha returned to his abode of bliss. Kama, with his wife, went back to the Mayura region. There, he worshipped the Lord of obstacles according to the rituals and performed penance to reside there. After one year, the Lord of obstacles was pleased, and having been worshipped and praised, he granted him a boon. Kama, full of devotion, knowing that the body and other worldly things are perishable, said:

Kama said: This is the land of Bhuswananda; I wish to reside here. O Lord of the Ganas, give me a place here to serve you. O Ganesha, grant me that by dying here, I may attain the state of Brahman. Therefore, O Lord, please protect me.

Then the Lord of the Ganas said, "So be it." The Lord of obstacles then spoke to the Shakti residing at the western gate: "O Goddess, on your left and right, keep Rati and Kama in this region for the sake of their residence." Saying "So be it," Kama, with his wife, was placed there by the divine illusion of Ganesha. He resided in the Mayura region to serve the Lord. From that day on, O sages, Kama became respected by all and, as the conqueror of the three worlds, he deluded everyone without fear.

Whoever hears or reads this wonderful story of Kama will never be overcome by the delusion of desire.

Thus ends the fourth chapter named "The Story of Kama" in the third section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 5 : Skanda Mahatmya

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Thereafter, filled with desire, Lord Shankara enjoyed with Parvati, filled with joy, for the sake of begetting a son. O best of sages, sixty thousand years passed while the great Lord Maheshwara was engaged in this sport. Lord Shambhu, united with Mahamaya, remained unaware of day and night. Then, the gods, desiring to interrupt the union of Maheshwara, sent Agni (the Fire God) to him. Disguised as a brahmin, he went and asked for alms. Hearing this, both of them arose.

The seed of Shiva fell upon the ground as he stood up. O Brahmins, Parvati gave that very seed to the mendicant. Shiva’s seed was then consumed by Agni, who, feeling a burning sensation, cast it into the Ganga. For the sake of the Kartik bath, the six Krittikas arrived, and they drank that wonderful seed during their ablutions. After bathing, they returned to their respective homes, radiant and bright. However, their husbands, seeing them pregnant with another’s seed, abandoned them.

They went to a cluster of reeds, left the great seed there, bathed, and returned home. There, a six-faced child was born and shone brilliantly. Narada, passing by, saw the infant and, realizing through meditation who he was, went to Kailasa. There, the great sage narrated the incident to Shakti. Wandering at will, he then went to Parvati and told her about the child.

The illustrious Parvati took the child and suckled him. Agni, Ganga, the Krittikas, and other mothers all came to him. Shiva honored all the mothers, and they returned to their homes. Then, the child was consecrated by Brahma, Shiva, and other gods and sages. He went with an army to become the commander of the divine forces, having been prayed to by the gods to destroy Tarakasura.

A fierce battle ensued between Skanda and Taraka. Skanda struck him repeatedly with his power. The great demon fainted but regained consciousness, and in his rage, he struck Skanda with a mace. Seeing him fallen, the gods fled. The demon took Kartikeya and went to his own abode. There, the gods revived him with medicinal herbs. Skanda, deeply distressed, sought refuge in Shankara. He asked, "O Brahma, tell me the way to victory and the path to Shiva’s peace. I wish to worship the one who is the fulfiller of all desires."

Then, Shambhu gave him the six-letter mantra of Ganesha and imparted the highest knowledge to Vishakha (Skanda). Shambhu said, "Listen, Skanda, to the great yoga by which you will find peace. By realizing the Chintamani (wish-fulfilling gem) within the mind, we attained peace in the past. Know that whatever is obtained by the mind is perishable. Know the mind to be of the nature of Brahman through the practice of yoga. All distinctions of internal and external arise from the mind. The Omkara, combined with meaning, is undoubtedly of the nature of the mind. Know that the mind is the creator of both bondage and liberation; it is the mind that creates delusion through the intellect. Self-bliss and liberation are undoubtedly the nature of the mind. What more to say? The mind is everything. Know it to be of the nature of Brahman and the world. Abandon the five-fold mind through yoga. The Brahman reflected in the mind appears as illusion; abandon that reflection and become the Chintamani, my son. By abandoning the mind, you will not be different from Me. This is the supreme knowledge, which they call the form of peace."

Ravi said: Hearing the words of Shiva, Kartikeya bowed to him and went to the forest. Meditating on Vighnesha (Ganesha) according to the prescribed rites, Skanda worshipped him continuously with the six-letter mantra and performed great penance. Living only on air, he remained devoted to japa and meditation. In the Dandaka forest, he pleased Gajanana (Ganesha). After a thousand years, he attained the yoga of peace. O Brahmins, Kartikeya continued to worship Vighnesha. Pleased, Ganesha went to his great devotee. Seeing him, Kartikeya bowed and worshipped him with devotion. Then, the radiant Kartikeya praised Vighnesha with folded hands.

Skanda said: "Salutations to you, the embodiment of yoga, the one who possesses the manifested and unmanifested forms, and the union of both. On your left side is the delusion-like Siddhi, the giver of all, O Lord. On your right side is the intellect, the holder of delusion. You reside in the union of Maya-Siddhi and the intellect, O Ganesha; salutations to you. The letter 'Ga' represents the world, and 'Na' represents Brahman. Salutations again and again to you, Ganesha, the union of both. The four-fold world is the Brahman-system; you are the four-armed one, salutations to you. You are the player in the Brahman that is self-evident. You dwell in self-bliss; salutations to the Lord of self-bliss. You move in the hearts of devotees, and because you dwell there like a thief, you have the mouse as your vehicle, O Lord. Dwelling in the Brahman of the world, you enjoy the fruits of yoga. Your actions are not known by the worlds or the Brahmans. Because you are an enjoyer like a thief, your vehicle is the mouse; salutations to Heramba, who rides the mouse. How can I praise you, O Lord of the Ganas, the supreme holder of yogic peace? Even the Vedas have attained peace through you; therefore, I bow to you."

Having heard this hymn, Ganesha said to him, "Ask for a boon, O great one, for I will grant you even the unattainable. This hymn composed by you will grant yogic peace and devotion to me, and will bestow all success. I will grant whatever is desired by those who read or listen to this hymn, especially Kartikeya." Hearing these words of Ganesha, the yogi Kartikeya, filled with joy, spoke to the Lord of the Ganas.

Skanda said: "What shall I ask, O Ganesha, when everything is full of illusion? Therefore, O Lord of obstacles, grant me firm devotion to you." The Lord of the Ganas, who is fond of his devotees, said, "O sinless one, your devotion to me will always be intense. You will kill the great demons like Taraka. Whatever you desire will be fulfilled." Having said this, Ganesha disappeared. Kartikeya remained there, remembering the Lord of the Ganas. He had the brahmins install a supreme image of Ganesha, which he named 'Laksha Vinayaka' as instructed by the sages. By the mere sight of it, a man attains his desires, and upon dying there, he becomes one with Brahman.

Then, the powerful Kartikeya went to his home and, along with the gods, killed the fierce Tarakasura. After killing the demon, he went to the Ganesha-kshetra and worshipped Vighnesha there with great devotion daily. Thus, the Skanda Mahatmya, which grants all things, has been narrated. Whoever reads or hears it attains the state of Brahman.

Thus ends the fifth chapter named Skanda Mahatmya in the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 6 : Coronation of Mohasura

The Valakhilyas said: O highly fortunate one, we have heard everything regarding the knowledge of Brahman from the Sun, yet we are not satisfied, just as one is never satisfied by drinking nectar. Now, O Lord of the Universe, tell us the greatness of Moha. Born from the energy of Shiva, what did he do? Tell us, O Sun.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of the sages, the Sun, who is the inner soul of all, spoke to them about the deeds of Moha.

The Sun said: Moha, born from the energy of Shiva, was extremely beautiful. He went to take refuge with Shukracharya, the best among the knowers of the Vedas. Having bowed to him, he humbly narrated his entire history and the nature of his origin. After telling this, he said, "Master, instruct me, your disciple. Tell me, O great sage, what work should I, who am without a lord, perform under your guidance?"

Hearing his words, Shukra, filled with compassion, performed all the rites including the sacred thread ceremony for the demon. He accepted Moha as his son and taught him all the Vedas and other scriptures. A demon named Pramadasura gave his daughter, who was endowed with beauty and charm and named Madira, to him.

Moha, having bowed to his father and having received the great mantra from Shukra, went to the Sun's abode and performed supreme penance. He fixed his gaze upwards, meditating on the Sun within the solar disc, and chanted the mantra with devotion, observing a strict fast. After a thousand divine years, the Sun was pleased and, filled with devotion for his devotee, went to grant him a boon.

Seeing Moha reduced to skin and bones, the Sun was astonished at the great courage of the demon. The Sun, the best of gods, himself awakened him and said, "O great Moha, ask for a boon; I am pleased and will grant it to you."

Hearing the Sun's voice, the leader of the demons awakened. Seeing the Sun standing before him, he bowed with folded hands. Rising, he worshipped him with various offerings and, with folded hands, praised him.

Mohasura said: Salutations to the Sun, the best of those who shine, the one who takes all forms, the light, the illuminator, salutations again and again. Salutations to you, the giver of the fruits of actions, the foundation of karma, the embodiment of karma, and the Lord who is the Self. Salutations to you, the Supreme Soul, who assumes many forms yet is devoid of all distinctions, and who is the embodiment of eternal nectar. Salutations to you, O Sun, who is the truth, the vessel of truth, the infinite and boundless form, and the life-force of the universe. You are the knowledge of the three Vedas, the three Vedas themselves, and the initiator of Vedic rituals. You are the root of all ego and the destroyer of all ego. Salutations to you, the giver of knowledge, the light of lights, the beginningless, the great Lord, and the supreme master. You are the seed of rain for all, the mover of time and karma, the support of Maya, and the controller of Maya. How can I praise you, O Lord, who embodies the soul? Yet, based on the authority of knowledge, I praise and bow to you. If you are pleased and have come to grant a boon, then grant me this: let me never die. Since everything that possesses a name and form is subject to death, let me be free from it. Grant me the kingdom of the three worlds, O Lord of the Universe. Grant me a body endowed with health and strength, surpassing all others, and grant me victory in battle. May everything I desire be easily attainable for me, and grant me devotion to your lotus feet, O great Lord.

Having said this, he bowed to the Sun. Rising, he stood near him with humility. Then, the Sun, astonished, said to him, "O demon, you shall attain everything you have requested. This hymn composed by you, which increases my joy, will grant all desires to anyone who listens to it."

Having said this, the deity vanished and returned to his abode. The demon, filled with joy, went to his own home. He bowed to his mother and father and honored his friends and relatives. Then he went to Shukra, bowed to him, and narrated the entire event. After worshipping him, he returned home and built a city. The sage named it Vishayavasaka, and the highly radiant Shukra resided there.

Hearing this news, demons from here and there came and settled in his city. The city, adorned with the four varnas, shone brightly; it was incomparable, surpassed all others, and provided full enjoyment on earth. Then, all the great demon leaders, led by Prachanda, Chanda, Mahakala, and Kalantaka, came there, inspired by Shukra, and stood in his service.

Then, the sage Shukra himself performed the coronation of the demon king to establish his kingdom. The demons were delighted and returned to their respective places, while the main sages remained there, eager to serve. Thus, Mohasura became the supreme leader of the demons, and relying on him, the great demons wandered the earth in joy.

Chapter 7 : The Description of the Victory of Mohasura

The Sun God said: From Madira, five sons were born, who were powerful, brilliant, and possessed the same nature and character as their father. Ugra, Krura, Medhavi, Shochana, and Harana—these five, well-versed in the scriptures, shone in that city, O Brahmins.

After some time had passed, the great King Mohasura summoned all the demon lords with his men. Having gathered them, he spoke words that were beneficial, full of policy, pleasant, and affection-increasing.

Mohasura said: O lords of the demons, headed by Prachanda, listen to my beneficial words. I shall now conquer the entire world. If it is agreeable to your minds, O demons, then I shall certainly accomplish the task for all of you; there is no doubt about this. Protected by Shukra, and by the boon of the Sun, we shall become invincible and powerful.

Hearing this, they bowed to him and praised the great-souled, fortunate one, who was endowed with the brilliance of penance. The demon lords said: O Lord, we have considered this well and are satisfied by the nectar of your words. We shall conquer all the worlds, being under your command. There is no one in the world equal to you in prowess, O Lord. Therefore, we shall now conquer the gods, led by Vishnu, and others.

The Sun God said: Hearing their words, the powerful Mohasura commanded the demon lords to be ready with joy. They, being experts in the ways of war, went before him, armed with various weapons, ready like those destined for Yama. Seeing them, the powerful and heroic demon was delighted; he himself mounted his chariot, armed, and set out with the demons.

He shone in the midst of his four-fold army like the presiding Lord Rudra, causing fear to all. Accompanied by Shukra himself, and intent on conquering the earth, he wandered over the surface of the world with the demons. His boundless army covered the earth, and the Sun was obscured by the dust raised by their movement. Who could be capable of fighting on the earth against these great demons who were like death itself? They had conquered everything by their own brilliance.

Having conquered the earth with its seven islands, those best of demons went to conquer the gods, led by Vishnu. Hearing that Mohasura had arrived with his army, the gods, distressed by fear, sought refuge with Indra. Together with Indra, they all went to Brahma, and with him, they sought refuge with Shankara at Kailasa. Accompanied by Shankara, the gods went to Vaikuntha to see Keshava, and with Keshava, they all went to the Sun God.

The Sun God, sighing, spoke words of benefit to the fearful lords of the gods. The Sun God said: Because of the boon granted by me, the great demon Mohasura is powerful; who among the lords of the gods is capable of defeating him? The fruits of actions are severe, and they cannot be averted by anyone. Therefore, let us live in the forests today, filled with fear. Abandoning all kingdoms and the like, let us protect our bodies. After his merit is exhausted, his death will surely come; there is no doubt. Otherwise, the powerful demon king Mohasura will kill the lord of the gods; therefore, follow my advice.

Hearing this, Brihaspati, the preceptor of the gods, who was well-versed in all policies and highly renowned, spoke. Brihaspati said: Listen, O lords of the gods, to the beneficial words. The words spoken by the Sun are indeed beneficial for us. Then all the gods said, "Well said, well said," and agreed to follow the advice of the Sun and the preceptor. Having decided this, the lords of the gods went to the forest and stayed in caves and mountain regions, filled with fear.

Mohasura, with all his demons, went to the cities of the gods, but not finding the lords of the gods there, he was very happy. Having stationed his demons there, he himself sat on the throne of Indra and enjoyed supreme pleasures, served by the demons. The Gandharvas and Apsaras, trembling with fear, served him, as did the inhabitants of the heavenly world and the great sages.

Then, the powerful Mohasura went to Satyaloka with all the demon lords, but he did not see the Grandfather (Brahma) there. Seeing the city empty, he was filled with joy and went to Vaikuntha, but he saw it in the same state. Then, having come to Kailasa and seeing it deserted by Shankara, the great demon went to the solar realm. Finding it devoid of the Sun, he went to the realm of Shakti. Seeing it devoid of Shakti, the demon leader rejoiced.

Then, the chief of the demon lords installed his sons, who were proud of their strength, in the cities of the gods. The great demon made his son Ugra the lord of Kailasa, and similarly, the cruel one was installed in Vaikuntha with demons. He made the proud and intelligent one the lord of the solar realm, and in the realm of Shakti, he placed the war-loving Shochana. He made Harana, the destroyer of all, the lord of Satyaloka, and placed Prachanda and others as the lords of Indraloka and other realms.

Then, Kalantaka was sent to the nether regions (Patala), and the soldier went to the serpent king Shesha. When the serpent king saw the demon king Kalantaka arrive, he approached him with humility and made peace with the demon. Thus, the powerful demon leader Mohasura conquered the universe and rejoiced with his demon lords.

Then, the demon went to his own city with all his followers. He shone in that city of his residence. Staying there, he ruled the crowded universe. The demon king, accompanied by his demon lords, ruled the kingdom. He enjoyed various incomparable pleasures, and being fond of worldly objects, he felt he had accomplished his purpose, filled with pride along with the demons.

Chapter 8 : The Bestowing of Boons by the Lord of Devas and Sages

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

The Sun God said: Then, the powerful and highly arrogant demon Mohasura made an effort to destroy the lives of the gods. For this purpose, he sent out extremely fierce demons, who diligently set out to dismantle the path of rituals. In a short time, O Brahmins, the earth was rendered devoid of rituals by the demons, and those who performed them were terrorized. Everywhere, the earth was stripped of the Varna and Ashrama systems, and the very foundation of Dharma was gradually destroyed.

There was no Svaha or Svadha anywhere, O great sages. The demons respectfully installed the idol of Mohasura. They forced people everywhere to worship it, punishing those who refused with severe beatings. Consequently, the groups of sages became particularly degraded; some died, while others fled to deep, terrifying forests. Even there, filled with fear, the sages were unable to perform their rites. Thus, the demon, an enemy of the gods, caused the severance of Dharma.

Then, all the gods, filled with great fear and observing fasts, held a council. The gods and the best of Brahmins gathered with the sages to find a way to destroy Mohasura, but they could not find a suitable solution. Then Aryama (the Sun God) said to them: "Listen, O best of gods and noble sages, to my beneficial words. Since everything is composed of name and form, death does not truly exist. I shall tell you the solution regarding Mohasura. This demon will be destroyed by the hand of Ganesha. He is said to be the embodiment of Yoga himself, so let us worship him. He, residing in his own realm, will undoubtedly destroy the lord of the demons through his own divine consciousness. Therefore, O lords of the gods, praise him."

Hearing the words of the Sun, the gods, led by Vishnu, were filled with joy and spoke these excellent words. The gods said: "You have spoken well, O Sun; you are considered the most intelligent. There is no doubt that you are the giver of life to all."

Then the gods and Brahmins, through penance, diligently worshipped the remover of obstacles, the leader of the Ganas. They worshipped him continuously with the single-syllable mantra, chanting the primordial mantra while meditating on Ganesha in their hearts. The sages and the lords of the gods, engaged in various penances and fasting, pleased Vighneshwara.

After hundreds of years had passed, the leader of the Ganas, highly pleased with their devotion, appeared to grant them a boon. He arrived riding a mouse, full of divine radiance, complete in form, with an elephant face, four arms, full of compassion, and adorned with a large belly. He was the Lord with one tusk, large eyes, and winnowing-basket-like ears. Accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, and adorned with a serpent around his navel, he wore the Chintamani gem around his neck and a serpent as his sacred thread. Thus, the Lord appeared before the gods and sages.

Seeing him, the gods and Brahmins were filled with joy and devotion; they bowed down and worshipped him according to the scriptural rites. Then, with folded hands and great respect, they praised him with enthusiasm, according to their understanding.

The gods and sages said: "Salutations to you, O Lord of the Ganas, who possess the form of a large belly. You are the protector of the helpless; salutations to you again and again. Salutations to the Lord of primordial success, the Lord of wisdom, Dhundhi, and Heramba, who bestows everything upon his devotees. Salutations to you, the four-armed one with winnowing-basket-like ears. Salutations to you, O Lambodara (the large-bellied one), the Lord of obstacles, the remover of obstacles. Salutations to the elephant-faced God, the great-souled one who rides a mouse, who has the serpent at his navel, and who possesses one tusk. Salutations to you, O Great Ganapati, the cause of all causes, the destroyer of the obstacles of those who desire the fulfillment of their wishes. Salutations to you, who are the refuge of Maya, the support of Maya, and the destroyer of the desires of those who are not your devotees. Salutations to you, the Lord of Yogas, the giver of Yoga, the imperishable one, who dwells in self-bliss and is the embodiment of Yoga. You reside in the bellies of all and consume all enjoyments; there is no one who can consume you in their belly, hence you are Mahodara (the large-bellied one). Who can know that God who resides in the Brahman of consciousness within the union of the body and the soul? Yet, you have come within the range of our vision. You have appeared out of affection for your devotees, and by this, we are truly fulfilled, O Lord of the gods. We are blessed to have seen you, the one who plays within the bellies of all, the Mahodara, the pleased soul, the Lord who is the master of Brahman himself. How can I praise you, O Lord of the Ganas, when you reside even in the bellies of the Vedas? We bow to that eternal one who is attainable through Yoga."

The Sun God said: Hearing this praise, the Lord of Ganesha, pleased by their affection and bound by their devotion, spoke to them.

Mahodara said: "Choose whatever you desire, O lords of the gods and sages. Pleased by your devotion and this hymn, I shall grant you everything. This hymn composed by you will increase my pleasure; for those who read or hear it, O gods, it will grant all success."

Hearing his words, the gods, along with the sages, were filled with joy and, bowing down, spoke to the Lord of the Ganas.

The gods and sages said: "If you are pleased, O Lord of the gods and remover of obstacles, then grant us special devotion to your lotus feet. We have been displaced from our homes by the wicked Mohasura. The sages have been deprived of their rituals; destroy that arrogant one. He has conquered the universe, and the people there are in great misery. Be the giver of happiness to them; O Lord, salutations to you."

Hearing their words, Mahodara, with a pleased heart and out of affection for his devotees, said, "So be it."

Thus ends the eighth chapter of the third section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Bestowing of Boons by the Lord of Devas and Sages."

Chapter 9 : The Imparting of Knowledge to Mohasura

The Sun God said: Then, the powerful and glorious Mahodara, filled with rage and wielding his weapons like the battle-axe, shone brilliantly. Seated upon his mouse and accompanied by the host of gods and sages, the Lord set out toward the city to slay Mohasura. Meanwhile, the great demon Mohasura was seated in his city, surrounded by all the demon lords, shining with pride. Suddenly, Narada, ever devoted to singing, arrived there. Seeing him, Mohasura, along with all his retinue, bowed down. The great demon, seated, asked with humility: O Sage, tell me something wonderful, for you wander everywhere.

Hearing the demon's words, Narada, filled with joy, spoke to him a sacred yet poisonous tale. Narada said: O Mohasura, great soul, listen to my beneficial words. I shall tell you something wonderful that I have seen with a heart full of realization. The gods, requested by the sages dwelling in the forest, have now sought the Lord for the sake of your destruction, O demon king. That Lord, mounted on a mouse, has arrived to slay you and is now only ten yojanas away. Having said this, Narada became silent. The demon king became agitated with fear. Suppressing his intense sorrow, Mohasura, his heart trembling, spoke again to Narada.

Mohasura said: Listen, Narada, to my words; my death shall not come to pass. What can the Lord of gods do to one who is beyond name and form? He has come in the form of Mahodara, but I shall slay that Lord; there is no doubt about this. Hearing these words of the demon lord, the great yogi Narada spoke to Mohasura with deliberation. Narada said: Listen, Mohasura, to my beneficial words. Mahodara is neither a man, nor a god, nor a demon. That which is described in the Vedas as Brahman, devoid of name and form, has come here in the fullness of name and form. When he becomes angry, he will slay you; therefore, take refuge in the great Mahodara, and by that, you shall attain happiness.

Then the demon king, filled with fear, said: O Narada, knower of Yoga, tell me words to dispel my confusion. You say he is devoid of name and form, yet he has come as a being endowed with name and form? How can Brahman, which is both with and without name and form, exist everywhere? There is no doubt about this. Confusion born of self and other never exists in him; he is always endowed with equanimity toward gods, demons, and others. Why does this Mahodara, who promotes the side of the gods, intend to destroy the demons? O Brahmin, your words seem contradictory to me at this moment. O Lord of yogis, dispel my doubt. Tell me of the other deeds of Mahodara, O Brahmarshi. What is his form, his nature, whose portion is he, and what is his prowess? Where does he dwell, and what is his Brahman? O ocean of compassion, explain all this knowledge to me in detail.

The Sun God said: Being thus asked, the great yogi and devotee of Ganesha, the glorious Narada, spoke to the great demon Mohasura as before. Narada said: O Mohasura, great soul, your intellect is approved by yogis. You have asked about the form of Yoga, and I am greatly pleased by that. Therefore, O demon, I shall tell you everything in detail. Listen with a focused mind to the supreme deeds of Dhundhi (Ganesha) that grant Yoga. Know that the entire fourfold world is composed of name and form. That which is devoid of name and form is the embodied form that is beneficial to the world. In the union of these two, O demon king, knowledge is universally accepted. Know with your heart that which is described as Brahman in the Vedic discourses. It is always endowed with name and form, and it is always devoid of name and form. That Brahman, existing in its fullness, is the speaker of Yoga. O chief of demons, know that he is indeed Mahodara.

The enjoyment created by the body and the embodied soul is the supreme giver of bliss to one's own self. He who pervades everywhere and consumes all enjoyment is called Mahodara (the Great-Bellied). If enjoyment is sought by those who are externally oriented, they meet with death in a short time. But the enjoyment that is full of self-bliss is experienced in every way by all Brahmas and all worlds. The enjoyment that is different in the world is immortal in the Self. Know that the enjoyment of one essence and the enjoyment of the world are both considered to be experienced. Know by the service of Yoga that the belly in the bodies and the belly in the Self are of one essence, O demon. If those two, the souls of the world, were devoid of enjoyment, why would they have made such an effort? The world, endowed with name and form, was born for the sake of enjoyment, and the embodied one, devoid of name and form, was born for that purpose. If there is no desire for enjoyment, why would there be two types? Therefore, know, O demon king, that they are specifically endowed with enjoyment.

This Lord of the Ganas, who resides in the belly of the world-soul, is called Mahodara. O demon king, the belly of the two is called 'small' because of their respective enjoyments; think upon this, O great-minded one. He always resides in the bellies of all beings, and therefore he is called Mahodara by the Vedic scholars. The world is his body, and the Self is his head; in the union of these two, he is called Ganesha, the elephant-faced one. Know that the elephant form is indeed that from which everything is born and into which everything goes at the end. The two world-souls are united, and for the sake of knowledge, he became embodied through the worship of penance. Do not see his body as material like others, O demon king. He takes on a body by his own will for the sake of enjoyment and liberation. This Lord of the Ganas is attained through self-realized Yoga. Know that his city is everywhere.

Siddhi (perfection) is the illusion that causes confusion, and because of it, people wander. Intellect is said to be the bearer of confusion, specifically in the form of the mind. The mind is said to be fivefold, and that lordship is of the nature of illusion. By abandoning it, the master yogi attains peace through the service of Yoga. Therefore, Mahodara is called the master of Siddhi and Buddhi. Worship him, O great demon, otherwise he will slay you. The world, consisting of gods and demons, was created by him, and the Lord of Obstacles plays with it, endowed with his own laws. When the gods strive for the destruction of the demons, O Lord, then the Lord of Siddhi becomes the giver of success to the demon. Becoming embodied himself, the Lord becomes a bestower of boons; know him to be Mahodara, O demon, there is no doubt. When demons strive for the destruction of the gods in every way, then this embodied one slays the demons, there is no doubt. The gods, who are counted in the Vedas, are not specifically slain by him because they are devoid of growth and decay, O demon king. The demons are of infinite forms and are subject to growth and decay; therefore, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, slays them swiftly. When these gods and demons are engaged in their own respective duties, then this Lord remains in the belly as the form of Yoga. Everything has been told to you, O demon; he is called the Brahman-incarnate. From his parts, the entire world is born, and he is called Brahman. Therefore, take refuge in him if you wish to live, O demon king, whether for the sake of enjoyment or if you have a longing for liberation. Having said this, the ocean of compassion, Narada, disappeared, absorbed in singing the praises of Ganesha and playing his veena.

Chapter 10 : The Description of the Pacification of Mohasura

The Sun God said: After the sage departed, the highly fortunate Mohasura became very joyful. Remembering the words of Narada, he began to contemplate in his mind. Staying in seclusion, the demon became absorbed in the meditation of Ganesha. Upon realizing the greatness of Mahodara, he felt happy in his heart. He approached Shukracharya and narrated the entire account. Having been enlightened by the poetic words of Narada, he returned to his home, where he became enchanted by Ganesha. Mohasura, now free from his delusion, shone brilliantly himself.

In the meantime, Lord Vishnu arrived there, having been sent by the Lord of the Ganas to the demon for the purpose of reconciliation. The powerful Mohasura honored Keshava, worshipped the great demon, and offered him a beautiful seat. He asked the great Vishnu, "Why have you come here? O glorious Vishnu, tell me the reason, and I shall fulfill it."

Vishnu said: Mahodara, who is intent on protecting Dharma, has arrived. O Asura, I have been sent by him along with the gods and sages. You are blessed, O leader of the demons, for he desires friendship with you. The Lord of the Ganas is indeed the Brahman, the embodiment of the body and the soul. He is known as Mahodara by the seers who perceive the truth. Because he resides in the bellies of all and consumes all enjoyments, he is called Mahodara, while we are considered to have small bellies. The Lord is stationed ten yojanas away from the city, being praised by gods and sages. I am telling you exactly what he said; listen to those words, which are beneficial to all.

O King of demons, abandon hostility and remain happily in your own place. Let the gods receive their offerings and the Brahmins perform their duties. Let the people live according to their castes and stages of life, free from suffering. Do this, O great demon; otherwise, prepare to fight against the Lord of the demons. This is the message specifically conveyed to you by Ganesha. Do this, O noble one, and become a friend of the Mahatma.

The Sun God said: Hearing these beautiful words of Vishnu, the powerful Mohasura, whose soul was now pleased, spoke to him.

Mohasura said: O Vishnu, you speak well and for my benefit. I shall do as you say and honor Mahodara. Having said this, he quickly summoned the other demon leaders and conveyed to them the words spoken by Vishnu. Hearing the words of Mohasura, those great demons became filled with anger and, trembling with fear, retreated into the netherworld. Mohasura, along with Shukracharya, went to seek refuge in Ganesha.

Upon arriving, the demon king bowed to Mahodara. Filled with devotion, he worshipped him with great effort. After worshipping him according to the rites, he bowed again. With folded hands, Mohasura praised Mahodara.

Mohasura said: Salutations to you, O Brahman-form, O Mahodara of beautiful form. You are the enjoyer of all, the embodiment of the body and the soul. Salutations to the one who rides the mouse, the three-eyed one. Salutations to the four-armed Lord of the gods. Salutations to the beginningless one, the primordial form of all. Salutations to Vinayaka, Heramba, the protector of the distressed. Salutations to Ganesha, the Lord of self-bliss, the leader of Brahman. Salutations to the giver of success and wisdom, the one who is Brahman itself. Salutations to the giver of Brahman, the embodiment of yogic peace. Salutations to the Lord of yogis, the giver of yoga to the yogis. Salutations to the Lord of success and wisdom, the single-tusked one. Salutations to the brave, heroic one with ears like winnowing baskets. Salutations to the one who causes delusion in all, the giver of happiness to devotees, and especially the creator of obstacles for those who are not devoted. Salutations to the master of Maya, the foundation of Maya. Salutations to the one who causes confusion through Maya to those who are deluded. How can I praise you, O leader of the Ganas, when even the Vedas and their limbs have attained peace by praising you? My parents, my knowledge, my penance, and my studies are blessed. My body is blessed, O Lord of gods, for it has seen your lotus feet.

Having said this, he bowed down. Seeing his devotee filled with such emotion, Mahodara spoke these beautiful words.

Mahodara said: O Mohasura, ask for any boon you desire in your heart. I am now captivated by your devotion and shall grant it. I came here filled with anger to kill you, but I will not slay one who has come to me for refuge and is filled with devotion. This hymn of mine that you have composed will grant everything to those who read or hear it, O Asura. It will destroy delusion, grant worldly enjoyment and liberation, and provide wealth, grain, and the happiness of children and grandchildren.

The Sun God said: Hearing these words of Ganesha, Mohasura was filled with joy and spoke with folded hands.

Mohasura said: Salutations to you, O Mahodara. If you will grant me a boon, then grant me firm devotion to your lotus feet, O Lord. Grant me the status of being a friend of your devotee, and provide me with food and a place to stay; I shall remain there steadfastly.

Hearing these words of Mohasura, Mahodara, whose soul was pleased and satisfied by his devotion, replied.

Mahodara said: Your devotion to me will be intense, O wise one. You will undoubtedly attain the status of being a friend of my devotee. In matters of karma and knowledge, you will not need to perform rituals; simply by remembering me, O demon, you will become the enjoyer of all pleasures. Remain in your own place, O king of demons, and respectfully follow your own Dharma. Make men free from delusion and worship me.

Saying "So be it," the powerful Mohasura bowed to the Lord of the Ganas, returned to his own place, and became filled with peace.

Chapter 11 : The Disappearance of Mahodara

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

The Sun God said: When the demon Mohasura had attained a peaceful state, the lords of the gods, along with the sages, worshipped Mahodara. Having worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with devotion and humility, the divine sages, filled with joy, praised Him especially.

The divine sages said: Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who possesses immeasurable illusion and infinite valor, who is untainted, the Lord of the Ganas, who is free from deceit, and who enchants through illusion. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who remains beyond mind and speech, yet is the one who possesses the elephant-face that is endowed with mind and speech, and who is the embodiment of both union and separation. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the formless one, the bestower of knowledge, the enjoyer of the "I am" consciousness, and who always resides in the body that is the essence of the subtle point (Bindu).

Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the lord of qualities, the state of deep sleep, the subtle one, and the waking state, residing within Hari, Ishvara, Brahma, and others. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who assumes auspicious and inauspicious forms, who is worshipped at the beginning of all auspicious and inauspicious deeds, and who grants success in all actions. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the doer, the worthy vessel, the destroyer, the light of time, the Sun, the embodiment of action, and the giver of power. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is Yama, the Moon, the Wind, Kubera, Indra, Shiva, Fire, and Nirriti.

Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the infinite form, the lord of waters, the creator, the wielder of the noose, the guardian of the directions, and the embodiment of the directions themselves. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the balance of gods and demons, the creator who assumes the forms of both, and the embodiment of ghosts, Gandharvas, and Yakshas. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the earth, the light of water, the essence of water, the brilliance of fire, the wind, and the space that is the abode of all. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the beginning, middle, and end of all play, who is the wonder of the beginning, middle, and end, the light of truth, and the embodiment of truth.

Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the human, the one established in the social order (Varnashrama), the Brahmin, the king, the Vaishya, the Shudra, and the lowest. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is subject to the Guru, the householder, the forest-dweller, the renunciant, and the one who is beyond all social orders, O Yogi. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who resides in trees, creepers, and grass, who is the form of the mountains, the animals, and the birds. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the essence of taste, the lord of flavors, the food, the life-force, and the form of all moving and non-moving beings, O Lord of the Nagas.

Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is marked by the goad and other symbols, who has the mouse as a banner and vehicle, who possesses three eyes, and who has ears like winnowing baskets. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the lord of the world of bliss, the God of gods, the source of success and intellect, the Supreme Soul, the one-tusked, and the four-armed. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is worshipped by the primordial beings, adorned with beautiful garments and ornaments, and praised by the Ganas like Pramoda and Moda. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, whose lotus-hands are engaged in the play of the ocean of nectar, O Ganesha, Heramba, and protector of devotees.

Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who enters the womb of the universe to enjoy the play of existence, who is the embodiment of the universe, the perfect one, free from change, and the bestower of wisdom. Salutations to You, O Mahodara, who is the one with the small belly, yet all the gods and others are skilled in their own enjoyments because of You; You are the one who grants happiness to them all.

The Sun God said: As the divine sages praised Him thus, a sense of devotion arose in them, and they danced before Him. Seeing them with tearful eyes, horripilation, and choked voices, the Lord, who is fond of His devotees, spoke to them.

Ganesha said: O lords of the gods, O gods, and O sages, ask for boons. I am pleased with your devotion and hymns, and I shall grant your heart's desires. Hearing the words of Ganesha, the joyful sages bowed to the Great Soul and spoke to Mahodara.

The divine sages said: If You are pleased, O Lord of gods, then grant us supreme devotion. We are satisfied with that alone. By Your grace, the wicked Mohasura has been pacified, the entire world is fulfilled, and we are protected by Your boons. Now, O Ganapati, we are free from suffering. You are the Lord of the world, and You protect the helpless. Having said this, the sages bowed to Ganesha. Saying "So be it," Ganesha disappeared.

The gods and sages returned to their respective abodes, and the people, free from distress, became engaged in their own duties. Thus, O Brahmins, the Lord Ganapati turned the wicked Mohasura into a peaceful being and made the world free from delusion. Many incarnations of Mahodara are remembered, and it is impossible to describe all their stories. Rama, distressed by the separation from Sita, took refuge in Mahodara; through Him, he became peaceful and remained devoted to the Lord along with Sita. This place of Mahodara, established by the divine sages in the southern region, became famous on earth.

I have narrated to you this story of Mahodara, which destroys delusion and grants peace. Whoever reads or hears this will surely attain peace, worldly enjoyment, and liberation; there is no doubt about it. Deluded by ignorance, people call me the Supreme of the supreme, but those who possess little knowledge should understand this truth. I have told you all about my own story, which is filled with devotion to Ganesha; therefore, I worship Him.

Thus ends the eleventh chapter of the Mahodara-charita in the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 12 : Dialogue Between Nara-Narayana and Vishnu

Mudgala said: Having heard the wonderful story of Mahodara narrated by the Sun, the Valakhilyas were filled with astonishment. They held in their minds that this Ganesha is the giver of supreme peace and is undoubtedly the embodiment of Yoga; they felt enlightened by the Sun. Filled with joy, those Valakhilyas asked the Sun about the deeds of Mahodara that bestow peace.

The Valakhilyas said: O Sun, you are blessed, for you are the revealer of the peace of Yoga. We are now enlightened, and no doubts remain in us because of you. Blessed is the birth of Ganesha, and blessed is the story of that great Lord; having heard it, we are satisfied in our minds by your grace. You are omniscient, O fortunate one, you are Ganesha himself in person; you are the Sun in the form of the Soul, and there is no wonder in this. Now tell us, O Lord of gods, how Rama worshipped Ganesha with devotion and became the embodiment of peace. Blessed are those people in this world, O Sun, who ask about, read, or listen to the story of Ganesha that grants the peace of Yoga.

Mudgala said: Being thus asked by the Valakhilyas, the glorious Sun, having become joyful, said, "O sages, listen to that."

The Sun said: I shall narrate here an ancient history, the dialogue between Narayana and Markandeya, which grants success. The great sage Markandeya was born to Mrikandu; having performed his sacred thread ceremony, he performed supreme penance. Endowed with celibacy and being the best among those who know the Vedas and Shastras, he constantly chanted the Gayatri, especially desiring liberation. When ten thousand years had passed in his hermitage, Shankara arrived there, surrounded by his attendants and accompanied by Parvati. Markandeya, having fixed his mind on Vishnu in meditation, did not notice him. When Maheshana arrived, Shiva performed a wonder. By the power of his Yoga-Maya, Shiva entered the sage's heart and caused the Vishnu who was residing there to disappear. Seeing Shankara in his heart, the great sage was astonished; he broke his meditation, looked outside, and bowed to Shankara. Having praised Shambhu with the Atharvashirsha, he stood before him, bowing again and again with joy. Mahadeva said to him, "Ask for a boon that you desire in your heart. I am pleased by your penance and your chanting of the Gayatri, and I shall grant it."

"The Gayatri is the mother of the Vedas, and Omkara is called the father. Know, O best of ascetics, that we were born from Omkara. Therefore, I am undoubtedly pleased by your chanting of the Gayatri and your profound praise; O Brahmin, I shall grant what you desire in your heart." Hearing the words of Shiva, the great sage said to him, "Grant me your devotion and liberation from the ocean of worldly existence." Then Shiva, who is skilled in speech and full of satisfaction, especially for his devotees, said this to him:

Shiva said: O great sage, your devotion to me will be intense, and you will attain eternal and complete liberation, as well as the status of a Puranic teacher. Your lifespan will be seven kalpas, O great sage; whatever you desire will be fruitful, there is no doubt in this. Having said this, Shiva, along with his power, disappeared. The great sages were left in wonder. The power of the mantra, which is the embodiment of Gayatri, was proclaimed, and the deity in the form of Shambhu was conceived by the great soul. He worshipped Shambhu through the Shaiva path with a single-minded heart, knowing him to be the Lord of Time, the destroyer of delusion, and the knower of the essence. There, he composed the Purana that grants all success, which is endowed with the greatness of Shiva's power.

After a long time, the two great Lords, Nara and Narayana, went to the hermitage of that divine sage.

The Valakhilyas said: Tell us, O Sun, the auspicious character of those two. How did they come to be known as the Supreme Lords?

The Sun said: My son is the righteous Dharma, who is equal to all. O sages, he performed penance for Vishnu along with Kirti. When a hundred years were completed, Vishnu became the granter of boons. He said to them, "May you be our son," and he did accordingly. Thus, Nara and Narayana became their sons. Nara is said to be the embodiment of the living soul, and Narayana is the Supreme Shiva. The Brahman that is called Vaishnava by the wise in their union is why I have described them to you as the Supreme Lords. O Brahmins, having taken the command of Dharma, you went to Naimisharanya, and there those two gods performed supreme penance. Vishnu himself arrived there by his own will. Seeing him, the two gods bowed and praised Keshava. Having propitiated him with various hymns, they bowed to him with devotion. The great Vishnu said to them, "What do you desire, O great sages?" Then those two highly righteous ones said to Keshava, "O great one, tell us the Yoga that grants supreme peace." Being thus asked, the great Yogi said to them with joy, speaking of the Yoga that is complete, peace-giving, and always dear to the followers of Ganesha.

Vishnu said: The mind is said to be fivefold, and whatever is remembered as divine power is also that Maya; abandon it quickly and you will attain Yoga. Whatever is attained by the mind, O givers of honor, should be made into the form of Yoga through meditation on that form. Whatever is not known by the mind is the indicator of liberation; that itself should be made into the form of Yoga through the service of Yoga. The Chintamani (wish-fulfilling gem) always resides in the mind as the mover of the mind; worship him, O great ones, and by that, you will attain peace. The mind, endowed with Maya and delusion, is greedy for various objects; the mind that is free from Maya will always be the embodiment of peace. The mind desires Dharma, wealth, and desire with respect, and also liberation and the state of Brahman; it is undoubtedly filled with illusion. I am the Chintamani in person; there is nothing different from me. Why does this illusory nature make me wander? By this path of Yoga, O great sages, abandon the mind, and you will undoubtedly become the embodiments of the peace of Yoga.

Having said this, Vishnu disappeared and went to Vaikuntha. Nara and Narayana remained there, absorbed in Yoga.

Thus ends the twelfth chapter named the dialogue between Nara-Narayana and Vishnu in the Mahodara-charita of the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 13 : The Meeting of Nara-Narayana and Markandeya

The Valakhilyas asked: O Sun God, O great Yogi, please tell us what the sages Nara and Narayana did after they became established in the state of Brahman.

The Sun God replied: The two divine sages, Nara and Narayana, resided in their hermitage and practiced the supreme Yoga as instructed by Lord Vishnu. Gradually, they attained tranquility and became devotees of Ganesha. Having received the great mantra of Ganapati from Lord Shiva, they began to chant it. They worshipped the four-syllabled mantra of Heramba and, having fashioned an image of Him made of jewels, the great sages performed His worship. They meditated upon Heramba within their hearts with great devotion. O Brahmins, having attained peace, they never let go of the thought of the Elephant-faced Lord.

When one hundred years had passed in this manner, Heramba appeared in their hermitage. He arrived riding a lion, wearing a serpent as his sacred thread. He was four-armed, three-eyed, adorned with excellent ornaments, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, and was the Lord with the large belly. O Brahmins, He held a garland of various flowers, a trident, a mace, and a goad in His hands, shining with great brilliance. Seeing Heramba in this form, the two excellent sages, who were great yogis, prostrated before His feet like a staff. Rising, they worshipped Heramba according to the prescribed rites and, having bowed to the Elephant-faced Lord, they praised Him.

Nara and Narayana said: Salutations to You, O Lord of the Ganas, protector of Your devotees. Salutations again and again to You, O Heramba, the bestower of devotion upon Your devotees. Salutations to You, O Elephant-faced Lord, the protector of the orphans. Salutations to You, O four-armed, large-bellied Lord. Salutations to You, O Dhundhi, the essence of all, who manifests in various forms. Salutations to You, O Lord who is devoid of duality, O Chintamani. Salutations to You, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, who is the very embodiment of Siddhi and Buddhi. Salutations to You, the Lord of Yoga, the elephant-eared one. Salutations to You, who are both with attributes and without attributes, the Supreme Soul. Salutations to You, the God of gods, who is worthy of worship by all. Salutations to You, the Brahman of the Brahmins, the eternal bestower of peace. Salutations to You, the bearer of happiness and peace, the one who rests on the serpent. Salutations to You, the perfect one, the Lord of perfection, the embodiment of perfect bliss. Salutations to You, the one who plays with the Yoga Maya. Salutations to You, who are without beginning, the one whose form is the beginning, middle, and end. Salutations to You, the creator, the sustainer, and the destroyer, who rides the lion. You are undoubtedly the Lord of those who have abandoned ego; therefore, You are known as Heramba. Salutations to You, O Vinayaka. How can we praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, who are the supreme embodiment of non-dual Yoga? We simply bow to You; may You be pleased with us, O Lord.

Having said this, the sages Nara and Narayana bowed down. The Lord of Ganesha raised them up and spoke with a voice like the rumbling of clouds.

Heramba said: I will grant you the boon you desire in your hearts; speak, O great sages, who are the illuminators of the path of Yoga. This hymn composed by you shall be pleasing to Me. Those who read or hear it will be granted both worldly enjoyment and liberation. I will grant whatever is desired through the recitation of this hymn. It will bestow My devotion and lead to great success.

The Sun God continued: Hearing the words of Heramba, the two excellent sages were delighted. With folded hands, they spoke to the Elephant-faced Lord: If You are pleased and wish to grant us a boon, O Lord of Obstacles, then grant us intense and unwavering devotion to You.

Saying "So be it," the Lord of the Ganas, filled with devotion, disappeared and returned to His own blissful state. From then on, the two sages were constantly absorbed in the worship of Ganesha, respectfully chanting the mantra "Heramba" with their tongues.

After a long time, the two sages went to the hermitage of Markandeya, their hearts filled with compassion, to impart knowledge. Seeing Nara and Narayana, the great sage Markandeya prostrated before them with his whole body, worshipping them with devotion. After worshipping them, he sat at their feet, massaging them. The humble sage Markandeya said: My birth, my knowledge, my penance, and my wisdom have all become fruitful today. My parents are blessed by the sight of your feet.

Having been praised by the sage with various words, the two sages, pleased by his devotion, spoke to the humble Markandeya.

Nara and Narayana said: O Markandeya, ask for the boon you desire in your heart. We are undoubtedly pleased by your hospitality.

Hearing their words, the powerful Markandeya bowed and said, with his neck bent in devotion: O excellent sages, please impart to me the perfect knowledge that bestows peace. By that, I shall be satisfied and devoted to the Yoga of peace. Please show me the Great Maya, so that by knowing it, I may renounce it. Grant me the boon of devotion to the feet of the Guru.

The Sun God said: Saying "So be it," the two great souls imparted to him the supreme knowledge, the perfect Ganesha-related knowledge that bestows the Yoga of peace.

Chapter 14 : Description of Maya

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Nara and Narayana said: O best of sages, listen to the knowledge that grants peace and is complete. There is no doubt that you have attained perfection through penance and celibacy. This knowledge, which bestows Yoga, was told by Shiva to Ramachandra, through which the elder brother of Lakshmana became filled with peace. Due to his involvement in state affairs, he had forgotten it, but through the words of Vashistha, that knowledge was regained.

Markandeya asked: Who is that Rama, the fortunate one? In which lineage was he born? Whose son is he? O great sages, tell me all of this.

The Sun said: Being thus asked by the wise Markandeya, the two great-souled ones, filled with great joy, spoke of the deeds of Raghava.

Nara and Narayana said: Marichi was the son of Brahma, and from him was born Kashyapa. From Kashyapa and Aditi was born the Sun, who is the soul of all embodied beings. From the Sun came the one known as Manu Vaivasvata. His Manvantara is declared to be the seventh in the earthly realm. The day of Brahma is described by sages who understand time as consisting of a thousand cycles of four ages, and the night is said to be of equal duration. O Brahmana, in one day, fourteen Manus appear, each inspired by their own time to perform the duties of a Kshatriya. O great sage, a Manvantara is said to be slightly more than seventy-one cycles of the four ages.

The wise have described the Manus starting from Svayambhuva. Among them, the six categories differ according to their authority. The Manu, the gods, the sages, the Indra, and the sons of Manu—where the incarnation of Vishnu is declared—are all part of this. Through the Manu, all duties are revealed, and the task of protecting the earth is carried out according to the rules of the Varnas and Ashramas. O sage, the path of action and knowledge is revealed with respect by the sages to men for their easy understanding. O sage, the sacrificial rites of the patron and all such actions must be performed by them in their respective Manvantaras. The three worlds, especially by Mahendra, must be protected according to the fruits of their actions through rain and other means. The gods, having enjoyed their share of the sacrifices, must respectfully grant the fruits of their actions to those who perform them in heaven. O sage, whenever Indra faces terrible fear from the demons, Vishnu protects him with a portion of His divine power. The sons, grandsons, and descendants of Manu, devoted to the Kshatriya dharma, must always perform the task of protecting the subjects.

O sage, thus the six-fold Manvantara is described. When one Manvantara ends, the second one arises. At that time, the six categories, devoted to knowledge, always remain respectful. Having abandoned their bodies through penance, they depart to their own abodes. The dissolution occurring there is known as the dissolution of the Manvantara, where all beings, both stationary and moving, attain dissolution. O great sage, when the second Manu arises, sages like Bhrigu create themselves through the practice of Yoga. O sage, they again create the world of moving and stationary beings. This should always be known by the wise as occurring in every interval. At the end of Brahma's day, the dissolution known as Naimittika occurs, in which the three worlds undoubtedly perish. O sage, in this sequence of days, the full lifespan of Brahma is one hundred years, there is no doubt. At that time, the Grandfather (Brahma) himself will perish through dissolution. Thus, in this order, they speak of the destruction of the three Gunas. O Brahmana, this Maya, which is full of birth and death and manifests various dualities, has been described to you as the cause of delusion in the states of duality.

Thus ends the fourteenth chapter named Description of Maya in the third section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 15 : The Description of the Movement of Time

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Markandeya said: O Lords, you have described the wondrous nature of Maya in great detail. Having heard it, I am filled with satisfaction and great joy. However, O best of yogis, I have not yet fully understood the nature of Maya. Therefore, please explain it to me in detail. Please describe the division of Varnas and Ashramas along with their respective duties, the measurement of the Yugas, and specifically the characteristics of each Yuga. Also, explain the path of action that brings satisfaction to the Pitris (ancestors), Devas, and Asuras, as well as the history of the kings born from the Sun and the Moon. O best of sages, please answer these questions of mine so that I may gain knowledge of the dualities and other aspects of existence.

Bhanu said: Being thus asked, the two great sages, Nara and Narayana, spoke with compassion to the ascetic Markandeya.

Nara and Narayana said: O great sage, you have asked well. We shall tell you everything so that your knowledge of the nature of Maya becomes firm. Time is measured starting from a Nimesha (the blink of an eye), which consists of the smallest units of sound. Fifteen Nimeshas make one Kashtha. Thirty Kashthas make one Kala, and thirty Kalas make one Muhurta. Thirty Muhurtas constitute a day and night. Thirty such days make a month. A month consists of two fortnights, the bright and the dark. Twelve months make a full year. Two months constitute a season, and there are six seasons in a year, as declared by those who know time. Thus, time is measured from the Nimesha up to the year, manifesting the nature of time through various states.

Time, in the form of a year, is said to be fivefold: the first is Samvatsara, the second is Parivatsara, the third is Idavatsara, the fourth is Anuvatsara, and the fifth is Vatsara. Listen to their movements, O sage. The formation of the body and its limbs is brought about by time. The fire residing in the stomach accomplishes everything. The birth, existence, and destruction of the body are all caused by time; that fire is known as Samvatsara. This is the chief of the five, the mover of all actions, and is called 'Time' by the wise as it resides within the body.

Parivatsara is the Sun, the embodiment of time. By the nature of the solstices (Ayanas), it is known as the driver of time. It manifests the nature of time daily through the cycles of rain, cold, heat, and the order of months and seasons. Idavatsara is the Moon, as stated by the wise. It is the mover of the stars and constellations. It is the source of knowledge for the Pitris, and there is no doubt that it is a form of time. Through the passage of time, the Moon nourishes the world with its nectar-filled rays. Through its rays, it reveals the nature of medicinal herbs at the right time, and at the right time, it provides the nectar of immortality to the Devas.

Anuvatsara is the Wind, which is said to be everywhere. It is the mover and the fulfiller of desires through the functions of Prana and Apana. Through the cycles of the Avaha and Pravaha winds, it moves the host of celestial bodies; it is the knower of time and the wearer of the form of time. Vatsara is the form of Rudra, and thus time manifests. When the great dissolution occurs, all medicinal herbs are absorbed into the void. At that time, through the measure of time, Tryambaka (Rudra) again becomes the creator of the herbs, situated in time. Gayatri, Jagati, and Trishtubh are known as Ambika. By their unity and their own power, the herbs are created again by Rudra through time. Thus, the Lord sustains the world through this nature of time. He who knows this fivefold time by nature never suffers from the sorrows of worldly existence.

Now, O sage, listen to the measurement of time in detail. The Ayana is said to be twofold, consisting of six months each. This is considered one day for the Devas. The Uttarayana is their day, and the Dakshinayana is their night. Thus, thirty days make a month for them, and twelve months make their year. In this way, one should understand the body of time everywhere. The bright fortnight is the night and the dark fortnight is the day for the Pitris; this time is pleasing to them, where a month equals a day.

Months are said to be of two kinds: Solar and Lunar. The month determined by the transit of the Sun is called the Solar month. The Moon is seen as full on the full moon day; based on that, the Lunar month is to be understood. Or, it is known by the wise as ending on the new moon. Thus, the Lunar month is said to be of two kinds. We shall tell you the reason for this for the sake of understanding the truth. The Adhimasa (intercalary month) and the Kshayamasa (lost month) are considered impure. The lunar month is known by the mark of the Moon on the new moon day and not otherwise. For the purpose of bathing, the greatness of the month is especially observed on the full moon. O sage, it should always be understood by the wise through the mark of the Moon. Furthermore, the time of the Pitris is determined by the lunar month. It is said that the Devas and Pitris in heaven drink nectar, which is the full essence of the Moon, during the lunar month. The time of the Devas is determined by the mark of the Sun, and the movement of the bright and dark fortnights is said to be the cause.

Three thousand years of human measurement, plus thirty, is considered the cycle of the Seven Sages (Saptarshi). Nine thousand years of human measurement, plus ninety, is known as the Dhruva year. Thus, the measure of time that has been told to you, O best of twice-born, is always associated with death, always wandering, and should be known as being full of dualities.

Thus ends the fifteenth chapter, entitled "The Description of the Movement of Time," in the third section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 16 : Description of the Characteristics of the Ages

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Nara and Narayana said: O Great Sage, know that a period of twelve thousand divine years constitutes the four ages, as understood by the wise. The Krita Yuga is said to last four thousand years, with its transition periods (sandhi) consisting of eight hundred years. The Treta Yuga is said to last three thousand years, and the wise should know its transition periods to be six hundred years. The Dvapara Yuga is said to last two thousand years, and its transition periods are known to be four hundred years. The Kali Yuga is a very terrible period of one thousand years, and its transition periods should also be known by the wise to be two hundred years. These transition periods, consisting of hundreds of years, are to be understood as providing the fruits of the respective ages. Thus, the twelve thousand divine years are the measure of the four ages as described by those who know time.

First is the Krita Yuga, then the Treta, followed by the Dvapara, and finally the Kali Yuga. Meditation is the primary practice in the Krita Yuga, sacrifice and penance in the Treta, worship in the Dvapara, and chanting the names of the Lord in the Kali Yuga. The eternal Dharma of the Krita Yuga is said to have four feet. The best of those who know Dharma declare that in the Treta, it has three feet. In the Dvapara, Dharma is accepted by all scriptures as having two feet, and in the Kali Yuga, it is said to have only one foot, which will also perish at the end. In the Krita Yuga, knowledge is always worshipped; in the Treta, the Sun is praised; in the Dvapara, Vishnu is the deity; and in the Kali Yuga, they speak of Shambhu (Shiva).

Among the measures of the four ages, Ganesha is worshipped by all humans with devotion for the fulfillment of their respective desires. In the Krita Yuga, Vinayaka is known as the son of Kashyapa, radiant, riding a lion, and having ten arms. In the Treta Yuga, the deity worshipped is Mayuresha, the six-armed son of Shiva, who has the moon-like complexion and rides a peacock. In the Dvapara, the four-armed Gajanan, the son of the excellent one, is worshipped riding a mouse and is described as having a red complexion. In the Kali Yuga, people worship Ganesha, who is two-armed, smoke-colored, rides a horse, is a savior, and knows all states of being. Since he is the embodiment of Brahman, there is no difference in him; he is the special bestower of success in the Dharmas of the four ages.

In the Krita Yuga, the lifespan of humans is considered to be one hundred thousand years; in the Treta, ten thousand; in the Dvapara, one thousand. In the Kali Yuga, it is one hundred years, and at the end, it will be even less, with fifteen years being considered the maximum. In the Krita Yuga, people are devoted to their own Dharma, engaged in the welfare of others, and are free from distinctions, viewing each other with equality. They are free from jealousy and conflict, righteous from childhood, and always filled with joy. They consider worldly pleasures trivial and are always filled with knowledge and meditation. They remain within the divisions of Varna and Ashrama, follow their own conduct, are filled with faith in Brahman, and are devoted to the practice of Yoga. They are righteous, perform various duties, and are happy, being the same internally and externally.

In the Treta Yuga, humans are endowed with Dharma minus one foot. They are considered to have different feelings in their internal and external states. They are not equal in their self-interest and the interest of others, and they are gradually filled with worldly desires. O Great Sage, listen to the measure of the transition periods: eight hundred years are called the transition of the Krita Yuga, and four hundred years are at its end. Three hundred years are for the Treta, and with its half-portions, the transition is said to be seven hundred years. Day by day, the Dharma of the Krita Yuga gradually perishes, and the Dharma of the Treta Yuga certainly prevails. O best of sages, after seven hundred years pass, the Treta Yuga prevails in all its nature. Thus, one should understand the sequence of ages and their transitions everywhere.

In the Dvapara, Dharma has two feet, and humans become self-interested and filled with conflict. They are considered servants of virtuous Dharma in their external behavior, but O Brahmin, due to shame, they are fond of worldly pleasures internally. In the Dvapara, people practice Dharma only out of shame before others, while being filled with worldly desires and neglecting Dharma in their own homes. Know, O best of sages, that humans in the Dvapara are thus filled with impurities in all their states.

Thus ends the sixteenth chapter of the third section of the Mahodara-charita in the great Mudgala Purana, which is the essence of the Upanishads.

Chapter 17 : Description of the Kali Yuga

Nar-Narayana said: The Krita Yuga is known as the age of purity (Sattva), the Treta Yuga is dominated by passion (Rajas), and the Dvapara Yuga is associated with darkness (Tamas), though it is also considered to have Rajasic qualities.

The Kali Yuga is completely filled with darkness (Tamas), and everything within it perishes. Since it is the embodiment of sin, how can the duties of a Brahmin exist? In Kali Yuga, there will be constant diseases, fear of famine, suffering caused by drought, and the degradation of nations. People will be irreligious, ill-mannered, extremely angry, narrow-minded, greedy, and will speak lies; they will be born as wretched beings. Due to bad omens, improper education, wicked conduct, evil scriptures, and the sinful actions of Brahmins, the people will live in fear.

In Kali Yuga, the twice-born will neither study the Vedas nor perform sacrifices. Instead, those with little intelligence will recite the Vedas in front of Shudras. There will be social interaction between Shudras and Brahmins through the sharing of beds, seats, and food. Shudras will become kings and harass the Brahmins; these rulers will commit the sins of killing Brahmins and animals. The twice-born will abandon bathing, fire sacrifices (Homa), chanting (Japa), the worship of deities, and other prescribed duties. They will slander Lord Ganesha, Brahmins, the gods, the Vedas, the scriptures, and the Puranas.

O Sage, they will perform actions contrary to the Vedas without any foundation, and the twice-born will lose all interest in their own duties. The age will be filled with wicked conduct, hypocrisy, and the fear of useless lies; people of all castes will become beggars. Villages will be devoid of Shiva-worship, crossroads will be filled with evil, and women will be obsessed with their hair. Shudras will adopt the religious practices of Brahmins, wearing ochre robes, shaving their heads, and acting with false piety.

People will steal food and disregard the proper times for rituals. Thieves will rob other thieves, and those who steal will be robbed in turn. There will be excessive suffering, short lifespans, and people will be obsessed with their appetites and sexual desires. They will be addicted to gambling and intoxicants, and will be lustful for others' wives. There will be Kapalikas, false ascetics, and householders who sell the Vedas and holy pilgrimage sites.

Those of low intelligence will not rise from their seats when they see a Brahmin. Shudras and those serving kings will strike the Brahmins. Shudras will sit on high seats, worship Brahmins, and be intent on studying the Vedas. Brahmins will wait at the doors of Shudras who serve kings, hoping for gifts. Shudras, being clever, will preach religion and, having studied the Vedas, will instruct the Brahmins. Brahmins will sell the fruits of their penance, sacrifices, and good deeds.

Women will abandon their husbands and be constantly attached to other men, betraying their spouses. There will be hundreds and thousands of ascetics who will wander about, addicted to intoxicants and women. Brahmins and Kshatriyas will follow the Vama-marga (left-hand path), Pashupata, Sankhya, and other heterodox sects. When the path of knowledge is destroyed and the world becomes inactive, insects, mice, and snakes will torment mankind.

Due to the curse of Dadhichi at Daksha’s sacrifice, and especially due to the curse of Gautama, people will be born into Brahmin families who will lead others astray by following the Vama-marga. This will happen in all castes, as all were cursed at Daksha’s sacrifice for their stubbornness. These base individuals, with hearts filled with darkness and acting like hypocritical cats, will delude people by showing them false fruits.

Lord Vishnu, assuming the form of Buddha, will support these heretics due to the curse of Dadhichi. He will destroy the path of ritual action and lead people into corruption, acting as the establisher of the age's dharma. Those who do not know the truth and are filled with corruption will seek refuge in him, the one who is kind to those who surrender, but who is veiled in the illusion of Buddhism.

When ten thousand years of Kali Yuga have passed, Vishnu will leave the earth and return to his own abode. The river Bhagirathi (Ganges) will remain for five thousand years, and after half that time, the village deities will depart for heaven. Any action performed by humans according to their prescribed duties is always acceptable to the gods. Actions done in opposition to this become demonic. Because of this demonic nature, the gods have left their temples. Seeing humans with demonic natures, the deities of pilgrimage sites depart. In those places, demons will enter the idols and enjoy the offerings.

Pilgrimage sites and temples will remain hidden and fasting will be required. Seeing the presence of the divine, the great demons will flee, and the deities will return to their own abodes. Because of their demonic nature, humans will always be corrupt and full of faults. The great power of action exists on this earth, and Lord Ganesha, the giver of the fruits of action, resides here. He is the giver of success to both gods and demons, though he remains hidden in the Kali Yuga.

The four pillars of Dharma—penance, purity, compassion, and truth—will be destroyed in order. Compassion was lost in Treta Yuga, penance in Dvapara, and in Kali Yuga, conduct will be destroyed. Only the pillar of truth remains in Kali Yuga, and through it, people conduct their affairs. When the twilight of Dvapara ends, Kali, with great strength, will destroy truth through widespread falsehood. Then, truth will gradually perish, and there will be an outcry in the abode of the gods.

In Kali Yuga, people will be full of suffering and devoid of food; they will abandon their villages and live in the forests. Driven by hunger, they will lose their minds and eat one another. The earth will be devoid of truth. The lifespan will be sixteen years, and women will conceive at the age of five.

Thus, everything is conquered by the demonic nature. All the gods and sages will pray to Lord Ganesha. Then, Dhundhi (Ganesha) will incarnate for the welfare of the gods. As Dhumravarna, he will destroy unrighteousness and establish Dharma. After establishing Dharma, he will disappear, and then the Krita Yuga will return through the sequence of the ages. Thus, the duration and the nature of the ages have been described. Know that all this is illusory and a product of delusion.

Chapter 18 : Description of the Celibate Stage of Life (Brahmacharya Ashram)

Nara and Narayana said: O Great Sage, listen to the four Varnas and Ashramas. The four Varnas are declared as Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, and Shudra. The four Ashramas declared by those who know the Vedas are Brahmacharya (celibacy/student life), Garhasthya (householder life), Vanaprasthya (forest-dwelling life), and Sannyasa (renunciation).

For Brahmanas, these four stages are prescribed in order. For Kshatriyas and Vaishyas, the stage of Sannyasa is not prescribed. For Shudras, only the householder stage is prescribed. The wise should know that the same applies to the Antyajas (those born in the last category).

A Brahmana boy, when he reaches the age of five, seven, or eight, should undergo the thread ceremony (Upanayana). Having received the sacred thread, the Brahmana should go to the presence of his Guru. There, with humility, he should study the Vedas and other scriptures. The Brahmana should observe the vow of celibacy until his studies are complete. He should beg for alms in the morning and evening and practice the Gayatri mantra.

One should meditate on the Gayatri mantra with reverence: as the form of Brahma in the morning, as the form of Rudra at midday, and as the form of Vishnu in the evening. With self-control, one should perform Achamana (ritual sipping of water) using the three words Bhuh, Bhuvah, and Svah. With great brilliance, one should touch the organs while performing Nyasa (placing the fingers on specific body parts) accompanied by mantras ending in Svaha.

The wise person should touch the mouth with three fingers starting with the index finger. With the thumb and index finger, one should touch the nose. With the thumb and ring finger, one should touch the eyes. With the little finger, one should touch the ears. With the thumbs, one should touch the navel, and with the palm, the chest. With all fingers, one should touch the space between the eyebrows, and with the tip of the thumb, the tuft of hair on the head (Shikha). With all fingers, one should touch one's own arms during the rituals of Nyasa. Thus, the Achamana is described according to the names mentioned in one's own Vedic branch.

Sandhya (daily prayer) should be performed specifically with Vedic mantras. O Great Sage, one should also perform Nyasa of the Pranava (Om), the Vyahritis, the Gayatri, and the Gayatri-shiras, followed by Achamana. In the morning, one should specifically use the Surya mantra; at midday, the Apah Punantu mantra; and in the evening, the Agni mantra. Then, one should perform sprinkling (Marjana) twenty-nine times, followed by the offering of Arghya and Upasthana (standing prayer).

Then, one should perform the liberation of the Gayatri from curses, followed by meditation and Japa (repetition). A Brahmachari should perform Japa of the Gayatri one hundred times in each of the five periods of the day, silently and with a focused mind. A householder should perform one hundred and eight Japa at the prescribed times, and a Vanaprastha should perform one thousand Japa. Thus, the three-fold Sandhya is always performed by Brahmanas.

One should chant the three-footed Gayatri combined with Omkara. O best of sages, listen to the order of this. First, chant the Pranava, then the Vyahritis (Bhuh, Bhuvah, Svah), then the Pranava again. Then, the three-footed Gayatri is chanted. Thus, the Japa is performed daily by the twice-born according to the rules. For Sannyasis, the four-footed Gayatri should be chanted according to their specific stage of life.

O Great Sage, the Brahmachari should beg for alms from householders of his own lineage according to the rules and offer them to his Guru. A Brahmachari should always say "Bhavat" (respectful address) at the beginning of his request for alms, a Kshatriya in the middle, and a Vaishya at the end. O giver of honor, if the alms accepted by the Guru are sufficient, the Brahmachari should remain fasting and observe celibacy. Whether the alms given by the noble Guru are little or sufficient, he should eat that much and remain in the Guru's presence.

Carrying a staff of Palaasha wood, he should offer firewood to the sacred fires in the morning and evening, and he should keep his nails and hair trimmed. His Guru should be his supreme deity in every way. He should serve the Guru's wife and children as he serves the Guru. He should never stay alone with the Guru's wives. He should not engage in conversation with women if there is no work. He should not listen to songs or similar things sung by women, and he should remain self-controlled. A Brahmachari should especially never lose his semen. If semen is lost in a dream, he should perform a bath and chant the Gayatri mantra one hundred and eight times.

O Sage, he should study what is taught by the Guru. A wise person devoted to knowledge should not argue with the Guru. Thus, a Brahmachari, endowed with these various characteristics and devoted to the study of the Vedas, becomes a great soul. A twice-born person should observe this celibacy for twelve or sixteen years, or as long as he desires.

After studying the Vedas and other scriptures and memorizing them with reverence, he should worship his Guru and offer him Dakshina (the teacher's fee). When his mind is inclined toward worldly matters, he may become a householder. If not, he may take Sannyasa or go to the forest. Alternatively, a best of the twice-born may remain a Brahmachari forever. One should act according to the purity of one's own heart. The knowledge, yoga, and actions one has studied are under one's own control; therefore, one should practice them accordingly.

O Great Sage, this Brahmacharya has been described briefly. Listening to it grants merit and is beneficial to the world.

Chapter 19 : Description of the Duties of the Stages of Life (Ashrama Dharma)

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Nara and Narayana said: After performing the ritual tonsure, the twice-born (dvija) should remain devoted to his parents and reside in his own home, dedicated to the service of the gods and the twice-born. He should then choose a bride who is younger than him, possesses good lineage and character, and is complete in all her limbs. The householder should approach his wife during the appropriate season for the sake of progeny and should remain engaged in serving gods, Brahmins, and guests. He should perform the six duties: performing sacrifices, officiating at sacrifices for others, giving charity, studying, receiving charity, and teaching. The householder should daily perform bathing, sandhya prayers, japa, homa, hospitality, worship of deities, self-study of scriptures, and the Vaishvadeva ritual.

Those residing in the householder stage must observe the Chaturthi Vrata (the fourth-day vow) and other specific vows. One who is devoid of the Chaturthi Vrata has no authority to perform other religious vows. One must always remain pure and free from the four-legged sins. O Great Sage, he who eats food on the fourth day of the bright fortnight is considered a meat-eater and is destined for hell. The householder should daily perform the Agnihotra and Shraddha rituals, remaining always compassionate. He should never consume forbidden food and must remain disciplined, observing purity and proper conduct. He should perform the tarpan (libations) for ancestors, gods, and sages, and daily observe the five great sacrifices as prescribed in the scriptures.

The householder should perform these duties according to his own means. O Great Sage, listen to the division of wealth: the householder should dedicate half of his earnings to his wife and children, one-fourth to his friends, and one-fourth to the service of the gods. In this way, the householder should perform various duties. When one-third of his life has passed, he should enter the forest stage (Vanaprastha). He may leave with his wife or place her under the care of his sons, and then go to the forest for the sake of dharma and the perfection of his actions. Whether he has children or not, he should go to the forest and perform penance, living on fruits and roots.

Living in the forest, he should perform Agnihotra and serve guests while engaged in various austerities. Even if his wife is with him, the Vanaprastha should never engage in sexual intercourse, not even in his mind. He should practice breath control (pranayama) and may subsist on air alone. In summer, he should practice the five-fire penance; in winter, he should reside in water; and in the rainy season, he should live under the open sky. In times of distress, the forest-dweller should never live in a village; that noble soul should not even enter a village.

When one-fourth of his life remains, the ascetic should enter the stage of Sannyasa. At that time, he should renounce all attachments, including his wife, after informing her. The wife should grant him permission and, remaining in penance, should wither her body until death. The Sannyasi should perform the Agnihotra within his own self, become free from all desires, and wander the earth. With the realization "I am Brahman" and following the stages of yoga, the yogi becomes a Sannyasi, free from the fluctuations of the mind.

United with the renunciation of actions, he may wander anywhere, staying in a village for only one night. He should be free from the duality of opposites and should beg for his food daily. O Sage, the Sannyasi may use his hands as a vessel (karapatri) for food. By eating each morsel in this way, he attains the merit of an Ashvamedha sacrifice, without a doubt. During the four months of the rainy season (Chaturmasya), he should reside in holy places, having conquered his senses and breath, and remaining unattached to all actions. Non-violence is the supreme dharma, especially for Sannyasis; O Brahmin, one should never even cut grass.

I shall now describe the rules for the Tridandi Sannyasi, known as the Kutichaka. The daily practice of one's own dharma is called the staff of the body (deha-danda). Silence is called the staff of speech (vachika-danda). The mental staff (mano-danda) is the practice of keeping the mind devoid of worldly tastes everywhere. The yogi who treats his body as a mere hut and remains absorbed in Brahman is called a Kutichaka.

Then there is the Bahudaka yogi, who is a Dvidandi (two-staffed). Being free from the bodily staff, he is particularly free to act as he wishes. Just as water collected in a single pit does not become tainted by the pit itself, similarly, the soul residing in the body is not tainted by the defects of the body. The one who is established in Brahman and free from duality is called a Bahudaka, unattached to the body.

Then there is the Hamsa stage, where the staff is held in the mind. He is free from the staffs of body and speech and acts according to his own will. When the body and the indwelling soul are understood as Brahman, the yogi becomes one with that. The duality of body and soul is like water and milk; the Hamsa is the one who extracts the milk (Brahman) from it.

Then there is the Paramahamsa, who is devoted to the staff of knowledge. He is free from the staffs of body, speech, and mind. The Brahman that appears as the body and soul is actually a delusion; the one who transcends this is the Paramahamsa. The great yogi who is established in the Supreme Brahman, which is the eternal witness, is beyond these three divisions. "I am not the body, nor am I in the body, nor am I the union of both; I am the eternal witness, full of knowledge and bliss." If there is any ignorance, it is a product of transformation; renouncing that and being free from duality, he becomes free to act as he wills.

This is the fourfold Sannyasa. Through the yoga of "I am Brahman," a man becomes Brahman-realized. O Sage, if a Sannyasi harbors mental desires for worldly objects, he will surely go to hell. If he accepts even a single coin, he will certainly fall into the Kumbhipaka hell for a thousand years. If he harbors a desire for the pleasure of sexual union with a woman even for a moment, he incurs the sin of killing a thousand cows.

In the Krita age, the mind was always controlled through dharma, and meditation was the primary means to attain everything. In the Treta age, the mind became restless due to the loss of one foot of dharma, and penance became the primary means. In the Dvapara age, when physical suffering increased, the performance of one's own dharma became the primary means. In the Kali age, dharma is destroyed by the demonic nature of the times; in this age, a man attains everything merely by remembering the Lord's name. In the path of remembering the name, the rules of Varna and Ashrama do not strictly apply; by following the conduct of Sannyasa and other virtues, the demonic influence is destroyed.

Due to the prevalence of the demonic nature in Kali Yuga, there is no qualification for Sannyasa; the body and mind are restless, and thus one is destroyed. One who is devoid of Varna and Ashrama is called "Adandi" (staffless) in the Vedas. By renouncing the staff of knowledge, a man becomes a yogi himself, free from prohibitions and injunctions, following his own will. If he performs what is prescribed in the Vedas, he acts according to the rules. If he does not perform what is prohibited, he is a man of virtue. Therefore, the yogi is described as being beyond both injunctions and prohibitions. The conduct he follows becomes his path to yoga.

Thus, the yogi is called staffless; he is beyond Varna and Ashrama and becomes Brahman. He is called Vinayaka (the leader), for he is free from all other leaders; he is the leader of all. If one who has become Brahman through the service of yoga chooses to remain in the Varna and Ashrama system, he performs his duties for the sake of guiding the world, though he remains unattached to the fruits of his actions.

O Great Sage, I have thus described the path of the stages of life. Know that all of this is created by Maya and is illusory.

Thus ends the nineteenth chapter, titled "Description of the Duties of the Stages of Life," in the third section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 20 : The Illumination of Varna Dharma

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Nara and Narayana said: O Sage, listen as I describe the four Varnas (social orders) along with their respective duties: the Brahmana, the Kshatriya, the Vaishya, and the Shudra. O Great Sage, know without a doubt that these Varnas have originated in various ways due to the mixing of lineages. I shall now briefly describe the duties of the Brahmana to help you destroy the illusion of Maya.

The scriptures have declared four types of livelihoods for Brahmanas: Uncha (gleaning), Shila (gathering fallen grains), Shukla (receiving unasked gifts), and Mrita (living on what is obtained without effort). I shall explain their paths, so listen attentively. One may sustain the body by gathering grains left behind in fields by farmers. Alternatively, one may sustain the body by gathering grains left behind at the homes of merchants. This is the first livelihood; now listen to the second. One may sustain the body through grains that grow naturally due to rain without the tilling of the land; this is known as Mrita. If one sustains oneself through gifts received without asking, it is called Amrita. A Brahmana should never sustain his body through grains harvested from fields tilled by farmers. O Great Sage, I have already described your own duties; a Brahmana should never engage in service-oriented livelihoods.

O Great Sage, the Kshatriya is ordained to sustain his body through the bearing of arms and should not engage in servitude. He should have the Brahmana perform fire sacrifices (Homa) on his behalf. O Lord of Brahmanas, a Kshatriya should never take up Sannyasa (renunciation).

A Vaishya should engage in buying and selling, agriculture, and the diligent rearing of cattle. O respectful one, he should always remain free from the path of Sannyasa. For Kshatriyas and Vaishyas, O Brahmana, the performance of sacrifice (Yajna) and giving charity are prescribed, but the performance of priestly duties (Yajana) is not. A Vaishya who knows his duty should have the Brahmana perform fire rituals and deity worship to ensure the preservation of his own Dharma. He should perform Tarpana (libations) through the mouth of a Brahmana using mantras. This is the path of the Vaishya, O best of the twice-born.

For the Shudra, the service of the three Varnas is prescribed. There is no doubt that he should live like a servant within the household. He should give charity to the twice-born and listen to the Puranas. He should never listen to the syllables of the Vedas. In the Puranas, the incarnations of the Gods are described. The idols of these deities were established by the divine sages in ancient times, and one should never touch them. However, if an idol is established by ordinary people according to their own will, then O Great Sage, the Shudra may touch it daily. He should perform worship and remembrance using the Name-mantra. He may worship an earthen idol using the Name-mantra.

From the invocation to the dismissal of the deity, the Shudra should have the Brahmana perform rituals such as Shraddha and Tarpana using mantras from the Puranas. O fortunate one, the Shudra should not recite the verses of the Puranas, though listening to them is always permitted. Those born in the lowest castes should always engage in the remembrance of the Divine Name. They should never touch the idols of the Gods, nor should they perform any rituals using Puranic mantras. They should remain devoted to their own Dharma, and by offering wealth or grain in the name of the ancestors and the Gods, they follow the ancient path. Those who are devoted to their own Dharma will undoubtedly attain heaven and enjoy great happiness.

The wise have declared that those devoted to their own duties attain the realms of Prajapati for Brahmanas, Indra for Kshatriyas, Vayu for Vaishyas, and Gandharva for Shudras. There is no doubt that they will enjoy the pleasures of those realms. Whoever hears or reads this brief illumination of Varna Dharma, which is a manifestation of Maya, will be freed from the delusion of Maya.

Thus ends the twentieth chapter entitled The Illumination of Varna Dharma, in the third section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 21 : Description of Duties towards Gods and Ancestors

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Nara and Narayana said: O wise one, listen as we describe the duties of women. From the moment a girl is born, she is cherished by the gods for three years. O wise one, the Moon, the Gandharvas, and Agni each protect the girl for two years, thus safeguarding her for six years in total. During this period, relatives should not give the girl in marriage to anyone. If she is given away, it causes distress to the deities and leads to the destruction of righteousness.

Relatives should give a girl in marriage to a virtuous man of good lineage when she is between seven and ten years of age. If a girl who has reached the age of ten and has attained puberty is not given in marriage, it leads to the growth of great sin. At every step, her parents and others incur the sin of killing a brahmin. Therefore, a girl should be given away according to the prescribed rites, which bestows heaven. There is no merit equal to the gift of a maiden, through which the lineage grows and brings happiness to all.

Once a woman attains puberty, she becomes subject to her husband, and he is considered her deity. She should serve her husband with devotion, like a maidservant. If the husband goes to the forest, she should follow him. If there are young children, she should stay behind to care for them while the husband is in the forest. O great sage, if the husband becomes a renunciant, the wife should grant him permission to do so. If the husband dies while living at home, the virtuous wife should follow him in death; the same duty applies if he dies while living in the forest.

If a wife is pregnant or menstruating when her husband dies, she should not join him in death, but should follow the dharma of a widow. On the fourth day, she becomes pure for performing duties related to her husband, and on the fifth day, she becomes pure for the worship of brahmins and deities. A widow should always be devoted to eating only one meal a day and should constantly and respectfully emaciate her body through vows and penance. This is declared to be the primary duty of chaste women; O great sage, hearing this bestows merit upon them.

Humans must perform duties towards ancestors and gods with respect. Listen briefly to the duties towards ancestors. O best of brahmins, the Shraddha ceremony should be performed on the new moon day. The Pindanvaharyaka Shraddha, performed with devotion, grants the fruits of both worldly enjoyment and liberation. This Shraddha is also praised during the solar equinoxes, performed in the afternoon by the twice-born with pure offerings.

Except for the fourteenth day, all lunar days of the dark fortnight, starting from the full moon, are praised for Shraddha rites. O great sage, the Shraddha for those who died by weapons or poison is prescribed only on the fourteenth day and is not recommended at any other time. The three Ashtaka days in the dark fortnight of the three months beginning with Pausha are always meritorious for Shraddha. The Yugadi and Manvadi tithis, especially those combined with the thirteenth day, are considered highly meritorious for Shraddha.

This is considered a conditional rite, as are the ten Pinda offerings for deceased relatives during Vyatipata and Vaidhriti yogas. Optional Shraddhas are praised during eclipses, solstices, equinoxes, and at holy places. Shraddhas performed on specific days of the week, one's own birthday, and all constellations are especially fruitful. O sage, for the sake of happiness here and in the ancestral realm, Shraddha is said to be desire-fulfilling and should be performed regularly.

Shraddha performed at Gaya, Prayaga, Amarakantaka, Mayureshapura, Kashi, and other such places is considered inexhaustible. The ancestors sing this song, and the wise ones dance to it: "We desire many sons who are virtuous and endowed with good qualities. If even one of them goes to Gaya and performs the Shraddha rite, the ancestors are liberated, and that son attains the supreme state."

If one performs Shraddha at Varaha Parvata, Gaya, Mayura, Kashi, the site of the eight Vinayakas, Gangadwara, Prabhasa, Bilvaka, Nilaparvata, Kurukshetra, Setubandha, Bhrigutunga, Mahalaya, Kedara, Phalgutirtha, or Naimisharanya, it grants both worldly enjoyment and liberation. The fourth day, the eleventh day of both fortnights, Sunday, Pradosha, and the eighth day are all capable of granting success.

Daily worship of the gods, especially through vows, is prescribed. Men should engage in various such vows. Serving the gods through the path of Agnihotra and other rituals, and performing acts of charity and public welfare at holy sites, is considered proper. Thus, various duties towards gods and ancestors are described. When these are offered to the Supreme Brahman by the wise, they become a means to yoga. This entire system of duties based on caste and stage of life is said to be an illusion, created by the Source of the Universe to dispel the delusion of Maya.

Thus ends the twenty-first chapter of the third section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "Description of Duties towards Gods and Ancestors."

Chapter 22 : The Story of Mandhata

Nar-Narayana said: The six Manus out of the fourteen mentioned earlier performed severe penance to please the great Lord Ganesha. By chanting various mantras, practicing deep meditation, and observing strict fasting, they pleased the elephant-headed Lord. After a thousand years, the Lord of Obstacles appeared and asked them to seek their desired boons. They worshipped Him with various hymns—the description of which is impossible due to their vastness—and after granting their wishes, the Lord returned to His abode, Svananda. They then performed their respective duties.

In subsequent Manvantaras, they worshipped Ganesha again with great devotion to attain the peace of Yoga. Gradually, these sages, who were well-versed in Yoga, attained the state of Brahman by focusing on the form of the Lord. They had many children, and the earth was ruled by their descendants in succession.

Now, I shall tell you briefly about the lineage of the humans in the current Vaivasvata Manvantara. Manu had ten sons who were equal to him in virtue. Sudyumna was the eldest, and he also had a daughter. The others were Ikshvaku, Nriga, Sharyati, Dishta, Dhrishna, Karusha, Narishyanta, Prishadhra, and Nabhaga. These nine were equal to Indra in prowess. The eldest child became the progenitor of the Lunar dynasty due to her feminine nature.

Once, while Sudyumna was hunting in the forest with his army, he entered the Gauri forest by divine providence. Upon entering, he was transformed into a woman. O son of Mrikandu, listen to the reason for this: Once, Goddess Parvati and Lord Shiva were enjoying themselves in that forest in a state of nudity. When the great sage Vashistha and others arrived there to see Lord Maheshwara, the Goddess and Shiva felt ashamed. The sages, seeing them, turned away in embarrassment. Consequently, Shiva cursed the forest, saying that any man who enters this forest shall immediately become a woman.

Sudyumna, having turned into a woman, became exceptionally beautiful and youthful. Budha, the son of the Moon, saw this beautiful woman and was captivated. She also fell in love with him, and after being with him, she returned to her place feeling ashamed. From their union, Pururava was born, who became the progenitor of the Lunar dynasty and was famous for his devotion to his ancestors.

Vashistha then gave the great mantra of Ganesha to the princess, who was now in the form of a woman, to worship the Lord. She went to the forest and performed severe penance. After a hundred years, the Lord of Ganesha was pleased and appeared before her. When she asked for the boon of regaining her masculine form, the Lord granted it, transformed her back into a man, and returned to His abode.

Sudyumna then had three sons: Utkala, Gaya, and Vinita. After installing them in the kingdom, he went to the forest and worshipped Ganesha through the path of Yoga. He attained peace and, in the end, merged into the divine essence of Ganesha. When Sudyumna had become a woman named Ila, Manu had placed Ikshvaku on the throne.

Manu Vaivasvata went to the forest and worshipped the Lord by chanting the six-syllable mantra received from the Sun. He attained the state of Brahman and merged into Ganesha in Svananda. Ikshvaku, desiring a son, worshipped Ganesha with the eight-syllable mantra as advised by Vashistha. Pleased after a hundred years, the Lord granted him a boon, and his son Vikukshi was born, who was powerful and respected by all.

After ruling, Ikshvaku also went to the forest, worshipped the Lord, and attained the state of Brahman. Vikukshi had many sons, the eldest of whom was Kakutstha. When the gods were tormented by demons, they sought refuge in him. He fought the demons, riding on the shoulders of Indra (who had taken the form of a bull), and defeated them. Thus, he came to be known as Kakutstha. He was a great devotee of Ganesha and eventually attained the Lord.

From him came Anena, then Prithu, Vishvarandhi, Chandra, and Yuvanashva. Then came Shaba, Brihadasva, and Kuvalayashva, who was known as Dhundhumara for killing the demon Dhundhu. Dhundhumara had three sons: Dridhashva, Kapilashva, and Bhadrashva. From Dridhashva came Haryashva, then Nikumbha, Barhanashva, and then Krishashva and Ranashva.

Krishashva’s son was Yuvanashva, who was radiant and god-like in battle. Once, he performed a sacrifice to Varuna to beget a son. The sages enchanted the water with mantras. At night, the thirsty king drank that water. When the sages asked who had drunk the consecrated water, they were astonished to find it was the king. They told him that he would now carry the child in his own belly.

The fetus grew in Yuvanashva’s belly. When the time came, Indra tore open the king's side to deliver the child. When the gods asked who would nurse the infant, Indra said, "He shall nurse from me (mam dhata)," and thus the child was named Mandhata. Raised by Indra, he became a powerful emperor, equal to the gods. He was a great devotee of Ganesha and, following the path of Yoga taught by Vashistha, he eventually attained the Lord and merged into His divine abode.

Chapter 23 : The Story of Ambarisha

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

The Sage said: In the lineage of Manu, there was a highly fortunate king named Ambarisha, who was always devoted to the vow of Ekadashi. Listen to his story.

Nara and Narayana said: In the lineage of Manu, there was a powerful son named Ambarisha. He always worshipped Lord Vishnu with a focused mind. By observing the Ekadashi vow, he pleased Lord Madhava. After a long time, on the day of Dvadashi, the sage Durvasa, known for his quick temper, arrived as a guest at the time of breaking the fast. The royal sage bowed to him and said with folded hands, "O Bhagavan, the time for breaking my fast on this Dvadashi is very short. I pray to you, O great sage, for the sake of completing my vow, please allow me to perform the necessary rituals quickly and return." The Brahmana said "So be it" to the king and went to the river for his ablutions.

The sage performed his rituals quickly, but due to the passage of time, the Dvadashi tithi was nearing its end. The king, having only a moment left, broke his fast with a small amount of water to ensure the completion of the vow. Afterward, the king waited for his guest. Durvasa soon returned, and the king explained the entire situation to him in detail. Hearing this, Durvasa became extremely angry and cursed him, saying, "O King, you invited me and yet broke your fast without me? You shall be devoid of the fruits of your merit, without a doubt." Having said this, Durvasa left as he pleased.

The king remained deeply distressed, continuing his fast in his home while waiting for the sage. He thought, "How can I eat after inviting a guest?" A year passed, and the sage Narada arrived. The king worshipped him and narrated the entire incident. Narada then said, "Listen, O wise King. You broke your fast with only water, and by remaining in a state of fasting, you have upheld your duty. You have completed the Dvadashi vow successfully. This obstacle arose despite your innocence. Durvasa, filled with anger, left hungry. Therefore, O giver of honor, worship the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) with effort. By doing so, you will undoubtedly be free from all obstacles. I have been sent by Vishnu for your sake, O best of kings. He is the primordial form of all, and in the end, He is the Lord of Ganas (Ganesha). Shiva, Vishnu, and others are his Ganas, there is no doubt. He has established them in various tasks, O King. The Chintamani resides in the mind, which is the form of intellect. Go to Him for refuge, for He is the Lord of the intellect. A foolish person who desires success while abandoning the Lord of Success (Ganesha) will always face failure."

Having said this, the sage gave him the six-syllable mantra that Lord Vishnu himself chants, and then he disappeared. From that day on, Ambarisha took refuge in Ganesha, chanting the king of mantras and worshipping Vinayaka. He realized the non-difference between Vishnu and Ganesha. After some time, Durvasa’s mind became restless, and out of compassion, he himself went to Ambarisha. Seeing the great sage arrive, the king was astonished and, filled with devotion, bowed and worshipped him. The great sage Durvasa, now filled with great joy and humility, spoke to the noble king.

Durvasa said, "Forgive my offense and my rashness, O best of kings. I cursed you in my anger despite your innocence. You remained in your home with a virtuous nature, observing your fast, and by that, you have conquered everything. Because you broke your fast with water while I was away, that specific Ekadashi will be fruitless for you, but may all your other vows be fruitful, O best of kings." Having said this, the great sage ate the food offered by the king. After blessing him, Durvasa went to his auspicious hermitage. Ambarisha continued to worship Lord Ganesha always.

Sons were born to him, and after installing his eldest, powerful son on the throne, the king went to the forest. There, he worshipped Ganapati with a focused mind. In time, he attained the abode of Ganesha through yoga. Whoever reads or hears this wonderful story of Ambarisha will attain devotion and eternal liberation.

Thus ends the twenty-third chapter of the third section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Story of Ambarisha."

Chapter 24 : Story of Muchukunda

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Nara and Narayana said: Purukutsa had two sons of great strength, the highly fortunate Ambarisha and the other, Muchukunda. Ambarisha, after ruling his kingdom, handed it over to his son and went to perform great penance, eventually attaining the status of Ganapati. Muchukunda, that royal sage, ruled the excellent kingdom with righteousness; he was of great brilliance and possessed valor equal to Indra.

Once, a great and wonderful war broke out between the gods and the demons, in which all the gods were defeated by the fierce demons. The gods then respectfully sought refuge with Muchukunda. By the power of his weapons, he destroyed the great demons. O sage, due to the duration of the war, much time passed, and that great soul reached the end of an era. Observing the change of the era, he spoke to all the gods, saying that they must now grant him the supreme primordial knowledge.

The gods, being highly pleased, said to him, "O Lord, now go and sleep in the mountain cave. O King, whoever wakes you up there shall immediately meet his death upon approaching you. There, you will have the vision of Vasudeva. He, the great yogi and best of kings, will be the one to bestow knowledge upon you." Muchukunda, the mighty one, agreed to their words and went to the forest, where he slept in the valley of Gandhamadana.

After a long time had passed, the son of Vasudeva, Lord Vishnu himself, the great yogi, was born, having been propitiated by penance. After killing the mighty Kamsa, He resided in Mathura. Kamsa's father-in-law, Jarasandha, became extremely angry and went there with his army to wage war, accompanied by kings like Shalva and other great warriors. His army consisted of twenty-three Akshauhinis. The brilliant Krishna, along with Sankarshana, crushed that army. The king fled, but returned again and again, only to be defeated each time. In this way, the mighty Jarasandha came seventeen times, but he did not attain glory and remained miserable.

Then, O best of sages, that wicked king sought the help of the king of the Yavanas, known as Kalayavana. That Yavana king, who was the destroyer of the Yadavas, had been born due to a curse from Garga and arrived in Mathura. Knowing of his arrival, Krishna, through His divine illusion, created Dwaraka in the ocean and sent the Yadavas there. He then approached the Yavana king and, revealing Himself, fled. Narada had already described the signs of Krishna to the Yavana king, and recognizing Him by those signs, the Yavana pursued Him. The Yavana, who was like the time of death for Krishna, rebuked the great soul, asking why He was running away like a thief in the Yadu lineage.

Krishna went to the place where the king Muchukunda was sleeping and entered the cave. The Yavana king, seeing the sleeping Muchukunda, kicked him with his foot and said, "Why are you sleeping, you shameless one? Fight with me!" Awakened by the Yavana, the powerful Muchukunda opened his eyes, and the fire emanating from them burned the Yavana to ashes. Seeing Kalayavana destroyed, Janardana (Krishna) approached the king with a joyful heart. Seeing Him, Muchukunda bowed down and, filled with devotion, spoke to Krishna, the Lord of Yogis.

Muchukunda said: "O Krishna, O Lord, the gods had told me about this. Now, O ocean of mercy, please instruct me on the path of knowledge." Hearing the words of Muchukunda, Krishna, the knower of supreme yoga, spoke to that fortunate soul.

Sri Krishna said: "I am the complete Lord of the Ganas, and I am not different from them, O wise one. This is the supreme knowledge you must understand, O King. By abandoning the five-fold mind and the pride that causes delusion, and through yoga, you shall become Ganesha in the Brahman. O King, the great intellect is the form of the mind, and the illusion is the form of the earth. The Siddhi is the Maya of Ganapati, and the image that resides there is the Lord of the Ganas. Behold Him through the service of yoga. O King, abandon the illusion born of the image and keep the wish-fulfilling gem in your mind. Know that the syllable 'Ga' represents Siddhi and the syllable 'Na' represents intellect; the Lord of both is Ganesha. Worship Him, O fortunate one, and you will attain peace. I have told you this most secret knowledge, O best of kings."

Having said this, Krishna was worshipped by the great yogi. He then returned to Mathura and destroyed the Yavanas. When Jarasandha arrived, Krishna, along with Rama, ran away, deceiving him and leading him toward Dwaraka. The royal sage Muchukunda practiced the yoga described by Krishna with great effort and attained peace. Wandering the earth, the glorious king worshipped Ganesha with devotion until the end of his life. Any mortal who listens to or reads this supreme story of Muchukunda will be granted everything by the Lord Ganapati.

Thus ends the twenty-fourth chapter named the Story of Muchukunda in the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 25 : Story of Sagara and Bhagiratha

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Nara and Narayana said: Ambarisha had a son who was known as Yauvanashva, and Harita was the son of Yauvanashva. Purukutsa had a son named Trasadasyu, whose son was Anaranya, and his son was Haryashva, who was respected by the virtuous. His son was Aruna, who was born by the grace of the Sun. His son was Satyavrata, a learned man of noble intent. He became known as Trishanku, who ascended to the heavenly realm while still in his physical body. His son was Harishchandra, who was devoted to all righteousness. Having learned of the Lord of Obstacles from Vishvamitra, he became a devotee of Ganesha, and at the end of his life, the glorious king attained the abode of Ganesha, Svānanda.

His son was Rohita, and from him came Harita. From him came Champa, then Sudeva, then Vijaya, and then Bharuka. From him came Vrika, then Bahu, and Sagara was his son. Sagara had two wives, Prabha and Bhanumati. They worshipped the yogi Bhrigu, who is supreme and purifying, and he granted them a boon for a son. Prabha chose to have sixty thousand sons, while Bhanumati, O great sage, chose one who would carry on the lineage. By the grace of Bhrigu, a son named Asamanjasa was born to Bhanumati, who was the heir to the lineage. Being endowed with yoga, the glorious Asamanjasa would take all the children and throw them into the Sarayu river, causing the destruction of the young ones. He was then abandoned by his father and went to the forest, O great sage, but he restored all the children to life through his yogic power. His son was Anshuman, who was devoted to righteousness. Meanwhile, sixty thousand sons were born to Prabha.

Sagara then performed a hundred Ashvamedha sacrifices by force, which caused Indra to become agitated. When the final sacrifice began, the Lord of the Gods, through his illusion, stole the horse and placed it in the netherworld near the sage Kapila. The sixty thousand sons of Sagara, who were extremely difficult to defeat, became angry. Seeing the hoofprints of the horse, they dug up the earth everywhere with great strength. By their actions, they expanded the salty ocean, which from that time became known as the Sagara (ocean). They saw the king of horses wandering near Kapila. Thinking him to be the thief, the sons of Sagara raised their weapons to kill him. They were reduced to ashes by the fire of Kapila's anger. Sagara was saddened by this, but he was a powerful and heroic king. Learning from Narada, Sagara sent his grandson Anshuman to the sage. Anshuman bowed to the sage, and having been praised and worshipped by him, Kapila gave him the horse. He took it and went to his grandfather Sagara.

Sagara then completed the Ashvamedha sacrifice. After crowning his grandson as king, the glorious Sagara went to the forest. Having received the great Ganesha mantra from his guru, which grants peace through yoga, he practiced it and became highly radiant. The king worshipped Lord Ganesha with a focused mind, and at the end, O sage, he attained the abode of the Lord of the Ganas, being devoted to the Supreme Brahman. Anshuman, having attained knowledge from Kapila, also worshipped Ganesha daily, following the nature of a Gana. His son was Dilipa, and from Dilipa came Bhagiratha. Dilipa went to another kingdom to worship the excellent Lord of Obstacles. Then, King Bhagiratha, established by Dilipa, ruled the entire kingdom with righteousness.

Once, Narada went to the king, and being worshipped by him, he spoke these excellent words. Narada said: "O fortunate Bhagiratha, listen to my beneficial words. Your ancestors were burned by the anger of a brahmin. Those who are burned by the anger of a brahmin have no path to salvation. Therefore, O great king, do what is best for them. Bring the river of heaven (Ganga) and bathe them with its waters; only then will your ancestors be able to reside in the heavenly realm." Having said this, the great yogi Narada, remembering the Lord of the Ganas, went to heaven. The king became sorrowful, left his kingdom to his ministers, and went to the forest. Bhagiratha worshipped the river of heaven through penance.

After a hundred years, the river of heaven came to him and asked the king, who was filled with devotion, to choose a boon. The king worshipped the Goddess with various hymns, and the powerful King Bhagiratha said: "O fortunate one, if you are pleased, then bathe my ancestors who are on this earth." She replied, "So be it. But, O wise one, look for a vessel to hold my fierce speed, for I shall descend to the earth." The king then worshipped Shankara. After one year, Shambhu was pleased and became the grantor of the boon. "If you are pleased, O Shankara, then hold the river of heaven," the king said. Shiva agreed and remembered the great river.

When she arrived, he said, "Fall upon the earth, O Goddess; the compassionate Shankara will hold your speed." The heavenly Ganga, filled with pride, said "So be it," and fell upon Shiva's head with a thousand streams from all sides. Thinking to herself, "I will bathe Shiva's head and then go to the netherworld," she descended with great speed. Realizing her pride, Shiva, through his illusion, hid the Goddess in his matted hair, where the river wandered. She could not find the end of Shambhu's hair, and so we bow to him. Then, for the sake of the king, Shiva released her, and she went to the earth.

There, the river of heaven saw the sage Jahnu, who was performing severe penance. She said, "Move, O ascetic, or I will submerge you." When he did not respond, she became angry and moved to submerge him. Realizing she was coming, the angry king (sage) drank her up through the power of his penance and remained in meditation. Then the glorious Bhagiratha bowed to him, and upon being prayed to, the sage released the river by splitting his thigh. Because she became his daughter, she is called Jahnavi. Again, filled with speed, she submerged the oceans.

The ancestors, riding in a celestial chariot, appeared before Bhagiratha and ascended to the heavenly realm the moment they were touched by the water. Thus, the river of heaven was worshipped by King Bhagiratha, and she became known as Bhagirathi, having been accepted as a daughter. Thus, Bhagiratha was powerful and glorious. Bhagiratha's son was a king named Shruta. After installing him on the throne, he went to the excellent forest. Having learned the Ganesha yoga from Vashistha, O sage, the king practiced it and became a devotee of Ganesha. At the end, he attained union with Ganesha through the service of yoga.

Whoever, O best of men, listens to or reads this story of Bhagiratha, will be granted both worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Thus ends the twenty-fifth chapter of the third section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled the Story of Sagara and Bhagiratha.

Chapter 26 : Story of Ramachandra

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Narayan and Nara said: Listen, O Sage, Nabhaga was the son of Shrutayu, and from him was born the wise and illustrious Sindhudvipa. His son was Ayutayu, and from him came Rituparna, who was a friend of Nala and an expert in the science of horses. From him was born Sarvakama, and from him Sudasa. His son was the famous Saudasa, also known as Kalmashapada. In the territory of Kalmashapada, the great sage Vashistha gave birth to Ashmaka, who increased the glory of the Ikshvaku dynasty. Ashmaka’s son was the king named Mulaka, who, out of fear of Rama (Parashurama), went to the forest in great distress. There, protected by women, he assumed a feminine form, and because of that act, O Sage, he came to be known as Narikavacha. From him was born Dasharatha, then Ailavila, then Vishvasaha, and from him Khatvanga, who attained Dhundhi. From him came Dirghabahu, then Raghu, then Aja, and his son was Dasharatha, who is remembered as an expansion of Kashyapa.

O Sage, he had three wives of incomparable beauty: Kausalya, Kaikeyi, and the virtuous Sumitra. As youth passed, the king did not have an excellent son, despite performing various efforts, vows, and pilgrimages. Then, having entrusted his peaceful kingdom to his ministers, the king, along with his wives, sought refuge with the great sage Vashistha. Vashistha gave the king the ten-syllable mantra of Ganesha and instructed him and his wives. He said, "That Gajanan (Ganesha) is the presiding deity of the Sun’s lineage. How could you, born in his dynasty, forget him? By merely forgetting him, a person does not attain success and is troubled by obstacles here and in the hereafter. Therefore, O King of Kings, worship Ganesha with devotion. Do not abandon this king of mantras; there will be loss in abandoning it."

Saying "So be it," Dasharatha bowed to Vashistha and went to the forest. The illustrious Dasharatha performed severe penance. Living only on air, he worshipped the Lord of the Ganas along with his wife, chanting that excellent mantra with a humble heart. After one hundred years, the elephant-faced Lord was pleased and appeared, riding his mouse, to grant him boons. Seeing him, the king and his wife stood up, bowed to the Lord, worshipped him, and praised him with hymns. Then the Lord of the Ganas said to the king, "Ask for a boon, O mighty-armed one; I will grant you what your heart desires." Hearing this, the pleased Dasharatha, with great humility, said to Ganesha, the bestower of all success.

Dasharatha said: O Lord of Gods, if you are pleased, then grant me devotion to your feet, O Lord, through which ignorance does not exist. Grant me another boon, O Lord of the Ganas: a son who will bring great fame, be incomparable in strength, and be devoted to you. And wherever I have performed this penance, may you remain firmly established there, fulfilling the desires of people's hearts with devotion.

The Lord of the Ganas said "So be it" to Dasharatha, and after making himself invisible, he returned to Svananda with respect. Then, the idol installed by Vashistha with devotion became a site of all success. After worshipping there with his wives, Dasharatha returned to the city of Ayodhya, bringing joy to everyone.

Then, for the destruction of Ravana, the Lord commanded Vishnu, who became the son of Dasharatha, O Sage. The eternal Hari divided himself into four parts. Rama, the best of weapon-wielders, was born to Kausalya. Bharata was born to Kaikeyi, and Lakshmana and Shatrughna were born to Sumitra; all shone like Vishnu. Later, King Dasharatha, engrossed in worldly pleasures, forgot the Lord of the Ganas. Then, O Sage, Rama’s initiation ceremony was performed, and he was requested by Vishvamitra for the protection of his sacrifice. That sacrifice was protected by Rama and Lakshmana, and the terrible demon Subahu was killed. The mighty sage Vishvamitra was pleased and gave all the knowledge of weapons to Rama.

Then the sage Vishvamitra went to Janaka’s sacrifice, accompanied by Rama and Lakshmana, where he was worshipped by Janaka. There, the bow of Maheshwara was strung by Rama and, with great force, was broken in the middle, O Great Sage. Then King Janaka, filled with love, gave Sita to the noble Rama, and Urmila to Lakshmana. The king also gave his brother’s daughters to Bharata and Shatrughna with great affection.

Then King Dasharatha, heavily pressured by Kaikeyi, granted the boon for Rama’s exile and the kingdom for Bharata. Because of the power of the boon, he did as requested. Rama, out of respect for his father, went to the forest for fourteen years with Lakshmana and his wife. There, Rama lived happily in Nashik. Ravana arrived there in the guise of a guest, kidnapped the lonely Sita, and took her to Lanka. Rama, with Lakshmana, did not see the daughter of Videha; he fell unconscious and lamented in great distress.

Then Rama went into the forest searching for Sita. There in Karnataka, he saw the primary Shaiva site. Rama, accompanied by Lakshmana, worshipped Shambhu there with great devotion for a month, assuming the form of a Shaiva. Pleased, Sadashiva granted Rama a boon and gave him the instruction regarding the attainment of Sita.

Shiva said: Listen, O noble Rama, you are the eternal Vishnu. Due to Bhrigu’s curse, you have taken a human body. You are suffering from the separation from Sita and the forest exile. Therefore, worship the Lord of the Ganas with devotion. Through him, you will attain ancient knowledge, and there is no doubt, O mighty-armed one, that you will attain Sita.

Hearing Shiva’s words, the illustrious Rama, deeply sorrowful, asked him with humility. Rama said: Tell me, O Shambhu, O Maheshana, the nature of Ganesha. How is he described as the Lord of all? Hearing Rama’s words, Shankara spoke to him, explaining the complete Ganesha-yoga, O best of sages.

Shiva said: Listen, Rama, I will tell you the knowledge of Ganesha, through which you will know all things and attain peace, O Lord. His intellect, which resides in the heart in five ways, is undoubtedly his form. His nature, the great Prakriti, is the form of consciousness. The illusion (Maya) that causes various delusions is seen everywhere as success (Siddhi). All people are deluded by it, wandering in search of success. That is called his Prakriti, situated on his left side; on his right side is the intellect, and it is called Maya. Ganesha is the master of both, and he is considered the embodiment of yoga and peace. Knowing him, O Rama, we attain peace and worship him. Whatever is known by the intellect, O Rama, is his form. That which is born of success is not attained through yoga and other practices. Abandoning both forms of Maya, worship the Lord of the Ganas. Through that, you will become a knower of the path of yoga and Ganesha himself.

He is called the Lord of Obstacles (Vignaraja) by those who speak of the Vedas. Without respecting the Lord of Obstacles, how can one be free of obstacles? Everything described in the Vedas is subject to obstacles, O noble one. Therefore, everything is either with obstacles or without them. Obstacles are said to be of the nature of existence, and their master is the Lord of the Ganas. Worship him according to the rules, and you will be free of obstacles. Worship Heramba with devotion; I will tell you the path of devotion. Through that, you will attain the Lord Ganesha, there is no doubt.

If a human is devoid of his own essence, he is called dependent by nature. Those who are the protectors of the helpless are the Lords, the Supreme Lords; they are called the protectors of the helpless, consider this, O wise one. In the Vedas, the word 'He' is determined to mean 'the helpless,' and 'Ramba' means the 'Lord.' In their union, he is called Heramba. The helpless are those devoid of essence, and the protectors are those endowed with essence. He is the master of both; worship that Heramba according to the rules. Abandon the two types of pride, O Rama, O Lord: the pride of being helpless and the pride of being the supreme protector. Knowing that the world and Brahman are subject to him, who has made the helpless beings and the protectors, go to him for refuge.

Having said this, Mahadeva disappeared, and Rama remained there with Lakshmana. In Vidyadhishapura, Rama performed excellent penance. With the mantra taught by Shiva, he pleased the Lord of Obstacles. Rama and Lakshmana worshipped Heramba with great devotion, fasting. Through meditation on that Lord, Rama soon became peaceful through the practice of yoga, O Sage. Seeing his firm devotion, the compassionate Ganesha came after six months to grant him a boon. The Lord of Obstacles enlightened Rama and Lakshmana. Then, ceasing their meditation, they bowed to the supreme Lord of the Ganas. Then Rama, the best of gods, worshipped him and praised the Lord of the Ganas with a humble heart.

Rama said: Salutations to you, O Heramba, the creator of the helpless and the compassionate one. Salutations again and again to you, who are the form of the Lord of the helpless. Salutations to you, who are the non-differentiation between the Lords and the helpless. Salutations to you, the Lord of Obstacles and the master of the Ganas. Salutations to you, who are the beginningless one and the primal form of all. Salutations to you, O Lambodara, who are established in the end, middle, and beginning. Salutations to you, O Chintamani, the mover of hearts. Salutations to you, who are devoid of difference and non-difference, and who are that form. Salutations to you, the one worthy of worship by all, the one worthy of worship at the beginning of all, the Supreme Soul, the infinite, the Supreme Lord, the Dhundhiraja. Salutations to you, the complete one, the Lord of the complete, the one residing in Svananda, the bestower of success and intellect, and the boon-giver of success and intellect. Salutations to you, who bring misery to those devoid of devotion to the capable, and who bring capability to the helpless who are devoted. Helplessness and capability are subject to you, there is no doubt. Therefore, protect me, O Lord of Yoga; salutations to the Lord of Yoga. What can I praise you with, O Lord of the Ganas, the embodiment of peaceful yoga? Even the Vedas are not capable of describing you, let alone a person like me.

As Rama praised him with such devotion, his hair stood on end, and his throat choked with emotion. Seeing Rama immersed in the nectar of devotion, Gajanan said to him, "Ask for the boon your heart desires with respect. This hymn composed by you will increase my love, grant peaceful yoga, and increase the signs of devotion. Whatever one desires, I will grant through the recitation of this hymn. There is no doubt that by listening to it, one will become dear to me."

Hearing Heramba’s words, Rama, the protector of the people, spoke with great devotion and folded hands. Rama said: O Gajanan, if you are pleased and a bestower of boons, then let there be eternal devotion to your lotus feet. Grant me yoga, peace, and constant remembrance as spoken by Shankara. Make me worthy of being worshipped by the masters of yoga, O Lord of the Ganas. Sita has been abducted by Ravana, and I am prepared for his destruction. Give me victory, O Heramba, especially in that. Make the glory of my name wonderful and respected everywhere, and make me full of fame, O Gajanan.

Hearing his words, Heramba said to Rama: "O Vishnu, let it be as you have prayed for, always. By merely remembering me, everything will be easy for you. Through the power of your name, living beings will cross the ocean of existence." Having said this, Ganesha disappeared. Rama, accompanied by Lakshmana, remained there.

Then, bringing Agastya and other great sages, Rama installed the idol of Heramba according to the rules. From that day on, the Lord of the Ganas, Heramba, remained in Vidyadhishapura, bestowing all success upon his devotees, O Sage. After worshipping him, Rama, free from sorrow, went with Lakshmana for the destruction of the demons. He made friends with Sugriva and the monkeys, killed Vali, and went to Lanka with great strength. Surrounded by Hanuman, Angada, Sugriva, and other monkey-warriors who were like gods, and others like Jambavan, Rama and Lakshmana fought the demons. He killed the hordes of demons and slew Kumbhakarna. Lakshmana quickly killed Indrajit. Then, filled with great anger, Ravana fought fiercely. By him, all the monkey-kings were killed or rendered unconscious. Lakshmana was unconscious there, and Rama was greatly troubled. Rama’s weapons were rendered ineffective. Then, the highly distressed Rama remembered the Lord of the Ganas. By merely remembering him, the Chintamani in his heart gave him inspiration, and Rama killed Ravana, O Sage.

The Lord of Intellect himself became the giver of intellect for Rama’s sake. The world and Brahman are especially subject to intellect, O Great Sage. Then, having killed the great warrior Ravana, the descendant of the Sun (Rama) made Vibhishana the king of Lanka. He took Sita in an aerial chariot and went to his own city. He ruled righteously, as he was a knower of Dharma. His mind attached to Sita, he forgot the supreme knowledge of Ganesha, and consequently, he suffered the separation from Sita. After performing the Ashvamedha and other sacrifices, he remained engaged in royal duties. Then, through Vashistha’s yoga, he regained that knowledge. Then, filled with peace, he always worshipped Ganesha in his mind and with an external idol, O Sage. In the end, Rama, the eternal Vishnu, went to Vaikuntha. There, he worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with a single-minded heart.

Whoever listens to this story of Rama with devotion, or reads it, will attain worldly enjoyment and liberation. This is the incarnation of Mahodara himself, known as Heramba; listening to it bestows all success, O Great Sage.

Thus ends the twenty-sixth chapter named the Story of Ramachandra in the third section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 27 : Describing the Conclusion of the Dialogue between the Sun and the Valakhilyas

Nara and Narayana said: It is impossible to describe the greatness of the kingdom in its entirety. Know, O Markandeya, that everything is especially illusory. The entire universe is created by Brahma, sustained by Vishnu, and dissolved by Hara, all through the power of Maya. Know, O respectful one, that infinite universes exist everywhere, held together by the Supreme Cause. All these must be known as being of the nature of Maya. There is no doubt that they are known through Yoga by destroying delusion. Everything consisting of mind and speech is said to be of the nature of illusion. That which is devoid of mind and speech is considered to be the true reality. O Brahmana, Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, as well as the state of being Brahman, appear in the hearts of yogis due to the delusion of Maya. The story of Rama has been told to you in summary. What else do you wish to hear, O wise one? We shall now depart for our hermitage.

The Sun said: Hearing their words, Markandeya bowed down to them and spoke again, seeking the fullness of Yoga. Markandeya said: O divine Nara and Narayana, I have been purified by you. My thirst is not quenched by hearing this story, just as one is not satisfied by drinking nectar. Therefore, please tell me of the Yoga of Ganesha, by which I, like Rama, may attain peace and become one with Brahman.

Nara and Narayana said: We shall explain the path of Ganesha-Yoga to you with proper reasoning. O best of sages, it is beyond the power of even the mind to describe it. O Brahmana, the mind is fivefold, and the Chintamani (wish-fulfilling gem) resides within it. By restraining the five fluctuations of the mind, it is attained, there is no doubt. The intellect, which is the source of delusion in all states and is established everywhere, is called by the wise as the form that holds this delusion. That which is described as the reflection of the two (Nara and Narayana) is the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha). O best of sages, he plays in his nature, bound by delusion. Abandoning the state of reflection, become the Lord of the Ganas yourself. I am the form of Ganesha; this Yoga is the giver of peace. Abandoning the fivefold mind and that illusory opulence, you will now become the Lord of the Ganas through this Yoga. The syllable 'Ga' represents everything consisting of mind and speech, and the syllable 'Na' is said by the wise to be that which is devoid of mind and speech. The Lord of both is Ganesha; behold this in the Vedas, O great sage. Worship Him, and you will undoubtedly attain peace.

Having said this, the sages Nara and Narayana, the Valakhilyas, ceased speaking, letting him know the Lord of the Ganas. Markandeya, the powerful one, bowed to them and said: I am blessed by the sight of you, O great sages. O Lords, I wish to see the eternal Maya that causes delusion. Please show me the Great Maya; I bow to the Divine.

The Sun said: The two best of sages, the Valakhilyas, said "So be it" to him, adding, "O Markandeya, you shall surely see the Great Maya." Having received the command and having worshipped those best of yogis, the great sages departed for their hermitage through Yoga. Then, once, while the best of Brahmanas, Markandeya, was in his hermitage, meditating on the two yogis and longing to see Maya, he suddenly heard a great, booming sound. He became terrified, and then the clouds thundered. They rained down torrents like the trunks of elephants. The sage watched as the entire world was submerged by the clouds. Then, the entire world, both moving and stationary, was flooded by the water born of the ocean, and the best of sages wandered there. The best of Brahmanas was slightly injured by fish and other creatures. Overwhelmed, Markandeya remembered the Lord of the Ganas.

Then, he saw there an infant lying on a banyan leaf, the size of a thumb, the Supreme Vishnu who grants all desires. Markandeya, the great sage, bowed to Him and praised Him. He satisfied the Deity with the Atharvashira hymn. The Lord said to the best of Brahmanas: "What do you desire, O great sage? Tell me, and I shall grant it; there is no doubt I am the granter of boons." Markandeya, with a pleased mind, bowed and said: "O Vishnu, show me Maya; having known it, I shall renounce it." Then, that child, who was the form of Vishnu, bound by devotion, spoke with a smile, intent on protecting His devotee.

The Child Vishnu said: "O great sage, behold Maya with the eye of knowledge. I have given you the eye of knowledge due to the power of your devotion." Then, O Brahmanas, Markandeya, endowed with the eye of knowledge, saw Maya, which manifests in various forms. It exists everywhere in the form of the pervader and the pervaded, and through the differentiation of various qualities, it always deludes men. That yogi saw where the universe is destroyed, where it is sustained, and where it is created. The best of sages saw the Supreme Brahman, who is both void and full, established in meditation. He saw where men are extremely devoted to the goal of Yoga, where they are attached to enjoyment, and where they strive for liberation. He saw how they are created again and again, and how they die again and again. He saw where men become lords and where they are not. He saw nectar as poison and poison as nectar, and he saw the Sun in a state of coldness. The best of sages saw land in water and water on land, and he saw both the liberated and the bound man. He was astonished to see that the Lord, due to delusion, does not attain the goal, and that the world is the soul of the world. The best of sages saw such various states that I cannot describe them even in hundreds of millions of years.

Then, the great sage praised the Child Vishnu with various hymns and prayed with devotion: "O Hara, withdraw this Maya and make me as I was before." As he prayed, He placed him back in his hermitage. Then, it appeared as if the best of Brahmanas had not seen anything, just as a person does not see a dream once they have awakened. There was no water, no ocean, no fear, no sorrow, and no Keshava. He was astonished that the child-form and the Maya were gone.

Then, the Brahmana Valakhilya, having abandoned the illusory Maya, became devoted to the worship of Ganesha and chanted the supreme mantra. He practiced the Yoga that was taught by Nara and Narayana, and through the state of the Heramba mantra, he practiced it again. Just as it was practiced by Rama before, he also practiced it. This Yoga, which is peaceful and full, is of the nature of Ganesha. Becoming devoted to the peace of Yoga, he worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with a single-minded heart, and that Markandeya, the knower of Yoga, attained it. In the end, he attained union with the body of Ganesha through devotion. Thus, O Brahmanas, various devotees of Ganesha come into being. Whoever listens to or reads this story of the son of Mrikandu will not be bound by Maya. The Deity is known as Mahodara, called Heramba, and He resides in Vidyadhishapura, granting the fruits of devotion. Therefore, I worship that Lord of the Ganas, O Valakhilyas, having made it attainable through the state of Yoga and filled with devotion.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of the Sun, the Valakhilyas bowed to the Sun, who is the Self in all, and praised Him with various hymns. With folded hands, they said to the Sun, who is dear to all: "O Lord, we have fulfilled our purpose; there is no doubt, O Sun. What can we offer You, O great one? This body is surrendered to You. Be pleased with that, O Lord, and be our Guru out of respect." Then, all the Valakhilyas, whose wealth was their penance, worshipped Him, bowed, and departed to the best forest for penance. Having practiced the Yoga as heard from the Creator, they became especially revered by all as yogis. Always devoted to Heramba and intent on meditation, they all attained the peace of Yoga with single-minded intellect.

O protector of the people, the incarnation of Heramba, which is a part of Mahodara, has been told to you along with various stories. There are many such incarnations of Mahodara, O respectful one, and their stories are most wonderful. It is impossible for anyone to describe them; therefore, I have told them in summary, O Daksha Prajapati. This dialogue between the Sun and the Valakhilyas is most wonderful, said to grant the state of Brahman, and bestows Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha. It is especially capable of granting all prosperity and is the destroyer of delusion. What more do you wish to hear?

Thus ends the twenty-seventh chapter in the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the conclusion of the dialogue between the Sun and the Valakhilyas.

Chapter 28 : The Description of the Birth of Budha

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O Suta, you have narrated a great story that grants all accomplishments. Just as a person who drinks nectar is never satisfied, I am not satisfied even after hearing this. O Brahman, please tell me again what was heard by Daksha from the great sage Mudgala, which is considered the best by all.

Suta said: Having heard the dialogue between the Sun and the Valakhilyas, which bestows knowledge, Daksha humbly asked Mudgala.

Daksha said: I have heard the greatness of Heramba, which grants the status of Brahman. O best of sages, please tell me again the story of Mahodara. O best of yogis, I am never satisfied hearing this divine, complete story, which is like the essence of nectar.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Daksha, the glorious sage, with his hair standing on end, spoke to the protector of the people. Listen, O Shaunaka, to what he said.

Mudgala said: O protector of the people, the Lord Mahodara, who grants honor, was worshipped with great devotion by Yudhishthira, and that Pandava became a great yogi.

Daksha said: O best of sages, please tell me the story of Yudhishthira now, starting from the Lunar dynasty, as it is very virtuous.

Mudgala said: Long ago, the nectar-like Moon was born from the eye of Atri. He worshipped the Lord Ganesha with great penance. By the boon of Ganesha, he became the lord of the stars, the herbs, and the Brahmanas, and he became powerful and full of yoga. Later, O lord, the king became deeply deluded by ignorance; he abandoned the worship of Ganesha and became engrossed in worldly pleasures. Then, afflicted by obstacles and deluded, listen to the sinful act the Moon committed.

He abducted Tara, the youthful wife of Brihaspati, who was incomparable in beauty and had taken refuge in a holy place. Despite Tara crying out "No, no!" and wailing, the foolish Moon forcibly kissed the wife of his preceptor. O protector of the people, the son of Atri, tormented by the arrows of desire, enjoyed himself with her in forests and mountain valleys. After a long time had passed, a pregnancy grew within her. Even then, the deluded Moon did not release her.

When Brihaspati learned of this, he was greatly agitated. He requested the Moon to return her through various means, but the wicked one did not comply. Then, the preceptor Daksha (Brihaspati) propitiated Lord Shiva. Shiva, who was a disciple of Angiras, became filled with anger. Along with all the gods led by Indra, Shiva, with eyes reddened by rage, went to destroy the Moon.

Terrified, the Moon sought refuge with Shukra. Because of this, Shukra also became an enemy of the Angiras lineage. Shukra summoned the demons led by Prahlada, and they went to battle against Shiva and the gods. A fierce and hair-raising battle ensued between the demons and the gods, leading to mutual destruction. For a whole month, the battle raged intensely, and no peace was found, day or night.

Then, Brihaspati, in his sorrow, remembered the Lord of the Ganas and began to blame himself and the Moon. He lamented, "Alas, due to the conflict with my brother, my great wisdom has vanished. Ganesha has left me, and I am now filled with obstacles. Because of me, the destruction of gods and demons has arrived. What will happen? O Lord of the Ganas, I bow to you. O Dhundhi, O ocean of mercy, make me free of obstacles now. The very root of the gods and demons is being destroyed."

Similarly, the Moon also remembered the Lord in his heart with devotion. Then, O Daksha, the remover of obstacles appeared for both of them. Lord Brahma, the grandfather of all, also arrived there, having learned of the destruction of the gods and demons with a compassionate heart. The all-knowing Creator, who is the source of all existence, restrained Shiva and the other gods, as well as Prahlada and the other demons.

Hearing the words of Brahma, the gods and demons stood still, bowing to the Creator with folded hands. Then, the grandfather rebuked the Moon and returned Tara to her preceptor. A dispute arose between the two regarding the paternity of the child. The gods could not determine if the pregnancy in Tara's womb was that of the Moon or Brihaspati. Then, the Creator said to her, "O virtuous one, release the pregnancy. Tell me, O daughter-in-law, whose seed is this?"

The pregnancy, thus released by her, fell to the ground. Seeing the son, who was full of great brilliance, the gods were astonished. Tara, with her face cast down in shame, did not say anything. Then, the child himself spoke to Brahma with respect, saying, "I am undoubtedly the son of the Moon." Tara also confirmed this. The gods named him Budha because of his virtuous deeds. The Creator gave him to the Moon, and everyone returned to their respective abodes.

Thus, long ago, the battle known as the Tarakamaya war took place for the sake of Tara, which was extremely fierce and caused the destruction of demons and gods. O protector of the people, the story of Budha has been told to you. His son was the glorious Pururava. His birth from the womb of Ila was told by me earlier; he was a royal sage who brought satisfaction to his ancestors.

Thus ends the twenty-eighth chapter of the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the birth of Budha.

Chapter 29 : The Story of Pururavas

Mudgala said: King Pururavas ruled the earth righteously and protected his subjects with great wisdom. Once, while wandering in Kurukshetra, the king saw Urvashi, who had been cursed by the sages, and fell in love with her. She told him, "O Lord, I will live in your house and eat ghee, but you must protect my two rams, which are like my own children. Furthermore, you must never show yourself to me naked, except when we are intimate." The king agreed and took her home.

They had six sons, all as radiant as Indra: Ayu, Mayu, Amayu, Vishvayu, Shatayu, and Sthayu. They were all glorious and continued the lunar dynasty. Later, Indra sent the Gandharvas, led by Vishvavasu, to the king's palace to bring Urvashi back. At night, the Gandharvas stole the two rams. Hearing the cries of the animals, Urvashi rebuked the king, saying, "I did not know you were such a weakling, O King! My sons have been stolen by thieves; do you not see?"

Enraged by her words, the king immediately grabbed his sword and rushed out naked to pursue the thieves. At that moment, a flash of lightning, created by the gods, illuminated the room. Seeing him naked, Urvashi vanished instantly. The Gandharvas took the rams and flew to heaven, where they and Urvashi bowed to Indra with great joy. When the king returned and did not see his beloved, he collapsed on the ground in despair. He wandered the entire earth, longing to see her again.

After a year, he found her again in Kurukshetra and was overjoyed. He pleaded with her not to abandon him, but she replied that she was bound by the Gandharvas. She advised him to perform the Pitri-yajna (ancestral sacrifice) with devotion, which would eventually lead him to heaven where they could be together. She promised to meet him once every year. The king followed her advice, and the ancestors, pleased with his devotion, granted him the boon that Urvashi would be his and that he would attain an eternal abode in the Pitri-loka.

After some time, the king passed away and ascended to heaven due to his merits. There, he enjoyed celestial pleasures with Urvashi. Much later, the sage Narada visited him and warned him, "O King, why are you so attached to these worldly pleasures? This body is merely a prison. Seek liberation through yoga. You are trapped in the cycle of eighty-four lakh births. Having attained a human body, which is rare and capable of performing noble deeds, why do you waste it on fleeting desires?"

Hearing Narada's words, the king realized his folly and asked for guidance. Narada advised him to worship Lord Ganesha, the bestower of all perfections and the remover of obstacles. Narada initiated him into the single-syllable mantra (the Ganesha mantra). Pururavas then abandoned his attachments and engaged in deep meditation. Before long, he attained the state of Ganapatya (a divine state associated with Ganesha). He reflected on his past with regret, lamenting how he had wasted his human life on the base desires of the flesh instead of seeking the Divine. Filled with devotion, he worshipped Ganesha and, in the end, attained the Lord through yoga.

The glory of this story is such that it destroys all sins. Whoever listens to the account of Pururavas will be cleansed of their transgressions.

Chapter 30 : Nahusha Charitam

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, King Ayus had five sons of great prowess, who were endowed with brilliance, born of Prabhā, the daughter of Svarbhānu. The first among them was Nahusha, whose valor was equal to that of Indra. He performed a hundred sacrifices after conquering all the kings of the earth. Indra became extremely agitated by this, but he was unable to defeat the king, who was well-versed in all scriptures, and thus he remained silent.

Later, when Mahendra (Indra) was cursed by Gautama for molesting his wife, he lost his kingdom, and the gods became terrified. Unable to bear the fierce onslaught of the demons, they approached Brihaspati and asked him for a way to safety. Instructed by him, the gods and sages appointed Nahusha, the best of the radiant ones, as the King of Heaven. Having become Nahusha-Indra, he ruled the heavenly realm, sat upon the throne of Indra, and protected the gods. The gods and sages granted him a boon: "Whoever you look upon with anger shall immediately lose their power." Thus, that king became endowed with immense brilliance and was served by the gods and others.

Thereafter, the king became extremely arrogant and lustful. In his shamelessness, he summoned Shachi through the gods. The goddess replied to the gods, "I will not go to that king. I am the wife of Indra, the chief of the gods; I belong to a superior being." Having said this, she went to the house of Brihaspati, bowed to him, and said, "O noble one, protect your daughter-in-law." Hearing this grave news, the preceptor of the gods himself kept her nearby and prepared to help Indra. He suddenly went to the great sage Gautama and said, "By my word, please make Mahendra free from his curse." The great yogi Gautama asked, "What shall I do? Tell me the remedy, O dear one, and I will perform it immediately."

Gautama was then told the supreme remedy: "O great sage, give the mantra of Vinayaka to Indra." Gautama then gave the mantra of Ganapati to Indra, and through it, Indra became free from his ailment. Indra then chanted that king of mantras, the mantra of Ganapati, and by meditating on the Lord of Lords, he performed excellent penance. In the desolate forest of Vidarbha, the god lived on air alone, with his gaze fixed on the tip of his nose, and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles.

When a thousand years had passed, the Lord, who is fond of his devotees, came to grant him a boon, having been greatly pleased by his penance. He appeared mounted on a lion, with long arms, adorned with four hands, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, and holding the serpent around his navel. He asked Indra to speak his heart's desire, saying, "O Maghavan, I am pleased with you; I will grant you whatever you ask for now through your penance." Then Indra bowed to him with devotion and, having become steady, praised the Elephant-faced Lord according to his intellect.

Indra said: Salutations to you, O Chief of the Ganas, the giver of success to your devotees. Salutations again and again to the Lord Vinayaka, to Heramba. Salutations to you, who are without beginning and the origin of all. Salutations to you, the first to be worshipped, the Lord of Obstacles, the one worthy of worship by all. Salutations to you, who are without beginning, middle, or end, who are the beginning, middle, and end, and the final form; salutations to the remover of obstacles. Salutations to you, who hold the noose and the goad, who ride the lion. Salutations to you, the immeasurable one, who assumes various illusions. You are the creator of obstacles for the non-devotees, always dwelling in your own bliss. Salutations to you, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the one with the serpent at your navel. Salutations to you, who are Brahman, the form of Brahman, the giver of Brahman, and the compassionate one. Salutations to you, the giver of Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha. Salutations to you, O Ganesha, the complete one, the knower of the state of completeness, the giver of complete bliss, the creator, the destroyer, and the vessel of all. Salutations to you, O Lambodara (Pot-bellied one), who are the subtlest among the subtle, the enjoyer of the gross among the gross, and who are beyond both the gross and the subtle. How can I praise you, who are always the form of peace? Therefore, salutations again and again. Be pleased with me, O Lord; salutations to you, the embodiment of compassion.

Mudgala said: Hearing this praise, Ganesha, pleased by the devotion of his best devotee, spoke to Maghavan. Ganesha said: O Indra, the hymn you have composed will grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation. Whoever reads or hears it will have all their desires fulfilled. O King of Gods, you too should recite this hymn daily with effort. By doing so, you will become free from obstacles, O wise one. Ask for the boons that are in your heart, O noble one; I will grant them without doubt, as I am pleased by your devotion and this hymn.

Then Maghavan bowed to the Lord and said: O Lord, make me free from obstacles and established in my own position. Grant me devotion to your feet, O Lord; salutations to you. Make this well-known region a place of Ganesha worship. Make this holy site, marked by your feet, a place that grants everything, O Lord; there is no doubt that bathing here grants self-bliss. May this place, known as Kadamba, be famous in the world as the place of Ganesha that grants all desired objects through your grace, O Lord of Obstacles.

Saying "So be it," Ganesha then disappeared. Indra had an image of the Lord of Ganas installed by the Brahmins. Having worshipped it, the King of Gods stayed there, and by meditating on the Lord of Ganas daily, he honored him.

Now, O Daksha, listen to the deeds of Nahusha. At that time, the wicked king, having been afflicted by obstacles by Ganesha, said to Brihaspati, "Indra is surely dead. He is not seen anywhere by anyone. O Guru, give me Shachi." Then the Lord of Speech, filled with anger, said to him, "O King, go to Shachi riding a vehicle of Brahmins." The goddess, having been courted by Indra on various vehicles, desired an unprecedented vehicle; therefore, do as she wishes.

Saying "So be it," the arrogant Nahusha then acted. He forcibly gathered Brahmins to carry his palanquin. Seeing the suffering of the Brahmins, Durvasa arrived there. He was seized and made a carrier. Then, the king, deluded and sitting in the palanquin, said to the Brahmins, "Move faster, you serpents (sarpa)." Durvasa, extremely angry, gave a terrible curse: "O wicked one, become a serpent and fall to the earth, O lowest of kings."

Then the king, his pride gone, bowed to him and said, "O Brahmin, please tell me the way to be released from this curse, out of your compassion, O ocean of mercy. Shame on me for acting against the Brahmins. I am completely deluded; please save me, O great sage." Then the great sage Durvasa, filled with compassion, replied to the repentant Nahusha.

Durvasa said: O great king, in your lineage, Dharma himself will be born. At that time, you will have the vision of Yudhishthira. By knowing the knowledge imparted by him, you will be free from the curse; there is no doubt. Now go to the earthly realm. Brahmins should always be worshipped by kings, never insulted. You insulted the Brahmins and made them carriers by force. Because of that, your Dharma was destroyed; there is no doubt. Once you see Dharma again, you will become righteous.

Having said this, Durvasa went his own way. Nahusha became a serpent and fell to the earth. Then Brihaspati brought Indra back and, having established him on the throne of Indra, became joyful. Nahusha wandered in the forest, remembering Ganesha. "Alas, the entire world, both moving and stationary, is filled with obstacles. I have been afflicted with obstacles by the Lord of Obstacles. Therefore, I will take refuge in him; through him, everything will become auspicious."

Then, gradually, he conquered his mind, O Daksha, by the nature of Ganesha worship, even while in the body of a serpent. After a long time, Yudhishthira came to the forest with Draupadi and his brothers, having been exiled due to the fault of gambling. Then Bhima wandered in the forest in search of fruit. He was seen by Nahusha, who, knowing him to be a Pandava, bound him. The powerful king, in the form of a serpent, wrapped his body around Bhima and threw him to the ground with force. Bhima let out a great roar like a cloud. Hearing that, the King of Dharma (Yudhishthira) went to the mighty Bhima. Seeing Bhima surrounded by the serpent and filled with sorrow, the powerful serpent spoke to Yudhishthira.

The serpent said: I am Nahusha, your ancestor, O King; there is no doubt. I have captured Bhima now to have a vision of you. I ask you, O King, answer me truthfully. Then I will release Bhima, as you are considered all-knowing. Hearing the words of Nahusha, Yudhishthira was pleased and spoke to the noble one, the ornament of his lineage.

Yudhishthira said: Speak, O noble serpent; you are worthy of respect, O wise one. I will resolve your doubts by your grace, O giver of honor.

Nahusha said: What is truth, what is untruth, and what is the supreme Dharma, O King? Tell me about sin, bondage, liberation, yoga, and meditation. What is action, what is inaction, and what is considered forbidden action? What is knowledge, what is the root of penance, and who is the deity? Tell me. What is essence, what is non-essence, what is worthy of respect, and what is worthy of being disrespected, O King? Tell me all this, O Yudhishthira. Tell me these, O dear one, and then I will release Bhima and go wherever I wish.

Yudhishthira said: Truth should be known as Brahman, and the world is of the nature of untruth. Dharma should be known as one's own duty, O best of kings. That which is devoid of one's own duty is called sin by all. Bondage is the sense of "I" and "mine," and liberation is being free from both. Yoga is the restraint of the mind, and meditation is a mind free from objects. Action should always be known by the wise as righteous deeds. Offering everything to Brahman is called inaction. Forbidden action is the performance of sin, as declared by all scriptures. Knowledge is the realization that "I am Brahman," and penance is the balance of the pairs of opposites. Know that the deity worthy of worship by all is Ganesha, O giver of honor. The worship of the Lord in nine ways is the essence, O King. The worship of worldly objects is considered non-essential. What is worthy of respect is what is stated by the Vedas, Puranas, and Smritis. That which is devoid of the foundation of the Vedas is considered worthy of disrespect by the wise. Know that the Lord of all resides in the heart and is the mover of the intellect. Everything has been told to you, O Lord; release the son of the wind (Bhima).

At that moment, a celestial chariot arrived there. Yudhishthira saw it, endowed with divine splendor. Nahusha then released Bhima from his bondage. The king abandoned his body, ascended the chariot, and went to heaven. There, he sat on a half-throne near Indra and worshipped Ganesha daily with a focused mind. At the end of his time in heaven, the king attained the Elephant-faced Lord through the path of light. Free from obstacles, he became renowned for his virtuous deeds.

The story of Nahusha, which destroys sins, has been told to you. Whoever hears or reads it will have their desires fulfilled.

Thus ends the thirtieth chapter named "Nahusha Charitam" in the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 31 : The Story of Yayati

Mudgala said: Nahusha had five sons who were equal to the gods in wisdom: Yabhyati, Yayati, Samyati, Ayati, and the fifth, Ashvaka. Yayati became the king. O Prajapati, I shall now narrate his great story to you. He married Devayani, the daughter of Ushana (Shukracharya), and also Sharmishtha, the daughter of Vrishaparva, the best among kings. Devayani gave birth to Yadu and Turvasu, and Sharmishtha gave birth to Druhyu, Anu, and Puru. The wise king, bypassing his elder sons, anointed his youngest son, Puru, who was devoted to his father’s commands, as the king.

Daksha asked: O Lord, why did that king do the opposite? O Yogi-king, tell me everything, for I listen with respect.

Mudgala said: Vrishaparva was known as the lord of the Daityas, and his daughter, the fortunate Sharmishtha, was highly respected. Shukracharya’s daughter, Devayani, was also greatly cherished and pampered by her father out of affection. Once, the young princess Sharmishtha, surrounded by her companions, went to a garden full of joy. Devayani also came there to play, and the maidens engaged in various games. After becoming tired, they went to bathe, leaving their clothes on the bank and playing in the water.

When Sharmishtha came out with her companions, in her haste, she mistakenly put on Devayani’s clothes. Seeing this despicable act, Devayani scolded the princess, saying, "O wicked one, I am the daughter of a Brahmana; why have you worn my clothes? You are like a dog touching the sacrificial offerings. According to the scriptures, a Brahmana is superior, and you, the daughter of a king, are not worthy of this."

After Devayani scolded her with various words, Sharmishtha, filled with anger, replied: "O wicked one, why do you boast in my presence? It is only because of the patronage of kings that Brahmanas are respected. You eat the food and wear the clothes provided by our grace, yet you dare to touch me like a crow? After insulting her, the Asura princess threw Devayani into a dry well and went home in anger and sorrow.

By divine providence, King Yayati, who was hunting alone, arrived there and saw her in the well. Filled with compassion, he extended his right hand, and Devayani grasped it. After pulling her out, the king prepared to leave, but Devayani, filled with joy, said: "O gentle and wise one, you have held my hand. Tell me everything about your background."

The king replied: "O beautiful lady, know me as Yayati, the son of Nahusha, born in the Kshatriya lineage. I came here while hunting and had the fortune of seeing you. Go home, young lady; I shall return to my palace."

Devayani, filled with delight, said to the king: "Know me as the daughter of Kavya (Shukracharya). Be my husband, O Lord. You have held my hand, and I will not choose anyone else; therefore, be my husband." Hearing this, Yayati replied: "O goddess, how can I, a Kshatriya, marry the daughter of Kavya?"

Devayani then said: "Listen to my words. Because of Kacha’s curse, a Brahmana will not be my husband. Therefore, be fearless and choose me, O best of kings. I shall invoke my father and become your wife." The king agreed and went to his city, while she remained there. She sent a messenger to tell her father that she was staying there. Shukracharya came and, seeing his daughter weeping, asked what had happened. Upon hearing the story, the sage became angry, left the Daityas, and went to the forest to live a life of austerity.

The king of the Daityas, hearing this, became distressed and ran to the great sage, bowing down to him. He said with devotion: "O sage, this kingdom and my very body depend on you. Where do you wish to go, leaving me? I shall abandon my kingdom and follow you." Hearing this, Shukracharya told his daughter to forgive the princess. But Devayani, filled with anger, said: "Father, I will not return to the city; I will give up my life."

Shukracharya, filled with sorrow, asked his daughter what she wanted. Devayani replied: "If Sharmishtha becomes my maid along with her companions, then I will return to the city of the Daitya king." The sage then told the Daitya king, who, out of love for his daughter, agreed and brought Shukracharya home.

Shukracharya gave his daughter to Nahusha’s son according to the rites, and Sharmishtha went along as a maid. Once, when Sharmishtha was in her season, she approached the king in the garden and said: "O King, when you were chosen by my friend Devayani, I also chose you in my heart. O Lord, please favor me." The king agreed and secretly accepted her. Devayani did not know about this.

When Sharmishtha became pregnant, Devayani asked her: "O goddess, tell me whose seed you are carrying? By what divine providence have you received this mark of the Daitya king?" Sharmishtha replied: "O friend, I have not done anything wrong; I have secretly received the seed of a great Brahmana."

Three sons were born to her, who were like gods and were always honored by the king in the garden. Once, Devayani went to the garden and saw the boys calling Yayati their father. Hearing this, she was deeply disturbed and went home. Yayati, filled with humility, tried to console her, but the king was terrified and could not bring her back to his house. She went to her father and wept bitterly.

Cursed by Shukracharya, the king became aged. He went to the sage, filled with sorrow, and said: "O father-in-law, I am not yet satisfied with worldly pleasures. Tell me a way to overcome this old age, and I will strive to do it. I have never touched any woman other than Devayani." Shukracharya, pleased, gave him beneficial advice: "O fortunate one, your sons are in the prime of youth. Transfer your old age to them and take their youth. If a son accepts it by your command, you shall become the king again, and there will be no fault in that."

Yayati agreed and returned to his city with Devayani. He asked his elder sons for their youth, but they refused due to their foolishness. Then he asked Puru, who agreed. By Shukracharya’s blessing, there was no fault in Puru. Yayati enjoyed many pleasures and was satisfied. He performed all the sacrifices, visited various holy places, and gave away all his wealth and ornaments to Brahmanas, becoming very charitable.

When the sage Galava came to him, begging for help to pay his teacher’s fee, the king gave him his daughter Madhavi, through whom the sage accomplished his work. Later, the king took back his old age, gave youth back to Puru, and made him the king. He appointed Turvasu in the southeast, Yadu in the southwest, and Druhyu and Anu in the west and north. They ruled the earth righteously.

Puru, who was virtuous, humble, and truthful, shone as the emperor. Later, the king went to the forest, performed great penance, and eventually attained the supreme heaven. There, he enjoyed the best pleasures, surpassing all others. Indra, seeing his pride, said: "O King, tell me what great merit you have earned." Yayati, blinded by delusion, replied: "O Lord of gods, my merit is beyond count. The gods in heaven do not possess even a fraction of my merit."

Hearing this, Indra said: "O great king, you do not know the power of their merit. You have insulted everyone and, in your delusion, are boasting to me today. Because of this, all your great merit is destroyed. Fall to the earth and perform merit again." Yayati fell to the earth, filled with sorrow. His grandsons, led by Shibi, saw him falling. Recognizing their grandfather, they gave him their own merit, and by that, the king returned to heaven and rejoiced like a god.

Then the yogi Narada said to him with compassion: "O King, you have encountered an obstacle that destroyed your merit. Worship the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha), and you will attain eternal bliss; otherwise, you will fall to the earth again." Narada gave him the ten-syllable mantra of Ganesha, and Yayati chanted it with devotion. When his merit was exhausted, the king, through the path of light, attained Ganesha by his devotion and became a glorious attendant of Ganesha.

This holy story of Yayati has been described briefly. Whoever listens to it will undoubtedly have all their desires fulfilled.

Chapter 32 : The Instruction of the Vrata to Dhanaka

Mudgala said: Yadu had four sons who resembled the gods. The eldest was Sahasrajit, and he had a son named Shatajit, who was well-known. Shatajit had three sons: Haihaya, Haya, and Vapurhaya. Haihaya’s son was known as Dharma, whose son was Netra, followed by Kunti, and then Sohanji. Sohanji’s son was the king Mahishman, who founded the city of Mahishmati. His son was Bhadrasena, whose son was Durmada, and the wise and prominent king Dhanaka was also among them.

Dhanaka had four world-renowned sons: Kritavirya, Kritagni, Kritavarma, and Kritauja. Among them, the eldest, Kritavirya, was a valiant, charitable, and righteous king. It was he who brought the fourth Krishna-paksha Chaturthi (the dark-fortnight fourth day) to the earth, which grants all auspiciousness and was brought down from heaven for the protection of the subjects.

Daksha asked: O great yogi, how did that king bring the fourth day to the earth? Tell me his story. Hearing this nectar-like tale increases the joy in my heart, so please narrate it in detail.

Mudgala said: Long ago, in the great city of Mahishmati, there lived a man named Bhima, who was born into a low caste. He was greedy for wealth and addicted to sinful deeds. He would kill people he saw on the road and constantly slaughter animals to sell their meat. Once, on the day of the Krishna-paksha Chaturthi in the month of Magha, he was wandering in the forest in a mountainous region to kill people, but by divine providence, he found no one. Driven by greed and lacking food and water, he wandered aimlessly until the sun set. Seeing this, he decided to return home. On his way, he encountered twelve Brahmins, killed them, took their belongings, and went home. When the moon rose, he was hungry and called his son, Ganesha, to eat. He committed such grave sins again on another Krishna-paksha Chaturthi, and he died at the time of moonrise.

The messengers of Ganesha came to take him and carried him in a celestial chariot to Kailasa, the abode of Ganesha. On the way to heaven, he was praised by gods like Brahma and Vishnu, and he entered the abode of Shiva. After enjoying pleasures there, he fell back to the earth and was born as the son of Dhanaka in Mahishmati. He was known as Kritavirya, a king devoted to various virtues, who performed sacrifices and served gods, Brahmins, and guests.

The valiant King Kritavirya was childless and, being very righteous, he strove hard to have a son. When he could not obtain offspring, he became sorrowful. He entrusted his peaceful kingdom to his ministers and went to the forest with his wife to perform penance. Due to the impressions of his previous life, he became devoted to Ganesha. He performed various austerities, but despite his efforts, the king remained childless and was reduced to skin and bones.

Seeing him in that state, the sage Narada went to the heavenly abode of Dhanaka and told him the situation. He said, "O King, your son is in the forest striving for a child. He is performing various penances and has become mere skin and bones. He has not obtained a child and will die soon. Tell me, what will be the fate of a sonless man?" After saying this, the sage went to Kailasa with reverence.

Dhanaka then pondered deeply and decided to seek refuge in the all-knowing Brahma. He went to Brahma, bowed down, and praised him with various hymns. Dhanaka said, "O Brahma, my righteous son is childless and is striving for a child by performing various vows. He has abandoned his kingdom and is performing penance in the forest, becoming skin and bones. Tell me the way by which he can have a son, so that I may ensure the growth of my lineage."

Brahma then replied, "The vow he performed in his previous life, which was associated with the killing of Brahmins, was done in ignorance. Yet, because it was the Chaturthi, all his sins were destroyed. Great sins are destroyed by the practice of the Sankashti Vrata. Whatever is done on the day of the Sankashti Vrata remains imperishable; there is no doubt about this. O King, one should perform meritorious deeds on the Krishna-paksha Chaturthi. Your son killed twelve Brahmins in his previous life on that day, and that great sin remained. Even through various penances, pilgrimages, and sacrifices, that sin would not have been destroyed. The sin committed on the Sankashti day is like a diamond-hard mark, but listen to my words: the one who observes the holy Sankashti Chaturthi will never have to face birth and death again.

Know that this world is full of Maya and the four types of suffering. When the Sankashti Chaturthi of Ganesha is observed, those four types of suffering never occur. By the power of this vow, one attains happiness in this world and eventually merges into the Supreme Brahman. Those who commit sins on Chaturthi find no liberation; they go to heaven due to their sins, but then return to earth to suffer the fruits of their actions. Because of this, the low-caste man was taken to Kailasa by Ganesha’s messengers to suffer for his sins, and that is why your son is now suffering. Since the Chaturthi destroys the sins committed on that day, your son shall observe this vow. He will then be free from sin and will surely be blessed with a son. One must perform meritorious acts to destroy the sins committed; the glory of the Chaturthi is infinite. To destroy those sins, let him observe twelve Sankashti Vratas, and the sin of killing the twelve Brahmins will be eradicated."

Filled with compassion, Brahma instructed him in the vow, and the King bowed to him and departed.

Chapter 33 : The Description of Obtaining the Vow by Kritavirya

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: After obtaining the vow and writing down the book, Dhanaka, through his illusory power, immediately appeared in the dream of Kritavirya. Seeing his father Dhanaka, Kritavirya bowed to him. The king took his son into his lap and spoke to him.

Dhanaka said: Son, you have made various efforts, especially for the sake of a child. Now, by my command, perform this one vow. By this merit, you will surely have a son, there is no doubt about it. Take this book which was told to me by Brahma. Having said this, he respectfully placed the book in his son's hands and disappeared. The prince then woke up. Seeing the book in his hand, he was amazed and overjoyed. Becoming sorrowful, he remembered his father and wept.

Then King Kritavirya, along with his wife, went to his city, where he was respectfully honored by his ministers and others. The king then summoned the Brahmins, bowed to them, and showed them the book. The Brahmins explained its meaning. They said: O King, you are very fortunate. In this book, an excellent vow is written, which removes all troubles and grants everything. On the fourth day of the dark fortnight, at the time of moonrise, Ganesha, the bestower of success, should be worshipped with special devotion. After worshipping the Lord of Ganas, the one performing the vow should offer Arghya to the lunar day, to Ganesha, and to the Moon.

O King, from sunrise, one should remember Ganesha and abstain from drinking water and other things. After offering Arghya to the Moon, O best of kings, one should worship the Brahmins and bow to them along with their wives. Feed them respectfully with excellent food, sweets, milk pudding, and sweet items. Then, one should dine with friends. Give gifts to the Brahmins and garments to the women. Then, keep a vigil at night with the chanting of Ganesha’s praises. In the clear morning, after bathing, worship the Lord as before. Then, O King, feed the Brahmins as before, give them generous donations, and bow to them.

Perform this Ganesha vow every month. Being free from all troubles, you will eventually attain the state of self-bliss. O King, you have received a vow of infinite glory. Through your association, it will become a source of success for the people. O King, this vow has never been heard or seen by anyone before. It will be especially beneficial for the people. The entire world is said to be full of four types of troubles; therefore, this vow will be for the destruction of those bonds. By the power of this vow, one enjoys all rare divine pleasures here and attains liberation in the end.

Having said this, all the Brahmins went to their respective hermitages. King Kritavirya performed this excellent vow.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Mudgala, Prajapati (Daksha), with his hair standing on end, spoke again to clear his doubts.

Daksha said: Great Sage, you said that the vow came to the earth starting from Kritavirya. How then did the one named Bhima perform it? You described that by the merit of the Chaturthi vow, he himself became Kritavirya, which creates doubt in my mind. Therefore, O Mudgala, tell me the truth to destroy my doubt. You are omniscient, there is no doubt, you are a great Yogi.

Mudgala said: O Prajapati, listen to my words, O wise one, by which your doubt will surely be destroyed. O Daksha, know that this Ganesha vow, known as Chaturthi, is ancient and is said to be the remover of all troubles. It is accepted in the heavens, on earth, and in the netherworld. It was established from the time the fourth day (Chaturthi) originated. People on earth do not know it anywhere, O Lord. It was from Kritavirya that this vow became known everywhere. I have told you everything; listen to the story of King Kritavirya, which grants merit to those who hear it.

Thus ends the thirty-third chapter of the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the attainment of the vow by Kritavirya.

Chapter 34 : The Story of Kritavirya

Mudgala said: By observing the vow for just one year, the great sin known as the twelve-fold Brahma-hatya (killing of a Brahmin) was destroyed, and the King shone with radiance. By the power of the merit of this vow, the Queen became pregnant and, at an auspicious time, gave birth to a son who was the best among the radiant. Seeing that he was born without hands and feet, the mother wept. Kritavirya was then informed by all his ministers.

The King, setting aside his grief, performed the birth rituals and remained in the city, which was filled with great festivities. That night, in a dream, he saw the yogi Dattatreya. O Prajapati, listen to what was said in that dream: "O best of kings, after twelve years have passed, I will come to your house and make your son complete with all his limbs. Do not worry, O King of Kings, this son of yours, born from the grace of the Lord of the Ganas, will be very radiant and honored by the gods."

Having said this, the yogi Dattatreya disappeared. The King woke up full of joy, remembering the Lord of the Ganas. He then had a proclamation made everywhere on earth that people must always perform the vow on the fourth day of the dark fortnight. "If anyone does not perform this vow, I will punish them." From the Chandala to the Brahmin, everyone performed the vow especially. O giver of honor, men and women of eight years and above all performed the great vow of the Lord of the Ganas according to the rules. In his kingdom, no person was ever unhappy; there was no fear of infertility, nor was there any fear of diseases.

Then, after twelve years had passed, the yogi Dattatreya, who is served by the masters of yoga, arrived. The King bowed to him. Having been worshipped and fed, the sage said to him, "O King, show me your son; I have come to see the miracle." The King, filled with joy, placed his son in his hands. Holding him in his lap, the great yogi said to the King, "O King, you performed the vow without knowing the rules, and because of that, your son was born without limbs. During the vigil of the vow, O King, you yawned repeatedly and did not perform the ritual rinsing of the mouth (achamana); that is why he is like this. By the power of this vow, Vishnu himself in human form has become your son; he will be famous by his partial incarnation."

Then the great sage gave him a single-syllable mantra of Ganesha, according to the rules, for the wise Kartavirya. Having instructed him to perform the observance for twelve years, the Lord disappeared, and the King was filled with joy. Near the Coral region (Pravala-kshetra), in a forest filled with tigers and other animals, the King, along with his people, placed his son and felt sorrowful.

Then Kartavirya performed severe penance, subsisting only on air, meditating on the elephant-faced Lord in his heart. O Daksha, devoid of hands and feet, he chanted the mantra, transcending body-consciousness, and stood still like a log of wood. Due to the power of his penance, animals like elephants and lions always moved about in the forest without any enmity. When twelve years of penance had passed in this way, Dhundhi (Ganesha) came there to his most devoted follower.

Seeing the Lord of the Ganas emerge from the middle of a pond, the King bowed to Vighnesha, worshipping him with devotion. Filled with great joy, the heroic and glorious Kartavirya praised the elephant-faced Lord.

Kartavirya said: Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, salutations again to Vighnesha. Salutations to Vinayaka, the Lord of gods, the Lord of all. Salutations to the attributeless, the Supreme Lord, the one beyond the beyond. Salutations to the beginningless one, the one worthy of worship by all. Salutations to you, Heramba, the protector of the poor, the one worthy of universal worship. Salutations to the Brahman, the giver of Brahman to the Brahmins. Salutations to the formless, the one with form, the Supreme Soul. Salutations to you, the embodiment of Yoga, the giver of Yoga, the embodiment of peace. Salutations to you, the embodiment of knowledge, the one who initiates the path of action, the embodiment of eternal bliss. Salutations to you, the Lord of qualities, who creates through Rajas, sustains through Sattva, and destroys through Tamas. Salutations to you, the master of Maya, who is devoid of various illusions, the giver of delusion to the deluded. Salutations to you, the Lord of self-bliss, the bestower of success and wisdom, O Lambodara (the pot-bellied one). How can I praise you, O Lord of the Ganas, when even the Vedas are bewildered by you, and Shiva, Vishnu, and other yogis worship you as the embodiment of Yoga?

Then the Lord of the Ganas said to his best devotee, "O noble one, Kartavirya, ask for the boon you desire in your heart. The hymn you have composed for me will grant all desires. It will grant complete limbs to those who are limb-less. If a person performs my worship with devotion and recites this hymn, I will make everything complete for them through the power of this recitation. After enjoying all pleasures here with sons and grandsons, at the end, they will become one with the Supreme and attain self-bliss."

Then Kartavirya, the great devotee, said to the Lord of the Ganas, who is fond of his devotees: "What shall I ask for, O Lord of the Ganas? Everything is an illusion. By your mere sight, I have known this, there is no doubt. Still, for the satisfaction of my loved ones, O Lord, I ask: grant beauty to my body and devotion to your feet, O Lord. Whatever I desire, let it be easily accessible to me, O elephant-faced one. Grant me a complete kingdom by your grace. Stay in this region and protect your devotees, O Lord of obstacles. Grant them Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, and make them one with Brahman. Since I have seen the elephant-faced Lord in the form of coral, let this place be known forever as Pravala-kshetra. Make me always victorious, thousand-armed, and equal to Vishnu in prowess, O ocean of mercy. May my service to you, O God, always be like that of the gods, and may my mantras and other practices be successful by your grace. By my mere remembrance, grant me what is lost, O Lord. May there be no one equal to me in the three worlds, including Indra and others."

Then the Lord of the Ganas said, "So be it. Everything will happen for you, O King, by my grace, there is no doubt. By my remembrance and the service of mantras, you will always be free of obstacles, O King of Kings." Having said this, Ganesha disappeared.

Then, O King, his body became complete. He became thousand-armed and possessed feet. He became immensely strong, heroic, and radiant. The gods and others came there to see the King. Kartavirya shone then as a partial incarnation of Vishnu himself. Then, the King, along with the Brahmins, respectfully installed the idol of the Lord of the Ganas and worshipped it with devotion. The King established Brahmins there for the purpose of worship and worshipped the gods and Brahmins according to the rules. He bowed with devotion, and they too granted him boons. The gods, including Indra, were pleased and returned to heaven.

Kartavirya then went to his city in a chariot. Knowing the news through messengers, all the citizens came to him. The ministers bowed to him, and he was congratulated by his friends. They respectfully led him into the decorated city. He bowed to his mother and father with joy. He was made extremely happy by their blessings.

After some time, having established his famous son in his kingdom, Kritavirya went to the forest with his wife. There, the King worshipped Ganesha with a single-minded heart. In a short time, he, along with his wife, went to the realm of the Lord of the Ganas. In his kingdom, all the people became observers of the Sankashti vow and, in due course, attained the Lord of the Ganas, O Daksha Prajapati. Whoever listens to or reads this story of Kritavirya will have no fourfold obstacles.

Thus ends the thirty-fourth chapter named "The Story of Kritavirya" in the third section of the Great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 35 : The Invitation of Sahasrarjuna

Mudgala said: Kartavirya Arjuna then ruled his excellent kingdom righteously and protected his subjects according to the laws of statecraft. Having conquered all the kings of the earth, he enjoyed the rule of the seven continents with his great strength. He was respectfully named Sahasrarjuna (the thousand-armed) by the gods, and there was no one in the entire warrior class who could equal him. Even the gods in heaven were filled with fear, thinking that if the king were to become angry, he would surely conquer heaven itself. There was no counting his vast armies, and he filled the entire world with his glory and brilliance.

Once, the king was sporting in the Narmada River with his wives, playfully holding back the flow of the river with his thousand arms. At that same time, Ravana, the lord of the demons, was on the banks of the Narmada worshipping a Shiva Linga, and the water blocked by Arjuna reached him. After finishing his worship, the demon king asked his messengers who had obstructed the flow of the river. He told them to go and see who had caused such a disturbance, declaring that he would kill the person responsible for interrupting his daily rituals. The messengers hurried, saw Kartavirya Arjuna, and reported back to the mighty Ravana.

Hearing this, the powerful and angry Ravana went to the king with his six chief ministers. Seeing Ravana, the mighty Arjuna, still wearing his wet clothes, grabbed his mace and fought him alone. A fierce mace battle ensued between them, and the king struck Ravana heavily. The king shattered the armor of the demon king, causing Ravana to fall. The king then captured him, took him to his palace, and kept him in his assembly hall as a plaything. The king amused himself by watching the ten-headed Ravana.

Then, the great sage Pulastya suddenly arrived. The king bowed to him with great devotion, worshipped him, and fed him with care. After the king bowed again and stood before him with folded hands, the wise sage said, "O King, release my grandson Ravana, who is endowed with boons. Remain in friendship with him, O wise one." The king agreed and released Ravana. Pulastya then blessed the king and departed.

Possessing such radiance, the king was truly an embodiment of Vishnu, and his righteous nature was beyond description. During his conquest of the directions, he struck the ocean with his feet, causing it to tremble in fear. He performed many grand sacrifices with generous offerings, and the gods were delighted by the oblations. He gave whatever the Brahmins requested and ensured that his people remained devoted to their respective duties.

This excellent king was always devoted to Ganesha; he loved the Gana-patya tradition and was fond of visiting holy sites, gods, and guests. Once, he came to Mayuresha and performed a pilgrimage. Staying there for a year, he became eager to continue his journey. He had Ganesha installed by learned Brahmins, named him Pravala-Gananaatha, and worshipped him. In this way, the son of Kritavirya worshipped Ganesha with various sentiments and became famous throughout the three worlds.

He shone with the radiance born of his observance of the Chaturthi vow, but he eventually became deluded by a sense of incompleteness. Endowed with supreme wealth and enjoyment, he became arrogant and forgot Ganesha. Abandoning the mantra of Ganesha due to the intoxication of his own knowledge, the wicked king said, "I am myself the form of Ganesha, whom should I worship?"

O Lord, Arjuna then became afflicted with many obstacles. Listen, O great one, as I tell you what happened. The king went to the forest for hunting with his army, all dressed in blue. He killed many animals and sent them to his city through his messengers. Suddenly, the great king saw an excellent hermitage. He asked his ministers whose hermitage it was. They replied that it was the hermitage of the sage Jamadagni. The king, filled with joy, went to the sage.

Bowing with his ministers, the glorious king said to the great sage Jamadagni, "O great yogi, my birth, my knowledge, my lineage, and my ancestors are all blessed today by the sight of your feet." The sage welcomed the king with honor, offered him a seat, worshipped him with his disciples, and asked about his well-being. The sage honored the king, knowing him to be an expansion of Vishnu. The king replied with affection, "By your blessings, I am well. I came here intent on hunting, and by the power of my past merits, I have attained your great vision. I am purified by your sight, O Lord. Please give me your permission now; I shall return to my city with my army. There is no one equal to you in yoga and penance."

The sage replied with joy, "O King, you are an expansion of Vishnu himself, and you have arrived at my hermitage. I request you, O Lord, to eat something provided by me before you leave this afternoon." The king bowed and said, "O Master, my army is hungry and tired from wandering in the forest. How can I eat alone while leaving them? You do not have the power to feed them all, O sage. Therefore, O king of yogis, please give me permission; I will go to my city with my army."

The great sage then said to the king, "Do not worry, O King; I shall feed you and your entire army. By my penance, the entire universe is under my control. Therefore, go to the river and bathe with all your men." The king agreed, bowed to him, and went to the river with his army, filled with astonishment.

Chapter 36 : The Revivification of Renuka and Jamadagni

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: After the king had departed, the great sage Jamadagni called Renuka and explained the entire incident to her. Together, they invoked the divine Kamadhenu, the cow of the Creator. Upon her arrival, they worshipped her and requested her assistance. They asked her to prepare a grand feast for Kartavirya and his army, urging her to act swiftly. Hearing their request, Kamadhenu, through her divine power, manifested a magnificent city surrounded by moats. She created a kitchen filled with four types of food. Seeing this, the sage was filled with joy. He invited the king and his army, and the Brahmin fed them all with various exquisite dishes.

The guests, delighted, consumed the nectar-like food and, being satisfied, ate fruits and tubers. Afterward, they enjoyed betel leaves with great affection, and the sage honored them with garments and other gifts. The king, astonished, bowed to the great sage and, taking leave, returned to his city with his army. As he was leaving, his minister spoke to him with humility, suggesting that he should ask the Brahmin for the cow, as he would surely give it. He remarked that the cow was a jewel and that without it, the king’s vast kingdom seemed worthless. Hearing this, the king, overcome by delusion and troubled by obstacles, ordered his minister to return and bring the cow by any means necessary.

The wicked minister returned with the army and demanded the cow from the sage on behalf of the king. The great sage, filled with anger, refused to give the divine cow to the foolish king. The wicked minister then forcibly seized the cow and left the hermitage. The cow, enraged, created a powerful force that destroyed the king’s army and the minister. Upon learning of this, the prideful son of Kritavirya, Kartavirya, went to the hermitage to wage war. His massive army was again destroyed by the power of the cow. The king, now humbled and free from arrogance, felt great sorrow.

The Kamadhenu returned to heaven, and the king returned to the hermitage and struck the sage with an arrow, killing him. Renuka rebuked the wretched king, who then struck her all over her body with twenty-one arrows. She, deeply distressed, cursed the king, saying that he would surely die soon and that his thousand arms would be severed. Terrified, the king fled to his city.

After the king left, the sage’s wife wept bitterly and remembered her son, Rama, who was at Badarikashrama. Upon being remembered, Rama arrived at his mother’s side. Seeing his father dead, he wept, and seeing his mother pierced by arrows, he removed them from her body. Renuka comforted her son, telling him not to grieve and that she would remain near him. She instructed him to bring Dattatreya and perform the funeral rites according to the scriptures so that they might be restored to their bodies. She urged him to be diligent, to slay the wicked king, and to sever his arms. She told him that since the king had struck her with twenty-one arrows, he must rid the earth of the Kshatriya race twenty-one times.

Having said this, the goddess abandoned her body and ascended to heaven. Rama, with the help of Dattatreya, performed the rites. On the fifth day, Rama completed the rituals, and Dattatreya returned to his place to seek alms. On the sixth day, a powerful tiger appeared. Seeing it, a fearful Rama remembered his mother. She appeared in the form of just a head. Rama was astonished, and Renuka asked why he had called her. The tiger then vanished.

Meanwhile, Dattatreya arrived. Seeing the head, he asked Rama why he had invoked Renuka. He told him to perform the rites properly as she was incomplete. Rama explained the situation, and with Dattatreya’s help, he completed the rituals. On the twelfth day, Rama performed the Sapindikarana, and on the thirteenth day, the funeral rites.

Then, Jamadagni appeared in a divine body. The sage, filled with joy, embraced Rama. Dattatreya, the best of those who know yoga, spoke to them with deep insight, advising them to worship Ganesha with devotion using the supreme mantra to remain free from obstacles and attain peace. He explained that their death at the hands of a Kshatriya was a result of not knowing the Lord of Obstacles. The great sage gave them the Ganesha mantra, and the two Brahmins practiced penance. Dattatreya then returned to his hermitage, while Rama remained there, joyfully serving his parents.

Thus ends the thirty-sixth chapter of the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the revivification of Renuka and Jamadagni.

Chapter 37 : The Narrative of Parashurama

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: After a thousand years had passed, the Lord Vighneshwara, the bestower of boons, appeared before the son of Bhrigu (Jamadagni) and his wife, and spoke to them. O Renuka and Jamadagni, ask of me a supreme boon; I have been pleased by your penance performed with the recitation of the single-syllable mantra. Hearing these words, they were filled with joy and, with hearts full of devotion, spoke to the Lord of the Ganas. O Lord of Ganesha, our lineage is blessed by your vision, and surely, this is the fruit of our penance and study. Having said this, they bowed down, worshipped the Lord of the Ganas, and with hair standing on end due to ecstasy, they praised Him, O Prajapati.

Renuka and Jamadagni said: Salutations to you, the protector against obstacles, the remover of obstacles for your devotees, and the creator of obstacles for the non-devotees; salutations to you who are the embodiment of Brahman. Salutations to you, the beginningless one, the supportless one, the one in the form of the curved trunk, and the Lord of the infinite Ganas. Salutations again and again to you, Heramba, who possess various powers, who create various distinctions, and yet are devoid of all distinctions. Salutations to you, the one who plays with immeasurable illusion, the one who is Dhundhi, the companion of Siddhi and Buddhi, and the bestower of Siddhi and Buddhi. Salutations to you, the Lord of Svananda, the giver of Yoga to your devotees, the embodiment of Yoga, and the bestower of peace. Salutations to you, the Lord with the broken tusk, the one with the large belly, the embodiment of unbroken bliss, and the bestower of worldly enjoyment and liberation. Salutations to you, the Lord with the large belly, the Lord of obstacles, the giver of happiness, and the one whose flag bears a mouse; salutations to you, the one who has a mouse as his vehicle. Salutations to you, the four-armed one, the one worshipped first by all, the enjoyer, the eldest of all, and the embodiment of both father and mother. Since even the Vedas, the gods led by Shiva, and the yogis are unable to praise you, how can we? Therefore, we bow to you.

Having praised the Lord of Ganesha in this way, they bowed down. The Lord of the Ganas, filled with joy, raised them and said: O noble children, ask for the boon you desire in your hearts, for I am bound by your devotion. Hearing the words of Ganesha, the couple bowed again, touched his feet, and spoke with joy. They said: O Gajanan, if you are pleased and are a bestower of boons, then grant us devotion to your lotus feet that brings happiness. O Lord of obstacles, O Lord of Yoga, grant us supreme peace and the devotion that is dear to the Ganas and always brings bliss.

Saying "Om," the Lord of the Ganas spoke to them again, seeing their devotion and state of mind, for he is the giver of happiness to his devotees: This hymn composed by you shall be a bestower of Yoga. Whoever desires anything, I shall grant it to them through the recitation of this hymn. O Jamadagni, always remain peaceful and be a devotee of mine. O Renuka, you who are embodied, worship me with a yogic mind. People will surely see you in the form of a head here, but through the eye of knowledge, the gods and others shall see you with all your limbs. Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, disappeared, and Renuka and Jamadagni remained in their hermitage.

Then, one day, Rama (Parashurama), who was the powerful Lord Vishnu himself, bowed to his parents with folded hands and spoke. Shri Rama said: I shall destroy Arjuna and make the earth devoid of the Kshatriya race; there is no doubt about this, O parents, by your command. Then Jamadagni, filled with joy, said to him: Do not commit useless violence, O Rama, abandon your pride. Then Rama, the descendant of Bhrigu, spoke with humility: O great sage, I have made a vow before my mother. I shall fulfill that vow, for I am devoted to my mother; or else, I shall abandon my body for that sake, O sage. Seeing his persistence, he said to his son with respect: Go to Shankara, O Rama, he will fulfill your desire.

Bowing to them, he went to Kailasa to Lord Shankara. Having bowed to Maheshana and praised him, he spoke: I shall kill Kartavirya and make the earth devoid of Kshatriyas twenty-one times; tell me the means, O Lord. Then Shiva gave the single-syllable mantra of Ganesha to Rama. Having received it, the mighty one quickly went to the forest. Remembering the penance performed by Ganesha in a previous birth by Vamana, he remained engaged in that same penance. Consuming only air, he chanted the mantra with meditation. Through intense samadhi, he pleased the Lord of obstacles.

When a hundred years had passed, Gajanan came to him, who was full of devotion and engaged in intense penance. He said to the noble descendant of Bhrigu, the imperishable Vishnu: Ask for a boon, O noble one, I shall grant it, being pleased by your devotion. Hearing his beautiful words, the Brahmana rose, bowed to the Lord of the Ganas, prostrated like a staff on the ground, and worshipped him specially. Having worshipped and bowed again, Rama, overwhelmed with the essence of devotion and with tearful eyes, praised him with folded hands.

Rama said: Salutations to you, Lord of the Ganas, the increaser of the joy of devotees, the God who loves devotion, Heramba; salutations to you again and again. Salutations to you, the embodiment of the knowledge of Vedanta, the one who is both the mind and speech, and yet devoid of mind and speech, the embodiment of Yoga. Salutations to you, the one with the elephant face, the one who assumes the form of the attributeless, and yet, below the neck, the one who assumes the human form, the one with attributes. Salutations to you, the beginningless one, the one worthy of worship, the bestower of everything to all, the one worshipped first, the Lord of obstacles, the mover of various obstacles. Salutations to you, the remover of obstacles for the virtuous, the creator of obstacles for the wicked, the Lord of self-bliss, Dhundhiraja. Salutations to you, O Lord with the curved trunk, salutations to you, O Lambodara. Salutations to you, the embodiment of peace, the bestower of peace. Salutations to you, the Lord with the large belly, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the bestower of various opulences, the one who causes illusion. Salutations to you, the one who causes delusion through various types of knowledge, the Lord who always removes the delusion and ignorance of his true devotees. Salutations to you, the destroyer of various demons, the one who removes the pride of the gods, the embodiment of both gods and demons, the Lord of Brahman. O Lord of obstacles, how can I praise you, who exist everywhere in the form of Yoga, devoid of union and separation? O Lord of the Ganas, since even the yogis like Shesha and the gods like Shiva and the Vedas are unable to praise you, how can I, the Lord of the Ganas, praise you? Therefore, I have bowed to you. Being pleased with that, O Lord, protect me, who have taken refuge in you.

As he praised him with such devotion, O giver of honor, his hair stood on end due to devotion, and his throat choked. The leader of the Bhrigus danced, filled with supreme bliss, saying "Victory to Vighnesha, Heramba, Ganesha." Seeing him thus immersed in devotion, the Lord Gajanan, with tearful eyes and hair standing on end, O Prajapati, said: O Rama, O noble one, listen to my supreme words. You are blessed in every way, for you are my devotee who has attained immersion in me. This hymn composed by you shall be the bestower of the essence of devotion. Whoever reads or hears this constantly, I shall always destroy all his obstacles. After enjoying all worldly pleasures here, he shall attain Svananda (my abode) in the end. Whatever the wise one thinks of, I shall grant that, O Keshava. He shall always be dear to me; there is no doubt about this. Take my axe; with it, you shall be equal to me in strength. You shall defeat the great warrior Arjuna, who is surrounded by kings. You shall surely make the earth devoid of Kshatriyas. You shall be victorious twenty-one times. Your name shall become famous everywhere and shall be auspicious; it is called Parashurama, and its mere remembrance brings happiness. Having performed sacrifices, O noble one, and having established your fame on earth, you shall always worship me in a state of peaceful Yoga.

Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas disappeared. Rama then had Ganesha installed by the Brahmanas. Having worshipped him, Rama went to his home, filled with joy. He bowed to Renuka and Jamadagni, holding the axe. Having taken their permission, he, along with his brothers, challenged Kartavirya, who was of great strength and valor. All the kings came, equipped with perfected armors, invincible by the gods and others, and of great strength. By the boon of Ganesha and the power of the axe, the Lord Rama killed them all in battle with effort. He killed Kartavirya with great anger; after fighting a terrible battle, he cut off his arms in sport. Kartavirya died by the axe, O Prajapati. Then, the victorious Rama, intent on the slaughter of the Kshatriyas, made the earth devoid of Kshatriyas in his anger. Again, through the power of the Brahmanas, the Kshatriyas were born. Rama killed all those born in the Kshatriya lineage again. O Daksha, he made the earth devoid of Kshatriyas again and again. Having made the earth devoid of Kshatriyas twenty-one times, he satisfied his ancestors with the blood of the Kshatriyas. Having performed a sacrifice, the glorious one gave the earth to the Brahmanas. Then, the earth was given to him by the ocean out of fear, O Vidhi. Rama remained there, worshipping the Lord of the Ganas with exclusive devotion, and is now a great Ganapatya.

I have told you this most wonderful narrative of Kartavirya, Rama, Jamadagni, and Renuka. The person who hears or reads this with devotion shall attain the fruit; it is the bestower of worldly enjoyment and liberation, O Daksha, and the bestower of the desired objects.

Thus ends the thirty-seventh chapter entitled "The Narrative of Parashurama" in the third section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 38 : The Description of the Harivamsa

Mudgala said: Kartavirya had one hundred sons, all of whom were great chariot warriors. Among them, five were particularly powerful, heroic, righteous, and glorious. These were Jayadhvaja, Shurasena, Vrishabha, and the two Pururjitas. Among them, Jayadhvaja became the primary king, O Prajapati. Jayadhvaja’s son was the valiant Talajangha, and he had one hundred sons who were known as the Talajanghas. The eldest among them was the mighty-armed King Vitihotra, while others like Vrishabha and the rest were Yadavas of virtuous deeds. Vrishabha was the progenitor of the lineage, and his son was Madhu. Madhu had one hundred sons, of whom Vrishni was the eldest and the one who continued the lineage.

Vitihotra’s son was the highly glorious Ananta, and his son was Durjaya, who was well-versed in all scriptures. His wife was virtuous and devoted to her husband. Once, the king went to the forest and saw Urvashi. Captivated by her, he became lustful and forgot his own wife. After a long time had passed, he was enlightened by Kanva and returned to his home, where he performed penance. Kanva performed the sacrifices for him, and he became humble. His son was known as Supratika. I have thus told you everything regarding the lineage of Sahasrajit.

Now hear from me, O Prajapati, the lineage of Yadu’s son, Kroshtu. Kroshtu’s son was known as Vrijinavan. His son was Shahi, and his son was considered to be Kusheku. Kusheku’s son was the mighty Chitraratha. Chitraratha’s son was Shashabindu, who had ten million sons, the eldest of whom was Prithushrava. His son was Ushana, who is spoken of as righteousness personified. From him came Ruchaka, and he had five sons. Among them, the one who continued the lineage was the world-renowned Jyamagha. From him came the son Vidarbha, and from Vidarbha came Kusha and Kratha. Lomapada was the third, and Babhru was his son. Kriti was his son, and Ushika was his son. From Ushika was born a son, and from him came Chedi, from whom came Chaidya. His son was the mighty-armed Kaushika, known for his prowess. His son was the wise Sumantu, and from him came the pure Amala. Amala’s son was the mighty Nishveta. He had one hundred sons, all of whom were very powerful. Kratha also had a son, Kunti, then Dhrishti, and then Nirvriti. From him came Dasharha, then Vyoma, then Jimutaka, and then Vikriti. His son was the mighty Bhimaratha. From him came the mighty Navaratha. His son was Dasharatha, and his son was Shakuni. From him came Karambhi, and then Devarata. He performed the Ashvamedha sacrifice, and his son was Devakshatra. Madhu was his son, and from him was born Kuru. He had two sons, Sutrama and Anu. From Anu came Puruhotra, and his son is considered to be Ayu. Ayu’s son was Sattvata, who had seven sons: Bhajamāna, Bhaji, the divine Vrishni, Devavridha, Andhaka, and the famous Mahabhoja, O Prajapati.

Bhajamāna’s sons were Nimlochi, Kinkana, and Vrishni. Then King Devavridha performed penance for the sake of a son. Babhru was his son, who was virtuous and highly glorious, and well-versed in the meaning of all scriptures. He was renowned in the three worlds for his glory and brilliance. Those born in the lineage of Mahabhoja are known as the Bhojas. Vrishni’s sons were Sumitra and Yudhajit. From Anamitra came Shini, and from Anamitra came Nimna. Satrajit and Prasena were the famous sons of Nimna. Satrajit worshipped the Sun and obtained the Syamantaka jewel. From Shini was born a son who was expert in all matters, Satyaka, who was full of truth and highly glorious. Satyaki, also known as Yuyudhana, was his son, and Jaya was his son. Kuni was his wise son, and his son was Yugandhara. Another son of Anamitra was Vrishni, who was skilled in weaponry. Shvaphalka was Vrishni’s son, and another was Chitraratha. Shvaphalka married the daughter of the King of Kashi, and through her, he begot Akrura, who was respected by the virtuous. He had twelve other sons, including Asanga and Sarameya. Akrura’s sons were Devavan and Upadevaka. From Chitraratha came the sons Prithu, Aprithu, Ashvagriva, Subahu, and Sudhashva.

Then the mighty-armed Andhaka had four sons: Kukura, Bhajamāna, Shuchi, and Kambalabarhi. Kukura’s son was Vahni, and Vahni’s son was Kapotaroma. His son was Anu, and he was the friend of Tumburu. His son is known as Andhaka. From Andhaka was born the brilliant Dundubhi. He went to Govardhana and performed great penance. Brahma granted him the boon he desired in his heart: that his lineage would be eternal, and that he would possess glory, knowledge, and fame, always having friendship with the gods and the ability to assume any form at will. His son was Aridyota, and his son was Punarvasu, whose son is known as Ahuka. From Ahuka came Ugrasena and Devaka, O Prajapati. Devaka’s sons were heroic and god-like: Devavan, Upadeva, Sudeva, and Devavardhana. They had seven sisters, whom they gave to Vasudeva: Dhritadeva, Upadeva, Devarakshita, Shrideva, Shantideva, Sahadeva, and Suvrata. Devaka gave all of them to Vasudeva, O Prajapati. Ugrasena’s sons, led by Kamsa, were very powerful.

From Bhajamāna came the highly glorious Viduratha. His son was Shura, his son was Shini, and his son was Svayambhoja. Then came Hridika, Dehabahu, Shatadhanu, Kritavarma, Devamidha, and Shuraka. Vasudeva and others were the ten righteous sons of Shuraka. From the highly brilliant Vasudeva came Vasudeva, the teacher of the world. He was born as the son of Devaki, having been prayed to by the gods, to relieve the burden of the earth and to protect the virtuous. The highly fortunate Rohini also bore sons to Vasudeva, the eldest being Sankarshana, who was an expansion of Shesha and the wielder of the plow. Uma, in the form of Yogamaya, was born as the daughter of Yashoda by the command of Vasudeva. The other sons of Vasudeva, born before Vasudeva (Krishna), were killed by Kamsa immediately upon their birth. Then came Sushena, Dayi, the mighty Bhadrasena, Ritu, Sammardana, and Bhadra, who were known as the Kirtimans. After all of them were killed, Rohini bore to Vasudeva the lord of the world, Balabhadra, the wielder of the plow. When Rama was born, Devaki gave birth to Krishna, the primeval soul of the gods, the imperishable one, with the Shrivatsa mark on his chest.

Chapter 39: The Description of Vishnu Assuming a Human Body

Mudgala said: Rama had a beautiful and virtuous wife named Revati, and through her, he fathered two sons, Nishadha and Ulmuka. Krishna had sixteen thousand and one hundred and eight wives, all of whom were beautiful and possessed lovely eyes. From them, hundreds and thousands of sons were born, each having ten sons and one daughter. His sons, led by Pradyumna and Samba, were endowed with great brilliance, were mighty warriors, highly fortunate, and possessed great wisdom. Pradyumna’s son was the valiant Aniruddha, and his son was Vajra, who became the sustainer of the lineage. He was the sole survivor of the mace (musala) incident, and from him, many types of kings were born; it is not possible for me to describe them all.

Daksha asked: O giver of honor, how did Vishnu himself, who is the Supreme Being, come to assume a human body? Tell me the root cause of this.

Mudgala said: Listen, O Prajapati, to this wonderful story that happened in the past, by hearing which all your doubts will be dispelled. Long ago, the demons, having been defeated by the gods with the help of Vishnu, abandoned their kingdom and wandered in the forests out of fear. The gods, armed with weapons, went to destroy them completely. The demons, led by Prahlada, became terrified. With folded hands, they approached the gods and said, "We have abandoned our kingdom and are wandering in the forests. Why are you intent on killing us when we are unarmed? Everything we had has already been taken by you. Therefore, O wise ones, protect us who have sought your refuge. Do not kill us, especially since we are helpless."

Even then, urged by Vishnu, the gods began to kill the demons. The demons fled and sought refuge with their guru, Bhrigu, the treasure of yoga. The gods, filled with anger, followed them with weapons drawn. O Daksha, the demons saw Bhrigu, but the gods did not. Knowing that Bhrigu had gone to gather sacrificial wood, the demons approached his wife. They said, "O Goddess, protect us who are terrified and have sought your refuge. Otherwise, we will surely die before you." Hearing their plea, the compassionate lady said, "Do not fear; I will stop the gods." Hearing her words, the demons were relieved and stayed there, breathing easily.

In the meantime, the gods, led by Vishnu, arrived there with weapons drawn and began to kill the demons. Bhrigu’s wife said, "O best of gods, do not kill these demons who have sought my refuge, especially since they are now kingdomless." Ignoring her, Indra, prompted by Vishnu, approached with his thunderbolt (Vajra) to kill the demons, his eyes red with anger. Then, Bhrigu’s wife, through the power of her penance, paralyzed the King of Gods along with his thunderbolt. Seeing this, Vishnu used his razor-edged discus to sever the head of Bhrigu’s wife, and she fell to the ground.

At that very moment, Bhrigu arrived. Seeing his wife dead, he became enraged with crimson eyes. In his fury, he cursed Vishnu, saying, "O lowest of gods, you do not understand the sin of killing a Brahmin; therefore, fall to the earth." Through his penance and knowledge, Bhrigu restored his wife to life and protected the demons.

Deeply saddened and filled with grief, Vishnu sought refuge in the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) at the Mayuraka region. There, O Prajapati, the Lord spent a year in intense penance, fasting and worshipping the Lord of Obstacles with devotion. Pleased by his devotion, the Lord of Ganas appeared and told the great Vishnu to ask for his desired boon. Vishnu bowed to Ganesha, worshipped him with devotion, and praised him. Being praised with the Atharvashirsha, Ganesha said, "Ask for a boon, O Keshava; do not be sad, I will grant it."

Sri Vishnu said: If you are pleased, O Great Lord, then nullify the curse of Bhrigu for me. Grant me a place to reside here for your worship, and make me always free from obstacles and dear to your feet.

Saying "Om," Dhundhi (Ganesha) replied to his best devotee, "Do not worry, O Vishnu; listen to my supreme words. The words of Bhrigu, my devotee, cannot be false. Therefore, you will indeed fall into the mortal world. However, your knowledge will never be lost there. By my grace, O Lord of Gods, you will be free to act as you wish and will always be happy. At the request of the gods, you will take incarnations to destroy demons and establish righteousness. There, your fame will be immense, and you will establish great glory on earth. Your devotees will be there, and you will always fulfill their fourfold desires. After completing the work of the gods, O Janardana, you will return to Vaikuntha without doubt. You will assume a body of your own free will. By my grace, O Hrishikesha, all your desires will be fulfilled. Whatever else you have requested will also come to pass. Dwell in this region; I have given you an imperishable place."

Having said this, the compassionate Ganesha disappeared. Vishnu then resided there with devotion. He established the Lord of Ganas in the eastern direction, and the sages named him Vishnu-Vinayaka. Thus, O Daksha, I have told you the reason for the Lord of Gods, Vishnu, assuming a human body and taking birth in various wombs.

Chapter 40 : the Story of Mandavya

Mudgala said: In the lineage of Puru, there were many noble and righteous kings who performed sacrifices and were devoted to their own duties. Their history is vast and cannot be fully narrated due to its magnitude; therefore, O Daksha, listen as I tell you the reason for this. In the lineage of Puru, there was a king named Shantanu, who was famous, righteous, and dedicated to the protection of his subjects. His son was the noble Bhishma, who was well-versed in all scriptures, devoted to celibacy, and righteous. Shantanu had two sons by Satyavati: the mighty-armed Chitrangada and Vichitravirya. Chitrangada, being of great prowess, always competed with other Kshatriyas and was eventually killed in battle by a Gandharva. Vichitravirya suffered from consumption (tuberculosis) and died due to that ailment, leaving his friends grief-stricken. O Lord of the people, his two wives, the princesses of Kashi named Ambika and Ambalika, were filled with sorrow.

Satyavati then remembered the great sage Vyasa, her own son, for the sake of continuing the lineage. When he arrived, he asked, "Tell me, Mother, why are you sad? I will surely remove your sorrow." The queen replied, "O Lord, please increase our lineage. Be obedient to my command and beget children in the field of Vichitravirya." Saying "So be it," the great sage increased the lineage, though he appeared in a repulsive form during the night. From the union of the sage with Ambika, Dhritarashtra was born, and from Ambalika, Pandu was born. Vidura was born from a maidservant. This Vidura was Yama himself, born in a Shudra womb, and he became a master of ethics to experience the curse of Mandavya.

Daksha asked: O Lord, how did the curse of Mandavya fall upon Yama, who was devoted to the path of righteousness and equal to all?

Mudgala said: You have asked well, O Lord. Listen to the ancient story, and I will tell it to you in detail. The great sage Mandavya was engaged in penance in the forest. Some thieves, having stolen wealth, came running there. The king's chief warriors arrived that night to capture them. The thieves placed the stolen wealth near the sage and fled. The king's messengers saw the wealth, gathered it, and became very angry. They insulted the silent sage, seized him, and beat him as they brought him to their city. They reported the matter to the king, and the king, too, became angry. He ordered, "If this is the chief of thieves, he should be impaled on a stake." The soldiers obeyed, and the great sage was impaled through his hand.

While suffering there, Mandavya remained in penance and remembered his guru, Kashyapa, the creator. Upon being remembered, Kashyapa suddenly appeared before him. Seeing him in such pain, he became deeply distressed. He told his disciple, "Worship the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha), the destroyer of all obstacles and the giver of peace to his devotees." Mandavya then asked the great sage, "Tell me the path by which I may worship the Lord of Obstacles." Hearing this, Kashyapa explained the form of Ganesha, which brings supreme happiness to devotees.

Kashyapa said: This Gajanan (Elephant-faced one) is the mover of the five mental states. He is known as Chintamani by the knowers of the Vedas in the Vedanta. The body of the Lord is known as the Sampragnata (conscious) form, and the head of Ganapati is considered the Asampragnata (transcendental) form. Think upon the word 'Gaja' (Elephant); know that the 'Ga' represents the great illusion (Maya) and 'Ja' represents the Siddhi (perfection). Know that the intellect is the bearer of this illusion. The Lord of the Ganas is the master of both, appearing in a yogic form. Worship Him according to the prescribed rites, O sage, and you will be free from obstacles, without a doubt.

Having said this, the great sage gave him the armor (Kavacha) of Ganesha, which is sanctioned by the scriptures and based on the single-syllable mantra. After receiving the instructions, the sage Kashyapa returned to his abode. Mandavya worshipped the Lord of Obstacles there with deep devotion and wore the armor of the Lord of the Ganas. Once he wore the armor, O Daksha, he became free from pain. Just then, the king arrived there for some reason and saw the great sage; he bowed down with folded hands. The sage, weeping with fear, said, "Do not weep, O King; I do not curse you. It is due to the faults of my own actions that I am impaled on this stake. You did this in ignorance, O King, so I forgive you."

The king then brought skilled blacksmiths to help the sage, but they struggled to remove the stake. The tip of the stake remained embedded in his hand, and they were unable to extract it, becoming terrified. They cut the stake with weapons, but the part that remained in the sage's hand stayed there. The sage, worshipped by the king, went to the abode of Yama. With eyes red with anger, he spoke to Yama, the best of those who uphold justice.

Mandavya said: Tell me, what sinful act have I committed for which I have received this suffering from the stake, O great deceiver?

Hearing his angry words, Yama, filled with fear, replied with folded hands, indicating the deed: "O Brahmin, in your childhood, you pierced an insect with a thorn. You have received the fruit of that action, O best of sages." Hearing this, the great sage, filled with anger, said to the Lord of Dharma: "O great fool, I did that act in my childhood out of ignorance. What kind of fruit is this for such a small thing? Therefore, you are devoid of ethics and shall fall to the earth. You will be born in a Shudra womb and suffer for a hundred years before returning. From now on, listen to this rule, O son of the Sun: any act committed by a child under eight years of age shall be fruitless. By my word and the grace of the Lord of the Ganas, from this day forward, any act done by children shall be considered fruitless."

Mandavya went to his hermitage and performed supreme penance. By the power of the single-syllable mantra, he pleased the Lord of Obstacles. By the grace of the armor, the tip of the stake in his hand turned into flesh, and his hand became as it was before. From that day on, he became known as Ani-Mandavya. In a short time, the great yogi became dear to Ganesha. The Brahmin became devoted to the daily worship of Ganapati's idol. Always aware of Ganesha, he became highly renowned and worthy of worship by all. The best of men who hears or reads this holy story of Mandavya will always have the Lord of Obstacles pleased with him.

Chapter 41 : The Meeting of Yudhishthira and Krishna

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Mudgala said: After the death of Pandu, the Brahmanas left the blind Dhritarashtra and made Pandu the king, who then conquered the entire earth. Pandu, a devotee of righteousness and a conqueror of enemy cities, bestowed the royal insignia, including the umbrella, upon his elder brother Dhritarashtra. Dhritarashtra had a chaste wife named Gandhari, while Pandu had two wives, Kunti and Madri. Accompanied by his wives, King Pandu went to the forest, where he lived happily and enjoyed himself.

Once, the king shot an arrow at a sage who had assumed the form of a deer. As the sage lay dying, he cursed the king, who was engrossed in hunting, saying, "O lowest of kings, you have killed me while I was in the form of a deer, engaged with my mate. Therefore, you shall meet your end when you attempt to unite with your wife. At that moment, you will die immediately upon my words." Having said this, the great sage gave up his body.

Hearing this terrible curse, Pandu renounced his kingdom and lived in the forest with his wives, subsisting on roots and fruits. By the power of mantras, Kunti gave birth to the virtuous Yudhishthira from Dharma, Bhimasena from Vayu, and Arjuna from Indra, who was skilled in archery. Similarly, Madri gave birth to the valiant Nakula and Sahadeva from the Ashvini Kumaras, both of whom were virtuous, skilled, and handsome. Gandhari gave birth to a hundred sons of immense power and a daughter, all of whom were beautiful and virtuous.

Due to the curse, Pandu died while attempting to unite with Madri. She followed her husband in death, and Kunti raised the children. The noble Brahmanas brought them to Hastinapura, where Kunti and her sons lived, honored by Bhishma and others. As time passed, Duryodhana, filled with malice, began to harass the Pandavas with poison. He once placed them in a house of lac to burn them, but they were saved by their deeds along with their mother.

Because of such various malicious acts, the evil-minded Duryodhana continued to torment those heroes. Then Bhishma said, "Let the Pandavas take half the kingdom and leave the city." At the urging of Dhritarashtra, they went to Indraprastha. There, they achieved victory in all directions and became prosperous. Lord Krishna, the great soul, became their supporter. The Pandavas shone brightly after performing the Rajasuya sacrifice, which caused great distress in the heart of King Duryodhana.

At the instigation of Dhritarashtra, the evil-minded Duryodhana brought the Pandavas back and, having defeated them in a game of dice, became overjoyed. The daring Duryodhana sent the Pandavas to the forests for twelve years, followed by one year of exile in hiding. Renouncing their kingdom and all possessions, the Pandavas went to the forest. There, Krishna arrived with Balarama. He consoled the Pandavas, though he was filled with anger, saying, "O Yudhishthira, I will kill the wicked Duryodhana. I will give you a kingdom free of enemies."

Upon hearing Krishna speak thus, Yudhishthira replied, "O Janardana, I will rule only according to righteousness. I do not desire a kingdom gained through unrighteousness, which leads to sin. Therefore, O mighty-armed one, restrain your anger at my request. Tell me a righteous path to regain the kingdom." Then Krishna, free from anger, spoke to Yudhishthira, who was accompanied by Draupadi and his brothers.

Thus ends the forty-first chapter, titled "The Meeting of Yudhishthira and Krishna," in the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 42 : The Description of the Dialogue between Lakshmi and Narayana

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, listen to the story of the Lord of Obstacles, which the infinitely radiant Krishna narrated to the Pandavas.

Sri Krishna said: O Yudhishthira, you of virtuous nature, listen to these great words, by which you will be freed from your troubles and attain happiness. I shall briefly narrate an ancient history that removes all obstacles and is filled with the virtues of Mahodara.

Once, Lord Vishnu, the Lord of all gods, was residing in Vaikuntha, enjoying pastimes with Lakshmi and surrounded by Vishvaksena and others. He was always joyful, with Garuda nearby, and he shone, being served by Apsaras, Gandharvas, and gods. While all the gods were listening, Lakshmi, with great humility, spoke to that glorious and eternal Lord.

Sri Lakshmi said: O Lord, I wish to ask you, for you are my constant refuge. It is said in the Vedas that for women, there is nothing equal to their husband. You are the direct Lord of all gods, praised by Shankara and others, endowed with the six opulences, and the bestower of success upon your devotees. There is no doubt that the attainment of the status of gods and others comes from you; you are the provider of worldly enjoyments and liberation for all. You are the protector of everyone, and there is no one to protect you. Yet, you are absorbed in meditation; tell me, whom do you worship? In private, which deity do you worship, O Lord of the Lotus-seat? You are the supreme Brahman, the bliss itself, as declared by the Vedas. O Lord, your meditation fills my mind with wonder. Now, though I am your servant, I ask you without shame, for you are my master. O Vishnu, for the sake of removing my doubt, tell me specifically, as I am the servant of your lotus feet and consider you the one without a second. You are the same everywhere and always blissful; what is beyond that, and why do you delude people? What fruit will you attain by meditation and worship? Still, O Keshava, why are you absorbed in meditation?

Sri Krishna said: Hearing these words of his beloved, Janardana smiled, embraced the daughter of the ocean, and spoke to her.

Sri Vishnu said: Listen, my beloved, I will tell you what you have asked, which is auspicious, the savior of all beings, and the bestower of success to all. I do not meditate to delude people; I worship Ganesha, the family deity, with all my heart. O Goddess, the great gods were born from the Lord of Qualities (Gunesha), and those five gods were established in their positions by him. Gunesha performed penance and worshipped Ganesha, and in ancient times, Ganesha established Gunesha in his own position. Through Ganesha's boon, he became endowed with knowledge and existence. He, too, worships Ganesha with devotion. By serving Ganesha, the Lord of all, we have become endowed with knowledge and the like, and are engaged in our respective duties. Therefore, we worship him daily with devotion and respect. He is declared to be our family deity, the bestower of all goals.

Hearing the words of Vishnu, Lakshmi, with a surprised mind, spoke to the great Vishnu with humility.

Sri Lakshmi said: Who is this Ganesha? Tell me specifically about him, O Janardana, so that I may worship this family deity.

Sri Vishnu said: You have asked well, O Goddess. Listen to his form; I will tell you with affection if you can understand it. Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, resides in Svananda. Listen to the story of how Svananda became fivefold. O Goddess, the world exists in Brahman as name and form. The union of Svananda is called the non-existent form. That which is the form of truth and non-truth is the speaker of the Self; know that to be the true form, and that is called the true Svananda. That which is beyond truth and falsehood, and yet is both, is called the blissful form, the collective Svananda. That which is beyond the three states of consciousness, beyond the three qualities, and devoid of delusion, is called the unmanifest Svananda. The union of these four is called Samadhi. The fifth, Svananda, is called the union of all. That is said by the knowers of the Vedas to be Ganesha, who is full of Maya, and he plays daily within these four. Because he is devoid of Maya, he is called Dhundhi, the one who is not a union; the wise say there is no union for him. The absence of being with Maya is what causes illusion. Devoid of both, this Ganesha is called the form of Yoga. There, O Goddess, there is peace for the yogis, without a doubt. Therefore, that Yoga form is called the supreme peace. The letter 'Ga' represents all that is of mind and speech, and the letter 'Na' represents that which is devoid of mind and speech. He is the Lord of both, the Lord of the Ganas; see this in the Vedas, O fair-smiling one. Therefore, I worship him, the Lord of all, with devotion. Know that the great illusion, which is of the nature of delusion, is Siddhi (success), O giver of honor. For the sake of success, all people and gods wander. Know that the intellect, which holds the delusion, is definitely Buddhi. From Siddhi and Buddhi, delusion arises. He is the Lord of both, called the form of Yoga and Peace. By worshipping him, O Goddess, one becomes Brahman.

Having said this, the Lord Narayana became silent. Hearing this, the daughter of the ocean was filled with wonder in her mind.

Thus ends the forty-second chapter of the third section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of the Dialogue between Lakshmi and Narayana."

Chapter 43 : The Description of the Granting of Boons to Mahalakshmi

Sri Ganesha, salutations to You.

Sri Krishna said: O righteous one, listen to this sin-destroying story. Lakshmi, eager for the knowledge of Ganesha, spoke to Him again.

Sri Mahalakshmi said: O Lord, you have spoken of that which grants supreme peace, yet tell me the means by which I may attain perfect tranquility.

Sri Narayana said: If you practice the six-syllable mantra, the Lord of the Ganas will appear before you. By His mere sight, you will certainly attain peace. Worship Him with effort and yoga-infused penance.

Having said this, Mahavishnu gave the six-syllable mantra to Mahalakshmi according to the prescribed rites. She bowed to Him and went to the forest for penance. Situated on the peak of the Himalayas, she performed great austerity. Meditating on the elephant-faced Lord according to the rules of the six-syllable mantra, the virtuous one chanted the king of mantras with her eyes fixed on the tip of her nose. Fixing her mind and senses on the elephant-faced Lord, the Goddess remained in penance, abstaining from food.

When a hundred years had passed, only her bones and skin remained. O King, bound by her devotion, the Lord of the Ganas came to her. The great Goddess bowed to the four-armed Lord, who had a large belly, three eyes, and held an axe and other weapons. He was the one whose banner bore the mouse, who was complete, seated upon a mouse, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, and adorned with various ornaments. He wore the Chintamani gem on His throat, was the Lord of the luminaries, had the navel of Shesha, an elephant face, and was adorned in yellow garments.

She worshipped the Lord with various offerings. Rising after bowing, the virtuous one praised Him with folded hands.

Sri Mahalakshmi said: Salutations to You, the large-bellied One, the performer of various pastimes, who always dwells in Your own bliss and is attained through devotion. Salutations to the four-armed One, the guide of the four, the one who rides the mouse, and whose banner bears the mouse. Salutations to You, whose body is infinite, whose wealth is infinite, whose hands and feet are infinite, and who are eternal. You are the essence of the moving and the unmoving, yet beyond them; You are the giver of yoga and peace, and the very form of yoga. Salutations again and again to You, O Ganesha, who are beginningless, the form of beginning, middle, and end, and yet devoid of them, the remover of obstacles. Salutations to You, who are worshipped at the beginning of all and are worthy of all worship; the cause of all, the eldest of the great. Salutations to You, O Vinayaka, the leader of all, the Lord of Dhundhi, Heramba, and the Lord of devotees. Salutations to You, the creator, destroyer, and protector of creation; the Lord of the gods, who is beyond the three qualities. Salutations to You, O Lambodara, the giver of the fruits of actions, the guide of actions, and who are beyond action and inaction. Salutations to You, O elephant-faced Lord, the Lord of yoga, the giver of yoga to yogis, the embodiment of peace, and the Brahman itself. How can I praise You, O Lord, who are always the Lord of Brahman? I bow to You; may You be pleased with me. I am blessed, my purpose is fulfilled, and my life has become fruitful. Blessed is my father and my Lord, for I have seen the elephant-faced One.

O Yudhishthira, having praised Him with a heart full of devotion, she danced with tears in her eyes and a choked voice. The Lord of the Ganas said to her: "Choose the boon you desire. Pleased by your devotion, I will grant it to you, O Mahalakshmi. This hymn composed by you will grant worldly enjoyment and liberation to those who read or hear it, and will fulfill all their desires. By reading this hymn, one obtains progeny and all prosperity, and finds happiness in wealth and grain."

Hearing the words of Ganesha, Mahalakshmi, filled with great joy, spoke to Him with folded hands.

Sri Mahalakshmi said: If You are pleased and are a granter of boons, O large-bellied Lord, then grant me intense devotion to You. You are the family deity of our lineage, and I worship You with devotion, yet hear my request. My mind wanders in the world due to its fickle nature, and it always wanders for the sake of worldly attachments and affection for children. Therefore, O remover of obstacles, be my son. I will worship You constantly with maternal affection. I will worship Your elephant-faced form as a deity, and also as my son in this world. If You, who are the very form of yoga, become my son, then I will undoubtedly attain that peace which is the form of yoga. If You, O Lord, are bound by the relationship of mother and son, then all the Vedas and other scriptures would become false. Therefore, become my son, so that I may worship You with devotion as the Brahman. O ocean of compassion, this is the only boon I ask of You; I ask for nothing else.

Hearing the words of Lakshmi, the large-bellied Lord, who knows the hearts of all and is known as the embodiment of emotion, spoke to her.

Ganesha said: I will become your son to destroy the enemies of knowledge and to accept your devotion for the sake of the demon. Whatever you desire, you will surely obtain. By my grace, you will be worshipped by the yogis. O great Goddess, you worshipped me in the beginning, and through that, you became peaceful, established in yoga, and free from delusion. Then, through penance, the sage Bhrigu made you his daughter. You were lost due to the curse of the Brahmin and were filled with delusion. Later, you were worshipped by the ocean and became his daughter, and you have always been the beloved of Vishnu. Because of that, you forgot me, there is no doubt. Instructed by Vishnu, you attained me through the service of yoga. Now, remember all that; I have reminded you, O sinless one. In the beginning, Vishnu also prayed to me for a son. You have also asked me for a son; I will do what is beneficial for you and Vishnu, there is no doubt.

Having said this, Ganesha went to His own abode of bliss. Lakshmi became sad when He disappeared. Remembering the story of her past life through the power of yoga, she was amazed and remembered the elephant-faced Lord. She installed His image there, worshipped it with the help of Vedic Brahmins, and then returned to her place with devotion. She bowed to Mahavishnu and told Him everything. Hearing the account, He was pleased and said: "You are blessed, daughter of the ocean, for you have seen the Lord Himself. The remover of obstacles will come to your house as your son."

Thus ends the forty-third chapter of the third section of the Mudgala Purana, describing the granting of boons to Mahalakshmi.

Chapter 44 : Attainment of Boons by Jnanari

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Yudhisthira said: O Krishna, hearing the nectar-like story flowing from your lotus-like mouth, I am never satisfied. Who was the son of the demon king known as Jnanari? What were his deeds, and what were his actions? Tell me in detail, O Krishna. For what purpose did the Lord of the Ganas take a physical form? The character of that demon king, who is the teacher of the world, is not ordinary. How did Ganesha, the leader of the Vedas and son of Vishnu, come to be? Tell me in detail, O Keshava, what deeds he performed.

Mudgala said: Understanding the intent of Yudhisthira, the descendant of the Vrishni clan, Narayana, told him the story that destroys worldly existence.

Shri Krishna said: O Yudhisthira, listen to this beautiful story that dispels sins and grants the state of Brahman, which is based on the noble character of Dhundhi (Ganesha). Long ago, the demon king known as Durbuddhi was killed by the remover of obstacles, who had taken the form of Shiva's son, and who was the conqueror of the three worlds. His son, who was born and lived in his own home, became known as Jnanari due to his virtuous nature from childhood. Prahlada, filled with joy, gave his own daughter, named Sukala, who was endowed with beauty and grace, to him.

When he reached youth, he always remembered his father, who had been killed by Ganesha, and was filled with sorrow. He approached Shukra (the preceptor of demons) and received the five-syllable mantra. With that, he went to the forest and began to worship Shankara. The great demon Jnanari, practicing great effort and subsisting only on air, performed severe penance for ten thousand years, O Lord. Then, Lord Shambhu himself, accompanied by Parvati, went to him. The fulfiller of the desires of his devotees spoke to that great devotee.

Shri Shiva said: Choose a boon, O fortunate one; I am pleased with your penance. I will grant you your desire, O demon, even if it is very difficult to obtain.

Hearing the words of Shiva, the best of demons was awakened. Seeing Shambhu, he was filled with joy and bowed down with folded hands. After bowing and worshipping him again, the demon king stood firm and praised Shankara, who is fond of his devotees.

Jnanari said: Salutations to you, O Shankara, who are with Parvati. Salutations again and again to you, who have the bull as your banner and the mountain as your vehicle. Salutations to the unmanifest God, to Shiva, the bearer of the trident. Salutations to you, who are beyond qualities and the source of all qualities. Salutations to Sadashiva, the foundation of Maya. Salutations to you, O Shambhu, who are free from the delusion of Maya. Salutations to you, the creator, the vessel, and the destroyer of creation, who possess three forms. Salutations to the Lord of all, who is the protector of the helpless and the Supreme Soul. Salutations to you, who are the form of the moving and the unmoving. Salutations to you, O Mahadeva, the Lord of the gods. Salutations to you, who are beyond the reach of mind and speech, and who are meditated upon by the yogis. O Maheswara, salutations to you, the Lord of yogis. My lineage is blessed today because I have seen Maheswara. My penance, knowledge, father, and mother are all blessed; there is no doubt about this. O Great Lord, if you are pleased, then grant me this: the kingdom of the entire universe, and may I never meet death. May whatever I desire, O Lord of gods, always be fulfilled. Make me endowed with health and all other blessings, O Sadashiva.

Saying "So be it," Shiva disappeared. The demon was extremely happy and returned to his own home. Whoever reads or listens to this hymn composed by Jnanari, Shankara grants them their highest desires.

Thus ends the forty-fourth chapter of the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of the Attainment of Boons by Jnanari."

Chapter 45 : The Incarnation of Purnananda

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Sri Krishna said: Jnanari, endowed with great strength, was worshipped by the demons. Shukracharya arrived and, along with the Brahmins, anointed him to the throne. Namuchi, Shambara, Kalasura, the powerful Jambha, and Balasura were his five chief ministers. Accompanied by various demon hosts, the demon conquered the earth with its seven oceans, its islands, and even the netherworld with his might. Then, surrounded by his demon lords, the demon went to heaven. Indra, filled with fear, fled along with the gods. Jnanari, accompanied by the demons, went to Satyaloka. Brahma, knowing the demon's nature, hid himself. Vishnu left Vaikuntha and fled, O Yudhishthira. The demon arrived there and rejoiced.

Then, the demon lord went to Kailasa with great joy. Bowing to Shankara, he said, "O Lord, give me Kailasa. If not, I shall conquer you in battle; there is no doubt about this." Shankara, becoming agitated and trembling, gave it to him. Terrified, he went to the forest along with the gods. The demon, staying in Kailasa, rejoiced, being greatly infatuated. Then, he fearlessly established demon lords in the heavens and went to his own city, shining, surrounded by his ministers.

O Yudhishthira, a son was born to the great demon from Sukala. He was born at an auspicious moment, possessed of great brilliance, and was incomparable in beauty. Overjoyed at the birth of his son, the king gave gifts and performed the birth rituals according to the scriptures. He always sent his demon ministers to destroy religious rites. They caused destruction of all rituals everywhere on earth. There was no Svaha, no Vashatkara, and no Svadha anywhere, O Lord. The gods became miserable due to the lack of rituals.

Engaged in fasting, they deliberated with respect, but the wise ones could not find a way to destroy Jnanari. As much time passed, they were distressed, wondering what to do. Then Vishnu spoke these beneficial words. Sri Vishnu said: "Everything consists of the moving and the non-moving, therefore there is no fear for him. Hence, let us worship the Lord of Obstacles (Vighneshvara) here, there is no doubt. He is the Lord of Intellect; he will reveal the flaw through his wisdom. By that, we shall destroy him; may he be the giver of success to us."

Hearing the words of Vishnu, the gods said, "Well said, well said." Then they worshipped Dhundhi (Ganesha) with devotion. Meditating on the elephant-faced Lord with the ten-syllable mantra, all the gods were pleased, O Lord, having performed various penances. After a year passed, the Lord of the Ganas came, being pleased. At night, in a dream, he spoke these beneficial words to Lakshmi: "Listen, Mother, you previously prayed for a son. I have come to you in the form of a son; behold, O beautiful one."

Having said this, Ganesha, the beloved of the Ganas, disappeared. Then, the Lord of Obstacles became a child and played on Lakshmi's lap. Mahalakshmi awoke and remembered Ganesha in her mind. She saw a child with a trunk on her bed. Amazed and overjoyed, she told Narayana. The noble lady showed him the Lord of Obstacles. Then, along with his wife, Madhava bowed and praised the Lord of the Ganas with great joy, using the great hymns of Ganesha. "I am blessed in every way by the sight of your lotus feet. You are protecting me in the form of a son, O Lord, there is no doubt."

Then all the gods and sages arrived there. Amazed, they praised him with various hymns, O Yudhishthira. Then, they performed the birth rituals with great devotion. On the eleventh day, the sages named him. "He is devoid of duality and is the direct giver of bliss. He is equal in all dualities and free from their modifications. Therefore, his name shall clearly be Purnananda (Full Bliss). May he always be the giver of bliss to us."

Saying "So be it," they all gave that name to the great soul. In the fifth year, the imperishable Vishnu performed his sacred thread ceremony. In the house of Kashyapa, two daughters were born, Vidya and Avidya. They worshipped the Lord through penance in Aditi. Kashyapa, filled with joy, gave them to him. The lord of Brahmins bowed to Purnananda with devotion. The Lord of the Ganas reached the age of twelve. He deluded the gods with his childish play. Lakshmi herself performed his feeding, clothing, and other needs with a joyful heart. Vishnu, surrounded by the gods, always pampered him, as did Kashyapa and the Brahmins, O Yudhishthira.

Thus ends the forty-fifth chapter entitled "The Incarnation of Purnananda" in the third section of the Mahodara-charita in the Srimad Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 46 : The Description of Brahmayajna

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Yudhisthira asked: O Krishna, O glorious one, please tell me the deeds of the demon. How did the Lord of Supreme Bliss kill the demon, and what was the great wonder behind it? Since the Lord Narayana is the master of all moving and non-moving beings, this story seems very astonishing to me.

Mudgala replied: Hearing the words of Yudhisthira, Krishna replied to him. Listen, O wise one, to the deeds of Ganapa.

Shri Krishna said: A son was born to the demon Jnanari, who was accepted by all. On the eleventh day, the Brahmins arrived at his house. The best among the demons named him Subodha and, being pleased, gave gifts to the Brahmins as they had requested. O King, that son of the demon, though born in a demonic family, was full of wisdom and, even in his childhood, always meditated upon Vighnesha (Ganesha). He never remembered things like breastfeeding; when his mother remembered and offered him milk, he drank it. Thus, while staying with his mother, he drank milk while always remaining absorbed in meditation, remembering Ganapa in his heart.

O Lord, five and then seven months passed. Listen to how he played as he grew. In the dust, he made an image of Ganapa with his own hands and threw dust upon it as an offering for worship. He performed worship three times a day with discipline and, absorbed in meditation, served Vighnesha thereafter. Once, the demon leader Jnanari, while sitting for a meal, saw his son playing in his childish way. He saw that his son had made an image of Ganesha on the ground and was worshipping it with offerings of dust.

Filled with astonishment and extreme anger, he said to his wife, "This son of yours is worshipping the one who destroyed my father. Because I am not certain yet, I will not kill him now, my dear. Be careful and make your son stop these activities. Since this son is honoring my father's killer, if I see him doing this again, I will kill him." She said, "So be it," and remained humble. Being a devoted wife, she followed her husband's words. When her husband was away, she took her son and caressed him in such a way that the King would not see.

Later, when Subodha had passed the age of one year, he began to speak in an unclear voice: "Salutations to Heramba, Vighnesha, Ganesha, Mahodara, Lambodara, Vikata, and Dhundhi." With such sentiments, he always remembered Ganapa with a focused mind, devoted to righteousness and worship. O Yudhisthira, out of affection, his mother would take the boy far away with her companions when her husband was away. She tried her best to protect her son daily. Once, her husband ordered her, "Take the boy with you." The great chaste lady, filled with sorrow, delayed by saying, "My lord, the child is asleep."

When he reached the age of five, Jnanari happily performed his initiation ceremony (Vratabandha). Even then, the son remembered Ganesha in his mind. Fearing the interruption of his rituals, he performed his worship mentally. After the ceremony was over, he bowed to everyone.

Then, the King placed Subodha in a Gurukula (school). There, all the sons of the demons of various forms were present. The glorious disciples of Shukra always tried to teach them the demonic knowledge that bewitches the gods. This prince was highly respected by the teachers, yet he remained there, longing for devotion and remembering the King of Obstacles. When the teachers honored the prince to teach him demonic knowledge, he bowed to them and stood still. Hearing what the teachers said, the highly intelligent Subodha, who was focused on the truth, spoke to them.

Subodha said: "Brahmins are devoted to Brahman and always focused on Yoga. Those who are wise in the path of Ganapatya worship the Lord of the Ganas. Being wise in the essence of Ganesha, which is beyond duality and non-duality, one should abandon this bewitching demonic knowledge and become worthy of reverence. O learned ones, why do you tell me, who is devoted to Ganesha, the one worthy of worship by all, to follow this knowledge that only brings delusion? If Brahmins are established in their own Dharma, their words should be accepted without doubt; otherwise, they are merely deluding. If Brahmins are devoid of virtues, they are still worthy of respect and worship by all humans. Therefore, I bow to all Brahmins, and if their words are beneficial, I will accept them; otherwise, I will not accept them as my teachers."

Shri Krishna said: Hearing the words of Subodha, the great sages were astonished. They said to the demon's son, "Listen to the Dharma that brings happiness."

The Demon Teachers said: "Subodha, you are but a child, yet you speak of the highest Yoga, as if you are a great Yogi yourself. You have not been taught the Vedas, O wise one; how did you gain this knowledge? Tell us."

Subodha said: "When my mother was pregnant, she once went to my grandfather's house. There, the glorious Shuka, who is well-versed in all meanings, arrived. Prahlad, filled with joy, bowed to that Lord of Yogis, worshipped him, and asked with humility."

Prahlad asked: "O son of Vyasa, O great Yogi Shuka, you are Shiva himself. You are undoubtedly the teacher of the teachers of Yogis. It is only by the strength of past merits that I have attained your great vision. Therefore, I ask you, O ocean of mercy, please tell me what is universally accepted, what brings peace in Yoga, and what destroys the delusion of various opinions."

Subodha said: "Hearing the beautiful words of Prahlad, the best of Yogis spoke to him."

Shri Shuka said: "Prahlad, you are glorious and wise. I will tell you what is universally accepted and brings peace to all. Once, in the Naimisha Forest, the great sages of the universe gathered for the sake of Brahmayajna. The sages who speak of the Vedas call this the Brahmayajna. Marichi, Kashyapa, Kanva, Atri, Pulaha, Kratu, Vishwamitra, Yajnavalkya, Aruni, Ashtavakra, Vashistha, Bharadwaja, Parashara, Vyasa, Parashurama, Shwetaketu, Mudgala, Agastya, Hanuman, Vibhishana, Ashwatthama, Gritsamada, Kripa, Markandeya, Mrikanda, Bakadalbhya, Narada, Kapila, Raibhya, Kardama, Eka, Dwita, Trita, Devala, Asita, Pulastya, Bhrigu, Chyavana, Gautama, Angira, Dirghatama, Utathya, Galava, Dadhichi, Ushana, Jiva, Jamadagni, Vaishampayana, Shaunaka, Shunaka, Vamadeva, Sumantu, Paila, Shakalaka, Jaimini, Dhaumya, and many other great sages arrived with their disciples. Dattatreya, Durvasa, Nidagha, Bharata, Sanatana, Sananda, Sanatkumara, Ribhu, Sanaka, and many other Avadhutas and Yogis went to witness the sacrifice. Indra, Brahma, Vishnu, Shankara, the Sun, and other kings of the gods, along with their consorts, arrived. Shesha, Vasuki, Kambala, Ashwatara, Takshaka, and other great Nagas arrived. Gandharvas, Charanas, Siddhas, Sadhyas, Adityas, Vasus, Yakshas, and Apsaras also came. O Prahlad, those devoted to rituals, penance, and knowledge, and all the Yogis devoted to various gods, gathered there to witness the Brahmayajna, and everyone became focused on that which is universally accepted."

Chapter 47 : The Determination of Brahman in the Brahma-Yajna

Shri Shuka said: O Prahlada, there, the prominent ones, out of respect and according to their own intellects, engaged in various discussions that fostered diverse opinions. Some, devoted to various doctrines, called the Creator, Vishnu, Shakti, Shiva, the Sun, or Ganesha the form of Brahman. Others identified Brahman as food, life-breath, the science of the mind, bliss, the singular "I am," consciousness, or the subtle point (bindu). Some considered the self as knowledge, others as Sankhya or action, while others called the unmanifest form Brahman. Some, devoted to the Vedas, described it as self-evident, the essence of cessation and peace, or Yoga in its spiritual form. Others, O Asura, debated whether it was false or true, or the supreme Brahman of complete bliss and natural state.

O Lord of the Daityas, no consensus was reached among those seeking the truth, as the ignorant debated based on their own limited knowledge. Then, to pacify the debate, the eternal Vishnu, the all-knowing one, prompted by Shankara, spoke to all those gathered there.

Shri Vishnu said: There is no doubt that the Vedas are the very embodiment of the Guru for everyone. O gods and sages who have gathered here, whatever has been accomplished by them should be utilized to determine the nature of Brahman. That which is accepted by all will be the source of happiness. O great sages, the Vedas have sung of self-realization as it truly is; therefore, through the Vedas, supreme knowledge is attained by all. There is no doubt that some people, sages, gods, and demons attain the direct form of Brahman through Yoga and other means. Yet, their words do not possess the authority of the Vedas. O learned ones, where can one find a knower of Yoga equal to the Vedas? The Vedas are complete yogis; therefore, they are considered the Gurus. O great souls, meditate upon them, and then all doubts will be resolved.

Then, all the groups of sages, yogis, and leaders of the gods said "Well done, well done" to Vishnu, the best among the wise. Thereafter, remembered by those led by Shankara for the success of their task, the Vedas appeared in their entirety, accompanied by the Omkara. Seeing them, the gods and the assembly of sages rose suddenly, bowed down, and worshipped them with folded hands. Seated on their thrones, the Vedas, led by the Omkara, spoke to those led by Shiva with joyful hearts.

The Vedas said: O gods, sages, and people, why have you remembered us? Tell us the task, and we shall surely accomplish it. Hearing the words of the Vedas, Shiva, the expert in the truth, spoke with humility for the sake of those seeking the final determination.

Shiva said: We have all initiated a Brahma-Yajna for the welfare of all. However, we are engaged in debates and cannot reach a conclusion. Therefore, O Vedas, we have specifically remembered you. Being our Gurus, please tell us the truth that destroys all differences. There are no yogis here who can ever be equal to you; by the authority of your words, we too shall become knowers of Yoga.

Hearing the words of Shiva, the Vedas spoke to the gods, sages, and all the people, O Lord of the Asuras.

The Vedas said: How can we reach the end of the Brahma-Yajna? Being composed of letters, we shall still speak. We are not the form of Brahman, nor are we knowers of Brahman in any way; yet, by the power of the path of Yoga, we shall speak the truth. O gods, listen to the determination of Brahman, by which you will be freed from doubt and attain the supreme goal. Brahman has been described in many ways by me through the power of Yoga, but among the great statements (Mahavakyas), Yoga is the most excellent. The place of the union of all Brahman is the nature of Yoga; it is described that all things in Brahman are of that form. Brahman is not attained through anything higher than Yoga, and it is attained through the peace of Yoga. I have declared this Lord of Brahman to be Ganesha. Because he is the protector and sustainer of all Brahmans, O best of gods and sages, worship him with devotion. By this, all people will certainly become Brahman-realized. Having said this, the Rig Veda became silent.

Then, the Yajur Veda itself spoke in the assembly, offering words that bestow Brahman, based on its own experience.

Yajur Veda said: I have described Brahman in many ways through the service of Yoga and through the great statements for the sake of order; there is no doubt about this. Among them, the Brahman that is the form of Yoga is the most excellent; because of the union of all Brahman, it is called Yoga. It was previously prayed to by devotees for the attainment of Yoga, and thus the Lord of the Ganas became manifest. "Gana" means a group, and the groups are of the form of Brahman; he is their Lord, so worship him according to the scriptures without doubt. In the group of all foods, which are always one and many, Brahman should be known as the essence of food; this is what the Shruti says. O divine sages, in this way, many forms of Brahman should be known through contemplation as the forms of the Ganas. Therefore, through devotion to Ganesha, everything becomes easily attainable. I have declared him the Lord of Yoga through Yoga, the one who grants the state of Brahman.

Having said this, the Yajur Veda became silent. Then, the Sama Veda, the cause of the determination of Brahman, spoke.

Sama Veda said: By the power of serving Yoga, I am capable of description. O Lords of gods and sages, listen to a little about the word of Brahman. I have declared many Brahmans through the great statements, which should be understood through the service of Yoga for the sake of different levels of seekers. Among them, the Brahman that grants peace is the one called Yoga; where there is the union of Brahmans, it is called Yoga. There is no Brahman higher than Yoga, which is attained through the path of peace. O sages and gods, that is undoubtedly Ganesha. O gods, whatever I have proclaimed that is composed of mind and speech should always be known by the wise as originating from the letter 'Ga'. Whatever is described as being beyond mind and speech is all known to be in the letter 'Na' through the service of Yoga. Their Lord is declared to be Ganesha, the form of Yoga. Worship him specifically, as he grants the state of Brahman. He gives to those who worship him with various dispositions. Having said this, the great yogi, the chanter of Sama, became silent.

Then, the Atharva Veda, the great yogi, spoke to all. O Prahlada, listen to his words, for he is the knower of the Brahma-Yajna.

Atharva Veda said: Through statements like "I am the Self," I have established Brahman in many ways through Yoga, and it is associated with limiting adjuncts. The supreme Brahman called Yoga is attained through that Yoga. This Yoga, which is devoid of union and separation, stands as Brahman. Peace is declared to be in that Yoga; he is the best of those who know Yoga, and that Lord of the Ganas is the grantor of all peace. Ganesha alone is the Lord of the worlds and the Brahmans himself. He is the Lord of the groups, and thus he is remembered as the form of Yoga. He is the primordial form of all, the form of Yoga by his power. In the middle, he is the Lord who is the good Brahman of the world through his various parts. In the end, he remains as he is; therefore, he was worshipped in the beginning. O Lords of gods and sages seeking determination, he is the one to be worshipped by all. He who is the beginning is also the inner form, accepted by all. In the middle, through the play of Maya, he became associated with difference and non-difference. Therefore, worship him, the one called the form of Yoga, through the service of Yoga. You will become Brahman-realized and cross the Brahma-Yajna.

Having said this, the great yogi, the knower of all Yoga, became silent. Then the Omkara spoke.

Omkara said: I am abundant in the four Vedas. Through the service of Yoga, I have realized Brahman directly, and thus I have become a follower of the yogis. The Vedas taught by me have enabled the practice of Yoga, which grants Brahman; therefore, they are accepted by all as knowers of Brahman. There is no Brahman higher than Yoga, which is accepted everywhere. The world is the good Brahman, and through Yoga, Brahman is eternal. That is Ganesha, always determined by the yogis. He plays in the state of union and non-union, wearing the form of Yoga. Siddhi is the illusion of delusion, and Buddhi is considered the holder of delusion. Ganesha is the Lord of both, playing with these two Mayas. The attainment of enjoyment and the attainment of liberation are both granted by Brahman. The mind is fivefold, and Buddhi is attained through the Yoga of these two. The Brahman that is self-evident is declared to be supreme in the Vedas. It is called the form of the union of all, and he is seen there himself. Therefore, he is declared to be the dweller of his own bliss and associated with Maya. Without Yoga, his vision is never attained. Therefore, he is called the one without Maya, attained through the union of both. 'Ga' is said to be union, and 'Na' is said to be non-union. By that name, he is called Ganesha by the Vedic scholars. Worship him specifically for the sake of becoming Brahman. O gods and twice-born, you will undoubtedly become Brahman-realized. Having said this, the Omkara became silent.

Chapter 48 : the Dialogue Between Shukra's Disciples and Subodha

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shri Shuka said: Upon hearing the words of the Omkara and the Vedas, which are beneficial, the Nagas, the sages, and the gods were filled with joy. Abandoning their own individual opinions, they all bowed down to them, worshipped them, and with folded hands, the noble ones spoke these words.

The gods and others said: The words spoken by you, along with the Omkara and the Vedas, are worthy of being accepted by all and lead to the attainment of Brahman. The Vedas are declared to be the gurus of all, and what they have said is what should be done, as it is the path that is universally accepted and grants salvation. Whatever is devoid of the foundation of the Vedas should undoubtedly be abandoned by all; otherwise, a man becomes a dweller of hell and suffers torment. You, along with the leaders of the gods, sages, and assemblies of people, have certainly reached the culmination of the Brahma-yajna.

Having spoken thus, the noble gods and others, filled with joy, praised the Vedas and the Omkara, O leader of the Daityas. Having been worshipped by them, the Vedas and the Omkara disappeared, and all, being happy, returned to their own abodes. Thus, O virtuous one, the Brahma-yajna was performed by all in ancient times. Because of that, being free from doubt, they worship the Lord of the Ganas. I have told you this, which grants complete yoga and peace and is worthy of being accepted by all; especially, worship that Lord of the Ganas.

Subodha said: Having said this, the great yogi Shuka disappeared. Prahlada, filled with devotion, worships the Lord of the Ganas. Everything that was heard while I was in my mother's womb has been retained by me, and it is that which grants yoga. It is only by the strength of past merits that my memory has remained intact here. Therefore, O Brahmins, I am born and I worship the Lord of the Ganas. I have told you all that you asked, O chief among gurus; therefore, do not speak to me of knowledge that causes delusion, out of your compassion.

Shri Krishna said: Hearing the words of Subodha, the Brahmins were filled with joy. They spoke to him, who was skilled in yoga, to understand his disposition for the sake of spiritual attainment.

The Brahmins said: O Subodha, what you have said is true and not otherwise. Still, listen, O child, to what is beneficial and excellent. Your grandfather, the great Asura, was of evil intellect and was killed in anger by Ganesha, who took the side of the gods. Your father, O lover of your father, hates that Lord of the Ganas. Therefore, out of respect, abandon this Vighnesha, who is an enemy of your father. Otherwise, O son, your father will undoubtedly kill you. Therefore, O wise one, do not be so obstinate. See that your birth in the lineage of the Asuras is beneficial; always follow the path of your own lineage.

Hearing their words, Subodha, with reddened eyes, spoke to those Brahmins words filled with righteousness.

Subodha said: O Brahmins, Ganesha exists everywhere in the form of yoga. He is the regulator of everyone's dharma and is fond of various plays. The gods in heaven, humans on earth, and the leaders of the Daityas in the netherworld have been established by Him in their respective paths; those who desire their own welfare must remain in them. My grandfather, O Brahmins, ruled the three worlds, but he was devoid of dharma, and therefore he was killed by Him by force. My father, too, O Brahmins, is situated in a state of unrighteousness; Ganesha will surely kill him quickly, there is no doubt about this. Therefore, O Brahmins, what wrong has the Lord of the Ganas done? Speak to me especially of what is dear to my own dharma. He is undoubtedly the benefactor of all, the Lord of Obstacles. I will worship Him with effort, abandoning my wicked father. When the wicked one kills me for worshipping Ganesha, that death itself will be the best, as it will grant the attainment of Brahman. If a mosquito is angry, do wise men abandon their homes? O Brahmins, tell me now. The power of Ganesha, the Lord of Intellect, exists everywhere. There is no doubt about the Lord of Success; what will my father do to me? The dharma of all beings, for the sake of liberation, wealth, and desire, and especially for the attainment of Brahman, consists of the worship of Ganesha. Shall I abandon such a dharma and become a slave to the Asuras, being addicted to sense objects, like the dregs of the lineage, O Brahmins? You are not Brahmins, as you are addicted to sense objects; having abandoned dharma, you are here, undoubtedly dependent on the Daityas. Those who are engaged in the worship of Ganesha and are devoted to the attainment of Brahman are considered Brahmins in the scriptures; why do you speak to me like fools?

Shri Krishna said: Having spoken thus to the disciples of Shukra, the wise Subodha became intent on the worship of the Lord of the Ganas. Salutations to Ganesha, salutations to the Great Soul, salutations to the Supreme Lord, the Supreme beyond the supreme. Salutations to the beginningless, middle-less, and endless Dhundhi; salutations again and again to the Lord of Obstacles, the giver of great peace. Knowing that he was arguing in this way, the disciples of Kavya (Shukra), being full of fear, went to their teacher, who was an enemy of knowledge.

Thus ends the forty-eighth chapter named the dialogue between Shukra's disciples and Subodha, in the third section of the Mahodara-charita in the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 49 : Description of the Devotion of Subodha

Sri Krishna said: The disciples of Shukra went to the place where the king of the demons resided. Seeing him seated in the assembly, they spoke to him, filled with fear.

The disciples of Shukra said: O Jnanari, listen to our harsh and distressing words. Your son has become arrogant and always ignores us. He disregards everything we say, calls it a corruption of caste, and acts according to his own mind while instructing others to do the same. He constantly remembers with devotion the one who killed his father, and he insults you, O great king, and us as well; there is no doubt about this. Therefore, we have come to tell you this; please instruct your son. We are unable to teach such an undisciplined person, O wise one.

Sri Krishna said: Hearing their words, the demon king was agitated and sent a messenger to bring his son to him. Seeing his son, who had arrived with the messenger, the great demon, filled with anger, spoke to him as he offered his salutations.

Jnanari said: Why do you insult your teachers and remember that enemy, tell me? What is in your heart, you disgrace to the lineage?

Hearing his father's words, Subodha, deluded by the Maya of Ganapa, spoke to his bewildered father.

Subodha said: O father, I worship the Lord of the Ganas, who is the family deity of all, the benefactor of all, and the bestower of worldly enjoyment and liberation. He is worshipped with devotion by gods like Brahma and Vishnu, and by great sages like Kashyapa, for the prosperity of the lineage and for the peace of yoga. We, the gods, the demons, the humans, and various creatures were all born from Kashyapa, and He is the master of them all. Therefore, I worship the Lord of the Ganas with all my heart. Without respecting Him, O lord of demons, no man attains success.

Hearing these words, the great demon, spewing fire from his mouth like the God of Death, said to him:

Jnanari said: The gods are undoubtedly our enemies, and the demons are the enemies of the gods; see, you foolish one, this is stated in the Vedas. Even then, you uselessly worship him who is the killer of your father. In your childhood, I understood your actions were inclined towards the enemy. Considering you a child, I did not kill you out of mercy, you rogue. Now, knowing you to be like this, I will never let you go. Abandon this enemy, you great fool, or I will kill you right now. Having said this, the king, sword in hand, rushed towards his son.

Then the son said to the demon king: Why do you threaten me, father, deluded by a state of confusion? The Lord of the Ganas is declared in the Vedas to be the possessor of all power. Abandoning His power, why do you strike me? There is no doubt that Ganesha is the embodiment of both gods and demons. The Lord, assuming various forms, swiftly destroys the arrogant.

Seeing his son speaking without fear, O Yudhishthira, the demon king himself stood there filled with anger. He ordered his messengers to kill his son with weapons. Those demon troops became filled with rage. Making various sounds, they all went forth with naked weapons. Seeing them coming to kill him, Subodha remained fearless. Devoted to the worship of Ganesha, he did not move. He said: O Gajanana, may this happen by the will of my past deeds. O Vighnesha, Heramba, Jaya, Lord of the Akhilas, dweller of Svananda, victory to you, O Vakratunda. You are the Lord everywhere, O Jagat-Sudhundhi, Lambodara, son of Brahma, salutations to you.

With such various prayers, the son worshipped Ganesha. The powerful demons, filled with anger, struck him. O Yudhishthira, with the fall of various weapons and missiles, Ganesha, residing in his body, rendered them all fruitless. The Lord of Obstacles burned them all with the fire of his anger. Then the demons fled, wishing to save their lives.

Seeing this great wonder, Jnanari, fainting with rage, ordered the leaders to kill his son. The demon lords, equipped with four-fold armies, came to him. They struck Subodha with extremely terrible and infallible weapons. He, too, remained firm and fearless in his devotion to Ganesha: Victory to the Lord of the gods, Lord of obstacles, Mahodara, salutations to you.

Through the pretext of the weapon strikes, a great fire emerged from his body, which blazed and consumed the demon lords in a single moment. They created clouds of weapons, but by them, he was not pacified; rather, he burned them all like the fire of universal destruction. All the leaders of the demons died there, O king. Jnanari was filled with great wonder and sorrow. Having consulted with his ministers, he abandoned his son at that moment. Knowing that Shukra was engaged in penance, he restrained his anger. When that wise Shukra returns home, then, together with him, we will kill Subodha.

With this firm resolve, the demon lords remained. Jnanari acted accordingly with a pained heart. Subodha, with a single-minded heart, worshipped Ganapa there. Fearless, he treated all the demons as mere straw. Then, O Yudhishthira, Subodha instructed the demon children; seeing the evidence, they also worshipped Ganapa. The greatness of Ganesha is truly great; there is no doubt. Seeing the demon kings die like dry grass, the children, having thought this over, worshipped the Lord of Obstacles with respect, disregarding the sons of their teachers. Wherever they were, the demon children, accompanied by Subodha, performed the chanting and worship of Ganesha. Salutations, salutations to the Lord of obstacles, Ganesha, who always dwells in the city of self-realization, whose form is without beginning, middle, or end, the giver of great peace, victory to the one worshipped by all.

Seeing this everywhere, Jnanari became angry. Unable to bear it, he made up his mind to kill him. I will kill this wicked son; due to the power of my boons, my death will certainly not occur, he decided.

Thus ends the forty-ninth chapter entitled "Description of the Devotion of Subodha" in the third section of the Mahodara-charita of the Srimad Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 50 : The Story of Yudhishthira

Lord Krishna said: O knower of Dharma, listen now to what I shall narrate regarding the divine play of Purnananda (Ganesha) in the forests. The glorious Purnananda addressed the gods, led by Vishnu, and asked why they were suffering along with all other living beings. He declared that the entire world, both animate and inanimate, was suffering due to the demon Jnanari, and that he would certainly destroy him. He assured the gods that since he had taken the form of their son, nothing was impossible, and urged them to return to Vaikuntha.

He explained that Jnanari was striving to kill his son, who was a great devotee of his, and that he himself would go to protect his devotee and destroy the wicked one who hated him. He stated that the demon, being bound by the three gunas (modes of nature), was not beyond the reach of his power, and that he would destroy the great demon with a form beyond duality for the sake of his devotee.

Meanwhile, Jnanari, filled with rage, raised his sword to kill Subodha. Seeing this, Subodha, filled with devotion, spoke to Ganesha. He told his father that he was blinded by ego and Maya and did not realize that Ganesha resided within his own heart. He urged his father to seek refuge in Chintamani (Ganesha), the one praised by the Vedas, warning him that otherwise, he would perish. Despite his son's words, the demon struck him with his sharp sword, but the weapon proved useless. He then attacked with various divine weapons, but they too were ineffective. Seeing this, the demon tried to kill his son by slamming him against a stone, but he himself fell down unconscious, struck by the invisible power of Ganesha.

Upon regaining consciousness, the demon was bewildered, wondering who had struck him and how he could kill his son. He decided that he must first kill the one who protected his son. With this resolve, he asked Subodha where his protector was. Subodha, full of compassion, told him to look within his own heart and abandon his ego and enmity to seek refuge in Ganesha. The demon, feigning interest, asked to be shown Ganesha. Subodha then invoked the Lord.

Ganesha appeared, riding his mouse, accompanied by gods and sages who watched from the sky. Seeing Ganesha near Subodha, the demon was delighted and prepared to use his most powerful weapon. At that moment, Purnananda, residing in the demon's heart, withdrew his life force. The demon fell to the ground, lifeless, causing his followers to flee to the netherworld. The gods and sages descended from the sky and praised Ganesha. Ganesha then took Subodha and disappeared, leaving everyone saddened. Lakshmi and Narayana, feeling the separation from Purnananda, fell unconscious. After two hours, they awoke, and Ganesha appeared in their hearts, assuring them that he would never be separated from them and instructing them to worship him.

Thus, Mahodara (Ganesha) is the provider of all, the destroyer of obstacles, and the one who grants liberation. He is worshipped by Shiva, Vishnu, and all sages. O King, worship him, and you shall be free from obstacles and attain peace.

Mudgala said: Hearing these words from Narayana, Yudhishthira, with great love and wisdom, asked Krishna to teach him the mantra of Ganesha. Krishna then gave him the six-syllable mantra. The Pandavas, along with Draupadi, worshipped Ganesha with this mantra and observed the Chaturthi fast. Freed from their sorrows, they received their desires and were sustained by the inexhaustible food provided by the Lord. They wandered through the forests until they reached Svananda-pura, accompanied by Vyasa and Vasudeva. There, they worshipped Mayuresha and performed the funeral rites for their father, Pandu, enabling him to attain the heavenly realm through the grace of Ganesha.

The Pandavas lived there in secret for a year, free from obstacles. Later, they returned, defeated Duryodhana, Karna, and their army, and after gaining the kingdom, they continued to worship Ganesha. Thus, Yudhishthira worshipped Mahodara. This story of Yudhishthira, which destroys all sins, grants all desires to those who hear or read it.

Chapter 51 : Describing the Fruits of Listening

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, in the southern direction lies the region of Mahodara, where the Lord of the Ganas was established by the gods and sages. This region extends for ten yojanas, and in its center, the four-sided Mahodara resides. In ancient times, the sages established this Brahman-form Lord in the southern direction across all the various islands and their respective divisions. All the gods, sages, and yogis, along with the foremost holy places and sacred regions, reside there. Those who bathe in the ocean and in their own respective holy places always worship Ganesha, remaining devoted to the welfare of one another. Remembering the various forms and divine sports manifested by Mahodara, they become filled with ecstatic devotion. O Prajapati, they worship Him—who is accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi and sits with His mouse before Him—especially with durva grass and other offerings. The main festival takes place on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, and devotees perform a pilgrimage during that year.

By the mere sight of Mahodara, a human attains all that is desired in their heart, and the same is achieved by remembering the Lord. O Daksha, by dying in this region, a man becomes one with Brahman. It is said that there are eight Vinayakas, and their regions are eternal. One should know that all these regions are manifestations of Brahman, and for the sake of the devotees' welfare, Shiva and all other deities reside there. Those who are considered the best in the three worlds, such as Shesha and others, certainly reside in these eight regions. The Ganesha-tirtha located there brings joy to Mahodara; by merely bathing there, a person attains the fulfillment of all desires.

Especially in the holy places of Vishnu and others, and particularly in the regions of Ganesha, a mere bath grants the embodied soul the attainment of those respective divine abodes at the end of life, and they become one with Brahman, dwelling in eternal bliss. Those who are devotees of other gods but die in the region of Ganesha will undoubtedly go to the realm of their own chosen deity. If three gods merge into one at the great dissolution, they will go to their own blissful abodes. Because one becomes one with Brahman by dying in this region, a man should serve Ganesha with devotion and respect. Wherever the devotees may be, those who worship Mahodara should be known as purified and one with Brahman. Those who worship Shiva and others through many births, by the power of that spiritual refinement, eventually become devotees of Ganesha.

There is no Brahman higher than Ganesha, as is universally accepted; therefore, those who are devotees of Ganesha are considered to have attained the state of Brahman. O Prajapati, I have told you this story of Mahodara, which grants all accomplishments, in a concise manner according to my understanding. Any person who listens to this with devotion, or causes others to hear it, or reads it, will undoubtedly become one with Brahman at the end of life. There is nothing equal to this in the three worlds; it grants everything to humans, gods, and yogis alike. A noble person who performs various vows according to the scriptures attains the same fruit merely by listening to this. The fruit that one obtains by bathing in various holy places and offering libations to the gods according to the scriptures, one obtains by listening to this. The fruit that a mortal gains by living in holy places with devotion and knowledge of righteousness is obtained by listening to this. The fruit that a mortal gains by performing various types of sacrifices and seeing the gods in their true form is obtained by listening to this. A noble person who performs all acts of charity and public welfare immediately attains that same fruit by listening to this.

What more need be said? There is no doubt that listening to this grants the state of Brahman and bestows dharma, wealth, desire, and liberation. One attains sons, grandsons, wealth, grain, and especially kingdoms and horses by listening to this. If the greatness of Mahodara is written down in one's home, there will be no fear from demons, thieves, fire, or other dangers. By worshipping this book, O Daksha, all afflictions such as planetary influences and diseases are destroyed; there is no need for doubt in this. Whatever the listeners desire, the powerful and pleased Mahodara grants it to them. By satisfying the narrator, one attains the fruit instantly, and there is no doubt that listening to it in the presence of Ganesha brings results. The fallen are certainly uplifted by listening to this; what more can be said of those who are already devoted and have become one with Brahman? There is nothing like this in the entire universe of Brahman, where the Lord of Yoga, Mahodara, is described. I have told you everything in brief, O Lord; what more do you wish to hear about the greatness of Mahodara?

Suta said: Having said this, the great yogi Mudgala became silent. I have told you everything, and there is no doubt about it. O wise one, this is universally accepted, especially by the learned, and should be known as the essence of the Vedas and other scriptures. Having churned the various scriptures and Puranas, the great sage Vyasa, who is the Lord of Brahman-knowledge, narrated this to me. There is no Brahman higher than Ganesha, no penance higher than Ganesha, no action higher than Ganesha, and no knowledge higher than the Lord of the Ganas. A person who is devoted to action but abandons Ganesha, even if they seek Brahman-knowledge, will find it fruitless. A person who abandons the Lord of Success and desires eternal success is naturally devoid of fruit and becomes a dweller of hell. One who abandons the Lord of Intellect and desires steady wisdom will not attain yoga, just as an offering made into ashes is wasted. He alone is worthy of worship by all, the beginning of all, and the creator of all. O twice-born, worship Him especially, as He is accepted by the Vedas and other scriptures. I have told you the essence of the story of Mahodara. O wise Shaunaka, what else do you wish to hear? I bow to you.

Thus ends the fifty-first chapter, titled "The Description of the Fruits of Listening," in the Mahodara-charita of the third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

The third section is concluded.
The third section of the glorious Mudgala Purana is concluded.

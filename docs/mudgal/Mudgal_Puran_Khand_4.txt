Chapter 1 : Description of the Chaturthi Vrata

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O Suta, you are profoundly wise. You have narrated the character of Mahodara (Ganesha), which bestows peace and destroys delusion; I have heard it with great attention. I am not satisfied even after drinking it repeatedly, as if it were nectar. O Brahmana, there is nothing else that provides such peace. Therefore, please narrate the character of Gajanana in detail. You are all-knowing and noble; we have no doubt about this. O bestower of honor, please tell us the dialogue between Mudgala and Daksha. What did the wise Daksha, the son of Brahma, ask him in the beginning?

Suta said: Listen, O descendant of Bhrigu, O best among Brahmanas, O Lord, I shall narrate the greatness of Gajanana as I have heard it, which brings great knowledge. Having heard the story of Mahodara, which is filled with various narratives and destroys delusion, Daksha was filled with joy. He spoke again to Mudgala, the king of yogis and master of the Vedas, with humility, desiring to know about Ganesha.

Daksha said: O Lord, I have heard the greatness of Mahodara with devotion. By your grace, O Yogi, I am filled with joy. Now, O lord of Brahmanas, tell me about the character of Gajanana. How did the Lord manifest, and what is the nature of the Brahman there? O best of sages, for what reason did He take a physical form? What are His actions, and what are His qualities? Tell me everything, O wise one. By the merit of my past deeds, I have attained your company. I am blessed in every way to hear this auspicious story.

Suta said: Being thus asked by the wise Daksha, the great yogi, who is a devotee of Ganesha and knows the essence of things, replied to him.

Mudgala said: Listen, O noble Daksha, you are truly blessed; there is no doubt. You are increasing the wisdom of this supreme Ganesha-related story, which grants yoga. Pleased by your devotion, O Lord, I will tell you everything—the greatness of Gajanana, which bestows yoga and peace. Know, O Prajapati, that the Sankhya Brahman, which is called Videha and described by yogis as supreme, is the same as the Elephant-faced Lord. His body is the embodiment of Bindu-Brahman, and His face is the manifestation of that. He is the one who possesses a body while remaining non-dual in essence. Though the Lord resides in the three states, He is devoid of them; the Elephant-faced Lord, who holds the Sankhya, shines as the formless. O noble one, who can count the greatness of the Brahman who has renounced the ego? That is why the Sankhya of that Brahman is revealed in the Vedas.

Gajanana manifested to destroy the demon Lobhasura, as requested by the gods and sages who were longing for His devotion. He appeared in a physical form on the fourth day (Chaturthi) when the sun was in the middle of the sky. That day became very dear to Him.

Suta said: Hearing these beautiful words, Daksha, with a joyful heart, spoke to the Brahmana Mudgala to attain the knowledge of Chaturthi.

Daksha said: O Lord, tell me, which Chaturthi is called the favorite of the Lord of Ganas—the Shukla (bright) or the Krishna (dark)? How did it become so dear to Him? O Brahmana, you have spoken of this auspicious vow again and again. Therefore, tell me the entire greatness of Chaturthi.

Hearing the words of Daksha, the great sage Mudgala, filled with joy, told him the story of the origin of Chaturthi.

Mudgala said: I will tell you briefly the character of Chaturthi, which is complete and dear to the Lord of Ganas, O Prajapati. Long ago, after creating the universe, Brahma, the grandfather of the worlds, pondered over the time for the beings residing there. Thinking of Ganesha in his heart to ensure the success of various tasks, he sat in meditation, filled with contemplation. Then, from his body emerged the supreme Prakriti, the Great Maya, the mother of all lunar days, who can take any form. The Prajapati was delighted to see her, as she had four feet, four hands, and four faces. Then, she bowed to him and praised the Lord of the universe with various hymns. Having pleased him, she spoke with a deep voice.

Prakriti said: O Lord of the universe, know me as the one who has emerged from your body. Give me your command, O Father, and I shall act according to your will. O Lord, O ocean of mercy, give me a place, food, and various enjoyments. Salutations to you, O Supreme Lord.

Hearing her words, the Prajapati said: O honored one, contemplate Ganesha and create a wonderful universe. He gave her the six-syllable mantra of the Lord of Ganas. Having bowed to him, she went to the forest with devotion. She performed severe penance, keeping her eyes fixed on the tip of her nose. Meditating on Ganesha in her heart, she chanted the supreme mantra. After a thousand years had passed for the Prajapati, Gajanana, who is fond of His devotees, appeared before that noble one.

Ganesha said: O noble one, ask for the boons you desire in your heart. I am pleased with your fasting and penance, and I shall grant them to you.

Hearing His words, she was delighted and bowed to Him. Having worshipped Gajanana, she praised Him with folded hands.

Prakriti said: Salutations to the Lord of obstacles, to Ganesha, the Supreme Soul. Salutations to the Lord of the helpless, and especially to the Lord of all. Salutations to the one who rides a mouse and has a mouse on his banner. Salutations to the Lord of Svananda and the Lord of the Ganas. Salutations to the giver of Siddhi and Buddhi, who dwells in Siddhi and Buddhi. Salutations to the Lord of Yoga, the giver of eternal peace. Salutations to the beginning of all, who is worshipped by all, the protector of devotees. Salutations again and again to the King of the eldest and the Lord of the eldest. Salutations to the Brahman, the giver of Brahman, and the Lord of the Brahmanas. Salutations to the Lord of the Siddhas, the Lord of the gods, and the Lord of the demons. Salutations to the four-armed Heramba, the bearer of the axe. Salutations to the holder of the goad, the one who is beyond all control. Salutations to the Lord of qualities, who creates through Rajas, sustains through Sattva, and destroys through Tamas. Salutations to the one who is both stationary and moving, the embodiment of all, and the consciousness beyond all. Salutations to the one who has four forms, the giver of four types of happiness, the embodiment of four-fold bliss, and the one who is self-realized. Salutations to Vinayaka, the leader of all. Salutations to the Elephant-faced God, the Lord of the gods. How can I praise you, O leader of the Ganas, the embodiment of Brahmanaspati, whom even the yogis and the chief Vedic sages are unable to praise? Yet, by the knowledge gained from your vision, I have praised you. Be pleased, O Lord, and grant me firm devotion to you.

Having praised Ganesha in this way, she bowed down to Him like a staff. The Lord of Ganas lifted her up and spoke to the mother of the vow.

Ganesha said: Ask for the boons you desire, O beautiful one. I will grant them, being pleased by your devotion and praise. This hymn composed by you shall grant all accomplishments. Whoever reads or hears it, O Goddess, shall attain their desires. Whether one reads it once, twice, or three times, there is no doubt that the one who is not observing the vow will attain the fruits of the vow through this recitation.

As the Lord of Ganas was speaking, the Goddess, with tearful eyes and bowing with devotion, spoke to Him with a deep voice.

The Goddess said: O Lord of Ganas, you are the giver of boons; then grant me firm devotion to you. O ocean of mercy, grant me the strength to perform my duties. O Lord, grant me the power to create the universe. May I always be dear to you, and may there never be a separation from you.

Saying "Om," the Lord of Ganas, the best of speakers, said: O Goddess, you shall be the giver of four-fold fruits and will be dear to me. Be known as Chaturthi, the mother of lunar days. You shall always be Krishna (dark) on my left side and Shukla (bright) on my right side. You shall be my birth-tithi, there is no doubt. I shall always protect those who remain in your vow. You shall be the auspicious lunar day born of my vow. There is no other giver of fruits like you among all vows.

Having said this, Ganesha disappeared. The mother of the vow, the mistress of the people, remained there.

Thus ends the first chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the Chaturthi Vrata.

Chapter 2 : Description of the Boons Granted to the Shukla and Krishna Chaturthi

Mudgala said: Then, remembering the Lord of the Ganas, the Goddess (Devi) decided to create. Suddenly, she became twofold, dividing into left and right parts. The left part appeared black in color, while the right part appeared white, O fortunate one. She was astonished by this. Meditating on Ganapati again, she began to create. From her lotus-like mouth, the Pratipada Tithi emerged. The Dwitiya emerged from her nose, the Tritiya from her chest, and the Panchami from her fingers, O Daksha. The Shashthi was born from her heart, the Saptami from her eyes, and the great Tithi, Ashtami, emerged from her arms. The Navami Tithi emerged from her stomach, O Prajapati, the Dashami from her ears, and the Ekadashi is considered to have emerged from her throat. The Dwadashi emerged from her feet, the Trayodashi from her breasts, and the Chaturdashi from her ego. The Purnima was born from her mind, and the Amavasya was born from her tongue. In this way, the Tithis attained distinct forms.

The Krishna Chaturthi is known as the one from which the Krishna Tithis are declared; these fifteen Tithis should be known by the wise. The Shukla Tithis are known as the fourteen Tithis originating from the body of the Shukla Chaturthi. All these Tithis appeared, endowed with their own respective qualities. The parts of the day became the masculine form, and the nights became the feminine form; they became aware of their own nature and activities, and remained devoted to the welfare of all.

Accompanied by the Tithis, the Goddess Chaturthi remained in penance, constantly worshipping Ganesha, devoted to mantra and meditation. After one year, Ganesha, who is fond of his devotees, went to her. He appeared to the Shukla Chaturthi at midday and to the Krishna Chaturthi at moonrise. He said to her, "Ask for a boon, O Ganesha-like one." Having bowed to the Lord of Obstacles, worshipped Him, and praised Him, she spoke.

Chaturthi said: O Gajanan, make me the one who dwells only in You. I do not ask for any other boon, O Lord; I desire eternal devotion. Hearing her words, Gajanan said to her: "O Chaturthi, you have attained my vision at midday. Therefore, Shiva and others worship me at midday. Always remain the principal deity of my Vratas (vows) for people on the Shukla Chaturthi. When people worship me on that day while fasting, I shall grant them the four types of fruits (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha), even if they have not accumulated merit, O Devi, there is no doubt about this. If they have accumulated merit, you shall grant them the fruits, O wise one. Therefore, your name shall be famous as 'Varada' (the Granter of Boons). Those who do not worship you along with me according to the Vrata, may all their vows be fruitless."

Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Deities, disappeared. From that day on, that Tithi became known as Varada, O Prajapati. It is remembered as the birth-Tithi of Ganesha and is very dear to Him. Fasting should be performed on this day by those desiring their own welfare. The breaking of the fast (Parana) should be done on the Panchami in the presence of a Brahmana, O Daksha. There is no doubt that they will attain the four types of fruits. Whatever the observer of the vow desires, they obtain. In the end, they go to Svananda and attain union with Brahman. If, during this vow, people eat their favorite food, they will go to hell and be deprived of food in their next birth. Thus, the Destroyer of Obstacles granted this boon to the Chaturthi, and that Tithi became respected by all and the foundation of the vow.

Then, the Lord of the Ganas said to the Krishna Chaturthi: "Ask for a boon, O fortunate one, I shall grant your heart's desire." Hearing this, she bowed to Him, worshipped Gajanan, praised Him, and spoke with great joy.

Krishna Chaturthi said: If You are pleased and are the Granter of Boons, O Gajanan, then grant me firm devotion to You, O Mahodara. May I always be dear to You, O Lord, and may there never be separation from You. Make me respected by all, as I am solely devoted to You. Hearing her words, Gajanan said to her: "O great Tithi, you will always be very dear to me. You have attained me at moonrise, therefore, O Chaturthi, let the vow performed on that day be the principal one. There shall be no separation for those devotees who, dear one, worship me on that day while abstaining from food and water; you shall remove their distress. For five Prahars, my devotees shall worship me while abstaining from food and water, and you shall remove their distress. The four types of distress—birth, death, and those born of karma—shall never exist for those who observe this vow. O Devi, by my grace, you shall remove the four types of distress on this Chaturthi. There shall never be distress for a person who observes this vow and is devoted to me. Having enjoyed all pleasures here, they shall attain Svananda in the end. Your name shall be 'Sankashta-harani' (Remover of Distress). If a person who claims to be my devotee does not observe this Chaturthi, all their worship will be fruitless. Those sinful people who abandon you and perform other vows will find all their vows fruitless. If a person, while remaining in their Varna and Ashrama, does not observe this Chaturthi, all their religious duties will be fruitless. An ascetic should observe this vow at night, abstaining from food, to remove distress and attain Svananda, there is no doubt. In the night, one should eat in the presence of a Brahmana after worshipping me, O beautiful one. In Shravana, one should eat laddus; in Bhadrapada, yogurt; in Ashwina, it is said to be waterless; in Kartika, one should drink milk. In Margashirsha, one should consume water; in Pausha, cow urine; in Magha, white sesame seeds; in Phalguna, ghee and sugar. In Chaitra, Panchagavya; in Vaishakha, lotus seeds; in Jyeshtha, cow ghee; in Ashadha, honey. This vow is always prescribed for ascetics. For others, it is remembered as a vow to be performed with food or otherwise.

Rising quickly when one Yama of the night remains, one should perform the morning duties briefly. The midday duties should also be performed in the morning. Four Ghatikas before sunrise is considered the time of sunrise. Having performed these duties and worshipped me with a short mantra, one should perform the worship of the Sun at the time of its appearance. Until moonrise, one should chant at the root of a Shami tree, silently meditating on me, and afterwards, one should bathe. The best of men should perform the evening duties there. After worshipping me according to the rules, one should offer Arghya. First, offer Arghya to you, then offer Arghya to me. Then, the observer of the vow should offer seven Arghyas to the Moon. O Devi, after the Brahmanas are worshipped, the person should eat modakas, apupas, laddus, and payasa with devotion. One should keep a vigil at night, engaged in stories about me. On the Panchami, one should worship me with effort and offerings. A person who performs the vow in this way will attain the fulfillment of all desires. They will be respected by the gods and, in the end, will merge into Brahman. Just as I am the first to be worshipped by the gods and others, you shall be the first to be worshipped among all vows. Those gods and others who perform the Shukla and Krishna Chaturthi will attain success in all their vows. Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, disappeared. The Chaturthi, filled with devotion, remained there.

Chapter 3 : Description of the Vows of the Lunar Days (Pratipada, etc.)

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Then, O Daksha, Ganesha went to all the lunar days (tithis) and said, "O wise ones, ask for any boon you desire in your hearts." Hearing the words of Ganesha, the lunar days bowed to the Lord of the Ganas, worshipped Him, praised Him, and spoke.

The lunar days said: O Lord of Obstacles, O Master, if You are pleased and wish to grant a boon, O Ocean of Compassion, tell us what we should do now. Grant us wonderful power born of our own respective functions, and grant us devotion to Your lotus feet; everything is acceptable to us if it comes from You, O Lord.

Hearing their words, the Lord of the Ganas said: Listen, O creators, to that which is beneficial for all.

Ganesha said: There is no doubt that you will have My utmost devotion, O highly fortunate lunar days, you who are possessors of merit. May the gods, who are born from a portion of My essence, always please you. May the gods, accompanied by you, be the givers of joy to mankind.

Those men who always worship Agni (Fire) on the first day (Pratipada), offering oblations with sacrificial substances and observing the vow while consuming only milk, will be satisfied along with the gods and their lords. They will grant them their heart's desires. Upon death, they will go to the world of Agni, enjoying pleasures, and will travel in the sky in a chariot as radiant as fire. There is no doubt that those who perform the Pratipada vow will, by dedicating their actions to Brahman, attain My own essence.

Whoever worships the auspicious Ashvini twins on the second day (Dvitiya) while consuming only leaves will surely become beautiful. After enjoying all pleasures here, they will attain that world at the end. The performer of the Dvitiya vow will be honored by the gods. By performing this vow with the spirit of dedication to Brahman, they will merge into My own bliss.

On the third day (Tritiya), one should worship the Great Goddess (Maha-Shakti) with devotion, using all substances that bestow prosperity, while abstaining from salt. Such a person, having gone to the world of Shakti and enjoying pleasures, will also come to My world through the dedication of their actions to Brahman.

On the fifth day (Panchami), a person should bathe the chief Nagas with milk and worship them with effort, while abstaining from sour food. They will go to the world of the Nagas and enjoy pleasures there. If they are desireless, they will eventually come to My world.

On the sixth day (Shashthi), one should worship Skanda with devotion while consuming only fruits. They will roam in the world of Skanda, immersed in great pleasures. If they are devoid of desire, they will eventually go to My bliss, controlled by all sentiments, following the path of the bright fortnight.

On the seventh day (Saptami), one should worship the Sun while observing a fast. They will reside in the solar world and enjoy pleasures. If the performer of this desireless vow is in the great abode, they will become Brahman itself in My bliss, even while in their physical body.

Whoever worships the Divine Mothers (Matrikas) on the eighth day (Ashtami) with devotion, while consuming only Bilva leaves, will go to the world of the Matrikas. Always devoted to the vow with the spirit of dedication to Me, they will also reach My world in the order determined.

Whoever performs the worship of Durga on the ninth day (Navami), consuming only flour-based food, will enjoy pleasures and attain that world at the end. Through dedication to Brahman, they will go to My own world; there is no doubt that the highly fortunate one will become Brahman through the path of the bright fortnight.

He who is established in the vow on the tenth day (Dashami), consuming curd, and worships the guardians of the directions, becomes dear to them. After enjoying all pleasures here, they will attain that world at the end. Through the spirit of a desireless vow, they will become Brahman.

On the eleventh day (Ekadashi), a person should consume food not cooked by fire and worship the Lord of Wealth (Kubera) with a devoted heart. They will dwell in his world, always enjoying various pleasures. By performing the vow with dedication to Brahman, they will become happy.

He who worships Vishnu on the twelfth day (Dvadashi) while consuming ghee will dwell forever in Vaikuntha, immersed in various pleasures. When a person performs the vow with the method of dedication to Brahman, they become one with Brahman in My great abode.

One should worship Dharma on the thirteenth day (Trayodashi) while consuming milk. They will go to the world of Dharma and enjoy various happiness. If the Trayodashi vow is performed with dedication to Brahman, they will come to My bliss and become Brahman.

He who worships Shiva on the fourteenth day (Chaturdashi) with devotion, observing a fast and breaking it with wheat, will reside in Kailasa. If they are desireless, at the end they will become one with Me in My world.

On the full moon day (Purnima), one should worship the groups of gods and the Moon with devotion; they will attain the lunar world. After enjoying all pleasures here, they will go to the gods at the end. If they are desireless, they will become one with Brahman in My world.

He who observes a fast and offers oblations to the Moon at night, while being devoted to all gods, will be blessed. The full moon is described as beautiful and full of all gods; the lunar day is also known to be merit-bestowing.

He who offers libations to the ancestors on the new moon day (Amavasya) with devotion, or observes a fast, will attain all purposes. At the end, they will dwell in the world of the ancestors. If they are desireless, they will come to My bliss and merge therein.

The dark and bright lunar days are always to be worshipped by the performer of vows. In this way, with a devoted heart, one attains all purposes. There is no doubt that you will all be My favorite lunar days, and you will be accompanied by the gods who are portions of My essence.

Those who do not observe the fourth day (Chaturthi) find everything else meaningless. There is no doubt that your vows will be established. Therefore, O honorable ones, always be associated with the fourth day. By the measure of time, you will be worshipped by all.

Having said this, Ganesha, the beloved of the Ganas, disappeared. O Lord of the people, it happened just so, that the vows associated with the lunar days became eternal. Whoever hears or reads this greatness of the lunar days, especially that which is born of the fourth day, will attain all their desires.

Chapter 4 : The Instruction of the Vow to Dasharatha

Mudgala said: O Daksha, now listen to that ancient dialogue between the royal sage Dasharatha and the great-souled Vashistha. It is through this knowledge that you, O son of Brahma, have become highly skilled in the greatness of the Chaturthi vow, which is the foremost among all lunar days.

The illustrious Dasharatha, distressed by the fear of being childless, went to the sage Vashistha, bowed to him, and asked.

Dasharatha said: O great sage, please tell me the means to obtain a son. Instruct me as your disciple and save me from this ocean of worldly existence.

Then, the best of sages spoke to the excellent king: Worship Lord Ganesha, your family deity, while observing this vow.

Hearing the words of Vashistha, the king bowed to him again and asked with humility: O Brahmin, what is the nature of this vow to the great-souled Ganesha? Who has performed it, and what kind of success is attained through it? At what time should the vow be performed, and what is the method of worship? Tell me everything in detail, and I shall act according to your command.

Vashistha said: O best of kings, the Chaturthi (fourth lunar day) is extremely dear to the Lord of Ganas. Both the bright and dark fortnights are known to grant all successes. When the dark Chaturthi coincides with moonrise, it is called Sankashta-hara (remover of distress) and grants all four types of fruits. Know, O prince, that the world is bound by four types of distress. By performing this vow, one enjoys all worldly pleasures and, being freed from all troubles, attains the abode of Svananda.

If the Chaturthi does not coincide with moonrise, the one spanning the Pradosha (evening) time should be accepted for the removal of all distress. If both days coincide with moonrise, one may choose according to one's preference, though the one spanning the moonrise is preferred. If the Chaturthi begins during the Pradosha of the third day, that day should always be accepted, rejecting the latter.

The bright Chaturthi is known to be the bestower of boons for all four goals of life without doubt, and it clears all accumulated sins for those who perform the vow. The Chaturthi that pleases Ganesha grants the four goals: Dharma (righteousness), Artha (wealth), Kama (desire), and Moksha (liberation).

The bright Chaturthi should be accepted if it spans the midday. If it is not found on either day, the one touched by the previous day is considered valid. If it spans the time of a Prahara (three hours) on both days, and the third day is also present, then the one touched by the previous day is considered valid. If the third day extends beyond a Prahara, then either the former or the latter day may be chosen for the Chaturthi.

O King, for one who is devoid of the Chaturthi vow, all other vows at all times remain fruitless; therefore, O Lord, this vow must be performed first. This vow, O fortunate one, was performed by Brahma in ancient times, and by the power of this vow, he created the entire universe. Vishnu and Shankara also performed this excellent vow, and thus became capable of sustaining and dissolving the worlds. Yogis like Shuka, sages like Kashyapa, and many others performed it in the past to attain their respective goals. Having enjoyed all pleasures here, they attained the abode of Svananda. Know, O King, that there is nothing equal to this for achieving the four goals. People of all castes and stages of life who have performed this have become one with the Brahman and attained the city of Svananda.

O King, perform this vow with devotion; you shall be blessed with a son and shall attain the abode of Svananda.

Thus ends the fourth chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the life of Gajanana and the instruction of the vow to Dasharatha.

Chapter 5 : Description of the Significance of Chaturthi

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha asked: O learned one, tell me in detail how the Chaturthi vow was performed by Brahma and others, and how it became dear to the Lord of Ganas and complete in its results.

Daksha asked: O Mudgala, tell me in detail about the greatness of Chaturthi. O best of sages, I am not satisfied even after hearing such a beautiful story.

Having heard the words of Daksha, which were filled with devotion, the wise sage Mudgala said, "Listen, Shaunaka." Having heard the words of the best of kings, Dasharatha, the best of sages, Vashistha, who knows all, spoke.

Vashistha said: Once, during the time of dissolution, when all movable and immovable things were destroyed and the five elements were in a state of order, the universe appeared as if it were void, devoid of all attributes, and shone only as Brahman. This is what the Vedas describe as the Yoga-nidra of Ganesha. After some time, awakened by the words of the Vedas, Ganesha, the sustainer of all principles, created everything.

O King, from the three Gunas, the five deities were born: Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Shakti, and Surya, who became the supporters of all. They performed severe penance according to the single-syllable mantra, and through this penance, the Lord of Ganas became pleased. He revealed his supreme self, the giver of all, in their hearts. Through this, the five deities became filled with knowledge, O King. Requested by them, the yogi Bhushundi, who had mastered everything, sent the gods back to their own realms.

In the field of action, they established the idol of Ganesha in the region known as Svananda, as indicated by the Vedas. At the time when the gods installed the idol with reverence, it was the bright fortnight of the month of Bhadrapada. At the time of midday, that installation took place, and it became the primary one. Then, they worshipped the Lord of Ganas with special devotion.

O best of kings, in the bright fortnight of the month of Magha, Ganesha appeared before them as the giver of boons. By his grace, the entire world was created, and that lunar day became the primary day for the gods to grant boons. The Chaturthi vow was taught by Brahma and others to all. Initially, the one in the bright fortnight of Jyeshtha became the primary one. In ancient times, that vow was performed on the Jyeshtha Shukla Chaturthi, and because it was observed by all at that time, it immediately became a bestower of boons.

Therefore, the Chaturthi of Jyeshtha, Magha, and Bhadrapada in the bright fortnight always attained prominence, O best of kings. Among these, the Shukla Chaturthi of the month of Bhadrapada has become the most important today due to its inherent nature. There is no other vow equal to Chaturthi; hence, what more can be described of its greatness?

If a person observes the Shukla Chaturthi of Bhadrapada, they attain the merit of twelve Chaturthis. O King, if it is performed on a Tuesday (Angaraka), there is no doubt that the person attains the merit of sixteen Chaturthis. If a wise person performs it when the moon is in conjunction with the Chaturthi, they attain the fruit of twenty Chaturthis. If it is performed on a Tuesday when the moon is in the Swati constellation, one attains the merit of twenty-four Chaturthis.

If the Shukla Chaturthi of Magha is performed, there is no doubt that the person attains the fruit of eight Chaturthis. If it is performed on a Tuesday, the merit of twelve Chaturthis is attained. Whoever observes the vow on the Shukla Chaturthi of Jyeshtha attains the merit of four Chaturthis. If it falls on a Tuesday, it becomes equal to twelve; therefore, it should be performed by a person with effort, as it grants all desires.

The Chaturthi performed in other months, such as Shravana, is also considered a bestower of boons and is accepted in the scriptures as a giver of Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha. The Chaturthi of Shravana grants the merit of two Chaturthis to the observer. The glory of Chaturthi is infinite and impossible to describe; how can I describe that which grants the four goals of life?

Now, listen, O King, about the Chaturthi of the dark fortnight, which is always auspicious and removes all troubles. There are four types of troubles that everyone faces, O King, and the fourth is the fruit of actions known as the cycle of birth and death. There are many such divisions that cannot be fully described. The entire fourfold world is said to be the fifth, which is Brahman.

Because it destroys the fourfold troubles through the mere observance of the vow, the Chaturthi of the dark fortnight is called "Sankashta-harini" (the remover of troubles). Who is capable of describing its glory, which grants all accomplishments through mere observance?

Because it was first performed by Brahma, the source of the universe, in the month of Magha, it attained prominence equal to the eight Chaturthi vows. If performed with devotion on a Tuesday, it becomes equal to twenty. The Chaturthi of the dark fortnight of Shravana is undoubtedly equal to four Chaturthis. It was taught by Brahma and is even greater, as it removes all troubles for gods and sages.

O King, the Chaturthi of the dark fortnight of Bhadrapada is equal to four Chaturthis; listen to the reason. The Lord of Ganas became a bestower of boons to the gods, and therefore, through the mere vow, it became primary. If it falls on a Tuesday, it grants the merit of twelve vows to the observer. Both the bright and dark fortnight Chaturthis should always be performed for the attainment of the four goals; otherwise, the vows remain devoid of success.

Thus ends the fifth chapter of the Gajanana Charita in the fourth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the final Upanishad of the Puranas.

Chapter 6 : The Description of the Story of Removing the Sin of Moon-Sighting

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha asked: O Lord, how did the fourth day (Chaturthi) associated with Mars (Angaraka) become so auspicious? Please explain this to me in detail, as I have a great doubt regarding this.

Vashistha replied: O King, the highly intelligent Mars (Bhauma), born from Bharadwaja, worshipped the remover of obstacles (Ganesha) by chanting the 'Gananam Tva' mantra. He performed beautiful penance while meditating on the elephant-faced Lord, residing in a forest to the west of the city of Parinera. After a hundred years, the Lord of the Ganas was pleased and appeared before him, asking him to request a boon. Mars, the son of the Earth, worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with devotion and said, "O Lord, I wish to be endowed with a human body, yet I desire to drink nectar; make me a planet, O bestower of honor. Make me known as Mangala, the remover of debts, and the bestower of wealth. May the vision of you that I obtained on the fourth day of the dark fortnight of Magha be highly auspicious for all." The Lord of the Ganas granted all these requests, and thus, the Chaturthi associated with Mars became the most auspicious and primary.

Now, I shall tell you the special history of why the moon should not be seen on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada. I will explain the reasons in detail so that you, O King, may worship Ganesha and remain free from faults. Once, the gods and sages decided that there are five deities born from the essence of Ganesha. They are never separate from the form of Ganesha, and by merely remembering them, the Lord of the Ganas is pleased. Therefore, O sages, worship Shiva and the others first, and you will attain success. The gods and sages, led by Vishnu, followed this instruction. However, because they performed the rituals without proper knowledge, they became confused and devoid of success. They approached Brahma and narrated the incident, which made him anxious. He remembered Shiva, Vishnu, Aryama, and Shakti, who were all astonished to hear the news.

Suddenly, a terrifying sound, signaling the dissolution of the universe, echoed. Hearing this, the gods were filled with fear. Then, a formidable figure appeared. Seeing his terrifying form, the gods and sages fainted or fled. After a moment, Brahma and the others regained consciousness and saw the Lord standing before them. Filled with fear, they worshipped the Lord of the Ganas, meditating on his great elephant-faced form.

Brahma and the others said: Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, the Lord of obstacles, the protector of the helpless, and the remover of hindrances. Salutations to the formless witness, the immeasurable and inconceivable Heramba. Salutations to the elephant-faced God, the one with winnowing-basket ears, the large-bellied one, and the one worthy of being worshipped first. Salutations to the beginning of all, the great giver, the one worshipped by all, the one residing in all beings, and the Dhundhiraja. Salutations to the one residing in Svananda, the embodiment of yoga and peace, the giver of yoga to yogis, and the Lord of yogas. Salutations to the creator, sustainer, and destroyer of the universe. Salutations to Ganesha, the controller of the Gunas. Salutations to the Self and the non-Self, the illuminator of causes, the Lord of Brahma and Shiva, and the protector of the gods. Remove this sudden great obstacle, for otherwise, it will surely devour us. We are the servants of your lotus feet; if we perish, your glory will be diminished. Protect us, O Lord of gods, we cannot bear to look at this fierce form. Save us, O Lord of obstacles!

As they prayed, the great being assumed the form of Ganesha. The gods bowed and worshipped him with reverence. Shiva and the others offered their mental daughters to him: Brahma offered Saraswati, Vishnu offered Pushti, Shiva offered the Yogini, and the Goddess offered Mohini. The Sun offered Sanjivani. After being worshipped, the Lord of Ganas rose and spoke to them with a voice deep as the clouds.

Shri Ganesha said: O Shiva, Indra, and sages, listen. Even though you are parts of me, do not be proud. Everything in the universe is a part of me, and nothing exists without me. I take a body to grant success to those parts. If you worship a blade of grass while ignoring me, have you really worshipped me? When you are worshipped, I am satisfied, for you are my parts. Even if the entire universe is worshipped, I am not fully satisfied, as there are infinite universes in my pores. I am the Lord marked by the elephant face, residing in the universe as the giver of yoga and peace. By worshipping me, the entire universe is worshipped. Because you were deluded by ignorance and ignored me, you faced obstacles. Do not do this again. I have shown you my vision because you served me with devotion. If I am satisfied by a mere part, why would I take a body?

Having said this, the Lord of Ganas became silent. The gods bowed and asked for forgiveness. Meanwhile, the Moon, who was on Shiva's forehead, laughed at Ganesha, proud of his own beauty. He mocked the elephant-faced form repeatedly. Angered, the Lord cursed the Moon: "O Moon, those who look at you will be afflicted with obstacles due to my words." Having said this, Ganesha disappeared. The Moon became tarnished, and the gods, including Shiva, were humbled.

Ever since then, the pride of the gods was removed. Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, the Sun, and the Goddess, all of whom were associated with Ganesha's essence, began to worship him with devotion.

Mugdala said: King Dasharatha asked the sage, "What happened to the Moon? Tell me, O great sage."

Vashistha replied: Cursed by Ganesha, the Moon went home, filled with sorrow. He realized his mistake: "The body of Ganesha is the manifest form, and his head is the unmanifest. He took a body to grace his devotees. I did not recognize his true form due to delusion. O Ocean of Mercy, forgive my offense." He then went to the banks of the Ganga and performed penance, worshipping the Lord with the eighteen-letter mantra.

When the Moon disappeared, the world became sorrowful and scorched by the Sun's heat. The gods, terrified, performed penance for the Lord of Ganas. After a hundred years, Ganesha appeared to grant them a boon. The gods prayed to him with the Atharvashirsha. Pleased, Ganesha said, "Ask for a boon." The gods requested, "O Lord, make the Moon free from faults." Ganesha replied, "The Moon mocked me on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada; therefore, he must remain faulted on that day. My words cannot be otherwise." The gods agreed and went to the Moon. The Moon bowed to Ganesha and worshipped him with a beautiful hymn.

Pleased, Ganesha said, "I forgive your endless offenses. This hymn will grant all success to those who read or hear it. Remain as you were, but on the day you mocked me, you shall be faulted. You shall be the ornament on my forehead. Those who worship you with an offering on the fourth day of the dark fortnight will attain success. Since you worshipped me on the second day (Dwitiya), you shall be worshipped by humans on the second day of the bright fortnight."

After saying this, Ganesha disappeared. The gods worshipped the Moon and returned to heaven. Since then, Ganesha is known as Bhalachandra (the one with the moon on his forehead).

In another time, due to the ignorance of the Moon, Krishna also faced a false accusation on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada. A king named Satrajit performed penance for the Sun and received the Syamantaka jewel, which produced gold daily. Krishna asked for the jewel for the sake of the kingdom, but Satrajit refused. Later, Satrajit's brother Prasena went hunting wearing the jewel and was killed by a lion. Jambavan killed the lion, took the jewel, and gave it to his daughter. The Yadavas suspected Krishna of killing Prasena for the jewel. Krishna, feeling the weight of the false accusation, went to the forest, found the cave, and fought Jambavan for twenty-one days. The Yadavas, waiting outside, thought Krishna was dead and were filled with sorrow. Finally, Jambavan recognized Krishna as the Lord and returned the jewel along with his daughter. Krishna returned to Dwaraka, but later, the jewel was stolen by Shatadhanva, who was killed by Krishna. However, the jewel was not found on him. Krishna was blamed again and was deeply distressed.

Narada then told Krishna, "You saw the Moon on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, which is why you are suffering from this curse. Worship Ganesha with the Sankashti Vrata." Krishna followed this advice, worshipped Ganesha, and the Lord appeared, granting him the boon that he would be free from the curse. Krishna then discovered that Akrura had the jewel. He retrieved it and returned it to the assembly.

Thus, the story of the Syamantaka jewel and the Moon-sighting is told. Whoever hears this story becomes free from the sin of seeing the Moon on that day. Otherwise, one may fall into hell. This is the sixth chapter of the Gajanana Charita in the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 7 : Description of the Bhadrapada Shukla Chaturthi Vow

Dasharatha said: O knower of the Vedas, I have heard from you the great narrative regarding the essence and glory of both the Chaturthi (fourth day) vows. Nevertheless, O Brahmin, please tell me in detail about the origin of these vows and by whom they were performed in ancient times. What kind of success was achieved, and who attained the elephant-faced Lord? My thirst for hearing the glory of this vow remains unquenched.

Mudgala said: Being thus asked, the great yogi Vashistha, who knew the supreme truth, said to the king, "Listen, O Lord of the people, to that which brings supreme happiness."

Vashistha said: In the beginning, this supreme vow was performed by Shiva and others, who observed fasting on the Krishna and Shukla Chaturthi. Later, it was performed by Svayambhuva and others, granting them the fulfillment of all desires. Thereafter, it was performed by all the sages and beings of all castes. Now, I shall tell you separately about the origin of the vow and its holy, wish-fulfilling glory; listen to it in brief.

In ancient times, there was a king named Bharata, born in the lineage of Dushyanta. He was brilliant, a leader of heroes, skilled in weaponry, and powerful. Having conquered the entire earth, he became a universal monarch and ruled the seven-island world according to righteousness. By divine providence, a terrible drought occurred in his kingdom, and all the people became fearful. Seeing the mobile and immobile world in distress, the illustrious king went to the peaceful sage Kanva for refuge. He bowed to the great soul, worshipped him with devotion, and stood before him with folded hands. The great yogi Kanva, who was always fond of Ganesha and steadfast in the meaning of the Vedas, spoke to the noble king.

Kanva said: Why have you come, O wise King? Is all well? Please sit on this excellent seat. Hearing Kanva's words, the king was pleased and sat on the seat offered by the sage, keeping his hands folded. Bharata, who was well-versed in statecraft and filled with devotion, spoke to the great sage.

Bharata said: By your grace, O King of Yogis, all is well. Yet, due to divine providence, I have come to you in distress. I have always ruled the earth according to my duty, for the sake of gods, vows, guests, and the wise. All my subjects follow their respective duties, yet I do not know why this sin-born suffering has come upon us. A drought has occurred, causing suffering everywhere, and the world is agitated and devoid of vitality. For this reason, I have come to you, O great yogi; my life has become fruitful by your sight. Now, tell me the nature of this sin so that I may destroy it, and do what is necessary so that there may be abundant rain.

Hearing this, the sage, who was naturally compassionate, spoke to the king.

Kanva said: Listen, O King, to the great sin that has occurred in your kingdom, which is undoubtedly the cause of this drought. Your kingdom is certainly devoid of the four-fold path of righteousness. Your subjects have committed a grave sin. You, too, O lover of your people, have acted with a sinful disposition and have become devoid of Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha. O King, the Chaturthi vow, which is well-known as both Krishna and Shukla and grants all desires, has been neglected in your kingdom. Know, O King, that the four-fold world, in its gross and subtle forms, is in distress. The Sankashta Chaturthi (the Krishna Chaturthi) which I have described to you, removes this four-fold distress. The Shukla Chaturthi is considered the bestower of the four-fold boons. If there is no accumulated merit, the vow-taker suffers.

Kanva then explained the glory of the Chaturthi in detail to his disciple Bharata. Realizing that all vows are fruitless without the Chaturthi, the king, filled with joy, bowed to him.

Bharata said: Master, tell me the glory of Ganesha, which grants all success. I shall worship him daily with such a vow.

Kanva said: Listen, O King, to the supreme glory of Ganesha, which was told to me by the wise son of Brahma, Kratu. Once, I was in my hermitage, performing great penance, living only on air. My penance pervaded everything, yet I remained steadfast in yoga. Then, the creator Prajapati, the great yogi Kratu, who is worshipped by yogis and is a great devotee of Ganesha, came to my hermitage. I bowed to him, worshipped him, and he spoke to me.

Kratu said: O wise one, stay on the seat I have given you. Tell me your desire, and I shall fulfill it.

Hearing his words, I was delighted. I sat him on the seat and said with humility: "By your sight alone, I am fulfilled. Yet, O ocean of mercy, tell me the yoga that brings peace."

Kratu said: You have asked well. I shall tell you what I heard from Brahma. Once, I was practicing yoga but found no peace. I went to my father, Brahma, the knower of all, and asked him for the path to peace.

Brahma said: Know, my son, that the yoga which grants peace is related to Ganesha. Abandon everything related to mind and speech through the service of yoga. That which is beyond mind and speech is considered the true state. Ganesha is not different from me; he is the leader of the Brahmins. In Vedic parlance, the letter 'Ga' represents that which is related to mind and speech, and the letter 'Na' is accepted as that which is beyond mind and speech. The Lord of both is Ganesha. Through meditation, one attains this. By abandoning the knowledge and ignorance of the mind, you will attain peace. This is the supreme secret. Worship the Lord of the Ganas with devotion. Accept this one-syllable mantra, and through meditation, you will undoubtedly attain the Lord of Obstacles.

Having said this, he gave me the great mantra. I went to the forest and practiced it. By abandoning the stages of the mind, I focused my mind on the Chintamani (Ganesha) and became a devotee of Ganesha. Then, the Lord of Obstacles appeared before me, and I worshipped him. Since then, I have been a devotee of Ganesha. You, too, O King, worship the Lord of the Ganas with the desire for yoga.

Kanva said: Having said this, the great yogi gave me the one-syllable mantra and disappeared. I worshipped Ganesha and attained peace. Therefore, O King, you too worship the Lord of Obstacles with the Chaturthi vow, and you will become one with Brahman.

The great sage gave him the one-syllable mantra, and the king, after bowing to Kanva, returned to his city. At that time, the Bhadrapada Shukla Chaturthi arrived. He performed the vow with great devotion along with his subjects. He proclaimed throughout the kingdom that those who do not observe the Shukla and Krishna Chaturthi vows would be punished. Due to this merit, rain fell, and the people became prosperous and healthy.

King Bharata worshipped Ganesha with undivided devotion. Eventually, he entrusted the kingdom to his son, went to the forest, and attained the supreme state of Brahman. Through the merit of the Chaturthi, the people also attained the supreme abode. Even the insects and other creatures that were touched by Bharata attained the supreme state. No one was equal to Bharata in knowledge, strength, and glory.

I have told you the glory of the Bhadrapada Shukla Chaturthi, which grants the four goals of life and leads to Brahman. Whoever hears or reads this glory on Bhadrapada Shukla Chaturthi will surely attain all desires. On this day, at midday, the Lord appeared in the heart of Shiva as his son. Since then, this day has been the birth-day of Ganesha. There is no doubt that it grants the state of Brahman. In the beginning of creation, the five gods established the excellent image of Ganesha. He is the Mayuresha avatar who appeared at midday on this day. Those who worship the clay image of Ganesha on this day, along with the gods led by Shiva, become free from obstacles, enjoy happiness, and eventually attain the supreme abode.

Worship of Ganesha is prescribed at midday. After fasting on Chaturthi, one should perform the Parana (breaking of the fast) on Panchami with Brahmins, and immerse the clay image in water. Those who do not worship the clay image on this day find all their actions fruitless. They should not be seen by those seeking their own welfare, as they are considered fallen and destined for hell. They suffer from obstacles, diseases, and poverty. Therefore, Ganesha must be worshipped by all on this day.

I have told you the story of this Chaturthi. Now, I will tell you an ancient history. In the city of Dravida, there was a sinful Chandala who suffered from leprosy and was addicted to other women. On the Bhadrapada Chaturthi, he was suffering from a fever. Due to divine providence, he had no food or water. He died on the Panchami and went to the abode of Yama in a celestial chariot. The wind touched by his body reached the world of Yama, and those touched by it, even those in hell, were liberated and went to the supreme abode. Seeing Ganesha, he attained the state of Brahman. Even if a vow is performed without knowledge, it is fruitful; imagine the result if performed according to the rules! The glory of Chaturthi cannot be fully described; all four goals of life are attained by this vow alone.

Mudgala said: Hearing Vashistha's words, Dasharatha asked: "O great sage, tell me, how did the Chandala, who did not worship the clay image, attain the state of Brahman?"

Vashistha said: You have asked well. The Chandala did not have knowledge of the Chaturthi, but he did not violate the worship either. Therefore, he was free from sin and attained the supreme abode. He was devoid of both the merit of worship and the sin of violation. Thus, many people enjoy various pleasures and attain the supreme abode through this vow alone. This glory is infinite; I have described it to you in brief.

Chapter 8 : Description of the Vrata (Vow)

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Vashistha said: O noble one, listen to the details of the Vrata (vow) that falls on the fourth day (Chaturthi) of the bright fortnight of the month of Ashvina, which grants all desires. O King, I shall narrate an ancient history regarding this Vrata, which is capable of fulfilling all human goals.

In the era of Raivata, there lived a glorious king named Kirtiman, who was a follower of Dharma and an expert in all weapons. He was devoted to the service of gods, Brahmins, and guests, and performed the five daily sacrifices. Being wise and righteous, he ruled his subjects like his own children. His wife was equally virtuous, chaste, and devoted to the gods and Brahmins. The king possessed an uncountable number of elephants, horses, infantry, chariots, and archers. He ruled the earth consisting of seven continents and was invincible in battle.

However, due to the misfortune of being childless, the king had no son. He made many efforts and performed various pilgrimages, rituals, and worship of the gods, but remained without a son. Finally, leaving his kingdom, he went to the forest with his wife. While wandering there, he saw a terrifying forest filled with lions and other wild beasts. Despite his sorrow, the king entered the forest and saw the great sage Saubhari. The king and his wife bowed before the sage and stood before him with folded hands. The sage welcomed the king and offered him a seat.

The sage asked, "Who are you, and why have you come to this fierce forest?" The king, Kirtiman, replied with folded hands, "O great sage, I am a king from the Dravida country. I have come here in search of a son, as I am childless. I have performed many religious acts and pilgrimages, but to no avail. Your presence is the source of all success, and by seeing you, my life has become blessed. Please tell me the way to obtain a son; I will follow your instructions. O Brahmin, I have not committed any grave sins in this life, yet I am suffering from the curse of childlessness. I do not know the sins of my past life; please reveal them to me, for you are a great yogi and a manifestation of Brahman."

Vashistha continued: The great sage Saubhari replied, "O King, you have not committed any sin in this life, but in your kingdom, the supreme Chaturthi Vrata has been neglected. This Vrata is the source of all fruits, and if a human does not perform it, all other religious acts become fruitless. You have performed many virtuous deeds, but because of the absence of this Chaturthi Vrata, they have become ineffective. This Vrata is the giver of the four goals of life and removes all four types of hardships. A king bears the burden of the sins committed by his subjects; since your subjects were not performing this Vrata, you became the recipient of their sins. That is why you are childless."

Hearing this, the king asked, "O Lord, I committed this sin out of ignorance. Please tell me the procedure of this Vrata so that I may become free of sin and be blessed with a son." Saubhari replied, "Perform the Chaturthi Vrata regularly, and you will be free from all sins. By performing this Vrata, you will become pure and virtuous." The sage then explained the glory of the Vrata in detail.

Kirtiman asked, "What is the nature of this Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha), whose Vrata is four-fold and leads to the realization of Brahman? I wish to worship Him." Saubhari then narrated his own story: "Once, when I was deeply engaged in penance, the gods became fearful. Indra sent Kamadeva and the Apsaras, including Urvashi, to break my penance. I conquered Kamadeva through the power of my penance. Later, the great yogi Shuka came to my hermitage and advised me to worship the Chintamani Ganesha with the single-syllable mantra. I followed his advice, and after ten years, Ganesha appeared before me and granted me the status of a Ganapatya (a devotee of Ganesha). Since then, I have been a devotee of the Lord of Brahman."

After giving the six-syllable mantra to the king, the sage blessed him. The king returned to his city and began the Vrata on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Ashvina. He performed the worship of Ganesha with his subjects, observed a fast, and kept a vigil at night. He proclaimed the importance of this Vrata throughout his kingdom, and it became widely practiced. After ruling for a long time, he handed over the kingdom to his son and went to the forest with his wife to worship Ganesha. Ultimately, he attained the abode of Ganesha (Svananda) and became one with the Brahman.

O Dasharatha, listen to another wonderful history. There was a cruel hunter named Bhima who lived by killing people and stealing their wealth. Once, he tried to kill a Brahmin in the forest, but the Brahmin escaped. A soldier caught Bhima and brought him to the king. It so happened that the king had ordered the hunter to be imprisoned on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Ashvina. Because he was hungry and had inadvertently spent the day of Chaturthi without food, Ganesha granted him liberation on the fifth day.

Thus, many people attain the abode of Ganesha through the power of the Chaturthi Vrata. This is the wonderful story of the Chaturthi Vrata, which originated in the bright fortnight of Ashvina. Whoever hears or reads this will attain worldly enjoyments, liberation, and be blessed with children and grandchildren.

Thus ends the eighth chapter of the fourth section of the Mudgala Purana, describing the Chaturthi Vrata of Ashvina.

Chapter 9 : The Description of the Fourth Day of the Bright Fortnight of Kartika

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Vashistha said: O fortunate one, listen to the history of the fourth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Kartika, which grants all accomplishments.

There was a king named Sudhanva, born in the solar dynasty, who was righteous, powerful in weaponry, and possessed great radiance. He was virtuous, generous, truthful, respected by the wise, and devoted to the five sacrifices (Panchayajna) for gods, Brahmins, and guests. His wife, Kalavati, was beautiful, chaste, generous, and particularly virtuous. Having conquered the entire earth, the king, who was the best among the radiant, ruled his kingdom righteously. His vassals were submissive, his army was countless, and his wealth shone like that of Kubera.

However, this excellent king was afflicted by leprosy, caused by worms, and suffered greatly. Covered in pus, blood, and sweat, and emitting a foul odor, he found no comfort, as if pierced by a spear. He diligently sought various medicines and employed humans to perform various mantras and rituals, but none of the Vedic mantras or remedies provided any relief. He then traveled to various holy places for bathing and charity, yet his disease only worsened.

Resigned, the king told his ministers to govern the kingdom until his return. Consoling his wife, friends, and citizens, the king went to the forest and wandered aimlessly. Filled with sorrow, he began to remember Ganapati to remove his obstacles, and a miracle occurred. Suddenly, the great sage Pulastya arrived. Seeing him, the king, filled with joy, bowed down along with his wife. With folded hands, the king said to the sage, "What merit did I earn in the past that I have seen you, O sage? My birth, knowledge, parents, and all my penance and virtues have become fruitful by the sight of your feet."

The great sage asked, "O king, wise in politics, why have you come to the forest?" The king sat down as directed by Pulastya under the shade of a tree and narrated his story. Sudhanva said, "The Puranas say that sages are compassionate, and seeing your mercy, I know it to be true. Knowing me to be suffering, you have appeared. You are the son of Brahma, the creator. O great yogi, you are all-knowing; tell me, I have always ruled righteously, so what great sin from a previous life has caused this leprosy?"

Vashistha said: Hearing Sudhanva's words and seeing him suffering, the great sage spoke with a compassionate heart. Pulastya said, "O lowly man, you do not realize the sin committed in this life that caused this leprosy; listen, I will tell you. In your kingdom, the great vow of Ganesha, known as the Chaturthi (fourth day), which grants all accomplishments and is the means for the four goals of human life (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha), was neglected. If this vow is not performed, all other actions become fruitless. Even if a man performs his duties, if he ignores this, the sins of all castes fall upon the king. That is why you have become afflicted with leprosy. When you die, you will surely go to hell because you lacked the four goals."

Hearing Pulastya's words, the sorrowful king, with folded hands, asked, "O Lord, knower of all truths, what you have said is the truth. Now, tell me what this vow is, how to worship, and when it should be performed to grant all accomplishments. Tell me the remedy to destroy this leprosy; I will perform the penance to remove the sin of neglecting the vow."

Pulastya, the great yogi, was pleased and, remembering Ganesha, said, "Penance destroys sins committed through ignorance. Perform this vow immediately with your people. By doing so, you will be free of leprosy and become handsome. There is no doubt that your sins will be destroyed by your repentance." After explaining the vow, the sage told him the story of its origin. The king, filled with joy, bowed repeatedly and said, "My birth is blessed, O Lord, for I have heard of this great vow. I know of nothing equal to it. O wise one, tell me the true nature of Ganesha. Knowing that, I will worship the Lord of Lords with devotion."

Pulastya, the knower of all things and a great devotee of Ganesha, replied, "Sudhanva, listen to my words regarding the knowledge of Ganesha, which is full of the essence of Brahman and the form of Yoga. Long ago, I sought peace through various yogic practices. Despite my efforts, I remained restless and sought refuge in Shankara. I asked him about the best yoga, and he told me what you must know to become a devotee of Ganesha."

Lord Shiva said, "Know Ganesha as the embodiment of yogic peace and worship him with devotion. He is beyond mind and speech, yet he is the essence of both. In the Vedas, know the letter 'Ga' as the source of all knowledge. The letter 'Na' in the name Ganapati represents that which is beyond mind and speech. The Lord of both is Ganesha, who is attained through peace and yoga. Worship this Vinayaka by restraining the mind."

Having said this, Mahadeva fell silent. I went to the forest and practiced this with great effort. By meditating on Ganapati with the eight-syllable mantra and restraining my mind, I pleased him. In a short time, I attained peace. After twenty-one years, Vinayaka appeared to grant me a boon. I bowed, worshipped him according to the rites, and praised him with the eight names. He made me a devotee of Ganesha and returned to his abode, Swanandaka. Since then, I have worshipped the Lord of the Ganas."

Having said this, Pulastya gave the eight-syllable mantra to the king. Praised by this, the great yogi disappeared. The king returned to his city and, filled with joy in the month of Kartika, performed the great vow with all his people on the fourth day of the bright fortnight, remembering Ganesha. He broke his fast on the fifth day, gave charity to Brahmins, and satisfied everyone with food. Consequently, the king became free of leprosy and became as handsome as Kamadeva. People suffering from infertility and diseases were also relieved of their sorrows by observing this vow.

The king then spread the knowledge of this Ganesha vow throughout the earth. People began observing the Chaturthi of both the bright and dark fortnights, enjoying various pleasures. Later, Sudhanva placed his son on the throne and, living in seclusion, always worshipped Ganesha. At the end, he attained the abode of Ganesha and became one with Brahman, as did all his people.

O king, I have told you this; now listen to the glory of this vow that grants all accomplishments. A sinful Chandala living in Mahishmati went to the forest on the Kartika bright Chaturthi. There, he was spotted by a tiger and climbed a tree. The tiger waited at the base of the tree. The night passed, and the Chandala stayed awake, thus completing the vow unknowingly. A great snake then climbed the tree, and the Chandala, terrified, fell down. The tiger caught and ate him on the fifth day. By the merit of the vow performed unknowingly, he went to the abode of Ganesha, attained the state of a yogi, and became one with Brahman.

Thus, O king, those who perform the Chaturthi vow attain the fruits of the four goals of life and become one with Brahman. I have told you a fraction of the glory of the Kartika bright Chaturthi. Whoever hears or reads this will attain all accomplishments and, blessed with children and grandchildren, will become dear to Ganesha.

Thus ends the ninth chapter of the fourth section of the Gajanana Charita in the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 10 : Description of the Shukla Chaturthi of Margashirsha

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: Hearing the stories of Ganesha brings joy to my heart. O Sage, please tell me about the Shukla Chaturthi that falls in the month of Margashirsha.

Vashistha said: O King, I shall describe to you an ancient history regarding the Shukla Chaturthi of Margashirsha. Long ago, there was a king of Kashi named Ajatashatru, who was virtuous, a master of all scriptures, a seeker of the company of gods and learned men, and devoted to various righteous duties. He was diligent in protecting his subjects and honored them according to the scriptures.

Suddenly, Narada arrived to see the king. The noble king bowed to him and worshipped him with devotion. The king himself massaged the sage's feet and spoke to the all-pervading Narada with great joy.

Ajatashatru said: My birth, my progeny, and my kingdom, now free from thorns, have become blessed. My parents and my knowledge have become fruitful by the sight of your lotus feet. Please tell me the essence of all things, that which provides yoga and peace, by which a human is instantly liberated from the sorrows of the world.

Then, the yogi Narada, the foremost among the followers of Ganesha, smiled and spoke this essential truth to the king.

Narada said: You have asked well, O King. It is the supreme knowledge that gives liberation. Listen, I shall tell you of the yoga that bestows peace. Brahman is described in various ways in the Vedas according to the capacity of the seeker, but the ultimate state of being Brahman is one. Worship Ganesha, who is known as Brahmanaspati, with devotion. By restraining the fluctuations of the mind, you will become like the Chintamani. I am Ganesha, there is no doubt about this; how could there be any doubt in me? Through this union, O King, you will attain peace. His great vow, known as Chaturthi, is significant. Even if your kingdom is lost, by this vow, you will become a ruler of men. It is declared to be the fulfiller of Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha. O King, the Chaturthi vow is known to be a bestower of boons and a remover of crises. People perform various rituals for the attainment of all goals, but without the Chaturthi vow, they remain fruitless.

Having said this, the great yogi Narada, filled with compassion, narrated the glory of the Chaturthi. The king, filled with devotion, bowed to the sage who knew all paths and asked again about the worship of Ganesha.

Ajatashatru said: O great-minded one, I have heard the glory of Brahmanaspati. Please tell me the path of his worship; salutations to you, O knower of all.

Narada said: Worship Dhundhi Vinayaka using the single-syllable mantra. By this, Ganesha will be pleased and will manifest before you.

Narada then gave him the entire method of Ganesha worship, including the single-syllable mantra with its auxiliary rites, and then disappeared. When the Shukla Chaturthi of Margashirsha arrived, the king performed the fast according to the prescribed rules. All the people also performed it, as it grants all desires, and thus it became highly praised everywhere. Those base humans, including women, who do not observe the Shukla or Krishna Chaturthi are great sinners and deserve to be punished. The king, the leader of men, worshipped Dhundhiraja daily with effort and devotion, and he became a great yogi, respected by other yogis.

After much time had passed, the elephant-faced Lord manifested before the king and asked him to seek a boon. Pleased by the king's prayers and worship, Dhundhiraja granted him the status of a Gana and blessed King Ajatashatru. After granting the desired boon, the Lord disappeared. The king worshipped Dhundhiraja with a focused mind, and in the end, he attained the Lord of the Ganas and became one with Brahman, respected by all yogis.

Thus, O King, I have briefly narrated the great glory of the Shukla Chaturthi of Margashirsha; there is no doubt about it. Listen to another story, O King, which removes all fears and shows how a courtesan attained the state of Brahman through this vow.

Once in Mithila, there was a courtesan who enchanted all men. Seeing her, all men were infatuated. The king honored her greatly, and she chose to live there. Once, while going on a pilgrimage, she was spotted by a demon. The demon captured her and took her to his abode with joy. Seeing him, she was terrified and lamented in great distress. Though the demon tried to console her, she could not abandon her grief. At that time, the Shukla Chaturthi of Margashirsha arrived. Due to extreme grief, O King, she did not eat or drink anything. On the fifth day, she died with a fearful heart. Then, the messengers of Ganesha took her to the city of Svananda, and she attained the state of Brahman through the merit of the vow.

Even if performed out of ignorance, this great vow, known as the bestower of boons, is said to grant union with Brahman; what then to speak of one who performs it with knowledge and devotion? Whoever hears or performs this vow on the Shukla Chaturthi of Margashirsha attains all their desires.

Thus ends the tenth chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the Shukla Chaturthi of Margashirsha.

Chapter 11 : Greatness of the Pausha Shukla Chaturthi

Dasharatha said: O excellent Guru, I have heard about the vow related to the Chaturthi of the bright fortnight of Margashirsha, and I am satisfied. Now, O best of sages, please tell me the greatness of the Chaturthi that falls in the month of Pausha, which grants all desires.

Vashistha said: Listen, O King, I will tell you the greatness in brief, for who on earth is capable of describing it in full detail? In the city of Avanti, there lived a great Brahmana named Sudanta, who was well-versed in all the scriptures. He was the chief priest of the King and always followed the path of righteousness, guiding the King according to the Dharma Shastras. The King, named Brihadratha, was obedient to his commands and ruled the land by performing various righteous deeds.

Sudanta had a wife named Vilasini, who was a devoted wife but, due to past karma, was barren. Any child she bore died immediately after birth. For this reason, the Brahmana performed many vows and charities, yet he could not obtain a son who would bring him supreme happiness. Extremely distressed, the Brahmana and his wife went to the forest. With a wandering mind and having decided to end his life, he became absorbed in penance.

There came the powerful and great Yogi Vamadeva. While wandering at will, he saw Sudanta and bowed to him. After worshipping the great soul Vamadeva, Sudanta, filled with sorrow, spoke to the best of yogis.

Sudanta said: O Vamadeva, my life and penance are blessed by the sight of your lotus feet. I am barren, O great sage; what will be my fate? Tell me, O best of yogis, what happens to one who dies without a son and without heaven?

Vamadeva said: Listen, O virtuous Brahmana. Although you are always righteous, you have committed sins in the past; listen to how that happened. You were the priest of the King of Avanti, and whatever great sins the King committed, you also incurred. The observance of the Chaturthi vow ceased in the land, and the people became devoid of the fruits of the four goals of life (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha). The righteous conduct that was supposed to be performed by the King and you was rendered fruitless due to the neglect of this vow, and that is why you are suffering now.

Hearing these beautiful words of the wise Vamadeva, Sudanta was astonished and spoke with humility.

Sudanta said: O venerable one, tell me what that vow is, which is beneficial and fulfills the four goals of human life. How does one who is devoid of it become bereft of fruits? Explain the nature of this karma, O ocean of mercy.

Vashistha said: Being asked thus, the great Yogi explained the entire greatness in detail, and hearing it, Sudanta was amazed. With a joyful heart, the Brahmana again asked the great sage, who was an expert in Ganesha-knowledge, to enlighten him about Ganesha.

Sudanta said: What is this Lord of Ganas like? Tell me His form so that, knowing Him, I may worship Him daily with devotion.

Being asked thus, the great Yogi Vamadeva, who was skilled in imparting the knowledge of Ganesha, spoke to him.

Vamadeva said: Listen, O Brahmana sage, to the supreme knowledge of Ganesha, through which you will become filled with devotion. Long ago, I was devoted to penance. Through my penance, I pervaded all that is moving and non-moving. Then, O best of Brahmanas, I abandoned penance for the sake of Yoga, focusing on internal peace and the conquest of the mind. Through the stages of Yoga, I wandered here and there. Seeing that the natural state, which is free from delusion and full of self-reliance, was the ultimate peace, I abandoned it. I held in my mind the Brahman, which is beyond mind and speech. How can self-reliance and freedom from delusion exist there?

Thinking about what I should do, I sought refuge in Shankara, who is worshipped by yogis. I, a Shaiva, bowed to Him. Seeing me always devoted to the Shaiva path, Shambhu was pleased and said, "O best of sages, why have you come, O wise Vamadeva? Tell me your whole story; I will do what is dear to you."

Hearing the words of Shiva, I stood with folded hands and spoke to the Great God, who is like a wish-fulfilling tree for His devotees. I said, "O Lord, the natural state which is the supreme Brahman, the Shaiva state, is self-willed. There is nothing beyond it, yet please remove my doubt. How can there be self-will in one who has become Brahman? Self-reliance is considered a defect, and the natural state is not considered the ultimate. Therefore, I have come to ask you for peace. O ocean of compassion, tell me the complete path that grants the peace of Yoga."

Vashistha said: Hearing the words of Vamadeva, the joyful Shankara, who knows all Yoga, spoke to him about the nature of Ganesha.

Shri Shiva said: Know, O sage, that Ganesha is the one who grants the peace of Yoga. Do not seek the supreme Brahman beyond Him through the practice of Yoga. Know that which arises from self-bliss as both unreal and real; it is equal and natural, and it is divided into four parts. The union of these four is called self-bliss. Without this union, there is no union of any Brahman. Know the knowledge of the letter 'Ga' from your own realization, and the knowledge of the letter 'Na' is attained by people through renunciation. The Lord of these two is called Ganesha, the embodiment of Yoga. He is attained through peace, O Brahmana, and He is the giver of peace to those who are peaceful. Know the mind to be fivefold, which is undoubtedly the form of intellect. In the mind, the delusion-like perfection and Maya are described. Ganesha is the reflection of these two; abandon the idea of reflection, O Lord. Now you will undoubtedly become the Lord of Ganas.

Having said this, the Great God gave him the one-syllable mantra of Ganesha with the proper procedure, out of compassion. Bowing to Him, I went to the forest and worshipped the Lord of Ganas there with devotion. Satisfied with the one-syllable procedure, the Lord of Ganas granted me complete Yoga-peace out of affection for His devotee. Since then, I have been worshipped by yogis and accepted everywhere. Yet, I worshipped the destroyer of obstacles daily with devotion.

After ten years, the Lord of Ganas came to my hermitage to grant me a boon and spoke with a deep voice.

Ganesha said: O wise Vamadeva, ask for the boon you desire in your heart. I will grant it to you, as I am satisfied with your devotion, O best of yogis.

Hearing the words of Ganesha, I rose from my meditation, bowed to Him, worshipped Him, and began to praise Him.

Vamadeva said: Salutations to Ganesha, who always dwells in self-bliss. Salutations to the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the Lord of Obstacles. O rider of the mouse, Heramba, fulfiller of the desires of devotees, O Dhundhiraja, protect me, salutations to you. Salutations to the one who is without beginning, middle, or end, O Lambodara. Salutations to the one who is the form of beginning, middle, and end, the dear son of Shankara. You are the holder of various illusions and the giver of delusion to those who are deluded. You play with the differences of Maya and Mayika, salutations to you. Salutations to you, the son of Vishnu, the son of Shesha, the Brahmana, the son of Brahma, the Lord of all, the son of all. Salutations to you, the father of all, the mother of all, the all-pervading one, the one with a large belly, the Lord of the gods, the eldest. You are the fierce one, the Great Lord, Vishnu, the powerful one, the nectar, the sun, the one with various powers. Salutations to you, the Purusha, the Prakriti, the Lord of Gunas, the embodiment of Gunas, the one who is both one and many, the creator of obstacles. Salutations to you, the giver of all to the devotees, the Lord of Brahmanas, the Yoga, the Lord of Yoga, the Lord of yogis. How can I praise you, O Ganesha, who are beyond mind and speech? You are not made of mind and speech, therefore, O Lord, salutations to you.

As he praised Him thus, due to the essence of devotion, his hair stood on end and his throat choked. The Lord of Obstacles said to the dancing Vamadeva, "Ask for the boon, O great soul, that is in your heart. This hymn you have composed is the best, granting all perfections. Whoever hears or reads it, to him it will grant Yoga, devotion, sons, grandsons, wealth, and grain, and it will increase my love for him."

Hearing the words of Ganesha, the great sage Vamadeva, with a pleased heart, spoke to Him.

Vamadeva said: O Lord of Ganas, grant me devotion, especially the devotion of Ganesha. I do not ask for any other boon, O Lord of Obstacles, if you are satisfied.

Saying "So be it," Ganesha disappeared. Vamadeva, with a pleased heart, became a devotee of Ganesha. Since then, I have attained peace, O wise Brahmana. Therefore, you worship the Lord of Ganas, and you will attain peace.

Vamadeva then gave the one-syllable great mantra to Sudanta with the proper procedure and disappeared. Sudanta, amazed, went to his own place. Honored by the King, he worshipped the Lord of Ganas. He told the whole story to Brihadratha, and at his command, the King immediately proclaimed the vow.

In the month of Pausha, when the bright Chaturthi arrived, the Brahmana performed it immediately with the people and the King. By merely performing the vow, his wife became pregnant and gave birth to a son who was wise and long-lived. Then, everyone on earth performed this excellent vow, the bright and dark Chaturthi, O King. Everyone became free from diseases, blessed with sons, wealth, and grain, and in the end, they attained the state of self-bliss. Sudanta became worshipped by yogis through the practice of Yoga, and the King also became wise and a devotee of Ganesha.

This vow of Pausha Shukla Chaturthi has been told to you, O King, which grants all desires. Listen again, O giver of honor. A merchant who was on a journey, tired and greedy for wealth, was robbed of his great wealth by thieves. Struck by their weapons, he fell to the ground. In the forest, the merchant lamented, extremely distressed. By divine coincidence, that holy day of Pausha Shukla Chaturthi arrived, and he remained there without water or food. At night, due to his pain, he kept a vigil. The sinful, wealth-greedy man died on the fifth day, O wise one. By the power of the unknown vow, he saw Ganesha in self-bliss and became one with Brahman, O King.

Thus, people have been liberated from the ocean of worldly existence by this vow. Having enjoyed all pleasures here, it is impossible to describe it. Whoever hears or reads this greatness of Pausha Chaturthi, O King, will attain everything.

Thus ends the eleventh chapter of the Gajanana Charita in the fourth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, describing the greatness of Pausha Shukla Chaturthi.

Chapter 12 : The Glory of the Fourth Day of the Bright Fortnight of Magha

Obeisance to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O great yogi, hearing the glory of the Chaturthi (fourth day) fills me with joy. I am never satisfied; I consider it even greater than nectar. O sage, tell me the supreme glory of the Chaturthi that falls in the bright fortnight of Magha, by which all people may become enjoyers of happiness.

Vashistha said: O King, the Chaturthi in the bright fortnight of Magha is the day when Vinayaka (Ganesha) was born in the house of Kashyapa, accompanied by Angaraka (Mars).

Mudgala said: Having said this, Vashistha narrated the story of Vinayaka’s exploits, specifically regarding the destruction of the demons Devantaka and Narantaka. Hearing this, the son of Aja (Dasharatha) was filled with joy and once again respectfully urged the sage to explain the observance of the vow. Recognizing his sincerity, Vashistha, the best among the followers of Ganesha, spoke with great joy.

Vashistha said: I shall narrate to you an ancient history. If a noble person hears this, they will become complete and fulfilled. In the city of Bhanupuri in Karnataka, there lived a very righteous king named Soma. He was a seeker of the company of gods, Brahmins, and guests, and he ruled with justice. He was skilled in weapons, valiant, and after conquering the entire earth, he established an excellent and prosperous kingdom. His wife, Yashovati, was a devoted wife, virtuous, charitable, and beautiful.

After a long time of ruling well, a terrible famine occurred. To find a resolution to this suffering, the King went to the forest to meet the sage Shaunaka, who was a follower of Ganesha and well-versed in all truths. Upon reaching the great forest, he bowed before the great sage, prostrating with his whole body and folding his hands. Soma, filled with humility, said to the great sage: "My birth and actions are blessed today, for I have seen you, O sage."

Seeing the King speaking thus, Shaunaka, who knew the essence of all scriptures and had abandoned all illusions, asked: "O noble King, tell me everything. Why have you come to this difficult forest?"

Soma replied with a joyful heart: "O great sage, I live in Bhanupuri in Karnataka, desiring righteousness. However, a terrible famine has occurred there. I have ruled with justice and virtue, yet due to the accumulation of past sins, this suffering has befallen my people. You are a direct master of yoga; please tell me the remedy. I have abandoned my kingdom and come to you for refuge."

Hearing these pleasant words, the great sage Shaunaka said to the King: "Listen, O King, a great sin has occurred in your kingdom, which is why the people are afflicted with disease and infertility. The reason for the great suffering of the famine is that the Chaturthi vow—both the bright and dark fortnights—has been neglected. You must now strive to observe it. If you do not, you will never find happiness even in a thousand years. Therefore, perform this vow, which is famous everywhere."

Having said this, he explained the glory of the vow in detail. The King was astonished to hear it and asked Shaunaka again: "O Brahmin, tell me the supreme, peace-granting form of Ganesha."

Shaunaka said: "O King, I was once performing penance in my hermitage, absorbed in the contemplation of Brahman. Suddenly, the great yogi Bhrigu arrived at my hermitage to bless me. Seeing him, I stood up, bowed, offered him a seat, and worshipped him with devotion. Seeing me humble and standing with folded hands, he said: 'O son, are you established in yoga? Are you at peace? Tell me what you desire, and I shall grant it. You are endowed with virtuous qualities, and I am pleased with you.'

"I bowed to the sage Bhrigu and said: 'O great sage, tell me the yoga that grants peace and fulfillment.' Bhrigu replied: 'Know that Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, is the embodiment of yoga and peace. Worship Him according to the prescribed rites, and you will be at peace. Within the mind, which is fivefold, resides the Chintamani (wish-fulfilling gem). Knowing this, Brahma and others became one with Brahman. Abandon the mind that is deluded by objects and become the Chintamani yourself. Know the mind that is composed of speech and thought, and by abandoning it, be happy.' Having said this, the great yogi Bhrigu gave me the mantra of Ganesha and disappeared.

"O King, instructed by Bhrigu, I worshipped Ganesha with a yogic attitude, focused on restraining the mind. By the grace of the Lord of Ganas, I became worthy of being worshipped by yogis. Even now, I worship Him. Ganesha, who loves His devotees, granted me boons, and I became a leader of the Ganas."

Having said this, the learned Shaunaka gave the King the mantra of Ganesha. The King bowed to Shaunaka, took his leave, and returned home, where he was welcomed by his ministers. Along with the citizens, Soma observed the excellent vow on the Magha Shukla Chaturthi. Because of this, the vow became famous on earth, and people began observing the Chaturthi of both fortnights. Through the merit of this observance, rain fell, and all the people were filled with joy, devoted to worshipping Ganesha.

Soma worshipped Ganesha with love and built a temple for His pleasure. He installed a magnificent idol of Ganesha, known as Varada (the Giver of Boons), and worshipped Him daily with devotion. The King, blessed with children and grandchildren, ruled an excellent kingdom. All the people were immersed in happiness, free from disease and infertility. They were devoted to their duties and worshipped the Lord of Ganas. At the end of his life, Soma, along with his people, attained the abode of Ganesha, and after bowing to Him in Svananda, he became one with Brahman.

I have told you this, O King. Now listen to another beautiful story that destroys all sins. An outcaste named Bhanu lived in the city of Kaundinya in Vidarbha. He was a great sinner, addicted to evil deeds, lustful for other women, and always engaged in gambling and drinking. He would kill people on the road out of greed for money. Being devoid of any good work, he was even addicted to killing Brahmins.

Once, by fate, he wandered into the forest. That day happened to be the Magha Shukla Chaturthi. Having no food or water, he stayed between the mountains. Being hungry and thirsty, he spent the night there. At dawn, he returned home and drank water with great eagerness. Suddenly, due to extreme distress, he vomited that water and died. The attendants of Ganesha took him to the Lord of Ganas. By the mere sight of Ganesha, he became sinless and wise, and by His influence, he attained the state of union with the Lord of Ganas. Even though he was an outcaste, by the merit of this unintentional vow, he became one with Brahman.

Such is the great merit of the Magha Shukla Chaturthi; it is impossible to describe its full glory. Many people have become one with Brahman through this. If anyone hears or reads the glory of the Magha Shukla Chaturthi, O King, it will surely grant them success.

Chapter 13 : Description of the Legend of Phalguna Shukla Chaturthi

Dasharatha asked: O Brahmin, now tell me the glory of the Phalguna month, specifically the merit derived from the Shukla Chaturthi. I am never satisfied with the nectar of these stories.

Vashistha replied: I shall briefly tell you the primary glory of the Chaturthi that occurs in the bright fortnight of the month of Phalguna, which bestows boons. There was a famous city named Malava, adorned with all beauties and filled with people who knew all the dharmas. King Hemangada ruled there excellently; he was a master of weapons, wise, and had conquered the entire earth. He was respected by all kings, possessed radiant sons, and was devoted to Brahmins, gods, and guests.

Then, suddenly, that royal sage was afflicted with a severe pain in his stomach, as if pierced by weapons, and he wept. Everyone tried various remedies to remove the pain, but despite mantras and medicines, the king suffered even more. He served the gods and the learned Brahmins, but the pain only grew more intense. Tormented by this agony, the king, filled with despair, contemplated ending his life.

Just then, the great yogi Parvata arrived at his palace. The suffering king bowed to him. The king worshipped the omniscient sage through Brahmins and, controlling his pain, spoke with sorrow. Hemangada said: My birth, my parents, my lineage, my dharma, my sons, and my wife have all become blessed by the sight of your lotus feet.

The king then fell to the ground in agony and wept bitterly. Seeing this, the sage was astonished and asked the king’s ministers what had caused such severe suffering. The ministers replied: O great sage, he has a severe pain that cannot be cured by medicines, pilgrimages, mantras, or any other efforts.

Hearing their distressing words, Parvata entered into meditation. Realizing the king’s past sins, the great yogi spoke. Parvata said: O King, listen to the grave sin that exists in your kingdom; because of that, you are currently suffering from this intense pain. The main vow of Chaturthi was neglected in your kingdom, and due to that accumulation of faults, you and your subjects are suffering.

Hearing Parvata’s words, the king, with folded hands and humility, asked: O Lord, what is that vow? Who performed it in the past? Whom should one worship? Tell me everything clearly.

Parvata replied: O King, the vow of Ganesha is the best, capable of granting all accomplishments and the four goals of human life. He then explained the glory of Chaturthi in detail. Delighted, the king asked again: What is the form of this Lord of the Ganas? Tell me, so that I may worship that Lord of Lords.

Parvata said: I will tell you the story of Ganesha that I heard from Nidagha, which is capable of granting everything. I was once engaged in severe penance, and my power permeated everything. Indra, becoming agitated and proud, sent Kamadeva along with Apsaras to disturb my penance. But Kamadeva, terrified by the radiance of my penance, fled.

I then became a great yogi, focused inward. Suddenly, Nidagha, who knew the path of the liberated, arrived there. Seeing that perfect yogi, I bowed and worshipped him, saying: My birth and penance are blessed by the sight of your lotus feet, which grant the state of Brahman. Now, O Sage, tell me the yoga that grants peace, so that I may become worthy of being worshipped by yogis.

Vashistha continued: Thus asked, the radiant Nidagha spoke to Parvata with a joyful heart. Nidagha said: Listen to my ancient story. I was once deeply involved in yoga to attain the supreme state of Brahman. At the end, I reached a state of constant bliss. I saw the form of bliss everywhere. My peace was restored, and I went to Lord Vishnu. I bowed to Him and asked for the yoga that grants peace.

Vishnu said: O seeker, know that Ganesha is the one who grants yoga and peace. By knowing Him, all of us yogis have attained peace. Know that the 'Ga' represents the mind and speech, and the 'Na' represents that which is beyond mind and speech. The Lord of both is Ganadisha. Worship Him according to the rites, and you will undoubtedly attain peace.

After saying this, Vishnu became silent. I went to the forest and practiced yoga. After ten years of chanting the single-syllable mantra, the Elephant-faced Lord was pleased and appeared before me. Seeing me in meditation, He asked me to choose a boon. I worshipped Him and recited the hymn from the Sama Veda. Pleased, Ganesha granted me yoga-peace and departed. Since then, I have been a devotee of Ganesha. You too should worship Him.

After saying this, Nidagha gave him the ten-syllable mantra and disappeared. Parvata said: Since then, I have worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with devotion and attained peace. He showed me His yoga-form, and after being worshipped, He returned to His own abode.

After saying this, Parvata gave the king the ten-syllable mantra of Ganesha and disappeared. The king, along with all his people, joyfully observed the vow of Phalguna Shukla Chaturthi. The king announced throughout the kingdom that everyone should observe this vow with devotion. Consequently, the king was freed from his pain, and all the people became free from suffering. They were blessed with sons, grandsons, and wealth.

King Hemangada worshipped Ganesha with devotion for the rest of his life. Eventually, he placed his son on the throne and, with his wife, attained union with Ganesha. Many others also attained the state of Brahman through the power of this vow. Whoever listens to or reads this legend of the Phalguna Shukla Chaturthi will have all their desires fulfilled by Ganesha.

Chapter 14 : Description of the Fourth Day of the Bright Fortnight of Chaitra

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O Yogi, I have heard the primary account of the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Phalguna, but I am not yet satisfied. Therefore, O Guru, out of your kindness, please tell me the story of the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Chaitra, which grants boons and purifies all living beings.

Vashistha said: There was a famous and excellent city named Shobhabhadra in the region of Bangala. There, a king named Chandra-priya ruled with great excellence. Having conquered multitudes of enemies with his weapons and being endowed with the glory of a hero, he brought the entire earth, up to the shores of the ocean, under his control. All the neighboring kings became his subjects and served him. In his kingdom, all the subjects were devoted to their respective duties. The king himself was righteous, protecting those established in their social and spiritual orders (Varnashrama) and always punishing the wicked. There was no fear of thieves for those engaged in their own trades. Yet, despite this, the men and women became afflicted with diseases. People suffering from infertility and other ailments approached the king, who was well-versed in all statecraft, and asked about this great distress.

The citizens said: O King, we are established in our duties and are obedient to your commands. You are also righteous and respectful toward your ministers. Yet, we are severely afflicted by diseases, defects, and especially infertility, and we are devoid of wealth and grain. The earth is devoid of essence, the trees are devoid of fruits, and the cows are devoid of milk in your kingdom, O wise one. When a king is righteous, all people are happy, but the opposite is happening while you are ruling, O great King. Therefore, protect us who are afflicted by diseases and other troubles. You are our only strength and our supreme refuge.

Vashistha said: Hearing their words, Chandra-priya became very distressed. Entrusting his thornless kingdom to his ministers, he decided to go to the forest. There, he met the great Yogi Ashtavakra and bowed to him. After worshipping him and serving him by massaging his feet, the king, filled with sorrow, spoke to the great sage.

Chandra-priya said: My birth, deeds, parents, penance, fame, charity, and knowledge have all become blessed by the sight of your lotus feet, O Yogi. Then, he carefully narrated his entire situation. Hearing this, Ashtavakra spoke to the king.

Ashtavakra said: O King, a great sin is prevailing in your kingdom, and because of that, all your subjects have become sorrowful, there is no doubt. The great vow known as the Chaturthi (the fourth day), which grants the four goals of human life, has been neglected in your kingdom, O base king. Because of that, all men are now devoid of the goals of life, and in the end, they will go to hell along with you, O King. Hearing the words of Ashtavakra, the king bowed to him and spoke words conducive to his own welfare, filled with sorrow.

Chandra-priya said: O Brahmin, ocean of mercy, master, tell me about this excellent vow. What is it like, which deity does it please, and what are its details, O Lord?

Ashtavakra said: The vow of Ganesha, performed on the fourth day of the bright and dark fortnights, is the first and foremost duty to be performed for the attainment of righteousness, desire, and wealth, O King. Having said this, the sage narrated the entire history. Hearing the account of the Chaturthi, the king was amazed. He again asked the sage with humility: What is the form of this Lord of the Ganas, whose vow is so wonderful? Tell me his true nature so that, knowing him in every way, I may worship Ganesha, the giver of all accomplishments, with this vow.

Ashtavakra said: It is not possible for me to describe the form of Ganesha, yet listen, O King, so that you may know the Lord. Long ago, when I was engaged in penance, beautiful celestial nymphs came to distract me. Seeing their persistence, I became angry and cursed them, saying, "Fall into the mortal world and be troubled by thieves." Hearing my words, they were terrified and began to appease me. Then, filled with compassion, I said, "Lord Vishnu will be born among the Yadavas; you will be his wives, and in the end, you will be freed from the thieves." After the nymphs left, I performed penance. By the power of my intense penance, I attained supreme inner knowledge. I abandoned penance and became devoted to self-control. By contemplating various yogas, I attained Samadhi. In this state of supreme bliss, I was amazed to see the illusion in the midst of equality. I suffered greatly for the sake of peace. Then, suddenly, the great Yogi Ribhu arrived there. Seeing him beyond the social and spiritual orders, I was filled with joy. I bowed to the great sage and worshipped him. I said, "My penance, birth, and knowledge are blessed. By the grace of your feet, I am now fulfilled." Hearing my words, the great sage said, "What do you desire, O wise one? Tell me, and I shall do it." Then, bowing to him with folded hands, I said, "O best of Yogis, tell me the way to attain peace."

Ribhu said: The union of the five elements, whether good or bad, is attained through the yoga of self-realization, O sage. The absence of the five elements is attained by people through detachment. When these two are united, one attains the path of peace. Know that Lord of the Ganas and worship him, O wise one. Through him, you will attain the happiness of peace; there is no other way. The letter 'Ga' signifies union, and the letter 'Na' signifies the absence of union. The Lord of both is Ganesha; see this in the Vedas, O wise one.

I was previously deluded and engaged in various yogas, but then I took refuge in the natural state. Seeing the independence of the Lord, I was amazed and took refuge in Shankara (Shiva) with the desire for yoga. Instructed by Shankara, I worshipped the Lord of the Ganas. Through the method of the single-syllable mantra, I attained peace. Having said this, Ribhu gave me the mantra of Ganesha. By chanting that single-syllable mantra, I attained peace.

Ashtavakra, the pure-souled and glorious one, worshipped by yogis, gave the twelve-syllable mantra to the king. Having given the king the royal mantra with all its procedures, he disappeared. The king became devoted to the worship of Vighneshwara. In the month of Chaitra, when the fourth day of the bright fortnight arrived, he performed the vow along with the citizens and his own people. Then, he proclaimed that vow throughout the provinces. All the people performed the vows of the bright and dark fortnights with devotion. Consequently, they became free from diseases and were blessed with children. Free from various defects, they lived happily. Chandra-priya worshipped Ganesha with a single-minded focus. After handing over the kingdom to his son, he went to the forest with his wife. Then, after a short time, the excellent king, along with his wife, went to the abode of Ganesha and became one with the Brahman. Gradually, those on earth who performed the vow, by the power of its merit, also attained the state of Brahman upon seeing Ganapati.

Listen to another account of the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Chaitra, which destroys sins and grants all accomplishments. In Malava, a sinful Shudra lived in the forest. Greedy for wealth, the wicked man killed travelers. I am not capable of counting his sins; I have omitted them to avoid the fault of excessive length. Once, while he was in the forest, armed and waiting in a tree to deceive and kill people, a snake bit him. Terrified, he returned to his home. Then, O King, he became unconscious, afflicted by the poison. By divine coincidence, it was the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Chaitra. The Shudra was suffering and devoid of food. On the fifth day, the sinner died and went to the abode of Ganesha, by the power of the merit of the vow, even though he performed it unknowingly, O prince.

In this way, many people attained the state of Brahman, O King. It is not possible for me to describe their stories. Whoever listens to this greatness of the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Chaitra, or reads it, attains all desired objects.

Thus ends the fourteenth chapter of the Gajanana-charita in the fourth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the essence of the Upanishads.

Chapter 15 : Description of the Glory of the Fourth Day of the Bright Fortnight of Vaishakha

Dasharatha said: Now, O Sage, please describe the glory of the fourth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Vaishakha. I am never satisfied with the nectar of these stories, which bestow auspiciousness and perfection.

Vashistha said: In the land of Gujarat, there was a magnificent and prosperous city named Bhadraka. A great king named Brahmapriya ruled there. He was well-versed in all scriptures, a performer of sacrifices, always fond of charity, devoted to gods, Brahmins, and guests, and an expert in weaponry. Having brought all other kings under his control, he became the lord of the earth, powerful, highly respected, and wise.

His wife died suddenly just as she attained puberty. The king married a second wife of similar character, but she also died in the same manner. In this way, five of his wives died, and King Brahmapriya became deeply distressed. Entrusting his kingdom to his ministers, the royal sage went to the forest. He bowed before his guru, Shvetaketu, and stood before him. Shvetaketu welcomed his disciple with joy, honoring him with a seat and inquiring about his well-being. After feeding the king, he asked the reason for his arrival, saying, "O tiger among kings, why have you come to my hermitage alone, abandoning your kingdom? Tell me the main reason, and I shall fulfill it with respect."

Having been asked, the royal sage bowed with folded hands and narrated the entire story of the loss of his wives. Hearing this, the great ascetic Shvetaketu, who was a devotee of Ganesha, contemplated the matter and spoke with a touch of anger.

Shvetaketu said: O base king, O great sinner, listen to my beneficial words. You have neglected the primary vow of the fourth day (Chaturthi) in your kingdom. All actions of one who is devoid of the Chaturthi vow are fruitless, and there is no doubt that such a person goes to hell in the end. You are deprived of the four goals of human life (Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha). The wise have called this day 'Chaturthi' because it grants the four objects of human desire.

Hearing the angry words of Shvetaketu, the king bowed, feeling ashamed and sorrowful. Brahmapriya said: O Brahmin, whatever was done in ignorance regarding the vow, please forgive me, O ocean of compassion. Tell me, O Lord, what is the supreme vow that grants all desires, and whose worship should be performed according to the prescribed rites?

The glorious Shvetaketu then told him the greatness of the vow. Upon hearing it, the king asked, "Who is this Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) whose vow is so wonderful? Tell me his form, and I shall worship him with devotion."

Shvetaketu said: Listen, O King, I will tell you a story from the past. Through my own experience, you will understand the greatness of Ganesha. Long ago, I was a great ascetic, devoted to my father Uddalaka. Seeing me engaged in severe penance, my father, who was a source of peace and great fame, spoke to me with affection: "O son, for the sake of peace, abandon this penance and strive to realize that 'I am Brahman'."

I asked my father to explain the knowledge that grants peace, the nature of the mind, and the 'Chintamani' (wish-fulfilling gem) within it. The great yogi Uddalaka, filled with joy, explained: "Listen, O fortunate son. The mind is said to be of five types: distracted, dull, wandering, focused, and restrained. The Chintamani, which is the source of illumination, resides in the heart. It is attained by yogis through the destruction of the 'land' (the states of mind) via yoga. The mind itself is the intellect, and it is the cause of illusion. The success of Ganesha is called the play of Maya. Therefore, worship Ganesha with his mantra. Through that, you will attain the state of Brahman."

Saying this, Uddalaka gave the single-syllable mantra of Ganesha to his son. Through that, I attained Ganesha, the giver of all success, and became a sage honored by other yogis. Therefore, O King, you too must worship the Lord of the Ganas. By doing so, you will attain eternal happiness in this world and the next, and you will undoubtedly become one with Brahman.

Having received the thirty-two-letter mantra, the king returned home. When the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Vaishakha arrived, he celebrated it with the citizens. The vow was observed everywhere. The king, along with his wife, worshipped Ganesha. He installed his son on the throne and retired. He chanted the mantra, meditated on Ganesha, and at the end of his life, he became one with Brahman. His name 'Brahmapriya' became meaningful. In his kingdom, all the people, through the merit of this vow, gradually attained the state of Brahman in the city of Svanandaka.

Uddalaka and Shvetaketu became famous as great masters. Shvetaketu also established a moral code for the benefit of all. Once, when Aruni (Uddalaka) was in his hermitage with his wife, a Brahmin well-versed in all scriptures arrived. Driven by lust, he asked Aruni's wife to engage in intercourse with him. Seeing this, Aruni told his son Shvetaketu not to be angry, as women are considered free (in their nature). Hearing this, Shvetaketu, filled with rage, replied, "If that is the case, why does a woman choose only one husband? This is not a permanent law created by the Creator. I shall establish a moral boundary: from today, any man who touches another's wife out of lust shall incur the sin of killing a woman. Likewise, any woman who abandons her husband for another man shall incur the sin of killing her husband."

Since that day, women have served their husbands with devotion, established by the grace of Ganesha. I have told you this story of Uddalaka's conduct. Now, hear another glory of the Chaturthi. In Maharashtra, there was a Brahmin who was addicted to other women. He drank liquor, stole wealth, committed violence, and lived a sinful life. Once, driven by lust, he went to a Shudra's house, gave him money, and enjoyed himself. After some time, the Shudra returned and killed the Brahmin with a weapon. That day happened to be the Chaturthi. Suffering from the wounds, and without food, he died on the fifth day. Yet, due to the power of the Vaishakha-Shukla-Chaturthi vow, he attained the world of Svanandaka and became one with Brahman.

If even such a great sinner attained the state of Brahman through the merit of a vow performed in ignorance, what more can be said of the wise? Many people, by observing this vow knowingly or unknowingly, have become one with Brahman. Whoever listens to or reads this glory of the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Vaishakha will attain their desired fruits.

Chapter 16 : The Glory of the Shukla Chaturthi in the Month of Jyeshtha

Dasharatha said: O Yogi, I have heard about the excellent fruits of the Shukla Chaturthi in the month of Vaishakha. Now, please tell me about the Shukla Chaturthi of the month of Jyeshtha.

Vashistha said: O King, in the Andhra region, in a city named Sheshapura, there lived a king named Kardama. He was highly skilled in weaponry and devoted to various righteous duties. His kingdom extended to the shores of the ocean, and he was unparalleled in brilliance, policy, and fame. By the decree of fate, he was afflicted with a severe form of diabetes. Driven by past sins, his body burned as if consumed by fire. Suffering intensely, the king could not urinate and wept constantly due to the unbearable burning sensation. He tried many remedies, but the disease did not subside. Eventually, he renounced his kingdom and went to the forest with his wife.

Wandering from forest to forest, in a terrifying place filled with lions and tigers, he wept, awaiting his death. There, the highly illustrious sage Bharadwaja arrived. Seeing him, the king and his wife rose quickly and bowed. He stood before the sage with folded hands, but overcome by the intense burning, he collapsed and wept. Seeing the king in such a state, the great Yogi, after meditating, spoke with compassion.

Bharadwaja said: O King Kardama, listen to my beneficial words, by which you will become free from suffering. The Chaturthi vow has been neglected in your kingdom, which is why you, a lowly king, have become filled with sin. Therefore, perform this vow along with your subjects, O wise one. Through that merit, you will be free from sorrow.

Hearing Bharadwaja’s words, Kardama, filled with joy, asked him how to perform the vow. Kardama said: O Bhagavan, knower of all truths, you have shown compassion upon me. Please tell me the glory of this vow so that I may perform it.

Bharadwaja said: The Sankashta (on the Krishna Chaturthi) and the Varada (on the Shukla Chaturthi) are vows that grant all desires and are considered the fulfillers of the four goals of human life (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha). A person must perform this first, O King, to attain the fruits of these four goals. Since you have been devoid of these, you would surely have fallen into hell; therefore, make an effort for this.

Having spoken thus, the sage narrated the glory of the Chaturthi to the king, who listened with great delight. Kardama then asked the sage again: O omniscient one, please tell me the true nature of Ganesha.

Bharadwaja said: O King, even the Vedas are incapable of fully describing the nature of Ganesha, yet listen to the essence that grants happiness. Long ago, I performed great penance, and the entire universe came under my control. Yet, I sought to perform even more intense penance. Through the merit of that, I attained inner knowledge. I abandoned penance and became indifferent to the world, living like one who is dull or mad for the sake of Yoga. Gradually, I became absorbed in the bliss of the Self and attained peace. From that state, I perceived the differentiation of the world, which disturbed my peace.

Just then, the great Yogi Raivata arrived at my hermitage. Seeing him, I bowed and worshipped him. I asked the great sage, "Today, I am fulfilled by your sight. Please teach me the peace of Yoga, as you are the guru of all Yogis."

Raivata said: The bliss of the Self is described as fivefold. The union of the four leads to the true nature of the Self. The lack of union with one's true nature is considered the cause of all suffering. The union of these, O Bharadwaja, grants the peace of Yoga. The syllable 'Ga' represents the Self-realized, and 'Na' represents Yoga. The Lord of these is Ganesha, the master of the Vedas. Worship Him, O fortunate one, and you will attain peace; otherwise, you will wander in delusion for a hundred years.

Having heard this great knowledge from Vishnu himself, I attained peace and became a devotee of Ganesha. Saying this, he gave me the single-syllable mantra of Ganesha along with the prescribed rituals. After worshipping Raivata, he departed. I worshipped Ganesha with a focused mind and attained peace. After ten years, Ganesha himself appeared before me. I worshipped Him and praised Him with the eight-name hymn. He made me a Gana and became absorbed in the bliss of the Self. I worship that Ganesha with great devotion.

Having said this, the sage gave the eight-syllable mantra of Ganesha to Kardama. Then, the great sage Bharadwaja disappeared. Kardama went home and worshipped Ganesha with joy. In the month of Jyeshtha, when the Shukla Chaturthi arrived, he performed the vow along with the citizens. He declared that anyone who does not perform the Varada vow on the Shukla Chaturthi and the Sankashta on the Krishna Chaturthi should be banished from the city. Thus, the excellent vow was spread everywhere, and all the people on earth performed it.

The king was freed from the suffering of diabetes. He gave his kingdom to his son and lived in a garden with his wife, worshipping Ganesha with all his heart, eventually becoming one with Him. In his kingdom, all the people, through the power of the merit of this vow, attained the state of Brahman.

O Dasharatha, listen to another story that grants success. In the Gauda region, there lived a sinful man of low birth. He would go to the forest to rob people and committed many sins. Once, he tried to kill a Brahmana, who fled in terror. Hearing his cries, five men arrived and killed the sinner with weapons. It happened to be the Jyeshtha Shukla Chaturthi that day. By divine coincidence, he was fasting, and he died on the fifth day. Through the merit of this unintentional vow, he attained the state of Brahman. If such a result can come to a Chandala, what more for the wise?

Thus, O King, through the power of this vow, people enjoy all pleasures here and attain the bliss of the Self in the end. The glory is infinite, and those who listen to or read this glory of the Jyeshtha Chaturthi will have all their desires fulfilled.

Chapter 17 : Story of the Shukla Chaturthi of Ashadha

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O Brahmana, please narrate the great story of the Shukla Chaturthi of Ashadha. I am never satisfied hearing the stories that grant supreme success.

Vashistha said: O King, in the region of Mithila, there was a great city named Gandaki. There, a king named Bhadrasena, possessing immense radiance, ruled. He was highly skilled in weaponry, a conqueror of enemy nations, and having conquered the entire earth surrounded by the ocean, he ruled with righteousness, policy, and justice. He was a performer of sacrifices, humble, honorable, and loved by Brahmanas, gods, and guests. All kings were submissive to him and served him; those who were not, paid him tribute.

In his kingdom, O King, parrots, locusts, and mice in countless numbers forcibly devoured the grains and clothes. To destroy them, the valiant Bhadrasena tried hard and killed many with his weapons. Even after burning them with fire-weapons, the King was astonished to see them reappear. They roamed everywhere in the houses. Unable to control them, he went to the forest, feeling very distressed. The King observed fasting and performed excellent penance. Remembering Shiva, he worshipped and praised Him with devotion.

After a year, the great yogi Bakadalbhya arrived. By divine providence, while wandering, he saw the King. The King worshipped him, fed him, and with a distressed heart, asked the yogi.

Bhadrasena said: O Master, in my kingdom, mice, locusts, and parrots are tormenting the people and me, engaged in eating everything. The remedies suggested by the Brahmanas have all failed. Due to this distress, I have abandoned my kingdom and am living in the forest. While worshipping Shankara here, I have attained your audience by the power of my penance. Please tell me the remedy, O Lord.

Vashistha said: Hearing the words of Bhadrasena, the glorious devotee of Ganesha, Bakadalbhya, spoke to the King as if to gladden him.

Bakadalbhya said: O best of kings, I have been sent here by Shiva for your sake. Listen to my supreme words that destroy great distress. O Bhadrasena, the vow of Chaturthi has been neglected in your kingdom; therefore, you are currently afflicted with obstacles. The subjects are terrified because the ruler is wicked. O sinner, because you are the ruler, you are heading to hell. Therefore, O Lord, this vow must be performed by all as a daily duty; it is the bestower of the four goals of life and the remover of obstacles. If it is not performed, all other actions are futile, as they lack the four goals of life. Consider this, O wise one.

After saying this, Bakadalbhya narrated the greatness of Chaturthi to the King. Hearing it, the King spoke.

Bhadrasena said: What is this Lord of the Ganas like? Tell me His form. Knowing Him, the Lord of all gods, I will worship Him specifically.

Bakadalbhya said: Listen, O King, to my past actions. By that, you will become skilled in the knowledge of the Lord of the Ganas. Formerly, I was engaged in penance and lived only on air. The entire moving and non-moving world was filled with my radiance and was terrified. Yet, O King, due to my penance, I had the wonderful vision of the Universal Form, which is all-pervading. Because of that knowledge and yoga, I abandoned penance and became absorbed in a state of being like a dullard or a madman. Practicing self-control and restraint, I sought the yoga that grants complete happiness, always intent on controlling my mind.

In this way, O King, I became established in the natural Brahman, and being in a Brahman-like state, I was joyful. Then, due to great delusion, I saw a void that seemed to be under my own control. I became confused, wondering how such a thing could exist in Brahman. Then I went to Shambhu for refuge and praised Him with hymns. Seeing the pleased Shankara, I asked, "O Lord, tell me about this yoga."

Shiva said: Know that the real and the unreal are both associated with the natural state, and they arise from self-bliss through the knowledge of the Self. In union, one's own true nature should be known through the Vedas. In disunion, all connections are destroyed; there is no doubt about this. Union and yoga—the union of these two, O wise one, is called the giver of yogic peace by yogis through the practice of yoga. Know Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, to be the embodiment of yogic peace. Worship Him according to the prescribed rites, and you will become one with Brahman. The syllable 'Ga' stands for union, and 'Na' stands for yoga. The master of these two is the Lord of the Ganas; see this specifically in the Vedas.

Having said this, the Great God gave me the mantra. It was the single-syllable mantra of Ganesha, which is auspicious and grants success. After bowing to Shankara, O King, I went to the forest. I worshipped the Lord of the Ganas, meditating and chanting. By His grace, I attained peace. Even then, I remained devoted to His worship, meditating on Him in my heart. Then He showed me His form adorned with a trunk. Seeing Him, I bowed and praised Him with joy. After granting me devotion, the Lord of the Ganas disappeared. Since then, O fortunate one, I have been a devotee of Ganesha with reverence.

Having said this, the sage gave him the five-syllable mantra and disappeared. Seeing Bakadalbhya gone, the King was filled with joy and returned to his city to rule righteously. Then, along with the citizens, he performed the excellent vow of the Shukla Chaturthi of Ashadha with great devotion. From then on, that vow was performed everywhere. People performed the vow of the Shukla and Krishna Chaturthi. The parrots, mice, and locusts were destroyed. The people, endowed with wealth and grain, attained prosperity and joy. Abandoning various sorrows, they were filled with bliss, and in the end, they all attained the Supreme Bliss and became one with Brahman. After crowning his son, the King went to the forest with his wife. There, he worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with undivided devotion. In the end, attaining the Supreme Bliss, he became one with Brahman by the grace of this vow.

Listen to another story born of this vow that increases merit, which grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, and is dear to the Varada (Ganesha) in Ashadha. In the land of Bengal, there was a merchant who was a sinner. Abandoning his own duty, he became engaged in forbidden acts. He indulged in gambling, drinking, and violence daily. He forcibly took women who were not willing. Knowing his wicked deeds, his father rebuked him. Disturbed by this repeatedly, the wicked man gave poison to his father. Afflicted by the poison, he died, and the son cremated him. Then he went to his mother and forcibly took her wealth. All the people, knowing his wicked and painful actions, were distressed. The noble people went to the King and told him everything. The King, enraged, had him brought there. By the King's order, the wicked man was impaled on a stake. By divine providence, that day was the Shukla Chaturthi of Ashadha. He remained there without water and food. On the fifth day, the wicked-minded one died in pain. By the merit of the vow performed unknowingly, he attained the Supreme Bliss. O King, by the power of the vow, even a great sinner, upon seeing Ganesha in the Supreme Bliss, became one with Brahman.

Thus, by the power of the vow, people attained Brahman. How can one ever count them? Even in ten thousand years, no yogi could count them all. If this Chaturthi is performed with knowledge, what is the wonder if the Varada grants him Brahman? Whoever reads or hears this story of the Ashadha Chaturthi will attain his desired fruits.

Thus ends the seventeenth chapter of the Gajanana Charita in the fourth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the essence of the Upanishads.

Chapter 18 : The Narrative of the Shravana Shukla Chaturthi Vrata

Dasharatha said: O Lord, please describe to me in detail the greatness of the Varada Chaturthi that falls in the month of Shravana. I am never satisfied hearing about the greatness of this Vrata, which bestows the state of Brahman.

Vashistha said: In the land of Anga, there was a prosperous city named Shatatara. A king named Ashvasena ruled there. He was righteous, skilled in statecraft, devoted to charity, and always eager to serve gods, Brahmins, and guests. He was a mighty warrior who had conquered the entire earth with his weapons and was renowned as a universal monarch.

Once, while he was ruling, a severe and burning fever suddenly afflicted him. Despite trying various remedies, he could not find relief. After a year had passed, he was reduced to mere skin and bones. Suffering intensely, the king lamented and, in his despair, even contemplated suicide by poison. Just then, the great yogi Devala arrived. Seeing him, the king bowed and worshipped him.

Devala said: O King, you are suffering from this fever and contemplating death because you neglected the Chaturthi Vrata, which is the source of the four goals of life (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha). Any action performed without observing this Vrata is fruitless.

The king asked: O great sage, please tell me the greatness of Lord Ganesha.

Devala replied: It is impossible for me to describe the full glory of Ganesha, but I will tell you what I learned from my father, Asita. Once, I was performing penance when the great yogi Jaigishavya visited my ashram. I observed his ways, but he remained silent and detached. Later, I traveled to the ocean and the heavens, and everywhere I went, I saw Jaigishavya already present, being worshipped by the gods. When I finally asked him how to attain liberation, he advised me to renounce violence, practice self-control, and worship the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha).

My father, Asita, further explained that the syllable 'Ga' represents union and 'Na' represents detachment; the union of these two is the Lord of the Ganas. He gave me a single-syllable mantra, and by worshipping Ganesha, I attained peace and the state of Brahman.

Following Devala’s instructions and receiving the ten-syllable mantra, King Ashvasena observed the Shravana Shukla Chaturthi Vrata with great devotion. By the grace of Ganesha, he was cured of his fever. The people of his kingdom also became free from diseases. The king eventually handed over his kingdom to his son and spent his life in devotion to Ganesha, ultimately attaining the supreme abode of Brahman.

Devala then narrated the story of a sinful Kshatriya who, despite a life of extreme wickedness, accidentally observed the Shravana Chaturthi Vrata due to his illness. Even that unintentional observance of the Vrata granted him liberation and a place in the abode of Ganesha.

Thus, this Vrata is the provider of all accomplishments. Anyone who reads or hears this greatness of the Varada Chaturthi of Shravana attains all the fruits of their desires.

Chapter 19 : Description of the Story of the Shukla Chaturthi during the Malamaasa

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Dasharatha asked: O Brahmin, please tell me about the Chaturthi that occurs during the Malamaasa (intercalary month). Describe the story of the Varada Chaturthi, which grants all accomplishments.

Vashistha replied: I will narrate an ancient history to you. Hearing the glory of this Chaturthi grants everything one desires. In the Andhra region, there was a king named Sushena who ruled excellently from the city of Champaka. He was a master of weaponry, righteous, famous, charitable, and devoted to gods, Brahmins, and guests. He performed many sacrifices and ruled the entire earth according to Dharma, with all other kings paying him tribute.

However, in his kingdom, giant serpents began devouring the people. These ferocious snakes roamed everywhere, causing such terror that people could not travel, nor could they stay safely in their homes or forests. The king was deeply distressed and fearful. Although the people performed rituals for Nagapanchami to appease the serpents, the snakes did not calm down. Despite visiting many holy places, the cruelty of the serpents only increased.

The king then sought refuge with his teacher, Jaimini. He bowed before the sage with folded hands. The sage welcomed him and asked why he had come. Sushena sighed and said: "O Lord, the serpents are tormenting my people, and we cannot live in peace. I have tried many remedies, but none have worked. Please tell me how to appease these serpents, or I shall give up my life here."

Jaimini replied: "O King, listen carefully. Because you neglected the Chaturthi vow in your kingdom, you and your subjects are suffering, and you will fall into hell. The Chaturthi is known as Varada because it grants the four goals of life, and as Sankashti because it destroys all troubles. If this vow is not performed, all other actions are meaningless. You must perform this vow regularly."

Sushena asked: "Who is this Lord Ganesha, whose vow is so miraculous? Tell me his form so that I may worship him."

Jaimini said: "O King, his true form cannot be described by anyone. I will tell you about the Dundhi (Ganesha) as I heard it from Vyasa and Lord Narayana himself. Everything is composed of the body and the soul. The letter 'Ga' represents the body, and 'Na' represents the Brahman (the soul). Ganesha is the master of both. Because he resides in the heart, he is called Chintamani. Buddhi (intellect) is his consort, and when they are united, one attains perfection. He is the lord of both."

Jaimini then initiated Sushena into the six-syllable mantra. After receiving the initiation, the king returned to his city. When the Malamaasa arrived, Sushena performed the Varada Chaturthi vow in the bright fortnight with great devotion. The citizens, following his example, also performed the vow according to the scriptures. By the merit of this vow, the serpents disappeared, and the people were freed from diseases and suffering. They lived happily with their families and wealth.

The king worshipped Ganesha daily with the six-syllable mantra. Eventually, he handed the kingdom to his son and retired to the forest with his wife. There, he continued to worship Ganesha and, in the end, attained the supreme abode of Ganesha. All the people who followed this vow in the bright and dark fortnights also attained liberation. There is nothing difficult to achieve through the power of this vow.

Vashistha continued: "O King, listen to another story from the Malamaasa. A cruel Kshatriya from Hastinapur, who had been a sinner since childhood, killed his own father for money and was addicted to theft and other people's wives. One day, while in the forest, he was bitten by a serpent. As he lay dying, he accidentally performed the Chaturthi vow of the bright fortnight of the Malamaasa without even knowing the proper procedure. He died on the fifth day, but because of the merit of that vow, he attained the abode of Ganesha.

Whoever hears or reads this glory of the Chaturthi of the bright fortnight in the Malamaasa attains all desires.

Thus ends the nineteenth chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the story of the Shukla Chaturthi of the Malamaasa.

Chapter 20 : The Glory of the Magha Krishna Chaturthi

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: Having heard the supreme and wish-fulfilling glory of the Giver of Boons, I feel completely fulfilled; there is no doubt about it. There is no other vow as supreme and capable of granting all desires as this one. Blessed are those men, O Brahmin, who perform the Chaturthi vow. Now, please describe the glory of the Sankashta (Chaturthi) in detail. I am not satisfied; please narrate this nectar-like story in full.

Mudgala said: Being asked thus, the great yogi Vashistha spoke to him. Listen, O King, to that which brings joy to the ears.

Vashistha said: Knowing your disposition, I am satisfied, O best of kings. You are virtuous, which is why you have devotion to Ganesha. It is not through little merit that one develops love for the story of the great Ganesha; you are indeed blessed, there is no doubt. Listen, O fortunate one, to the supreme story related to Chaturthi, which is highly meritorious in the dark fortnight and bestows all accomplishments. I shall tell you an ancient history, the hearing and reading of which grants great merit.

In the city of Sahasra, there lived a king named Shurasena, who was surrounded by his subjects and enjoyed music in his court. Suddenly, a chariot shining like fire fell there. Seeing this great wonder, he sent messengers to investigate. Informed by the messengers, the king, eager to see the chariot, went to the outskirts of the city surrounded by his citizens. Seeing Indra there with the gods, he was struck with wonder. He approached, prostrated on the ground like a rod, and then rising slowly with folded hands, spoke with devotion.

Shurasena said: Blessed is my birth, my age, my charity, my parents, and my city. I am fulfilled, O King of Gods, by your sight along with your attendants. Tell me, O Lord, where you are going or from where you have come. Why has your chariot fallen to the earth? I do not know by what merit I have been granted the sight of you and the assembly of gods. Tell me your command; what shall I do? Make an effort to move the chariot, O Lord of Gods. Tell me by what sin your vehicle has fallen.

Being asked thus, the glorious Mahendra spoke to the humble and devoted king with joy.

Indra said: A great sinner, a fisherman, worshipped Vighnesha, having been instructed by Mudgala, and became devoted to the name-mantra. Through that merit, after a thousand years, a trunk emerged from between his eyebrows, and he became the sage Bhrushundi. By his mere sight, one is freed from rebirth. I have come here to worship him. Because of the sight of this sinful leper Vaishya (merchant), who is your subject, O King, the chariot has fallen to the earth. If you, O King, perform the Sankashta Chaturthi vow of the dark fortnight with devotion and offer its merit to him, then this chariot will surely move. There is no other way for the chariot to move.

In the meantime, another wonderful chariot arrived, accompanied by the messengers of Ganesha. In that same city, there was a sinful Chandali (outcaste woman) who was blind, leprous, and infested with worms. Covered in pus and blood, she lived by begging to fill her stomach. On the night of the third day of the dark fortnight of Pausha, she drank liquor and fell asleep. Due to the intoxication, she slept through the night, and when the moon rose on the Chaturthi, she woke up. Driven by hunger, she wandered for alms and ate whatever was given to her. She died on the fifth day. The Ganesha-dutas (messengers) took her to Svananda (Ganesha's abode) because she had performed the vow. As she was being taken, the wind touching her body, which had become pure due to the vow, touched Indra's chariot. The wind, having touched the body of the vow-performer, moved the divine chariot of Indra. Seeing this, all the gods and sages were amazed. Knowing the reason through their divine vision, they explained it to the king.

After Indra returned to heaven, the king went to his city and, filled with wonder, brought me (Vashistha) there. I instructed him in the proper method of the vow, and he performed it along with the Angaraka (Tuesday) Chaturthi as per the scriptures. Having performed the supreme vow on the Magha Krishna Chaturthi and worshipping Ganesha again on the fifth day, he stood ready. At that moment, a great chariot accompanied by Ganesha’s messengers arrived in the city of Shurasena. The messengers descended and told the king to come to the Lord of Ganas due to the power of the vow. Hearing the messengers, the king was amazed and, after bowing and worshipping them, spoke with joy.

Shurasena said: From my birth until today, I have enjoyed all kinds of good and bad things with my citizens. How can I leave them and go to Gajanana?

The Ganesha-dutas said: O King, go to the Lord of Obstacles along with your citizens. Through the power of this vow, you are capable of uplifting the whole world.

Then, the overjoyed king, along with the people of the four castes, boarded the chariot. The Ganesha-dutas moved the chariot, but it did not budge; it remained still. Realizing that the leper Vaishya had to be left behind, the Ganesha-dutas arrived. The king, distressed, bowed to them and spoke.

Shurasena said: This Vaishya is a great sinner, due to whose sight the chariot of the Lord of Gods fell. Still, listen, O dear ones. What sin has he committed? Tell me, I will perform penance and go with him.

The Ganesha-dutas said: Listen, O King. This Vaishya was a Brahmin in his previous life named Budha, a great sinner. He beat his chaste mother and his father, a learned ascetic, and killed his wife. He committed many such terrible sins and suffered in hell. Having exhausted his karma, he was born as this leper and continues to commit terrible sins. O Shurasena, leave him and go to Gajanana. Touching him requires a full bath.

Hearing the messengers, the king trembled and, bowing to them, cried out in distress.

Shurasena said: There is no count of his sins, and there is no penance. What should I do now, O Ganas? Please tell me the supreme penance for all sins; I will perform it until my death.

The Ganesha-dutas said: O wise one, whisper "Gajanana" into the ear of this lowest of men. It is the essence born of the four Vedas, uttered by Brahma, and sung by the four-faced one.

Hearing their words, Shurasena, filled with joy, respectfully whispered "Gajanana" into the ear of the Vaishya. Upon hearing it, the Vaishya was freed from sin, his body became golden, and he was cured of leprosy. Then, the king, along with all his citizens, went to Vighnesha with devotion. Seeing Ganesha in Svananda, they became one with the Divine. The king also attained the state of a Gana.

Thus, the king attained the state of Brahman through the power of the merit of the vow. Who can describe the glory of this vow? The entire world is considered to be in distress, but by performing this vow, a man becomes one with Brahman. Whoever hears or reads this glory of the Sankashta Chaturthi of Magha attains all supreme goals.

Thus ends the twentieth chapter of the Gajanana Charita in the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 21 : Phalguna Krishna Chaturthi

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O Brahmin, having heard about the greatness of the Magha Sankashti, which grants all boons, is holy, and removes all troubles, I am filled with wonder. Please tell me the story of the Sankashti Chaturthi that falls in the dark fortnight of the month of Phalguna, O ocean of compassion.

Vashistha said: The demon Ravana, born in the lineage of Pulastya, performed severe penance and, having obtained a boon, ruled over the three worlds with great might. Entrusting the kingdom to his ministers, the best of demons went to a secluded place and became absorbed in meditation. Being a scholar of the Vedas and Upanishads and well-versed in the essence of Vedanta, he meditated, focusing on the path of knowledge. As much time passed, he did not attain the supreme knowledge. Filled with sorrow, he remained there, remembering Shankara.

Then, Lord Shiva sent the sage Narada to that very place. Seeing Narada, the demon bowed to him with folded hands. Filled with sorrow, he asked Narada, the best of yogis: O Master, please tell me something that grants knowledge, out of your compassion. I have been meditating with devotion for the sake of knowledge, but I have not attained that great wisdom. What should I do, O great sage?

Narada said: O best of demons, I have been sent by Shiva for your sake. Listen to these words that grant complete knowledge and follow them with devotion. The wise consider the four-fold world to be a source of distress. To destroy this, perform the vow known as Sankashti. Having said this, Narada told him the greatness of the Chaturthi. Hearing this, Ravana asked him with great joy.

Ravana said: O Lord, what is the nature of this Lord of Ganas, and what is the knowledge regarding Him, whose vow grants the four goals of life? If one does not perform it, it surely leads to destruction. Please tell me, O Brahmin, about this vow which is the foremost and grants all accomplishments.

Asked thus, the great yogi Narada, filled with great joy, spoke to Ravana, who terrified the world. Narada said: It is not possible to describe the great knowledge of Ganesha fully. I will tell you through a definition, O king of demons, listen. The word 'Gana' means a group, and groups are the indicators of Brahman. This group arises from the union of the external and internal. The letter 'Ga' represents the Brahman that is the body and the embodied soul. The letter 'Na' is considered to be the form of union and separation. In the union of these two, Ganesha is the Lord accepted everywhere. Worship Him according to the prescribed rites, and you will attain peace.

Having said this, the great yogi gave the ten-syllabled mantra of Ganesha to Ravana and explained the procedure. He said: If you abandon this, O king of demons, you will face a downfall. Therefore, O demon, never abandon the Ganesha mantra. Having said this, the radiant Narada disappeared.

O King, the Phalguna Sankashti arrived for Ravana. He performed it with joy and according to the prescribed rites. By the power of the merit of the vow, he attained immediate enlightenment. The king of demons then observed the vows of both the bright and dark fortnights. He educated the people and remained devoted to the Lord of Ganas along with them. Thus, the noble Ravana attained knowledge and went to his city, ruling the kingdom with pride.

However, due to the association with the wicked, his supreme knowledge gradually faded. That wicked one became addicted to consuming meat and women. He thought, "I am the form of Ganesha, there is no one else in the world. Who is there to enjoy the fruits of sin or merit? Whose worship should I perform?" Due to the arrogance of knowledge, he abandoned the mantra that grants happiness, abandoned this holy vow, and especially abandoned the worship. His knowledge was destroyed, his stability was lost, and the demon became a true demon. He became deeply engaged in the destruction of Dharma and violated all duties. Due to these very sins, he was killed instantly by Rama, along with his kinsmen and demons, O wise son of mine.

Dasharatha said: O Lord of Brahmins, I am barren, and Ravana is active. What is this confusing and contradictory thing you are saying, Master?

Vashistha said: In every Kalpa, that Lord Rama was your son. The hero killed Ravana, empowered by the strength of Ganesha. Now listen to another story regarding the Phalguna Sankashti, which destroys all sins and grants both worldly enjoyment and liberation.

In Maharashtra, there was a Brahmin who became a sinner. Abandoning his Brahminhood, he became attached to a Chandala woman. He engaged in relations with Chandalas, had children, and became addicted to meat and liquor. Once, while in the forest, he prepared to kill people, being greedy for wealth and lustful for other women. At that time, the Phalguna Krishna Chaturthi arrived. The fallen Brahmin was staying in a mountain valley. A king arrived there with his four-fold army. Fearing the king, the Brahmin stayed there without food or water. The king's massive army was passing through the path, and by the end of the day, they had all passed.

Then, looking outside, the wicked and hungry man came out, fearless, and went to his house at moonrise. He ate food with his children and slept at night in his house. There, he was bitten by a snake and died, O King. Then, the messengers of Ganesha took him to the city of Shivaloka (Svananda). Seeing the Lord of Obstacles there, he became one with Brahman.

O King, through the merit of the vow performed even without knowledge or proper procedure, he was liberated from the four-fold world; what then to say of those who are truly wise? Such great souls, whether following the rules or not, have become one with Brahman by the power of the merit of this vow. How many such stories can I tell you? Even ten thousand years would not be enough. O King, I have described the greatness in brief. Whoever listens to or reads the greatness of this Phalguna Krishna Chaturthi attains everything.

Thus ends the twenty-first chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of Phalguna Krishna Chaturthi."

Chapter 22 : Description of the Chaitra Krishna Chaturthi Legend

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O Great Sage, please tell me about the Chaitra Krishna Chaturthi. I am never satisfied hearing the auspicious stories of Ganesha.

Vashistha said: I will tell you an ancient history, which is full of the glory of this Chaturthi and grants all accomplishments. In the land of Kalinga, there was an excellent king named Ugrasena, who was devoted to righteousness, performed sacrifices, was charitable, and strictly followed his vows. He was skilled in weaponry, wise, truthful, and followed the path of policy. After defeating all other kings, he ruled his kingdom excellently.

O Best of Kings, in his kingdom, all people lived happily. However, suddenly, a large number of powerful tigers arrived in his city. Ugrasena tried to kill them with his weapons, but they could not be subdued, as if they were sent by destiny. The people, tormented by the tigers, began to blame the king, saying he was a sinner and the cause of their suffering. When a king is righteous, his subjects are happy; but what should the subjects do when the king is wicked? Hearing these complaints from his ministers, the king was filled with sorrow. He abandoned his kingdom and went to a dense forest. There, in solitude, he performed severe penance. He meditated on the Sun God and pleased him daily. After a year of fasting, the king was reduced to skin and bones, yet he continued his penance.

Then, the Sun God appeared before him in the form of a Brahmin. The king bowed to the Brahmin and honored him with fruits. After eating, the Brahmin asked the king why he was wasting his body with such penance. Ugrasena, with folded hands and sighing repeatedly, replied: "I always ruled with justice and righteousness, yet cruel tigers are devouring my people. I have tried many ways to stop them, but failed. That is why I am performing this penance."

Hearing the king, the Sun God, in the form of the Brahmin, rebuked him: "O King, you are a great sinner due to your past deeds. You neglected the primary vow of Chaturthi in your kingdom. This vow grants all accomplishments and should be performed first; only then do one's actions bear fruit. It is the best of all vows, granting the four goals of life. Because of this neglect, the tigers are tormenting your people."

After explaining the glory of the Chaturthi vow, the Brahmin revealed his true form as the Sun. The king was astonished and, realizing the Brahmin was the Sun God himself, bowed down and asked for guidance. Ugrasena asked: "Are you the Sun God himself, who has come to bless me? I am blessed by your sight. Now, O Brahmin, tell me about the form of Ganesha so that I may worship him."

The Brahmin replied: "Listen, O King, to the form of Ganesha, which grants yoga and liberation. In the heart, he is the Chintamani, the mover of the five senses. Through the restraint of the five senses, he is attained. The word 'Gaja' signifies the state of 'Asamprajnata' (deepest meditation); he whose head is that, and whose body is the entire universe, is Ganesha. The illusionary Maya, or Siddhi, resides on his left, and the intellect, or Buddhi, resides on his right. Ganesha is the master of both. Worship him according to the rituals, and you will find peace."

After giving him the ten-syllable mantra and the proper rituals, the Sun God disappeared. The king returned to his city, consulted his ministers, and shared the auspicious news. When the Chaitra Krishna Chaturthi arrived, he performed the worship of Ganesha with devotion. The citizens, seeing the king's devotion, also performed the Sankashti vow, which removes all suffering. Because of this, the tigers disappeared, and the people, freed from diseases, lived happily.

The king worshipped Ganesha with a focused mind and honored the Sun God as his Guru. After some time, he installed his son on the throne, retired, and devoted himself entirely to Ganesha. In the end, he attained the state of Brahman. All the people in his kingdom also attained liberation.

I have told you a fraction of the glory of this vow. Listen to another story that destroys sins. In the Dravida region, there was a sinful Kshatriya who lived among the Bhils and abandoned his own traditions. Once, while in the forest, greedy for wealth, he chased a man with a weapon. The man fled in fear. A powerful bear appeared in the forest and attacked the Kshatriya. The Kshatriya struck the bear with his weapon, but the bear severely wounded him. In the lonely forest, he lamented in agony. By divine coincidence, that day was the Chaitra Krishna Chaturthi. The sinner had been without food and water all day. At moonrise, a fruit fell from a tree, and the wounded Kshatriya ate it. He died on the fifth day and attained the state of Brahman.

Thus, O King, by the power of this vow, many people have enjoyed all pleasures here and attained the Supreme Brahman in the end. It is impossible to describe all these instances even in ten thousand years. There is no other vow like this that grants all desires. If even those who perform it out of ignorance attain the state of Brahman, what more can be said for the wise? Whoever reads or hears this glory of the Chaitra Krishna Chaturthi will surely attain everything.

Chapter 23 : The Story of the Fourth Day of the Dark Fortnight of Vaishakha

Dasharatha asked: O best of sages, please tell me now about the greatness of the fourth day of the dark fortnight of the month of Vaishakha. I am not satisfied; please explain it to me in detail.

Vashistha replied: The great sage Agastya, who was engaged in drying up the ocean, could not succeed despite his great power, so he sought refuge in Lord Brahma. Prompted by Brahma, he performed an excellent vow on the fourth day of the dark fortnight of Vaishakha according to the prescribed rituals. Knowing the immense greatness of this day, he, being devoted to mantras, performed this vow—which is sacred to both the bright and dark fortnights—along with other eminent sages.

Dasharatha asked: Agastya was full of the brilliance of penance and knew all the scriptures. How could he, who was so radiant, be rendered ineffective and devoid of the fruits of his vow?

Vashistha replied: Listen, O wise and fortunate King. I shall now explain its greatness to you. Agastya was indeed full of intense penance, but he was not equal to the other sages in certain aspects. He had not performed this specific vow due to ignorance. I will tell you the reason, which will dispel your doubts. O King, many types of actions are mentioned in the Vedas, scriptures, and Puranas, but who can perform them all? A human being performs actions according to their own choice. Listen carefully as I explain the path.

Actions are divided into two types according to the scriptures: daily (nitya) and occasional (naimittika). O King, Agastya performed his daily duties but, being absorbed in his penance, he ignored other vows and rituals, not knowing their necessity. Therefore, O best of kings, he did not observe this primary vow. Listen to what I tell you about why he was not successful: there is no doubt that the worship of Lord Ganesha is the most important of all, and it is declared in the Vedas and other texts to be the bestower of all accomplishments. O best of kings, those who lack proper refinement do not worship Ganesha, the leader of the gods. Only when sins are burnt away by penance and a store of merit is accumulated does one develop devotion to Ganesha. Know that Agastya, despite being a great sage, had not yet become a vessel for the knowledge related to Ganesha.

Agastya, filled with anger, tried to dry up the ocean to protect the world from the demon Vatapi, but he became ineffective and was astonished. He went to Brahma, bowed to him, and asked: "O Brahma, I am drying up the ocean with my penance, but why has the power of my penance been rendered ineffective? Tell me."

Brahma replied: "Listen, O fortunate son, I will tell you the reason. You are always engaged in daily rituals and penance, but you do not worship Lord Ganesha with devotion. That is why your actions are not bearing fruit. You must perform the excellent vow of the fourth day, which you have neglected. This vow is approved by the scriptures and grants the four goals of human life. Because you lacked this, you were rendered ineffective. Now, perform this primary vow with devotion, especially as a daily practice. Then you will be free from distress, attain the four-fold path, become a perfect yogi, and attain the realm of Ganesha."

O King, Agastya then performed this great vow of Ganesha and became endowed with the fruits of human life. By the power of the merit of this vow, he dried up the ocean and remained forever devoted to the Lord of the Ganas. Thereafter, this vow became famous among the great sages, and they performed it as a daily practice for the fulfillment of all desires.

Listen to another story of greatness that grants merit to those who hear it. In the city of Avanti, there lived a Vaishya who was a great sinner. From childhood, he committed immense sins until his death, which cannot even be described. He killed his mother for greed, killed his father, committed treason against his guru, and was guilty of the sin of killing a Brahmin. The people complained to the King, who banished him. He went to the forest and lived in a cave. He killed travelers and various living beings, regardless of whether there was a purpose or not. He accumulated much wealth and was joined by other thieves. With them, he built a fortress in a difficult mountain pass. Thieves from everywhere came to live there. He became their king and brought his wife there, surrounded by his children. He lived as he pleased, addicted to theft, and never performed even the slightest bit of merit. He would capture women on the road and violate their chastity; many women died there out of great fear.

The King of Avanti, informed by the people, set out to kill this thief to prevent the harassment of travelers. Soldiers were sent in all ten directions, and they surrounded the villain. The King captured the thief and his sons and brought them to justice. The King killed the thieves, but he threw the Vaishya into prison and tortured him daily. Suddenly, the fourth day of the dark fortnight of Vaishakha arrived. The Vaishya had not received any food. Suffering greatly from the daily torture and extremely hungry, the sinner was alone at night. Out of pity, the royal guards gave him a little food, which the Vaishya ate only after the moon had risen. He died on the fifth day in prison after being severely beaten. Yet, by the power of the merit of the vow he had inadvertently kept, he was taken by the attendants of Ganesha to the realm of Svananda (Ganesha's abode).

If such a wicked soul could attain the state of Brahman through the power of the merit of this vow, what more can be said for those who perform it intentionally? Thus, by the power of this vow, people have attained the Supreme. It is impossible to describe them all, O best of kings. Whoever hears or reads this greatness of the fourth day of the dark fortnight of Vaishakha will attain the highest state.

Chapter 24 : Description of the Jyeshtha Krishna Chaturthi

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O Lord, I have heard the supreme greatness of the Vaisakha Sankashti, yet I am not satisfied. Therefore, O best of sages, please tell me in detail the complete story of the Sankashti Chaturthi that falls in the month of Jyeshtha.

Vashistha said: In the land of Nishadha, there was a highly fortunate king named Nala. He was radiant, skilled in weaponry, and learned in the scriptures. He was handsome, wealthy, and possessed a complete army. By defeating powerful kings with his weapons, he shone brilliantly. With subordinate kings under his control and others paying him tribute, he ruled the earth as a universal monarch. Through righteousness, charity, diplomacy, and renunciation, he filled the three worlds with his fame. The gods in the realms of Indra, Brahma, Kailasa, and Vishnu spoke of his virtuous nature. Devoted to visiting holy men and serving gods, Brahmins, and guests, he protected the poor and the blind. His wife, the highly fortunate Damayanti, was incomparable in beauty and enchanted the world. The virtues of that noble soul cannot be described; he was a king of pure fame who conquered the influence of Kali.

Once, due to the influence of Kali, he was defeated in a game of dice by his brother, and abandoning his kingdom, he went to the forest. There, prompted by Kali in the guise of a fish, Nala abandoned his virtuous wife in the wilderness. Damayanti, wearing only half a garment, went to her father's house, suffering various miseries without Nala. For three years, the radiant Nala wandered the forest in disguise, wearing only half a cloth. Seeing the righteous king suffering due to Kali, the great sage Narada went to him. Seeing the sage, the king felt ashamed and bowed with folded hands. Understanding his shame, Narada said:

Narada said: Do not feel ashamed, O King; such is the course of actions. With a focused mind, listen to my supreme words. In your kingdom, O fortunate one, the wonderful vow known as Chaturthi was neglected, and because of that, you have fallen from your kingdom. This vow, which grants the fruits of the four goals of life, must be performed first; if not done, O King, all other actions are meaningless. Without these four fruits, you are undoubtedly suffering. Therefore, you must perform this vow, which grants all accomplishments.

Having said this, the great yogi Narada narrated to the king the greatness of the Chaturthi vow, which brings peace. Hearing this, the astonished King Nala asked him to explain the greatness of the Lord of Ganas so that he might worship Him.

Narada said: O King, the greatness of the Lord of Ganas, which grants the fruit of peace, cannot be fully expressed, yet listen to my words. Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Shakti, the Sun, the gods, and the great serpents like Shesha worship Him as their family deity. The best of kings, even Brahma, worship Him, the eternal family deity, as the source of life and sustenance. The Vedas worship Him, the bestower of auspicious unions, through great mantras. The supreme Nature, who grants all accomplishments, resides on His left side, and the intellect, consisting of five states of consciousness, is situated on His right. He resides in His own bliss and is called the one worthy of worship by all. He is the beginning of all, the Lord of Ganas, who dwells within the hearts of the wise. The Ganas are groups of internal and external forces, and Ganesha is their master; worship Him according to the prescribed rites.

Having said this, the sage gave him the six-syllable mantra and then disappeared. The king, meditating on Gajanana in his heart, chanted the mantra with devotion, deeply lamenting his own state. Later, he was bitten by Karkotaka, who gave him a garment that restored his original form. Rejoicing, the king went to Rituparna, who honored him greatly. Meanwhile, Narada went to Damayanti at her father's house and had her perform the same vow. Through the merit of the Sankashti vow performed by Nala and Damayanti in the month of Jyeshtha, Nala became wise. After three years of living in disguise, he returned to his father-in-law, joined Rituparna, and regained his true form using the serpent's garment. Accompanied by Damayanti, his father-in-law, and an army, the king returned to his own city. Honored by his brother, he ruled excellently, surrounded by happy and prosperous subjects, free from obstacles.

After a long time, having installed his son on the throne, he went to the forest with his wife and worshipped the Lord of Ganas. In the end, the king attained the Lord of Ganas in His own abode of bliss, becoming one with the Divine. Those who were instructed by Nala on this earth performed the main vow of the Shukla and Krishna Chaturthi. All of them enjoyed great happiness, blessed with children and grandchildren, and free from disease. In the end, they all attained the state of the Divine.

Thus, I have told you the greatness of the Sankashti vow in the month of Jyeshtha. Listen again to the greatness of the Jyeshtha Krishna Chaturthi, which removes all obstacles. In Malwa, there was a man born of a low caste who was a sinner. Greedy for wealth, he went to the forest and killed Brahmins and others. Seeing a woman alone, the wicked man assaulted her. The virtuous woman, refusing to be touched by another man, gave up her life. Thus, he committed various sins. Once, while in the forest, this man, armed with a weapon, saw a person and ran to kill him. The person fled in fear, crying out, but the sinner failed to catch him. Meanwhile, the king's armed men arrived, having heard the commotion. They captured and beat the wicked man, and the king threw him into prison. By divine providence, the Jyeshtha Chaturthi arrived while he was in prison, suffering from hunger. At moonrise, the compassionate guards gave him food, which the wicked man ate immediately. By the king's order, they executed the wicked man on the fifth day. Upon dying, he attained the state of the Divine.

Thus, O King, many people, through the power of the merit of this vow, enjoyed worldly pleasures and eventually attained the Divine. The infinite greatness of this vow cannot be fully described, yet I have told you the greatness of the Sankashti. Whoever listens to or reads this greatness of the Jyeshtha Krishna Chaturthi attains their desired fruits.

Thus ends the twenty-fourth chapter of the Gajanana Charita in the fourth section of the Mudgala Purana, describing the Jyeshtha Krishna Chaturthi.

Chapter 25 : Description of the Ashadha Krishna Chaturthi

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O great yogi, please describe the auspicious story of the Sankashti Chaturthi that occurs in the month of Ashadha. Tell me about this vow, which grants all desires and destroys all sins.

Vashistha said: O prince, I will tell you an ancient story that is complete, grants all accomplishments, and has been practiced by many.

In the past, Lord Indra, being greatly tormented by Vritrasura and filled with anxiety, abandoned his kingdom and lived in the forest, terrified. The sages lost their knowledge of the Vedas, moral conduct declined everywhere, and all the social orders (varnas) were distressed by fear, O King. Due to the cessation of religious duties, the gods became extremely miserable; they stopped eating and stood on the verge of death. While wandering in the caves of the mountains, they saw the great sage Gritsamada, the teacher of yogis, who had arrived there. Seeing him, Indra and the other gods bowed down to him like a stick. After worshipping the sage, who was seated on a proper seat, they asked him.

Indra said: My vision, my birth, my father, my mother, my religious vows, my sacrifices, and my knowledge have all become fruitful today by the sight of your feet, O Lord. By your mere presence, our welfare will be ensured, for you are a direct master of yoga and a bestower of all accomplishments. We have been defeated by the wicked Vritrasura; having abandoned our kingdom, we live in the forest like animals, O Brahmin. Deprived of our duties and suffering from starvation, we are surely going to die; what hope is there for us? Yet, through the merit of past deeds, I have attained your supreme presence. O Brahmin, please show compassion and tell us of a great way to destroy Vritra. The entire world is filled with corruption, O great yogi. What do you see now that the time of total destruction has arrived, O best of yogis?

Vashistha said: Being asked thus, Gritsamada, filled with compassion, spoke words for the protection of the world and the destruction of the wicked.

Gritsamada said: O fortunate one, you abandoned the Chaturthi vow, and that is why you lost your kingdom. O King of gods, you have fallen from your vow; perform that vow now. Due to the arrogance of your knowledge, you abandoned the mantra of Ganesha; what wonder is it that you are now kingdom-less, O foolish one, blinded by pride? After saying this, the great sage told him the greatness of the Chaturthi and personally gave him the mantra.

O King, when the sage left, Indra and the gods performed the vow after worshipping Ganesha. O King, the great lunar day of Ashadha Krishna Chaturthi was observed by the King of gods, and it proved to be the bestower of prosperity. Meditating on Ganesha in his mind, Indra took up the great thunderbolt (Vajra) made from the bones of Dadhichi and went to fight Vritrasura. By the power generated from the Chaturthi vow, he fought a fierce battle with the great demon and killed him with the Vajra. Having killed the mighty Vritrasura, Indra, along with the gods, became peaceful and rejoiced in Amaravati with his people, O King.

Thereafter, all the gods began to observe the Chaturthi vow regularly in both the bright and dark fortnights in their respective places. People on earth also performed this vow, as instructed by Indra, for it is a great vow that grants all accomplishments. As the Manvantara passed, Indra became absorbed in his own bliss; seeing the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) there, he attained the state of Brahman. O King, all the people on earth who performed this vow gradually attained the state of Brahman through the power of the merit of this vow.

I have told you this brief greatness of the vow. Now listen to another story that destroys the cloak of sin.

In Gujarat, there was a Brahmin who was sinful from childhood. He was addicted to base activities and committed the slaughter of living beings. Once, that wicked man even abducted his own sister. Living in the forest, he was addicted to meat and wine. Greedy for wealth, he killed Brahmins, animals, and birds, O King, for he was a person of evil conduct. He daily ate with barbarians (Yavanas); it is not possible for me to describe his sins fully.

Once, while in the forest, this defiler of his caste, who was a sinner, was there on the day of Ashadha Krishna Chaturthi. By divine coincidence, he did not find any food in the forest. Distressed by hunger, he wandered in the mountains. He found nothing, and being filled with sorrow, he was eager to return home in the afternoon. By divine fate, he found neither water nor food there. He returned home in the evening, filled with grief. At night, when the moon rose, he embraced a woman of barbarian origin and ate food with his children, completely deluded by Maya, O King.

Suddenly, he was struck by a fever and was destined to die. O King, he was touched by that sinful barbarian woman. He died on the fifth day, suffering greatly. The attendants of Ganesha took him and made him attain the state of Brahman. If such great sinners are liberated by this vow, what can I say about the merit gained by those who perform it with knowledge?

O King, those best of men who perform this vow according to the prescribed rules become saviors just by being seen by others. Thus, by the power of the vow, they attained the state of Brahman. Their infinite stories cannot be told. Whoever listens to or reads this story of the Ashadha Sankashti will undoubtedly attain their desired goals.

Thus ends the twenty-fifth chapter of the Gajanana Charita in the fourth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Description of Ashadha Krishna Chaturthi."

Chapter 26 : The Glory of the Fourth Day of the Dark Fortnight of Shravana

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Yudhisthira asked: O Vasishtha, please tell me the glory of the Sankata Chaturthi that falls in the dark fortnight of the month of Shravana, which brings happiness to all.

Vasishtha replied: I shall narrate the history of this Shravana Sankata Chaturthi. Hearing or reading it brings complete happiness and fulfillment.

In the lunar dynasty was born the glorious and righteous Yudhishthira, who was generous and respectful to all. He was the very embodiment of Dharma, truthful, compassionate, and a protector of his subjects. He was the son of Pandu, highly radiant, and always surrounded by Draupadi and his brothers. He was universally accepted and blessed by his mother Kunti. Krishna, the descendant of the Vrishni clan—who was Lord Narayana himself and engaged in removing the burden of the Earth—was always his helper.

Once, after performing the great Rajasuya sacrifice without Krishna, the King was in his palace surrounded by other kings. Dhritarashtra sent Vidura to him. Upon seeing Vidura, the son of Kunti honored him and served him a meal. With great joy, Yudhishthira said to him, "My birth and actions have become blessed by the sight of your feet. O Lord, we are always protected by you."

Vidura replied: Everything is well among your kin, O wise one. Listen to the words of the misguided Dhritarashtra. He is blinded by affection for his son Duryodhana, and it is at his behest that I have come. Duryodhana, the wicked one who increases the suffering of the clan, wishes to defeat you in a game of dice. He is instigated by Karna and especially by Shakuni. He is obsessed with taking your kingdom, aided by Duhshasana. Dhritarashtra, being greedy, also desires this. Do not go, my dear, to the one who is led by his son's evil counsel.

Vasishtha continued: Hearing these words, Yudhishthira sighed and spoke to the noble Vidura. Yudhishthira said: Listen to my words, O uncle. Dhritarashtra is the King, and we are all under his command, including Bhishma. Therefore, I will go with you; whatever is destined will happen. Hearing this righteous response, Vidura was saddened and remained silent.

Accompanied by Vidura, the Pandavas and Draupadi went to the court of Dhritarashtra. There, at his command, they were drawn into the game of dice with Duryodhana and his cohorts. Shakuni, having mastered the game through severe penance, ensured the outcome was in his favor. Gradually, Duryodhana won the entire kingdom and sent the Pandavas into exile. In the assembly, the wicked one brought Draupadi and insulted her. He even had his brother strip her, ignoring the presence of Bhishma and others. To protect her Dharma, the Pandavas endured this, but Draupadi remembered Krishna, who appeared in a hidden form. Through his divine illusion, he provided her with endless garments. Eventually, everyone left in anger.

Draupadi and the Pandavas went to the forest. There, Krishna arrived, filled with righteous anger. He said to Yudhishthira, "I will kill Duryodhana and his allies and restore your kingdom. Do not go into the forest." Yudhishthira replied, "O Janardana, abandon your anger and protect Dharma. Restore my kingdom through righteous means."

Hearing this, the radiant Krishna said: Perform the worship of Ganesha along with your wife and brothers. He is the creator of Svananda-loka and our family deity. Having said this, the Lord gave him the ten-syllable mantra and the method of worship that grants all success. Krishna then narrated the glory of this vow to Yudhishthira, which grants merit and destroys confusion. He explained that this vow is the foremost, and if performed, all actions become fruitful. "Because you neglected this primary vow, you lost your kingdom and were cast out. Now, perform it."

Yudhishthira, amazed and enlightened, meditated on Ganesha and performed the ritual. In the month of Shravana, during the dark fortnight, he observed the Sankata Chaturthi with his brothers and wife. By the power of this vow, they lived happily in the forest. Later, with Krishna's help, they killed Duryodhana and regained their kingdom. This vow became famous on Earth, and everyone began to observe it. After enjoying all worldly pleasures and crowning their grandson, the Pandavas attained spiritual knowledge and merged into the divine. Others who observed this vow also attained the abode of Ganesha and became one with the Brahman.

O King, I have thus told you the glory of this vow. Now hear another story that grants great merit. In Karnataka, there lived a cruel hunter who committed many sins. He killed people in the forest and committed acts of violence against Brahmins for the sake of wealth and meat. Once, while hiding in a cave to kill travelers, a heavy rain began. The rivers flooded, and the paths were blocked. Trapped by the rain and hunger, he happened to be there on the day of the Shravana Krishna Chaturthi. Without knowing the significance, he observed the fast. When the moon rose, he found some water and a coconut, which he ate. At the end of the night, as he stepped out, a tiger killed and ate him. The attendants of Ganesha took him to Svananda-loka, and by the power of the vow, he attained the state of Brahman.

If even a great sinner can attain the supreme state through this vow, what more can be said? Those who read or hear this glory of the Shravana Krishna Chaturthi attain both worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Chapter 27 : The Description of the Glory of the Bhadrapada Krishna Chaturthi

Dasharatha said: O Sage, please tell me the glory of the Bhadrapada Krishna Chaturthi. I am never satisfied listening to this story, which grants all accomplishments.

Vashistha said: In the Dravida country, there was a king named Virasena, who was radiant, famous, powerful, and a universal monarch. He was well-versed in statecraft and ruled excellently. Having brought all other kings under his control, he ensured they paid their tributes. He was righteous and always gave joy to his subjects with devotion. He could not bear to see any man or woman hungry, and he was a source of happiness to all. He protected all his subjects like his own children with great care. However, due to destiny, this slayer of enemy heroes remained childless. He made great efforts to have a son through japa, homa, and by visiting various holy places, but he remained in the same state.

As much time passed and the king remained childless, he became extremely sorrowful, abandoned his kingdom, and went to the forest. The king wandered here and there in the forest with his wife. Eventually, having reached a great forest, he fell down, overcome by hunger. The sage Sumantu, a knower of the Vedas and Vedangas, a great yogi, a disciple of Vyasa, and a scholar of the Puranas, came there to collect sacrificial wood. Seeing the king, he approached him with compassion. Seeing the great sage, Virasena and his wife stood up quickly and bowed to him with great joy. With folded hands, the king spoke excellent words to the wise sage.

Virasena said: My life, birth, parents, lineage, fame, knowledge, and vows have all become blessed by the sight of your feet. I am a sinner who left his kingdom and came to the forest for the sake of a son, and by the fruit of my past merits, I have obtained your vision. Despite many efforts, I have not had a son. I see you as the only source of grace and happiness.

Vashistha said: Hearing these excellent words of the king, the great sage Sumantu was pleased and spoke.

Sumantu said: Do not worry, O King; you will surely have a son who will uplift your lineage. Listen to my beneficial words. You are childless because you have neglected the great vow of Chaturthi in your kingdom. This vow is the foremost of all, grants all accomplishments, and provides the four goals of life; without it, everything is fruitless. You have performed many meritorious deeds, but they are devoid of the four goals because they lacked the Chaturthi vow.

After saying this, the sage narrated the great glory of the vow to the king. Upon hearing it, the curious king asked.

Virasena said: O Brahmin, tell me the knowledge of Ganesha, which grants the four goals. I will worship that vow, which is honored by all, every day.

Sumantu said: Who is capable of describing the true nature of Ganesha? Listen, O King, to the knowledge of the Absolute. Through the power of my penance in the past, I became capable of cursing and blessing in all realms. Through intense penance, inner knowledge arose in me, and I was amazed. I realized the various stages of existence and the nature of the Absolute. Gradually, I became absorbed in the bliss of the Self, attached to the peace and happiness of the Brahman. Seeing the duality of the world, I went to the great sage Vyasa and asked him about my welfare.

I asked: O Great Sage, tell me about the peace that is the nature of Brahman. I am your disciple; save me from the ocean of worldly existence.

The great yogi was pleased and said: Listen, son, I will tell you about the nature of Brahman, which is the peace of all peace, to be known through the practice of yoga. The union of the Self and the Absolute, where there is no difference, is called Self-Bliss. When the nectar-like nature of the Self and the Absolute become one, that bliss is called Self-Bliss. That which is devoid of the three qualities and yet possesses them, the unmanifest, is called Brahman. The union of these four is called the bliss of Brahman. The Lord of the Ganas is the master of this; know Him always. The Ganas are groups of internal and external energies; know them as the form of Brahman through yoga. Worship Ganesha, the master of these, according to the prescribed rites, and you will undoubtedly become one with Brahman.

The mind is said to be of five types, which is the indicator of intelligence. The mind is the form of delusion, and it is also said to be the form of success. The Lord of the Ganas plays with these two illusions. By restraining the five-fold mind, one attains the holder of the mind. Therefore, He is called Chintamani (the wish-fulfilling gem). Abandoning the deluded mind, become Chintamani yourself.

After saying this, the Lord gave me a single-syllable mantra. I bowed to him and returned to my hermitage. I meditated on Ganapati and chanted the mantra, and in a short time, I attained peace. Even then, because I did not worship the Lord of the Ganas specifically, the Lord of Obstacles showed me His own form. Seeing Ganesha, I was amazed and worshipped Him repeatedly. He, the leader of the Ganas, praised by the Atharva Upanishad, granted me His devotion and then disappeared. Since then, I worship Him daily with reverence. You too, O King, worship Him, and all will be well.

After saying this, the great sage gave him the sixteen-syllable mantra and disappeared. The king, filled with wonder, bowed to him with his wife, returned to his city, and worshipped the Lord of the Ganas. When the Krishna Chaturthi of the month of Bhadrapada arrived, it was celebrated by all the citizens with reverence. The king performed the vow with great devotion. By the power of the merit of this vow, the king was blessed with a son, and he and his subjects became free from all diseases. After handing over the kingdom to his son, the king, along with his wife, worshipped Ganesha daily with devotion. At the end, he went to the realm of Ganesha and became one with Brahman.

I have told you the glory of this vow, which removes distress. Listen to another story of Chaturthi that destroys sins. In Andhra, there was a fisherman who was a killer and sinful from childhood. He was a liar, a thief, and a source of conflict. He would go into the forest and kill travelers and Brahmins. His sins were countless. Once, during the Bhadrapada Krishna Chaturthi, he was in the forest and remained without food. Suddenly, a large group of people arrived, and out of fear, the fisherman hid in a cave. When they left, he came out and tried to go home, but he found nothing to eat. He stayed on the path, and at night, he reached his home. When the moon rose, he ate with his children. On the fifth day, he died of a snake bite. The messengers of Ganesha came to take him. They took the fisherman to the city of Svananda, and upon seeing Ganesha, he became one with Brahman. Even though his observance of the vow was ignorant and without proper rites, he still attained the state of Brahman.

Such is the power of the merit of the Chaturthi vow that it cannot be fully described. Whoever listens to or reads this glory of the Bhadrapada Krishna Chaturthi will attain all their desires.

Chapter 28 : The Glory of the Fourth Day of the Dark Fortnight of Ashwina

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O Brahmin, I am not satisfied with the glory of the fourth day of the dark fortnight of Ashwina. Please describe it to me in detail.

Vashistha replied: O King, I will tell you a story that grants all accomplishments; listen, and you will be satisfied. Lord Ganesha was attained as a son by Parvati and Shiva after they performed penance for a thousand divine years. Kartikeya was also born from Shiva’s own essence, and he was very affectionate. However, their love for Ganesha was not the same as it was for Kartikeya. Kartikeya would constantly compete with Ganesha, saying, "I shall marry first, and then you." Seeing this rivalry between their two sons, Shiva and Parvati pondered over the matter of their marriage. They realized that this Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) was the embodiment of Brahman himself, and if he were offended, he would surely cause trouble. If they ignored Ganesha and married off Skanda (Kartikeya) first, the commander of the army would be angered; yet, it did not seem right to ignore their own son, the commander. Thus, they decided to act with a clever strategy.

Shiva told Ganesha and Skanda, "Whoever returns first after circumambulating the earth, I will marry him first." Ganesha, being wise, said, "O Lord, why do you ignore me, your eldest son? I am heavy-bodied and my vehicle is a mouse; I cannot circumambulate the earth." Having said this, Ganesha stayed home, while Skanda set off on his peacock with great joy. While Skanda was busy with his journey, Ganesha performed a wonderful feat. He worshipped Parvati and Shiva and circumambulated them. He then said to Shiva, "Please perform my marriage. He who circumambulates his parents has, according to the scriptures, circumambulated the entire earth." Hearing this, a trembling Shiva said, "I will perform your marriage; do not be angry, my son."

Later, Parvati, overwhelmed with love, asked Shiva why he had honored Ganesha while ignoring Kartikeya. Shiva replied, "Listen, Parvati. If he (Ganesha) is angered, he will surely destroy everything. Therefore, set aside your attachment and protect the eternal dharma. This is not just a son; he is the embodiment of Brahman." Having said this, Shiva performed Ganesha’s marriage. When the marriage was complete, Kartikeya returned and, feeling deceived, created a great disturbance. Narada arrived and fueled the conflict, telling Kartikeya that his parents had cheated him. Feeling slighted, Skanda left for the Shailaparvata mountain.

When Shiva and Parvati learned of this, they went to the mountain to find him. Seeing him staying on the Krauncha mountain, they were filled with grief. The sage Mudgala arrived there, and they worshipped him. They asked him for a way to be reunited with Skanda. Mudgala said, "O Shiva, you are the knower of all. You are the embodiment of knowledge, and your Shakti is the giver of all fruits. Ganesha is the son born of your meditation, the Chintamani. You are the Purusha, and your body is the Shakti; through their union, the son of Brahman is born. You are Ardhanarishvara, and from that, Ganesha was born. He is the guardian of the state of Brahman. Skanda is the son born of your different aspects, representing worldly comfort, while Ganesha represents peace and happiness. You are deluded by the sense of duality. You love Skanda more, and because you lack faith in the Brahman (Ganesha), you are facing these obstacles. Worship him."

Following the sage's advice, Shiva and Parvati worshipped Ganesha. Realizing his mistake, Skanda also returned to Kailash, worshipped Ganesha with devotion, and became a follower of the path of peace.

The sage continued: "Listen to another story that destroys sins. In the land of Vanga, there lived a Brahmin who abandoned his path, became addicted to liquor, and lived with a Yavana woman, becoming a sinner. Once, while suffering from a fever on the fourth day of the dark fortnight of Ashwina, he accidentally consumed food and water after moonrise. When he eventually died, the attendants of Ganesha took him to the divine realm. If such a great sinner can attain the state of Brahman through the merit of this fast, what more can be said for those who follow the rules? Whoever hears or reads this glory of the Ashwina Krishna Chaturthi attains all their desires."

Chapter 29 : The Glory of the Krishna Chaturthi in the Month of Kartika

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Dasharatha asked: O Sage, please describe the greatness of the Sankata Chaturthi that falls during the dark fortnight of the month of Kartika, for it is known to be the supreme destroyer of all sins.

Vashistha replied: I shall narrate to you an ancient history regarding this. In the land of Maharashtra, there lived a king of great brilliance named Kardama. He was well-versed in the Vedas and their meanings, devoted to his duties, and ruled his subjects by collecting only a sixth of their produce, protecting them as if they were his own children. He possessed a vast army, a full treasury like that of Kubera, and was an expert in all weaponry. By his own strength, he brought all neighboring kings under his control and ruled the earth up to the oceans. All other kings served him like subordinates, obeying his every command. He was virtuous, charitable, and devoted to gods, Brahmins, and guests. He was a performer of sacrifices, humble, a builder of holy places, and a conqueror of enemies; his virtues were so numerous that I cannot fully describe them.

However, this king was struck by a terrible, sin-born disease (tuberculosis). He became overwhelmed with grief and was reduced to mere skin and bones. He tried many remedies for relief, but none worked. Distressed, he turned to pilgrimage, visiting many holy sites, yet he found no relief from his suffering. He engaged Brahmins in the worship of gods, but his illness only worsened. Finally, the king, seeking peace, developed detachment. He handed over the kingdom to his ministers and went to the forest with his wife.

While wandering in the forest, he met the great yogi Mankanaka. Seeing the sage, the king fell to the ground and wept bitterly. Moved by compassion, the sage spoke: "Listen, O fortunate King. I shall tell you the cause of your suffering. You have neglected the vow known as Chaturthi, which is the primary duty in your kingdom. Because of this, you are burdened with sin and are destined for hell. This vow, which grants all accomplishments and fourfold fruits, should be performed regularly. Being devoid of this vow, despite your other virtues, you have remained fruitless.

After the sage explained the glory of the Chaturthi, the king said: "My birth and actions have become blessed by the sight of your feet. By hearing of this vow, I am truly fulfilled. O ocean of mercy, please describe the form of Ganesha in detail, so that I may worship that God of all gods constantly."

Mankanaka replied: "The form of Ganesha is beyond description, but I will tell you of it in terms of its manifestations. Long ago, while I was performing penance, I lived only on leaves. After a long time, my hand was pricked by a thorn. When the sap of the leaves flowed from the wound, I was amazed and began to dance. I wondered how the sap of the leaves was flowing from my body. The intensity of my dance shook the heavens and the earth. Seeing my penance, the gods and Lord Shankara arrived. Shankara asked me, 'Why do you dance, O great one? Show me this wonder.' When he touched the spot, the sap turned into ash, and I was astonished. I bowed to Maheshwara and praised him.

I realized that there is no power greater than Shankara. I abandoned the worship of Vishnu and became devoted to Maheshwara. Gradually, I attained yoga and peace. As I wandered, I realized that the natural Brahman is free from delusion. Seeking peace, I took refuge in the Sun God. Pleased, the Sun told me, 'Worship Ganesha with devotion, and you will attain peace.' The Sun, Vishnu, and Shankara are all aspects of the divine, but in their union, Ganesha is the Lord. He is the one who grants peace through the union of Maya and Yoga. Worship the Chintamani Ganesha, and you will become revered by yogis."

The sage then gave the king the six-syllable mantra. The king returned home and, with great devotion, performed the Kartika Krishna Chaturthi vow. Through this, he became free from disease. The king and his people began to observe the vow, and the king eventually attained the state of Brahman.

The sage continued: "I will tell you another story. A sinful Chandala, who was a murderer and a thief, once happened to be in the forest during the Kartika Krishna Chaturthi. He was bitten by a snake and, suffering from the poison, he fell unconscious. He died on the fifth day, but due to the merit of the vow he had inadvertently observed, he attained the state of Brahman. Such is the power of this vow, O King. Whoever reads or hears this greatness of the Sankata Chaturthi will attain all their desired goals."

Thus ends the twenty-ninth chapter of the Gajanana Charitra in the fourth section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 30 : Description of the Glory of the Fourth Day of the Dark Fortnight of Margashirsha

Dasharatha said: O Lord, please tell me the glory of the fourth day of the dark fortnight of the month of Margashirsha, which grants all desires, so that I may be satisfied.

Vashistha said: I shall narrate to you a historical account in detail. There was a glorious and powerful king named Harishchandra. He resided in Ayodhya and was devoted to various righteous duties, always endowed with good qualities, a performer of sacrifices, and dedicated to his work. Whatever a Brahmin requested, he gave with joy; there was no one on earth equal to him in merit. Then, the yogi Vishvamitra came to deceive him and, through the path of charity, took away the king's entire kingdom. Having made the king destitute and sent him to a foreign land, the great sage deceived him there under various pretexts.

Even then, the king remained extremely righteous; he did not waver despite being filled with great sorrow, for he was a knower of the ultimate truth. Once, the fourth day of the dark fortnight of Margashirsha arrived. The king, afflicted by hunger and thirst, was deceived by the sage, and when the moon rose, the king ate food due to destiny. On the fifth day, Vishvamitra’s intellect became confused. Realizing this, the yogi was astonished and pondered in his mind. He then realized that the king had performed a great vow. Appearing in person as a bestower of boons, he said to Harishchandra:

O wise one, rule your kingdom and listen to my supreme words. Because of the vow of the fourth day being broken in your kingdom, you have become afflicted and fallen, O inferior king. I was also agitated because of that, there is no doubt. This vow, which grants the four goals of life, must be performed first and foremost; it is like a daily duty and grants all accomplishments. You have performed many types of meritorious deeds according to the scriptures, but all of them became fruitless due to the lack of this vow. O great king, you would have fallen into hell because of all the people, but you performed this chief vow now, albeit out of ignorance. Because of that, I am pleased, O best of kings. Perform this, and you will attain happiness for your people.

Having said this, the sage gave him the eight-syllabled mantra and the procedure for worshipping the Lord of the Ganas. The king then asked him again: Harishchandra said: O omniscient one, tell me the form of Ganesha and the procedure and power of this vow.

Vishvamitra said: Listen to the glory of the Lord of the Ganas in brief, O king. This Ganesha is one, and all the Ganas are said to be his. This universe, consisting of mind and speech, is the indicator of the Ganas. It is impossible for me to describe it as it is endowed with various states. Know that which is beyond mind and speech and which denotes the various aspects of Brahman to be the form of Ganesha, as seen in the Vedas. O king, Siddhi (Success) is said to be the cause of illusion, and Buddhi (Intellect) is the holder of illusion. The Lord of Obstacles plays with them, and the Chintamani (wish-fulfilling gem) resides in the heart. Worship Him according to the procedure, and you will attain peace. Even as a ruler, you will be venerable and will become a master of yogis. The mind is said to be of five types, and attachment is also fivefold. Abandoning the mind that is attached, you will become the Chintamani.

Having said this, the great sage made him hear the glory of the vow and then disappeared. Knowing that Vishvamitra had departed, the king, filled with joy and approved by his ministers, went quickly to Ayodhya. Along with the citizens, the king performed the excellent vow of the fourth day of both the bright and dark fortnights, remaining devoted to the worship of Ganesha. Everyone everywhere performed this praised vow with joy, and all the people on earth performed it especially. There is no other vow equal to this that grants everything. Consequently, he became free from diseases and filled with joy.

The king worshipped Ganapati daily with a focused mind. Entrusting the kingdom to his son, he went to the forest with his wife. There, he worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with great penance. Having attained special peace, that king became a yogi. In the end, becoming one who dwells in his own bliss, he merged into Ganesha. Thus, the glory of the vow has been told to you, O son of Aja. All those people gradually became one with Brahman through the supreme merit born of the fourth-day vow.

Listen to another story, O king. In the city of Malava, there was a courtesan named Karna who captivated men. She enchanted people instantly with her gestures. Many people abandoned their own duties and became devoted to her. Forsaking their own wives, they became loyal to her. O king, she made them all addicted to meat and wine. Brahmins and others became especially fallen and engaged in theft and other acts. What more can I say, O wise one? A person becomes a sinner by speaking of the faults of others. Such was that courtesan, sinful and extremely cruel. She was destined for hell along with many people, and she never ceased in enchanting men.

Once, she was afflicted with fever on the fourth day of the dark fortnight of Margashirsha and was tormented by the heat. She died on the fifth day and attained the state of Brahman. Such is the power of the vow, which is a helper to the fallen. Thus, many people became one with Brahman, O king. How many can I tell you? Even ten thousand years would not be enough. The supreme glory of the fourth day of Margashirsha, which has been told, grants all accomplishments upon reading or hearing it.

Chapter 31 : Describing the Glory of the Pausha Krishna Chaturthi

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Dasharatha said: O Guru, please tell me the glory of the Pausha Krishna Chaturthi. I am never satisfied listening to this supreme narrative that grants all accomplishments.

Vashistha said: I shall narrate an ancient historical account. There was a king named Virasaha in Hastinapur. He was powerful, righteous, skilled in weaponry, virtuous, generous, and highly respectful of those worthy of honor. Although he was engaged in various acts of charity, he suffered from the sorrow of being childless. Seeking a remedy, he became devoted to the service of the gods. He performed the Putrakameshti sacrifice through Brahmins, yet he could not obtain a son to continue his lineage. He performed many religious rites in holy places, but despite his personal devotion, he did not receive the gift of progeny.

Deeply saddened, the king abandoned his kingdom and went to the forest. Wandering with his wife, he suffered from cold and heat. Upon entering a great forest, filled with sorrow and grief, he walked fearlessly despite seeing lions and tigers. There, the great sage Trita arrived. Seeing him, the king bowed down and stood with folded hands. When asked by the sage, the king narrated his entire story. The great yogi wept and spoke with compassion.

Trita said: O best of kings, the vow that grants all accomplishments, which is born of Chaturthi, was neglected by you in your kingdom; that is why you are surely childless. One must perform this vow first, as it grants the fruits of the four goals of life (Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha). Only then do all other actions become fruitful. You were engaged in various religious acts, but without the power of this vow, those actions are fruitless and will lead one to the Raurava hell.

Having said this, the great sage narrated the entire glory of the Chaturthi. Then the king said: O ocean of compassion, tell me the glory of the Lord of Ganas (Ganesha). I shall worship Him forever, for such is His vow.

Trita said: How can the true form of Ganesha be described? I will tell you according to the nature of His manifestations; listen to that. Long ago, I was a practitioner of penance, capable of both cursing and blessing. O King, I had two brothers, Eka and Dvita, who were learned. For the sake of a sacrifice, I went to a great king. After completing the ritual, I was eager to return home. My brothers, who were worldly, threw me into a well, wanting the wealth and sacrificial animals I possessed. I fell into the waterless well, filled with grief. There, I performed a mental sacrifice that was beneficial. The gods arrived there out of respect to receive their share of the offering. Pleased and delighted, they were worshipped by me. O King, all the immortals granted me my desired boons and then, having lifted me out of the well, they departed.

After that, I begged from another king, received cattle, and returned to my hermitage with joy, where I remained practicing penance. Through intense penance, I attained universal knowledge. Abandoning penance, I attained the power of a seeker on earth through Yoga. I mastered the earth, the spirits, and the mad. In the end, I attained the state of Brahman, remaining in peace.

While I was in my hermitage, Mudgala arrived. He was righteous, generous, and respectful. Seeing him, I bowed down, for he was worshipped by the greatest of yogis. After he rested, I worshipped him and asked with folded hands: O Master, tell me the yoga of peace, the one that grants peace to all.

Mudgala said: The non-existent power, the true sun, the equal Vishnu, and the unmanifest Shiva—in their union, the Lord of Ganas (Ganapa) appeared. In this union, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, is endowed with Maya. In separation, He is devoid of Maya. The union of these two states is known as Ganesha. He is called the giver of peace; worship Him with devotion.

Having said this, Mudgala gave me the one-syllable mantra of Ganesha according to the prescribed rites and then disappeared. I worshipped the Lord of Ganas with all my heart. Through that, I attained peace and roam without fear. There is no Brahman higher than Ganesha, no penance higher than Ganesha, no action higher than Ganesha, and no knowledge higher than the Lord of Ganas. There is no Yoga higher than Ganesha, and no devotion higher than the Lord of Ganas. Therefore, He is to be worshipped by all, as He is the first to grant success. If one performs actions or seeks knowledge while abandoning Ganesha, all that becomes fruitless, like an offering made into ashes. One who abandons everything and worships Ganesha with a focused mind attains all accomplishments immediately and is said to have become one with Brahman.

Having said this, Trita gave him the ten-syllable mantra according to the rites and then disappeared. King Virasaha returned to his city with joy. He was honored by his ministers and citizens. There, when the Pausha Krishna Chaturthi arrived, he performed the vow with great joy along with his subjects. O King, that excellent vow was performed by him everywhere. People on earth began to observe the vow of the Shukla and Krishna Chaturthi. Consequently, he obtained a son who was a great warrior, wise in politics, righteous, and endowed with many virtues. The people were delighted. They became free from diseases and were happy and prosperous. People everywhere spoke of the glory of the Chaturthi.

Having established his son on the throne, the king attained liberation. Along with his wife, he worshipped the Lord of Ganas with devotion. In the end, he too became one with Brahman through the merit of the vow. Gradually, those on earth also became one with Brahman. Thus, the glory of the vow has been told to you, O best of kings. Listen to another great story that destroys sins.

In Gauda, there was a sinful merchant who was greedy. For the sake of wealth, he killed his father with poison. That wicked man similarly killed his mother and many people on the road, as well as other merchants, using poison. Having acquired much wealth, he became lustful for other women. He was addicted to liquor and meat and was wicked, lusting after virtuous women. Under various pretexts, he would quickly approach them. Because of this, he was caught by the king and beaten. Bound in chains, he remained in the royal prison. By divine providence, the Pausha Krishna Chaturthi arrived. On that day, deprived of food, he remained in sorrow. At moonrise, he ate the food given by the guards. On the fifth day, he was impaled by the king's messengers and died. Due to the merit of the vow, he became one with Brahman.

Thus, those who were ignorant became wise and attained the state of Brahman through the power of the vow, O wise one. How many such people were there? I cannot tell you all, so I have told you in brief. Whoever listens to or reads this glory of the Pausha Sankashti Chaturthi will always be blessed.

Thus ends the thirty-first chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the glory of the Pausha Krishna Chaturthi.

Chapter 32 : Description of the Greatness of Sankashti Chaturthi during the Malamaasa

Dasharatha said: O giver of honor, O ocean of compassion, please tell me the greatness of the Sankashti Chaturthi during the Malamaasa (intercalary month). I am not yet satisfied, and this knowledge brings great joy.

Vashistha said: In the Andhra region, during the Raivata Manvantara, there lived a famous king named Dharmadatta, who was devoted to various righteous duties. He was a performer of sacrifices, extremely humble, well-versed in statecraft, and pleased everyone. He collected only the sixth part of the produce as tax and was dedicated to the protection of his subjects. Suddenly, this king, who was a knower of all scriptures and a sovereign ruler, was afflicted with dropsy (Jalodara) due to some grave sin. O best of kings, he tried many remedies for his recovery, but the king could not be freed from the disease. Suffering greatly, he performed great charities like Tula-dana (weighing oneself against gold/grains), but his condition only worsened. He wandered to various holy places and performed rituals and worshiped deities, but the king remained diseased.

Distressed, the king decided to end his life by drowning or by consuming poison, and his people were deeply troubled. Suddenly, the great yogi Ashtavakra arrived at his palace. The king and his relatives bowed to him and worshiped him. After feeding the sage, the king, with folded hands and bowed head, spoke to him.

Dharmadatta said: I am blessed and fulfilled by your arrival, O Brahmin, for you are a yogi of the highest order. I was ready to end my life, but your arrival is a great blessing. By the merit of my past deeds, I have received your darshan, and surely I shall not fall into hell.

Vashistha said: Hearing this, the compassionate and joyful king of yogis spoke to the king.

Ashtavakra said: Why are you ready to destroy your body, O wise one? You will surely be freed from this disease and suffering. In your kingdom, the primary vow of Chaturthi has been neglected, and due to that grave sin, you are afflicted. The sin committed by the nation has reached you, and because you neglected the vow, you have fallen, O inferior king. First and foremost, that vow, which grants the four goals of life (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha), must be performed; only then do one's actions bear fruit. You performed many righteous deeds, but because they lacked this essential vow, they remained incomplete. You and your subjects will fall into hell; therefore, perform this supreme vow with all its requirements.

Having said this, the great sage, with a compassionate heart, narrated the greatness of Chaturthi to him. Hearing this supreme greatness, Dharmadatta was astonished and spoke to the great yogi with a joyful heart.

Dharmadatta said: My past merits are great, though I cannot describe them, for I have heard such a wonderful vow from you. Now, O ocean of compassion, tell me the true nature of Vighnaraja (Ganesha), so that I may always worship the great Lord. His character is not ordinary, for his vow is the best and grants all accomplishments.

Ashtavakra said: O fortunate Dharmadatta, listen to the nature of Ganapati. The formless Brahman is the head, and the manifested form is the elephant-faced Lord. Siddhi (success) is the form of illusion, and Buddhi (intellect) is the bearer of illusion; the Lord of Obstacles plays with these two illusions in reality. Through self-realized yoga, one attains his great vision, and that is why yogis call him the Lord who dwells in his own bliss. The syllable 'Ga' represents the union, and 'Na' represents the yoga; Ganesha is the master of both, and he is attained through the yoga of tranquility. The mind has five types of fluctuations, and there is delusion in each; by abandoning the deluded mind, one attains the Controller of the mind. That is why the Lord of Ganas is known as Chintamani. Worship him, O fortunate one, and you will find peace.

Having said this, the Lord gave him the mantra and the rosary with the proper procedure, and then he disappeared. The king was filled with great joy and considered himself blessed due to his good fortune. When the Krishna Paksha Chaturthi of the Malamaasa arrived, the king performed it according to the prescribed rites. Then, he propagated this supreme vow everywhere, and people everywhere began to observe it. Consequently, Dharmadatta was freed from his disease, and all the people became happy and healthy. The king worshiped Ganapati daily with a focused mind and gave various charities to please him. Later, he installed his son on the throne, retired to solitude, and with his wife, worshiped the Lord of Ganas through meditation. At the end, he attained the Lord of Obstacles and merged into him. All the people, in due course, attained the state of Brahman.

Thus, I have told you the greatness of this vow, which grants all accomplishments. Now listen to another story. There was a Kshatriya living in the Gandhara country who was a sinner, a thief, and a gambler. He was addicted to alcohol, lusted after other men's wives, ate beef, and was a liar and a false witness. He was wicked, caused quarrels everywhere, and was dedicated to violence. The king forcibly expelled this great sinner into the forest, where he killed various creatures. He was addicted to killing Brahmins, women, and children, and committed various sins daily. He accumulated wealth and enjoyed worldly pleasures with other thieves. Once, during the Malamaasa, he hid in a mountain valley, greedy for violence on the Krishna Paksha Chaturthi. He saw a man on the path and ran towards him with a weapon to kill him. The man, seeing the cruel sinner, fled in fear. The wicked man chased him for a mile but tripped and fell. Seeing him fallen, the man, filled with anger, struck the wicked man's head with a stone. The Kshatriya's head split open, and he became unconscious and died. At night, when the moon rose, the wicked man regained a little consciousness and, suffering, ate a small amount of milk-pudding (payasam). He died on the fifth day and went to the city of Svananda. There, seeing Ganesha, he attained the state of Brahman.

If such a sinner attained the state of Brahman through the power of this vow, then what to speak of those who perform it with knowledge? Many people have enjoyed their desired pleasures through this vow and attained the state of Brahman; it is impossible to describe it all. Whoever listens to or reads this greatness of the Krishna Chaturthi during the Malamaasa will attain all their desires.

Thus ends the thirty-second chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the greatness of the Krishna Paksha Chaturthi of the Malamaasa.

Chapter 33 : Description of the Observance of the Shami-Mula Chaturthi Vrata

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Dasharatha asked: O best of sages, O ocean of compassion, please tell me the greatness of the Sankashti Chaturthi for one who stays at the root of a Shami tree.

Vashistha replied: O King, I shall narrate an ancient history that grants all accomplishments to those who hear or read it. In the land of Panchala, there lived a Brahmana of the Atri lineage who, due to the consequences of his past deeds, became afflicted with leprosy. His body was covered with various sores, oozing pus and blood, infested with worms, and he became both blind and hunchbacked. Seeing his son in such a state, the father was deeply grieved; he went to the sage Atri, bowed to him, and asked for a remedy. The sage told him that if his son stayed at the root of a Shami tree and observed the Sankashti Chaturthi, he would be freed from his sins.

O King, the father then brought his son to the root of a Shami tree and tied him there with a rope to ensure he would stay. From sunrise until moonrise, the suffering man remained there, bound and in pain. When his father returned to take him home, the man was instantly cured; he regained his sight, his leprosy vanished, his hunchback was straightened, and he was free from all ailments. Seeing him in such a radiant state, all the Brahmanas were filled with wonder. Thereafter, he worshipped Lord Ganesha daily and stayed at the root of the Shami tree on every Krishna Paksha Chaturthi. At the end of his life, he attained peace and merged into the Supreme Brahman. Thus, one who stays at the root of the Shami tree gains control over even the impossible.

O King, listen to another story of greatness that grants all success. A great sinner and thief once entered a city. He broke into a house, stole wealth, and fled, but was seen by the people. The citizens raised a loud alarm, and hearing this, the King’s soldiers, armed with weapons, rushed to the scene. Knowing the gravity of the situation, they chased him in anger and struck the thief with their weapons. The soldiers captured the severely wounded thief and, as a punishment, tied him to a Shami tree before returning to the King.

O King, it so happened that the Krishna Paksha Chaturthi arrived, and the thief remained at the root of the Shami tree. By the fifth day, the thief’s wounds had healed, and he appeared perfectly healthy. The soldiers, noticing this, informed the King. The King arrived, bowed to the thief, and stood before him. Just then, a celestial chariot descended from the sky. Beings with elephant trunks (Ganas) emerged, took the thief into the chariot, and prepared to depart. The King, eager to know, asked them: O Ganas of Ganapati, what great deed has this man done? Tell us, so that we may also perform it.

The Ganas replied: Whoever stays at the root of a Shami tree on the Krishna Paksha Chaturthi, from sunrise until moonrise, fasting and worshipping the Lord of Obstacles, then feeds the Brahmanas and eats in silence, keeps a vigil at night, and worships the Lord of the Ganas again on the fifth day, shall attain all his desires. There is no doubt that he will immediately receive the fruits of his devotion and, at the end, will attain the state of Brahman.

Having said this, the Ganas departed with the thief, who had become one with the Supreme. The King, now devoted to Ganesha, began observing the vow at the root of the Shami tree on every Chaturthi. Through the merit of this vow, the King attained great status in the three worlds, moving freely in the assemblies of Brahma and other gods. He returned to his city of Champavati and flourished among other kings. Finally, along with all the citizens of his city, the King attained the Lord of Obstacles and merged into the Supreme Brahman.

O King, this is the great significance of staying at the root of a Shami tree on the Sankashti Chaturthi, which grants all accomplishments. Whoever hears or reads this will immediately receive the fruits of their desires through the grace of the Lord of the Ganas; there is no doubt about this. Many people have been freed from blindness and other defects through this; their stories are too numerous for me to recount.

Chapter 34 : The Description of the Conclusion of the Dialogue between Vasistha and Dasharatha

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Dasharatha asked: O Brahmana, please tell me the ever-auspicious greatness and the proper procedure of the vow involving the consumption of sweets and other items during the months starting from Shravana.

Vasistha replied: Ikshvaku, the youngest son of Vaivasvata Manu, was desirous of a kingdom and performed a vow that grants success. Intent on fulfilling the vow, he consumed five types of sweets on the day of Krishna Sankashti in the month of Shravana with great devotion. In the month of Bhadrapada, during the dark fortnight, he consumed only curd. In Ashvina, he performed a wonderful vow by remaining without water. In Kartika, the king remained steadfast in his discipline by consuming only milk. In Margashirsha, he worshipped the Lord of Ganas (Ganesha) while consuming only water. In Pausha, he performed the vow by consuming only cow urine. In Magha, for the sake of the vow, the king consumed white sesame seeds. In Phalguna, he consumed ghee mixed with sugar. In Chaitra, he worshipped Vinayaka and consumed Panchagavya. In Vaishakha, the observer of the vow consumed lotus seeds. In Jyeshtha, he performed an excellent vow by consuming cow ghee. In Ashadha, remaining disciplined, he consumed only honey after worshipping Lord Ganesha at moonrise.

O wise one, by the power of this very vow, Sudyumna, the son of Manu, who was the eldest, became the king. Later, the sages made him the primary king, and Manu made Ikshvaku, who possessed great radiance, the emperor of the world. O King, if a human desires to achieve the impossible, they can certainly attain it through the path of righteousness; there is no doubt about this. In this way, O King, many people, through the power of the merit of this vow, attained the state of Brahman on this earth.

Therefore, O tiger among kings, you are currently in a sinful state. The vow has been neglected in your kingdom, and you, O lowly king, do not realize it. Therefore, perform this vow along with your people. You will surely be blessed with sons and will eventually attain the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha).

Mudgala said: King Dasharatha bowed to him and went to his city, where he performed the excellent vow along with his people. Then, the king had this auspicious vow performed everywhere, and all the people on earth performed the great vow of Ganesha. By the power of the merit of the vow, Lord Vishnu himself appeared, dividing his own self into four parts as the sons of that great soul. Rama, Bharata, Lakshmana, and Shatrughna—these four sons were born, all endowed with great strength. In the end, the powerful Dasharatha attained the Lord of Obstacles, and the people became free from sorrow and attained the state of Brahman.

Whoever hears or reads this dialogue between the wise sage Vasistha and Dasharatha regarding the vow, to him the Lord of Obstacles directly grants excellent fruits. He is blessed with sons, grandsons, wealth, and grain. He becomes free from various diseases, attains great prosperity, and in the end, there is no doubt that he merges into Ganapati. O Prajapati, the account of the fourth day has been told in full. Tell me now, what else do you wish to hear?

Thus ends the thirty-fourth chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of the Conclusion of the Dialogue between Vasistha and Dasharatha."

Chapter 35 : Description of the Concluding Ceremony of the Chaturthi Vrata

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: O best of sages, please describe the concluding ceremony (Udyapana) of this vow, by which the practitioner attains the complete fruit of the ritual.

Mudgala said: O Lord of the people, the wise consider that the Udyapana of this vow should be performed at the beginning or during the year of the vow. O Prajapati, it should be performed in the bright fortnight of the month of Bhadrapada or the dark fortnight of the month of Magha.

If one has the means, one should construct a grand pavilion decorated with banana pillars and various ornaments. One should not be stingy with wealth but act according to one's capacity, decorating the space with umbrellas, mirrors, garlands of lamps, pearls, gems, and jewelry as appropriate.

For the bright fortnight, the worship should be performed at midday, and for the dark fortnight, it should be performed at moonrise. After smearing the ground with cow dung, one should create a heap of grain and, upon it, draw an eight-petaled lotus with the help of one’s guru. There, one should install a vessel made of gold, copper, silver, or clay, wrapped in a pair of garments.

A wise person should place a golden vessel upon it and then construct the Yantra of Ganesha. A metal idol of Ganesha, complete in all limbs and adorned with jewelry, should be installed and worshipped according to the prescribed rites by a learned Brahmin. One should invite twenty-one pure Brahmins.

One should chant the mantra "Gananam Tva" and perform a Homa (fire sacrifice) with a thousand, five hundred, or one hundred and eight oblations, or one-tenth of the total number of mantras chanted. Brahmins should engage in singing, playing instruments, reading the Puranas and Vedas, and discussing various scriptures, while narrating the stories of Ganesha.

Then, one should perform the Purnahuti (final offering), pour the Vasordhara (stream of ghee), offer the Bali (sacrifice), and distribute the Vayanaka (gifts of food). After feeding the Brahmins with twenty-one types of delicacies and twenty-one offerings, one should honor them with generous Dakshina (fees) and satisfy them along with their wives. The wise practitioner should also give blouses and ornaments to the women.

In the dark fortnight, one should offer Arghya to the moon, and in the bright fortnight, one should perform the ritual without the moon-Arghya. First, one should offer to the Tithi (lunar day), then to Vighneshwara, and then to the moon using the prescribed mantras.

(The mantras are as follows): "O Goddess, you are the mother of all lunar days and the granter of all desires; accept this Arghya offered by me. Salutations to you, O Chaturthi." "O Gajanana, salutations to you, the giver of various attainments; O Lord of wisdom, accept this auspicious Arghya offered by me." "O descendant of the Atri lineage, who increases the joy of Ganesha, O bearer of nectar for Rohini, accept this Arghya offered by me."

In the dark fortnight, one should eat only at night. In the bright fortnight, one should feed the Brahmins and others on the fifth day. One should keep a vigil on the night of the Chaturthi, immersed in the stories of Ganesha. On the fifth day, one should worship again with offerings and feed the poor, the blind, and the needy.

The practitioner should give special Dakshina to the Brahmins. After feeding everyone and satisfying them with gifts in the presence of the deity, one should respectfully pray to them for firm devotion to Ganesha. Then, one should offer the idol along with its accessories to one's own guru and give vessels to the twenty-one Brahmins.

A generous person should satisfy everyone and ask for their forgiveness. Thereafter, one should worship Ganesha daily with a focused mind. A wise person who performs the Udyapana of this vow in this manner will enjoy the complete fruit and become one with the Divine.

O Daksha, it is impossible to describe the full glory of this vow. I have described the Udyapana to you according to my understanding. If anyone hears or reads this account of the Chaturthi, Ganesha will grant them all their heart's desires.

Thus ends the thirty-fifth chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "Description of the Concluding Ceremony of the Chaturthi Vrata."

Chapter 36 : The Description of Lobhasura Obtaining a Boon

Daksha said: O best of sages, I have heard the great and primary greatness of the fourth incarnation of Gajanana, and by that, I feel my purpose is fulfilled. Now, please tell me the greatness of the destruction of Lobhasura, the character of Gajanana which grants all accomplishments.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, I shall narrate this ancient history, which grants all accomplishments upon hearing; there is no doubt about it. Once, Lord Shankara was residing on the best of mountains, Kailasa, surrounded by Parvati, his attendants, the gods, and the sages. Kubera, the lord of wealth, arrived there accompanied by his treasures. After bowing to Shankara, who was with Girija (Parvati), he stood before them. Standing near Shankara, he served the great Lord with a heart full of devotion while holding a yak-tail fan. By divine providence, he suddenly caught sight of the daughter of the mountain (Parvati), who was incomparable in beauty and adorned with perfect limbs. Seeing her as the embodiment of Maya (illusion), he became infatuated. Overwhelmed by the arrows of desire, he stared at her, completely agitated.

Seeing the Lord of Wealth staring at her with lustful eyes, the beloved of Shankara became filled with anger. Instantly, she caused the eyes of Kubera to burn with the fire of her wrath. He, too, became filled with sorrow. Due to his extreme agitation, his potent seed fell to the ground. From that, a son was born to the Lord of Wealth, who fell to the earth, endowed with radiance. Then, filled with shame, he bowed to the Great Goddess. Restraining himself, he praised her with various hymns. Then, the Mother of the World, being consoled by Shankara, showed her maternal affection and made him (the child) yellow-eyed. Kubera, having become ready for greed (lobha) due to the Mother of the World's influence, and because the child was born from a mother-seeking form, the child was named Lobha (Greed) by the Brahmanas. The Lord of Wealth took him and went to his own abode.

The sage Narada, knowing that Lobha was residing in Kubera's palace, went to the netherworld (Patala), absorbed in playing his veena. Narada went to the demon Tara, who resided in Patala, was a well-wisher of the demons, and was known to have been defeated by Indra. Tara, filled with joy, bowed to the Brahmana and, after honoring him, asked about the news that would be beneficial to the demons. Tara said: O Brahmana, I have been defeated by Indra and the demons have been severely crushed. Now, what should I do? Tell me, O best of sages.

Narada said: A son has been born to Kubera in Alaka due to the falling of his seed. Go there and take that son with respect. Protect him, O fortunate one, and through him, your great desires will be fulfilled. There is no doubt; follow my words. Having said this, the great yogi Narada disappeared. Tara felt extremely joyful and considered his purpose fulfilled. By his illusion, the lord of the demons went to Alaka. He took the sleeping child, Lobha, and returned to his own home. When the Lord of Wealth discovered that Lobha had been taken away by someone, he did not feel sorrow in his mind, as it was caused by an ill omen.

Tara brought the son and raised him with joy. He was amazed to see him playing with childish toys. The powerful Lobha used to uproot trees while playing, and with the force of his palm strikes, he would shatter formidable rocks. Seeing such various deeds of his, the best of the demons were filled with joy and considered their purpose fulfilled. Then, in his fifth year, he performed his initiation ceremony. The powerful one was taught the Vedas by the disciples of Shukra. Having studied the great demonic illusion, which deludes all the gods, Lobha became an expert in weapons and missiles. Then, bowing to the best of knowers, Shukra, Lobha respectfully asked for the complete method to attain everything. The great sage gave him the five-syllable mantra of Shiva. Lobha bowed to him and went to the forest.

There, the great demon performed penance, meditating on Shiva. He chanted with devotion, remaining completely without food. Smearing his body with ash and following the path of Shiva, he diligently pleased Shankara, the supreme granter of all. Lobha remained in this penance for many days, until a great anthill grew over his body. After a thousand divine years, Lord Shankara, being pleased, arrived with Parvati to grant him a boon. The demon did not realize that Maheshwara had arrived. Then, Shambhu sprinkled water on him and restored his consciousness. Seeing Shambhu, the lord of the demons stood up, bowed, and spoke with a joyful heart.

Lobhasura said: My penance and mantra are blessed today; my parents are blessed, O Lord, because I have seen Maheshwara. How can I praise you, O Maheshwara, the eternal Brahman himself? Still, I praise you, O Lord of gods, as my intellect is enlightened by your vision. Salutations to you, the Lord of the universe, the indweller of all. Salutations to Shankara, to Shiva, the storehouse of infinite virtues. Salutations to the three-eyed Mahadeva, the one endowed with power, Shambhu. Salutations to the attributeless one, the one who is the creator of attributes. Salutations to you, the immeasurable power, the Lord of the mountain, the one with the bull-emblem. Salutations to you, the creator, the sustainer, and the destroyer, the one who takes three forms. Salutations to you, who are capable of doing, undoing, and changing things. Salutations to the ever-peaceful one, who is always devoid of delusion. Salutations to you, the independent Supreme Lord, the one who plays naturally, the Lord of the gods who holds the trident. Salutations to you, the dweller of Kailasa, the one who smears ash, the one who is sky-clad (naked), the giver of all.

Having praised Maheshwara in this way, Lobhasura stood silently, his soul pleased, with tears in his eyes and hands folded. Mahadeva said to him: Choose the boon you desire in your heart. I am pleased by your penance and hymn, O best of demons. This hymn you have composed will increase my joy. There is no doubt that it will grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation. Whoever reads this, O king of demons, or causes others to hear it, or listens to it, will immediately attain their desires, for he will always be dear to me.

Hearing the words of Shankara, the great demon spoke to him with eyes blooming with joy, devotion, and humility. Lobhasura said: If you are the giver of boons, then grant me firm devotion to you. O Mahadeva, grant me the kingdom of the entire universe. May I be endowed with various types of prosperity, health, and all that I desire in my mind, through your grace. May I not meet death from anything created by mind or speech. O ocean of compassion, make me always filled with joy.

Hearing the words of Lobhasura, Shankara, whose mind was bound by the penance, said "So be it." Having made himself invisible, he returned to Kailasa. Lobhasura, with a pleased heart, returned to his own home.

Chapter 37 : The Description of Lobhasura Attaining the Position of Indra

Mudgala said: Lobhasura then arrived and bowed to his father and mother, and greeted all his friends and relatives according to their status. He married the beautiful daughter of Mahishasura, named Trishna, who was endowed with many virtues, and he became very happy with her. Thus, being a source of joy to his friends and endowed with great strength, he became highly infatuated with pride. Surrounded by demons, he came to the earth and built a city there that always fostered material desires. At the suggestion of the demons, he gave it a name that brought pleasure. Then, various groups of demons arrived in the city from all directions.

Lobhasura and all the demons then went to the great sage Shukracharya, bowed to him with devotion, and brought him to that city. Directed by Shukracharya, the demons—Devantaka, Vritra, Bali, the powerful Jrimbha, Narantaka, Praheti, Heti, Viprachitti, and many others—gathered there. Shukracharya then had the Brahmins perform the coronation ceremony and installed him as the king of the demons. All the great demons were delighted and returned to their respective homes, making loud sounds of celebration.

After a long time had passed, the powerful demon Lobhasura summoned those mighty demons through his messengers. Upon his command, all the terrifying demons arrived before him. Lobhasura honored all the demons according to the rules and spoke these great words to please them: "O Viprachitti and all others, listen to my beneficial words. Through your merit, I will conquer the entire world. If you, the elders and leaders gathered here, desire this, then follow my command, and I will act with all my strength."

Hearing his words, the great demons were delighted and said with deep emotion, "Well said, well said! By your grace, we will conquer the gods led by Vishnu, for your prowess is unmatched in the world." Then, highly elated, they brought Shukracharya there, and instructed by him, the great souls became ready for battle. Gajasura, Viprachitti, Devantaka, Narantaka, the powerful Tara, Praheti, Heti, Bali, Jrimbha, and others donned their armor. All of them appeared with their respective armies and various vehicles. Countless infantry, cavalry, those mounted on animals, those riding birds, and those on chariots—all the great demons armed with weapons came to Lobhasura.

Seeing them, the mighty-armed Lobhasura was filled with joy and set out to conquer the earth, causing the world to tremble. Where such great warriors stood, what could ordinary humans do against them? O king, some kings died in that fierce battle, while others sought refuge or fled. Thus, having conquered the earth up to the seven oceans, all the powerful demons became the rulers of the world. They installed the sons of those who had died as tax-payers, and in the places of those who had fled, they installed great demons. Those who sought refuge were also settled happily. Knowing of his prowess, Shesha also came to them. After paying the annual tribute and offering great wealth, the king of serpents was honored by them with sweet words and returned to his abode.

Then, the highly elated demons, accompanied by Lobhasura, went to the beautiful heaven with their great power and magic. Indra, armed with the thunderbolt and surrounded by the gods, bowed to Brihaspati and expressed his intention. Indra said, "Lobhasura has arrived with powerful demons. I will go to fight him, following your command, O Lord." Hearing Indra's words, the great sage Brihaspati sighed and said to him, "O Mahendra, that demon leader is endowed with a boon from Shiva. He can take many forms and cannot be defeated. Therefore, O god, abandon everything and take refuge in Vishnu. He is of great wisdom and will do what is best for you."

Hearing the Guru's words, the King of Gods, filled with grief and accompanied by the other gods, went to Vaikuntha. Seeing all the divine abodes empty, the demons entered them easily. Lobhasura, the king of demons, went to Amaravati, sat on the throne of Mahendra, and shone brightly. Honoring all the demons, he bestowed various positions upon them, and the demon leaders rejoiced in their enjoyments. The Gandharvas, along with the Apsaras, served that great demon, and the Vidyadharas and others also honored him. The great demons wandered happily in the divine gardens, enjoying themselves wherever they pleased. Bowing to Lobhasura, they lived without anxiety, saying, "There is no one equal to Lobha in the worlds."

Thus ends the thirty-seventh chapter of the Gajanana Charitra in the fourth section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 38 : The Defeat of Vishnu

Mudgala said: Indra, accompanied by the host of gods and led by Brihaspati, went to Vaikuntha, bowed to Vishnu, and praised Him with hymns. Pleased, Hari spoke to the great Indra with respect, asking why all the gods had come and what task was at hand. He asked why they had left Amaravati and urged them to speak of their great distress.

Indra replied: O Protector of the world, O Omniscient One, what can I say to You? Yet, hear the sorrowful tale brought about by fate. The demon Lobhasura, emboldened by a boon from Shiva, has arrived with the mighty demon lords. Instructed by our Guru, we have all come to You, O Janardana, leaving behind our kingdom and all else; please consider this situation. The demon is now in Amaravati, granting the heavenly kingdom to his fellow demons. You are proclaimed in the Vedas as the Protector; therefore, protect those who have sought refuge and kill Lobhasura, for You are the Lord of the gods.

Hearing their words, the mighty and wise Vishnu, filled with anger, spoke to the King of the gods. Shri Hari said: I shall slay the great demon Lobha along with his demon allies. Do not worry in vain; continue to rule the kingdom I have restored to you. Having said this, Madhava, along with Indra and the gods, swiftly mounted Garuda and went to Amaravati.

Learning from his scouts that the great Vishnu had arrived with a massive army, the demons prepared for battle. Lobha, filled with rage, came forth to fight, riding his chariot like Death incarnate. Seeing his massive form and terrifying power, the demons were initially fearful, but Vishnu encouraged them and prepared for war, joined by Indra and the other gods.

On the battlefield, Lobha, filled with fury, urged his demons forward. A fierce battle ensued between the gods and the demons, with both sides striking each other with weapons. Neither side could achieve victory, and the exhausted warriors fought until nightfall, when they paused due to their intense rage. For three days, a terrible battle raged. The gods, led by Vishnu and Indra, struck the demons with various weapons. The demons, terrified, fled in all directions, while the gods rejoiced, shouting praises to Vishnu.

Then, the demon lords, led by Viprachitti, returned with great fury and unleashed a rain of arrows. The gods were wounded and fell, which enraged Indra and the others, who then charged back. A fierce, hair-raising duel began. Indra fought the mighty Gajasura; Agni fought Tara; Vayu fought Viprachitti; Varuna fought the angry Bali; Kubera fought Praheti; Madana fought Hetika; the Sun fought the mighty Narantaka; Vishnu fought the angry Dantaka; Soma fought the powerful Jambha; and Yama fought the mighty Vritra.

The demon lords, proud of their strength, fought day and night without rest, raining weapons upon one another. Five days passed in this manner, with neither side gaining victory. Then, the powerful Gajasura, in a fit of rage, seized Indra and hurled him onto a great mountain. The King of the gods fell unconscious, and the other gods fled. Seeing the gods defeated and the army scattering in all directions, Vishnu became furious.

The mighty Lord hurled His terrible discus, killing many demons. It struck their heads and severed their limbs. The demons then surrounded Janardana and attacked Him with all their weapons. Vishnu, filled with great wrath, pierced Gajasura with ten arrows, rendering him unconscious, and struck Dantaka with seven, felling him to the ground. In this way, Vishnu struck all the demon lords, leaving them unconscious with a rain of arrows. The demon army fled in terror in all directions.

Then Lobha, filled with rage and delusion, approached Janardana and said: You have done well to appear before me, O Vishnu, but now I shall not let you leave alive. I cannot be killed by those who possess name and form; knowing this, why have you come like an ignorant man? Saying this, he struck Keshava with his mace. Vishnu fell unconscious, and the army was crushed.

After a short while, Vishnu regained consciousness and saw the King of the gods fleeing the battlefield. Lobhasura, laughing, restored his demons to awareness and returned to his city with them. Praised by bards and having distributed gifts, the great demon sat upon Indra's throne, shining in his glory.

Thus ends the thirty-eighth chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled The Defeat of Vishnu.

Chapter 39 : The Conquest of the Universe by Lobhasura

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Then, deeply distressed, Lord Vishnu, accompanied by the king of the gods, left Vaikuntha and sought refuge with Lord Shiva. Upon reaching Mount Kailasa, Janardana bowed to Shiva and, after offering prayers, narrated the entire incident in detail. Hearing that Vishnu had come to his abode, the great Lord Maheshwara consoled him with various appropriate examples.

Shiva said: O Vishnu, do not worry, for you are omniscient and the Lord of the universe. You have been defeated only because of the immense power of his penance. O Janardana, Lobhasura has performed this penance, and he shall perish only after enjoying the fruits of his actions; therefore, wait for that time. O great Vishnu, stay here with me along with the gods. Devoted to me, he shall never be able to come here. Having said this to Vishnu, Shankara, along with the gods, remained on Mount Kailasa.

Meanwhile, the great demon Lobhasura, surrounded by all the demons, was seated on the throne of Indra. The leading demons, such as Viprachitti, said to him: O Lobha, O great one, listen to our beneficial advice. Vishnu, along with all the gods, has gone to Shankara at Kailasa. Shankara is also our beloved deity and is worthy of our respect. If Shambhu protects our enemies, he will become our opponent. Therefore, send a wise messenger to Shankara to understand his intentions. If Shiva sides with the gods, O Lord, then we shall conquer Shambhu through the power of our penance. Do not think that Shambhu is the giver of boons; know that the gods themselves are made to act by the fruits of their deeds. The entire world, including gods, demons, and humans, is subject to karma. O demon, you are a great lord because of your karma. The gods do not grant the fruits of karma; rather, when they lose their merit, they fall from their positions. Therefore, abandon your doubt and leave Shambhu, who is siding with the enemy. Rely on your own karma, and that will bring you prosperity.

Having said this, the demon leaders fell silent. Lobhasura followed their advice and immediately sent the powerful Gajasura to Kailasa to discern Shiva's intentions. The great demon went to Shambhu, bowed to him, and conveyed the message sent by Lobhasura in a conciliatory tone.

Gajasura said: O Mahadeva, salutations to you. Listen to my beneficial words. I have been sent specifically by Lobhasura. Hear the message he has conveyed, which will bring you peace. You are the complete Lord himself and are always worthy of our worship. Vishnu, the best of gods, has come to your presence, O Lord. He is our enemy; therefore, abandon him, O Sadashiva. We are your devotees, and your friends are our friends. O Shambhu, protect us as you protect your friends and enemies. If not, we shall surely defeat you in battle. If you have become powerful through your karma, then enjoy the world. Lobhasura is currently endowed with all prosperity through his karma; therefore, O great one, protect us who are filled with devotion to you.

Hearing these words, Shankara sighed and spoke to Gajasura in a conciliatory and beneficial manner.

Shiva said: O Gajasura, tell Lobhasura my beneficial words after you return to that demon king who is surrounded by all the demons. Having left Vaikuntha with Vishnu and the gods, I am going wherever I please, but I will never abandon Janardana. If you have become powerful through your karma, then enjoy the world. The entire universe, movable and immovable, has come under your control; there is no doubt about this.

Having said this to the foolish demon, Shankara left his own Kailasa with the leaders of the gods and went to a mountain region. In a great forest, Maheshwara took shelter in a mountain valley, living like an animal along with Vishnu and all the gods, waiting for the time to pass.

Gajasura went to the assembly and told Lobhasura everything. Upon hearing this, Lobhasura was overjoyed. He then installed demons in various divine positions in Satyaloka, Vaikuntha, and the abode of Shiva. Abandoning the throne of Indra, the great demon went to the earth and lived in his own city, devoted to worldly pleasures. Being arrogant, he did not restrain himself and, being of evil mind, became addicted to women, meat, and wine. He considered himself blessed and successful, thinking, "The entire universe is under my control; there is no one greater than me."

Thus ends the thirty-ninth chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which narrates the story of Gajanana and the conquest of the universe by Lobhasura.

Chapter 40 : The Manifestation of Gajanana

Mudgala said: After a long time had passed, the demons, blinded by their power, became filled with greed and approached Lobhasura. The demon kings said: O Lobhasura, the gods are our enemies. According to the Vedas, the gods are the adversaries of the demons. They cannot be killed by weapons, for they are known in the scriptures as immortals. O great one, they depend on the performance of rituals; if those rituals are stopped, they will perish immediately. Therefore, make an effort to destroy their rituals. Why do you remain idle after conquering the universe? You will never be satisfied as long as your enemies are alive. In the past, Hiranyakashipu conquered the gods and ruled all the worlds. Then, the gods created Lord Nrisimha, who killed the demon king and restored the gods to their positions. Therefore, O king of demons, do what is beneficial for us so that you may live in happiness for a long time.

Hearing their words, the dull-witted Lobhasura ordered the demons to destroy all rituals. Then, Gajasura and others went out in anger, capturing Brahmins and destroying sacrifices wherever they found them. They broke the sacrificial altars, killed the cows, and destroyed holy places, causing the cessation of all religious duties. They demolished the temples of the gods and killed anyone who followed their own dharma. Everywhere, the demons installed idols of Lobhasura and forced the people to worship them. As a result, there was no Svaha or Svadha offered anywhere, and the earth became terrified as all Vedic rituals ceased. The people were filled with cries of distress. Some Brahmins fled to the forests, ready to die, while many others became fallen from their duties.

After a long time, the gods, suffering greatly, became devoted to fasting. They deliberated intensely on how to destroy Lobhasura, but they could not find a way. Distressed and bewildered, they hid themselves in the mountains. There, the great yogi Raibhya, who is dear to all, arrived. He was an avadhuta, appearing just like Lord Vighneshwara himself. The gods, led by Shiva, bowed to him and, emaciated and miserable, asked for a way to destroy greed. Seeing their plight, the compassionate and wise Raibhya spoke to the gods.

Raibhya said: Listen, O Vishnu, Shiva, and other sages. Lobhasura is a great sinner and is undoubtedly invincible. However, if you worship Vighnaraja with devotion, you will surely be able to defeat him. He is the Lord of Wisdom and will guide your intellect. Chant the mantra of Gajanana according to the prescribed rules. Having said this, the great yogi departed. The gods were filled with joy. They began to chant the mantra of Gajanana with devotion and performed penance. Pleased by their fasting and devotion, Gajanana appeared before them after one hundred years. Seeing Gajanana, the gods and sages were overjoyed and worshipped him with affection. With folded hands, they praised him and bowed down.

The gods and sages said: O Gajanana, salutations to you, the seed of all. Salutations to the formless Ganesha, the Lord of Obstacles. Salutations to the infinite, the single-tusked Heramba. Salutations to the four-armed Lord of all, who is worthy of worship by everyone. You are the giver of all success, the embodiment of gods and demons, the mover of intellect, and the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi. You are beyond qualities, yet the Lord of qualities, the embodiment of qualities, and the source of qualities. You are the essence of Maya and the one who moves Maya, the Supreme Soul. You are the beginning of all, the most generous, and the embodiment of valor. Salutations to you who dwell in Svananda and grant the bliss of Svananda. You are the form of Yoga, the Lord of Yogas, and the giver of peace. Salutations to you, the Lord of Brahman and the Brahman of the Brahmins. Salutations to the one who has no support, yet is the support of all. Salutations to the one who is without beginning, middle, or end, yet is the beginning, middle, and end. Salutations to the one who holds the axe and the goad, the three-eyed one with the large belly. Salutations to the one who rides the mouse and has the mouse as his emblem. Salutations to you, the complete one, the abode of all, the giver of happiness and bliss. You are the creator of the world, the destroyer of all, the Lord of the Ganas who is devoid of qualities. Salutations to you, the formless one, the supreme enjoyer, the one beyond ordinary knowledge, the great Sankhya. Salutations to you, the elephant-faced one who always dwells in the intellect of the wise.

The Brahman that is beyond knowledge is signified by the elephant. By the sign of the elephant, the great yogis perceive you, Gajanana. Just as a person is known by the features of their face, you are known by the yogis through the sign of the formless. Because your elephant form is formless, it is called your face. Salutations to you, Gajanana, who grants the attainment of Ganesha. How can we praise you, O Lord of the Ganas, when even the Vedas and the yogis seek refuge in you to attain peace?

After praising Vighneshwara, they bowed to him again and again. The Lord of the Ganas, knowing their hearts, spoke to them, bringing them joy. Gajanana said: O gods and sages, the hymn you have composed will be a source of all success forever. I am bound by your devotion and will grant whatever you desire. I am especially pleased by the reading and hearing of this hymn. I am the giver of boons; ask for what you desire, and I will grant it, for I am pleased by your penance, devotion, and this hymn.

Hearing the words of Gajanana, the gods and sages were delighted and replied with bows, asking for what would bring them happiness. The gods and sages said: O Lord, the three worlds are terrified by Lobhasura. Because of the lack of rituals, we have been forced into fasting. Therefore, O Gajanana, kill this demon king who is invincible to all. Grant us firm devotion to you, devoid of greed.

Hearing their words, Gajanana said: Do not fear, O wise ones. I will kill this great demon. You will have devotion to me that is free from greed, and I will surely defeat this demon king now. Having said this to the sages and gods, the glorious Gajanana remained there to accomplish their task.

Chapter 41 : Sukracarya Elucidates

Mudgala said: Gajanana sent Shiva as a messenger to that place. Having gone there, he spoke to the demon king Daksha; listen to those words.

Shiva said: Listen, O Lobhasura, to my beneficial words. If you act according to them with peace, you will be happy. Gajanana is known as the Lord of the Brahmas himself. He has arrived, surrounded by all the gods, the king of gods, and the sages. I have been specifically sent by him, who is stationed ten yojanas away at the edge of the city, O Lord of demons. You have become fearless due to my boon, O giver of honor; therefore, the Lord has sent me for your protection. Having brought the universe under your control, you are now the lord of all, constantly treating the gods like animals through the demons. Now, abandoning enmity, show affection, O great-minded one. Let the gods go to their own positions and the Brahmins perform their duties. Behave in friendship with Gajanana, O demon, and enjoy the pleasures; otherwise, he will destroy you.

Hearing Shiva's words, the great demon, filled with anger and pride, spoke to him, dismissing the great Lord.

Lobhasura said: Who is this Gajanana, O Shambhu? You are merely a divider. Why are you deluding me now, taking the side of the gods? Now, O Mahadeva, I will kill you in my anger. What can you do? You are just a messenger; go to the god Gajanana. Tell him my words: I will kill him along with the gods, there is no doubt; behold my great prowess.

Being thus rebuked, Shambhu, with eyes reddened by anger, spoke to the great demon, who was foolish and lacked discernment.

Shiva said: You are undoubtedly joined with the yoga of death, O sinner. That is why you insult Gajanana, the Lord of Brahma.

Having said this to the foolish one, Shiva went to the Lord Gajanana, bowed to him first, and narrated the entire incident again. Hearing those cruel words, the powerful Gajanana, with eyes reddened by anger, threw his axe. The axe, resembling an infinite number of suns and causing fear of death, arrived at the demon city, surrounded by garlands of flames. Seeing that brilliant great weapon, the demon hosts were terrified and fled, filled with cries of distress. Seeing Lobha sitting in the assembly, the demon leaders spoke terrible words that brought fear to everyone.

The citizens said: Why are you sitting here, O great demon, amidst the flames? A cruel weapon has arrived and is burning the demons.

Hearing their words, the demon, filled with both fear and excitement, spoke to the demon lords with great anger.

Lobhasura said: Gajanana has arrived with the gods, there is no doubt. He is certainly capable, as his weapon's power is such.

Hearing his words, the mighty demon leaders said with folded hands: Do not fear, O best of beings. We will kill Gajanana while you watch, O giver of honor. Having said this, they all went forth, armed and ready to fight. Then, they saw that great, brilliant, and cruel weapon of the Lord of Brahma, which illuminated the destruction. It burned the demons everywhere with its sharp heat; they all fled, terrified and crying out in distress. Then, the demon leaders released a great cloud-weapon. The clouds rained down terrible torrents of water from all sides. That great weapon dried up all the water instantly, and the clouds, losing their brilliance, became powerless. The axe then burned the demon leaders and chiefs with its flames, and they fled in fear in all directions.

Seeing this great wonder, Lobhasura arrived, mounted on his chariot, filled with great brilliance, eager to stop it. As the demon king watched, a flame suddenly appeared and burned his chariot, horses, and all his weapons. Then the demon king fled and went to Shukra. Bowing to that great soul, he asked with humility.

Lobhasura said: Master, the god Gajanana has arrived, and he is very powerful. He burned my chariot with an irresistible weapon. I do not see any way to stop it. Therefore, O Lord, I have come to your refuge; protect me, O sage. May I not meet death through mind or speech. How will he, who bears a name and form, burn me, O Lord? If my death has arrived, then let it be so now. I will never go to the refuge of the gods, O Lord. Therefore, tell me a beneficial remedy, O great soul. My life depends on your support, O king of yogis.

Hearing his words, Shukra, the chief of the all-knowing, spoke to the terrified Lobhasura, cheering him up.

Shukra said: Do not fear, my son; listen to my beneficial words. Do as I say, O demon king, and then there will be well-being. If Gajanana, the pleased soul, desires something, O great demon, that will surely become the truth, there is no doubt. He is called the Lord of Brahma; go to him with humility. He is not just a king of gods, O demon; he is the Lord who encompasses both gods and demons.

Hearing his words, Lobha spoke to him again, sighing: How can I go to fight him along with the gods? If he is a mediator, O great soul, I will go to him for refuge. I have no shame in that, O master; remove the doubt in my heart.

Shukra said: The great Brahma is the body and the soul, as stated in the Vedas. In their union, O great demon, you are the one who understands the word. When knowledge is destroyed, the formless Brahma is declared as happiness. Know that Gajanana is the one who grants supreme success. He is the seed-form of all, yet he has no seed; that is what is called the word 'Gaja' (Elephant) in the Vedas. Yogis attain Gajanana through the sign of the elephant; that is why he is named so by the sages who see the truth. Just as a person is known by the sign of their face, that Brahma is always known by the yogis through the sign of the elephant. The universe is established by him, O great-minded one, in the form of consciousness. He is complete with his own dharma and is stationed there. The kingdom of demons was created in the netherworld, the gods in heaven, and humans on earth—threefold in the past. When the gods, O great soul, became filled with greed and pride, they killed the demon hosts in the netherworld, acting as destroyers of the root. Then he, the embodiment, became a boon-giver to the demons. Ganesha, who knows all states, became the creator of obstacles for the gods. Then all the demon hosts, filled with strength, killed the kings of gods, O King, due to the lack of obstacles. When the demons, filled with great greed, became breakers of duty for the sake of destroying the lives of the gods, then the Lord of Obstacles himself, taking a body, killed the demons and made the gods return to their places, acting as a protector of dharma. When all gods and demons are devoted to their own dharma, then he, being in the form of Brahma, shines like a wish-fulfilling gem in the heart. The intellect itself is the form of the mind, and delusion is the form of the great-minded one. The Lord of the hosts plays with both of them as the Lord of Brahma. Since his vision arises from self-realization of the mind, he is called by the wise as one who dwells in his own bliss. Therefore, his refuge will be happiness, O demon king. Otherwise, having killed everything, you will die, there is no doubt. Gajanana's weapon is of the nature of Brahma; therefore, bow to him with devotion, and he will become peaceful.

Chapter 42 : Pacification of Lobhasura

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Shukra, Lobhasura bowed down to him. Agreeing to his advice, he went to seek refuge. At the edge of the city, the weapon of Gajanana, which was positioned nearby, blazed forth, confronting the demons. The great demon Lobha, terrified and trembling, bowed to the supreme battle-axe and, with folded hands, began to praise it.

Lobhasura said: Salutations to you, O King of weapons, the embodiment of Brahman among all armaments. O Lord of weapons, all various weapons are supported by you; salutations to you. Salutations to you, who possess a form resembling the fire of universal dissolution. Salutations to you, who possess infinite power and the immeasurable radiance of the sun. You are the embodiment of both the visible and the invisible, the destroyer of all pride, the establisher of righteousness, and the bearer of various forms. You are the very power of Gajanana himself, fixed and resolute. What can I praise of you? Salutations again and again. O protector of those who seek refuge, you who always dwell in your own bliss, protect me, for I am terrified. O Battle-axe, salutations to you.

As the powerful Lobhasura praised the King of weapons in this manner, it assumed a peaceful form. It then went and settled into the hand of Gajanana. Lobhasura, accompanied by Shukra, then approached the Lord of obstacles. Seeing the great demon arrive, the gods and sages were astonished and joyful, looking at each other’s faces. The powerful Lobhasura bowed to Gajanana and worshipped him with great devotion, guided by Shukra. The demon bowed again, and with folded hands, filled with emotion, he joyfully praised the Lord Gajanana.

Lobhasura said: Salutations to you, O Elephant-faced one, the bestower of various accomplishments. Salutations to you, the embodiment of Brahman who assumes the guise of the awakener of intellect. Salutations to you, O Heramba, who always dwells in your own bliss, the great soul. Salutations to you, O Lord of obstacles, who are beyond the beyond. O lover of gods and demons, you who are the embodiment of both, salutations to you for destroying those who are greedy and unrighteous. Salutations to you, O Ganesha, the protector of gods, the guardian of demons, and the destroyer of the pride of all. O Lambodara, Lord of gods, Lord of demons, O one whose banner bears the mouse, salutations to you, who are without beginning and the primordial form of all. Salutations to you, who are without beginning, middle, or end, yet are the embodiment of the beginning, middle, and end. Salutations to you, the Lord of Brahman and the protector of the great lords. Salutations to you, the giver of Brahman to the wise, the eternal bearer of peace. Salutations to you, who are the peace of all peace and the embodiment of Yoga. Salutations to you, O One-tusked Lord of all, O Curved-trunk one. Salutations to you, O Big-bellied one, the perfect one, the embodiment of perfect bliss. Salutations to you, the root seed of all, the Mother and Father. Salutations to you, the King of the eldest, the protector of the elders. O Lord of the Ganas, you are the seed, the universe, and the elephant-Brahman; because you are attained through this sign, O Gajanana, salutations to you. Salutations to you, who are beyond intellect, the embodiment of pure knowledge, the bodiless one who assumes a manifest form. O Lord of the Ganas, whom even the Vedas, Yogis, and Shiva are unable to praise, what can I say? Be pleased and grant me a boon. Through the greatness of your vision, I have praised you, O great Lord. Therefore, be a bestower of fearlessness and protect this servant.

Having praised him thus, the great Lobha bowed to Gajanana. The ocean of compassion, the protector of his devotees, spoke to him.

Gajanana said: Choose a boon, O great Lobha; I grant you what your heart desires. I am especially pleased with the devotion shown through Shukra. This hymn composed by you shall be the bestower of all accomplishments. Do not torment those who read or hear it. Surrounded by children and grandchildren, having enjoyed the pleasures they desire, at the end, they shall attain the world of self-bliss and become one with Brahman. Whoever praises me with this, he is dear to me, there is no doubt. He shall also be freed from his demonic nature. I came here filled with anger to slay you, but O best of demons, I do not kill one who has sought my refuge.

Hearing the words of Gajanana, Lobha, filled with joy and great wisdom, spoke to the Lord of Brahman with deep emotion.

Lobhasura said: O Gajanana, bestower of all, grant me a boon. Give me the nature of your devotion and the state of being dear to the Ganapatyas. O Lord, grant me a special place and an eternal occupation. Tell me what I should do, O Lord of all, and I shall perform it, O Gajanana.

Gajanana said: My primary devotion shall be yours, O great-minded one. Your affection for the Ganapatyas will increase daily. Remain fixed in your own place, filled with devotion to me. O best of demons, wherever I am not remembered or worshipped, there, O wise one, gather them in the beginning of all actions. Just as the gods are dear to me, so are you, O Lobha. Protect my devotees, O demon, and especially increase the form of my devotion within them.

Having been told this, Lobha bowed to the Lord Gajanana and, accompanied by Shukra, returned to his own city.

Thus ends the forty-second chapter of the Gajanana Charitra in the fourth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the final Upanishad of the Puranas, titled "The Pacification of Lobhasura."

Chapter 43 : The Description of the Hymns Offered by the Gods and Sages

Mudgala said: Seeing Greed (Lobhasura) subdued and peaceful, the pure divine sages were filled with joy and bowed respectfully to their Lord, Gajānana. After worshipping Gajānana according to the prescribed rites, the sages bowed again and praised Him with folded hands.

We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is formless, the destroyer of worldly bonds, always established in Himself, the bestower of His own vision, and attainable only by the immeasurable wisdom of Sankhya. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is worshipped by the greatest sages, beyond the reach of Vedic rituals, the giver of true intelligence, the possessor of wisdom, the peaceful one, the eternal, and the one who permeates all. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is of immeasurable form, residing in the heart, the singular truth of Brahman, the destroyer of illusion, without beginning, middle, or end, and infinite. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is the standard of the universe, the Lord of the world, inaccessible, the primordial one, without a beginning, and the one who bestows delusion upon those who lack self-realization. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is not earth, nor form, nor water, nor light, nor air, nor space, and is devoid of the five elements. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is not the universal, the subtle, or the causal state, who is not limited by the collective or the individual, who is beyond the Gunas, and is the ultimate reality. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is not the lord of qualities, not established in a point, not a mortal being, not mere consciousness, and not the seeker (Dhundhi); those who are free from duality speak of Him as established in Himself. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is neither the future nor the past, so how can we describe His form? Yet, He resides in the body of all beings.

O Lord, if You had not created anything, how would all this appear? Therefore, we worship with devotion the inconceivable and great-souled Gajānana. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who grants success to His devotees, happiness to those with desires, and liberation from worldly bonds to those without desires. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is served by the King of Gods and the demons alike, shining with equality, the infinite carrier, and the one with the mouse as His emblem. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who resides in the ocean of bliss and the nectar of sugarcane, and who destroys duality by His very presence. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, the Lord of the helpless and the pot-bellied one, whose four arms represent the four goals of human life. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who rides the great mouse, who is the death of death, attainable through the yoga of formlessness, and who is the giver of illusion to the deluded. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is the essence of the sun but without its glare, the essence of Vishnu but beyond His manifestations, and the essence of Shiva but beyond His dissolution. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who resides in the Great Goddess but is devoid of limited power, the Supreme Lord, the one worshipped by all, the mover of all, and the seed of all movement. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is worshipped by Shiva and other gods, by birds, humans, creepers, trees, and animals, and who is the Lord of all moving and non-moving beings. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, who is established beyond mind and speech, the essence of renunciation, the unborn, the imperishable, and yet the God who stands before us. We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana, for we are blessed by Your praise, by bowing to You, and by Your worship, as You have made us reflections of Your own form. The Vedas call You the seed of the elephant (Gaja), and the yogis recognize You by this sign; You are the one who moves towards the goal, and thus we worship You, Gajānana. The Puranas, the Vedas, Shiva, Vishnu, and the sages like Shuka are all baffled in praising You; how then can we praise You? We worship with devotion the Lord Gajānana.

Mudgala said: Having praised the Lord of the Ganas in this way, they bowed again and again. Raising them up, Gajānana spoke these beautiful words.

Gajānana said: O great beings, gods, and sages, ask for a boon. Pleased by this hymn, I shall grant you your heart's desire.

Hearing the words of Gajānana, the divine sages were filled with joy and, with tearful eyes, spoke to Him.

The divine sages said: O Gajānana, if You are pleased and are a bestower of boons, then grant us firm, greedless devotion to You. O Lord of Gods, You have brought peace by subduing the demon of greed, and by doing so, You have blessed the entire world. Now, O Lord, let the Brahmins and others on earth return to their duties, and let us return to our own abodes. You have established everyone in their own dharma, O Gajānana. We ask for one more boon: whenever we remember You, O Lord, please remove all our obstacles.

Having said this, they bowed to the auspicious Gajānana. Pleased, He replied, as His nature is bound by devotion.

Gajānana said: Whatever you have prayed for, O gods and sages, shall be granted without doubt, for you shall always have My remembrance. This hymn composed by you will be a source of success everywhere and will especially grant My devotion. For those who read or hear it, it will grant sons, grandsons, wealth, grain, and all prosperity. By reciting this hymn, acts of black magic and evil spells will be destroyed, and no harm from others will affect the devotees. It will grant victory in battle, success during travel, and will be excellent for overcoming enemies. It will break the bonds of those imprisoned. O divine sages, this hymn will accomplish the impossible. Whoever practices this for twenty-one days, reciting it twenty-one times, will attain all success. There is no doubt that this hymn, which increases devotion to Me, will grant Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha, and the state of Brahman.

Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas disappeared. The gods and sages felt a sense of longing. They then established an image of Gajānana in the southwest corner, which grants happiness upon sight. The gods and sages, filled with devotion, returned to their respective duties and abodes. Thereafter, humans on earth became established in their own dharma, and the entire world became joyful. This story of Gajānana, which destroys sins and grants both worldly enjoyment and liberation, has been told to you. Whoever reads, teaches, or hears this with faith, after enjoying the desired fruits, will attain the state of Brahman.

Chapter 44 : The Meeting of Sindura and Shiva

Daksha said: O Mudgala, I am blessed, and there is no doubt that through your association, I have heard this wonderful story that destroys greed and inspires devotion. I am not satisfied, just as one is never satisfied after drinking nectar. O Lord, please tell me now about the greatness of that sacred place, and describe the various incarnations Gajanana took for the sake of his devotees. O great one, you are omniscient; I bow to you.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Daksha, the compassionate and noble Mudgala was filled with joy and spoke to him.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, the incarnations of Gajanana are infinite, and it is impossible to describe them all for the sake of fulfilling the tasks of his devotees. Nevertheless, listen, I will tell you briefly about the incarnations of Gajanana. Gajanana was worshipped by Parvati and Shankara for a thousand divine years, and being pleased, he granted them boons. When they prayed to him to be born as their son to liberate the world, he agreed and disappeared. He was born to slay Sindura and was known as Mayuresha. After killing the powerful Sindura, he returned to his abode, Svanandaka. In this way, he took many incarnations in the house of Shiva and Parvati.

Eight crore incarnations of Ganesha are mentioned, known as the sons of Shiva, manifested for specific purposes. Some were born from Shiva, some from the great Shakti, and some from the union of both. Whenever a crisis arose, the Lord was remembered, and he would manifest as a son to bring happiness. After completing his task, he would return to his own realm, leaving Shiva and Parvati grieving in his absence. It is impossible to describe all the other incarnations he took in the homes of sages like Kashyapa. I will tell you a history that grants all success, listen, O Prajapati.

Once, Lord Shambhu went to Brahma while he was sleeping. He woke him for some work and then returned home. Brahma, upon waking, yawned with great anger, and from that yawn, a handsome, red-colored man was born. He was filled with a pleasant fragrance, like another God of Love. Seeing him, Brahma was astonished and asked who he was. The man replied that he was his son, born from his yawn, and asked for a place to live and food to eat. Brahma, pleased, granted him many boons: he named him Sindura, gave him the ability to enjoy all pleasures, and declared that no one in the universe would be equal to him in strength. He granted that whatever Sindura desired would be fulfilled, and that anyone he embraced in anger would be reduced to ashes. After receiving these boons, Sindura bowed to Brahma and departed.

Shaunaka asked: Why did the Creator grant such rare boons to one who had performed no penance? I have a great doubt in my heart.

Suta said: If a person who performs penance dies without achieving his goal, he is born in another life with the fruits of that effort, and the Lord grants him boons even with little effort. This clears your doubt. Now listen to the story.

Sindura, while traveling, thought to himself, "How did the Lord grant me such boons without me performing any penance? I must have been cheated by destiny. I will test if these boons are true by trying to kill the Creator himself." Thinking this, he went to Brahma in a rage. He roared and tried to embrace the Creator. Seeing this, Brahma was terrified and said, "O wicked demon, you have come to kill me using the very boon I gave you. Because of my affection as a father, I gave you these boons, but now Gajanana will surely kill you, you wretch." Saying this, Brahma fled to Vaikuntha to seek refuge with Narayana, and the wicked Sindura followed him.

Brahma told Narayana the whole story. Narayana sighed and remained composed. Meanwhile, Sindura arrived and spoke to Vishnu in anger, "O Vishnu, protect the Creator who has come here after cursing me. I will kill you both; see my prowess!" Vishnu replied, "I am of the sattva quality and devoted to the Vedas. It will bring you no glory to kill us. Know that we are under the control of Shankara, the destroyer of all; he will take away your glory."

Hearing Vishnu's words, Sindura left Vishnu and Brahma and went to Kailasa to fight Shankara. Seeing Shankara in meditation, he thought, "Why should I kill this naked ascetic like a fool? I will take the woman sitting on his left side, who is incomparable in beauty, and go away to enjoy myself."

Chapter 45 : The Description of Shiva's Deliberation

Mudgala said: Having thus firmly resolved, Sindhu grabbed the terrified and fainting Girija (Parvati) and flew away immediately. Due to the force of his flight, the Ganas, led by Nandi, fell down, but they rose again and began to wail in distress. Parvati, held in his hands, regained her consciousness and, being restrained by the demon's shackles, remembered the Lord, the leader of the Ganas.

Gauri said: O Gajanan, you who delight in wisdom, do you not know me, your supreme concern? O Ganesha, protect me, or else I, filled with devotion for you, shall immediately abandon this body. O enemy of obstacles, destroyer of the demon hordes, Lord of obstacles, Heramba, beloved of the pot-bellied, protect me, for I am devoted to you, O Lambodara, O preserver of love. Are you deluded by the expansion of Siddhi and Buddhi, or are you asleep at night? Are you busy contemplating gains and profits, or have you forgotten me and are resting comfortably? Are you occupied with the company of devotees and various offerings, O God of gods? Are you surrounded by modakas and engaged in various pastimes, O Vakratunda? Are you immersed in the enjoyment of your own bliss, having forgotten your maidservant, O great one? Are you so engrossed in your infinite plays that you have forgotten me, who am in great distress for the sake of your devotees? 

Alas, you are remembered by the gods who are skilled in drinking the nectar of Ganesha, as well as by the demons. Are you engaged in various rituals for them, having abandoned your maidservant who has no other refuge? Protect me, O most miserable one, O Supreme Lord, you who reside in the hearts of all. O Lord, you are the leader of the Ganas without delay; O Lord of Brahma, O God, salutations to you. Is not your primary name "the one who is proud of his devotees" in the Vedas, O great soul? Come, slay this demon, and protect me, your maidservant, who keeps your feet in her heart. Alas, it is not far, why have you not come, O Lord of wisdom? If you do not come, I shall abandon this body and will surely bring you infamy. Protect me, protect me, O ocean of mercy, and forgive my offenses. We, your maidservants, must be protected at every moment.

While Parvati was thus praying, Shiva, hearing the wailing of the Ganas, became filled with wisdom. Remembering Ganesha in his mind, he arrived mounted on his bull. In an instant, seeing the demon king, he struck him with his damaru. Then, the demon, seeing Shiva, ran to embrace him, but Shiva's weapons, including his trident, became blunt. Seeing this great wonder, the terrified Maheshwara also remembered the Lord of Ganas for the removal of obstacles. Hearing Parvati's prayer, Gajanan arrived in the form of a Brahmin while the two were fighting. Ganesha performed a great wonder there; he assumed an invisible form and dropped a battle-axe onto the demon's chest. Seeing the great demon struck and distressed, the Brahmin (Ganesha) said: "Listen, O demon, to these beneficial words. Release this Shakti, the mother of the world, or else Shiva himself, in an invisible form, will surely kill you." 

"I shall make Shambhu follow your commands. Abandon his wife and rule the three worlds." Saying "So be it," Sindhu followed the advice. He said to Shiva with a smiling face: "O Lord, be subject to the demon." You are not capable of defeating this demon who has obtained a boon from Brahma. Take the Maheshwara-Shakti and wait for the right time. Shiva said "So be it" and gave the Shakti to the demon. The demon, having become the master of Kailasa, rejoiced in his victory. Then, leaving Kailasa, he went to the earthly realm. The demon conquered the three worlds and became the lord of the world. Ganesha, in the form of a Brahmin, disappeared. Seeing this, Parvati became filled with grief. "Alas, this leader of the Ganas came in the form of a Brahmin, rescued me, protected you, O Shambhu, and then departed again." 

Then those two, Parvati and Shiva, who were the lords of the world, began to meditate on the Lord of Obstacles in their hearts through penance. Shiva, along with his wife, performed severe penance. When a hundred years had passed, Gajanan arrived. Ganesha said: "Ask for a boon, O Mahadeva, whatever is in your heart. Pleased by your penance, I shall grant your desire." Shiva, having bowed to him and praised him with various hymns, and having been worshipped by Parvati, bowed to the Lord of Obstacles and said: "O Lord of Ganas, be my son. Stay with me as long as I live, O Lord, salutations to you. You have taken various incarnations in my abode, but they were all for a short time, O leader of the Ganas. Do not do that, O Lord; dwell in my house forever. There, I shall worship you as my son, O Lord of Obstacles. In this world, I shall worship you as my family deity in the form of a son, with my heart focused on yoga, O Gajanan." Saying "So be it," the Lord of Ganas disappeared again. Shambhu returned to Kailasa, filled with great sorrow.

Then, after some time, Parvati was bathing. Maheshwara arrived there suddenly. Seeing him, the Mother of the World was ashamed and covered her body with her hair. Shiva stayed in the house. Then, once, the Goddess was with her companions. Jaya and Vijaya, the supreme companions, said to her: "The Ganas, led by Nandi, are all ours, there is no doubt. If we restrain Shiva, we shall remain as the embodiment of Shiva. Therefore, O great Goddess, make some Gana your own and under your control." She said "So be it." Parvati took the impurity from her own body, shaped it into a human form, and brought it to life. She said: "Stand at the door, O mighty-armed one, and always be under my command. Without my order, O son, do not let any man enter. I am here for bathing; even if my husband, Shambhu, comes, O son, do not let him in. Someone told me this, so do not do otherwise. By my command, forbid even my husband, Shiva." 

Saying "So be it," he bowed to her and stood at the door. She, along with her companions, remained happy. Meanwhile, Shiva arrived there with his Ganas. Despite being warned, the boy stopped him. Then Shiva became filled with great anger. Knowing this, she remained happy with her companions. Then, the angry Shiva ordered the Ganas, led by Nandi, to fight, saying, "He must be killed, there is no doubt." Then they attacked him with a barrage of weapons in their anger. The intense energy that Parvati had placed in the man made all the weapons ineffective. The mighty one struck all the Ganas with a mace. Then all the Ganas, filled with fear, fled. Then the angry Shiva sent the fierce Skanda. His weapons were blunted, he was terrified, and the commander was severely beaten. Then Shambhu fled and remembered the chief gods. Brahma, Vishnu, Indra, Yama, and all others arrived. Having bowed to Maheshwara, they stood before him. Shiva told them the story, and they went forth. Indra and the others, along with the Gandharvas, fought with the man. They too fled, their weapons blunted and filled with fear. Struck by the mace, Shiva himself became agitated. Then all the lords of the gods, led by Vishnu and the sages, held a deliberation there.

Chapter 46 : The Description of the Glory of the Impurity of Shakti

Mudgala said: After deliberating, the gods sent Brahma along with the Brahmins to Shiva to seek his affection, and he went to him. Seeing him, the man (the being created from the impurity) picked up a terrible mace and approached. Seeing him, the grandfather (Brahma) fled. The Brahmins said to him, "We are all unarmed, O son, listen to our words," but the man, knowing only how to fight, did not understand. Brahma went to Shiva and told him the whole story. Then Shiva, accompanied by Vishnu, his eyes burning with the fire of anger, went to the battlefield, and the man took up his mace. Upon arriving, the man, resembling Yama, struck Shiva in the chest; the trident fell from Shiva's hand, and he became distressed. Then Vishnu fought the terrible man with his mace, and Shiva cut off his head with a trident strike.

When the man died, Parvati, hearing this, became filled with anger and created nine crores of Shaktis. Commanded by her, the great goddesses set out to destroy the entire world. Seeing them, the gods became terrified. Narada and others, sent by Shiva, approached Parvati and bowed to her, saying, "Protect, protect the entire world, O Mother of the World, for it is born from you." As they praised her, the goddess, still filled with anger, said: "Why was my son killed by all these lords of the gods? I will destroy them while they are still alive, have no doubt." Having promised this, they went back to Shiva. The Shaktis were halted, and Shiva came with all the gods.

The Apsaras took the head of the boy who had died in the battle. Seeing him headless, Shiva became very sad. Shiva then told his attendants, "Bring the head of whoever you see first." Then Gajasura, who was very powerful and valiant, suddenly arrived, urged by Narada, to kill all the gods. Then, agitated, everyone fled in all ten directions. Shiva killed him instantly with a strike of his trident. By joining his head to the body, the gods made the man alive again. Everyone had exhausted their efforts, and then Brahma came and tried to revive him, but he did not come to life. Then Vishnu himself tried to revive him with his knowledge, but he did not come to life, and he became very distressed. Then Shiva himself tried to make him alive, but he still did not see him alive and became extremely sorrowful.

Then all the gods, including Shiva and others, praised Ganesha for the success of their task, using the Atharvashira Upanishad. They prayed intensely, "O Lord of Obstacles, make this task free of obstacles. Otherwise, this entire world will perish before its time." By remembering Ganesha, the gods regained their wisdom. The gods invoked the Pranava (Om) with Vedic mantras. The Omkara was attracted by the mantra and suddenly arrived; it entered the corpse and stood there, and then he arose. Then the gods, filled with knowledge and free from obstacles, seeing the elephant-faced one, the embodiment of the Pranava, praised the Lord of Lords with various hymns, O Prajapati. They worshipped him and made him the first to be worshipped.

Daksha said: "There is no doubt that this Ganesha is the direct form of Omkara. How then could he be born from the impurity of Shakti and be the bearer of a demon's head? O Brahma, you should resolve this doubt of mine. By hearing this wonderful story, I will become filled with knowledge."

Mudgala said: "O dear one, when Sati was burnt in the house of Daksha, she was reborn as the daughter of the mountain (Parvati). Shiva himself performed great penance on the Himalayas. Seeing her engaged in serving him, Shiva remembered his former wife. 'This goddess is not under my command, she went to Daksha's house; therefore, I will never accept this daughter of the mountain again.' Then the gods, afraid of Tarakasura, prayed to Kama. He went to Shiva's abode to enchant him, filled with passion. Shiva burnt him and left the Himalayas. The goddess became very sad elsewhere. Knowing Shiva's intentions through the path of knowledge in her mind, she became engaged in penance and worshipped Shiva in the forest.

When a hundred years had passed, Shiva was not pleased. Then she remembered the Lord of Obstacles with meditation. After one year, Heramba (Ganesha) was pleased. He, the holder of wisdom, created a difference in Shiva's mind. Then, filled with compassion, Shiva went to her in the form of a Brahmin. He stood near her and criticized Shiva with various words. Hearing that, the goddess trembled and tried to enlighten him with Shiva's deeds, but he criticized him again. The goddess left that place and went to that Brahmin again. Shiva, having discarded his disguise, showed his true form. Then she bowed to him and praised him with various hymns, meditating on Ganesha in her mind and praying, 'Be the giver of success.'

Then, with a pleased heart, Shiva granted her a boon, amazed to see her emaciated by penance. Shiva said: 'I choose you, O great goddess; I am always under your control, O sinless one. Through penance and devotion, there will be no separation. Your body is covered in dust, and your hair is matted. Therefore, the impurity of your body will be worshipped by all.' Having said this, Shiva disappeared. Parvati, filled with love, went to her home. For the protection of his devotee Shiva, the great word was fulfilled; the elephant-faced one was born from the impurity of Gauri's body. There is no one greater than Ganesha, who is worthy of worship by all, O wise one. By the boon of Ganesha, Shiva and others became truthful in their words."

Chapter 47 : The Description of Ganesha Being Subservient to His Devotees

Mudgala said: Listen to another story regarding the wearing of an elephant's head. There was a king named Mahesh, who was renowned and righteous. Once, Brihaspati, the preceptor of the gods, visited his palace, and the king, overwhelmed by his glory, honored him greatly. Pleased, the preceptor granted him a boon, saying, "O King, your head shall be bowed to by all, and you shall be worshipped by everyone." Later, the king, blinded by pride, insulted the sage Narada, who was passing by. Enraged, Narada cursed the arrogant king, saying, "O best of kings, because you have ignored me out of pride, you shall undoubtedly be born in the womb of an asura." In time, the king died and was born as an asura named Gajasura, possessing the form of an elephant-headed man.

Gajasura performed severe penance to please Lord Shiva, chanting the Shiva mantra with great discipline. After a thousand years, Shiva, the granter of boons, appeared and asked him to choose what he desired. Gajasura requested, "O Shambhu, grant me a mighty kingdom over the three worlds, strength that surpasses all, health, and universal worship. Finally, grant me liberation." Shiva agreed, saying, "So be it. Your head shall be worshipped by all, O Gajasura." Thus, it was for the protection of Shiva's word to Brihaspati that Ganesha later wore the head of Gajasura.

Listen, O Lord of the people, to another ancient history. Once, Ganesha was worshipped by Shiva himself. Ganesha, residing in his own idol, gave his 'Nirmalya' (the remains of offerings) to Shiva and revealed his greatness in a dream. Ganesha said, "O Shiva, you have worshipped me with single-minded devotion. I am pleased; listen to my beneficial words. Accept my Nirmalya, which grants all accomplishments. Whoever wears this flower from my head with devotion shall become universally worshipped and possess unparalleled glory." Ganesha then vanished, and Shiva awoke. Seeing the flower in his hand, Shiva was astonished and pondered the divine words. He realized that Ganesha is the only one in the universe worthy of universal worship, and the Lord had given this to test the greatness of his devotion.

Shiva did not wear the Nirmalya on his own head but placed it in his worship. Then, the sage Durvasa, son of Atri, arrived to see Mahesh and was honored by Shiva. Shiva, considering Durvasa a great yogi and a manifestation of Vinayaka, gave him the Nirmalya, explaining its great glory. Durvasa, after bowing to Shiva, departed. While wandering, he met Indra, the king of the gods. The yogi thought, "There is no one more worthy of universal worship than Ganesha. We are his devotees, and I am not worthy of this Nirmalya like Shiva." Seeing Indra, who was filled with the quality of goodness (Sattva), Durvasa decided he was worthy and gave the Nirmalya to him, explaining its greatness.

Indra, filled with royal pride, placed the Nirmalya on the head of his elephant, Airavata. Due to the disrespect shown to the Nirmalya, the king of the gods fell from his position. His Sattvic luster vanished, and the elephant became filled with fierce energy. The elephant, ignoring Indra, killed other gods and went into the forest, acting according to his own will. Indra, deprived of his glory and suffering greatly, saw the demons take over heaven. After a long time, instructed by his preceptor, Indra worshipped Ganesha daily. After a thousand years, Ganesha appeared, pleased by his worship and hymns.

Ganesha said, "O Mahendra, do not insult my Nirmalya. I am naturally subservient to my devotees and am pleased by your devotion. Lakshmi, the daughter of the ocean, will grant you success, and you shall regain your glory. The merit of worship resides in the Nirmalya; even by bowing to it, a person becomes successful. If one wears it with devotion, one should later place it on pure ground or in water where it will not be touched by human feet. By doing so, the person becomes one with my form. I was ready to cause you suffering for a long time, but considering you were guided by your preceptor, I forgive your offense. Knowing you have come to me for refuge, my anger has vanished."

Having said this, Ganesha returned to his abode, Svandaka. Indra regained his royal glory and, ever mindful of the Nirmalya and devoted to Ganesha, ruled without obstacles until his death. As for the elephant Airavata, he wandered the forest at will, obsessed with female elephants and enjoying various pleasures. After a short time, Ganesha entered his stomach, and through Parvati's boon, the demon's head was severed. With the head gone, the Lord of the Ganas appeared. Vishnu, seeing this, was filled with sorrow. Understanding the situation through meditation, he went to the forest, severed the head of Airavata, and immediately attached it to Ganesha. By invoking the Omkara and the mantra, he brought the Lord of Obstacles to life, and the gods rejoiced. The demon's head was discarded in the Narmada river, where a sacred pond was formed, imbued with Ganesha's glory. The red stones found in the Narmada are considered manifestations of Ganesha, granting all accomplishments.

Daksha asked, "O best of sages, why are those stones in the Narmada river considered Ganesha? Tell me, for that river is blessed." Mudgala replied that the four rivers—Ganga, Yamuna, Narmada, and Saraswati—originated from Brahma's water pot. The Narmada, feeling inferior to the Ganga, performed penance to Shiva for a hundred years. Pleased, Shiva granted her a boon, and the stones in her water became sacred. Still unsatisfied, she performed severe penance to Ganesha for a thousand years. Pleased, Ganesha granted her the boon that the red stones in her water would be his manifestations. Thus, the river became highly glorious, and seeing it grants the fruit of bathing in the Ganga.

I have told you all this in detail—the story of Ganesha, which destroys all sins and doubts. From the wearing of the demon's head to the story of Airavata and the protection of the devotee's glory, all is for the sake of the Nirmalya. Ganesha is bound by the words of his devotees; he is truly subservient to them. He even allowed his own head to be severed for the sake of his devotee. Having heard this wonderful story, O Daksha, now listen to the rest of the Gajanana legend. Parvati, filled with love, worshipped her son, and all the gods returned to their respective abodes.

Chapter 48 : The Slaying of Sindura

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Once, the yogi Narada went to the demon Sindurasura. Being worshipped by him, the sage, filled with joy, spoke to him.

Narada said: A supreme son has been born to Shankara on Mount Kailasa. He is Ganesha, the lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, who is satisfied by penance. Being prayed to by the gods, he will certainly slay you. Therefore, O demon, be prepared, for I am leaving, O bestower of honor.

Having said this, the great yogi Narada went on his way as he pleased. The demon king, having heard this, became filled with fear. Meanwhile, his ministers arrived suddenly. After consulting with them, the lord of demons went to slay the gods. Seeing him approach, Indra and the other gods fled. Terrified, all the gods sought refuge with Brahma. Sindura, filled with anger, arrived there. Brahma immediately mounted his swan and fled with the gods. He went to Vaikuntha to Vishnu, and the demon arrived there as well. Then Vishnu, along with the gods, quickly went to Shiva. The demon followed them there too. Shiva, for the destruction of the demon, praised Ganesha, the Lord of the Ganas, and Ganesha became the bestower of boons.

The powerful Lord Gajanana, holding an axe and mounted on a mouse, arrived there. Seeing him coming to fight, Sindura, filled with anger and intoxicated by his own pride, spoke, being extremely invincible.

Sindura said: Why have you come to the battlefield, O Gajanana? Being a child, you do not know me, O fool, urged on by the gods. My breath makes the Meru and Mandara mountains tremble. With the force of a strike of my palm, the universe would shatter into a hundred pieces. Shiva and the other lords of the gods have come here in fear. Seeing them, how have you come to fight, O Lord of the Ganas? Because you are a child, I will not kill you, O Gajanana. I will undoubtedly kill the gods who sent you. Whoever I embrace in my anger, O Gajanana, will surely turn to ash. Tell me, what will you do to me?

Hearing these arrogant words of the demon king, the Lord of the Ganas laughed and spoke to Sindura, the tormentor of the worlds.

Sri Gajanana said: O base demon, O mighty-armed one, you do not know my power. I am the one who dwells in Svananda, the complete one, and specifically, the Brahman itself. Having said this, Gajanana assumed his cosmic form. Crushing the demon with his hands, the all-pervading Lord smeared the demon's head. To protect Brahma, the wielder of the axe, Gajanana, became the Lord of devotees, filled with devotion. Seeing Sindura crushed, the gods were filled with joy. They praised him, having worshipped him with necks bowed in devotion.

The gods said: Salutations again and again to you, O Gajanana, the complete one, the embodiment of Sankhya, who resides everywhere in a bodiless state. Salutations to you, O immeasurable Heramba, wielder of the axe, whose vehicle is a mouse, the remover of obstacles. Salutations to you, O Lord of infinite glory, the supreme form of the supreme, the elder brother of Guha, the son of Shiva. Salutations to you, O son of Parvati, protector of the gods, the Lord whose body is worshipped by all, O Ganesha. Salutations to you, who dwell in Svananda, the family deity of Shiva, and especially the family deity of Vishnu and the others. Salutations to you, the embodiment of Yoga, the giver of peace through Yoga, the Lord of Brahma, the giver of the state of Brahman. Salutations to you, O Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the giver of Siddhi and Buddhi, the master of Maya, and the one who bestows delusion upon the deluded. Salutations to you, O Lambodara, who contains the entire universe within your belly, the one without Maya, the foundation of Maya. O Lord of obstacles, because the elephant is the seed of all, yogis know you by that sign and become of your form. Therefore, you are the elephant-faced one; how can we praise you, O Gajanana? The Vedas, the residents of Vaikuntha, Shiva, the protectors of the gods, Shuka, Shesha, and others are unable to praise you. Still, you are praised by the inspiration of your own vision.

Having said this, Shiva and the others bowed to Gajanana. Pleased in his heart and satisfied by their devotion, he spoke to them.

Sri Gajanana said: This hymn composed by you shall grant all desires. O gods, whoever reads or hears it shall attain the state of Brahman. Ask for a boon, O lords of the gods, for I am pleased by this hymn. I will grant all your desired wishes.

Shiva and the others said: If you are pleased and a bestower of boons, O Gajanana, then stay in the abode of Shiva forever as the remover of obstacles. Seeing you, we shall be forever free of obstacles. We shall see your manifest yogic form and thus find fulfillment.

Mudgala said: Gajanana then said "So be it" to the lords of the gods. He created his own realm right there by the side of Shiva. It was ten thousand yojanas in extent, O bestower of honor. He created Ganas like Kumuda and others, and delicacies like Modakas. From that time on, the Lord of the Ganas resides in the heavenly realms. That Ganesha-loka became the bestower of worldly enjoyment and liberation. Those with desires come to him and fall back to the earth, but those without desires merge into his body at the end.

This essence of the life of Gajanana has been told to you. By his nature as the son of Shiva, he became the Lord of Brahma. Any person who hears or reads this with devotion will attain success, O Lord of the people, and will be granted various kinds of happiness.

Thus ends the forty-eighth chapter named "The Slaying of Sindura" in the Gajanana Charitra of the fourth section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 49 : Describing the Glory of Ganesha’s Nirmalya

Daksha said: O Lord, I have heard the highly wonderful glory of the Nirmalya (offerings made to the deity). Please describe its character to me again in detail.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, the Lord Gajanana once resided in the hermitage of Parashara, and his character as a son has been narrated previously. Parashara himself installed an idol of Gajanana there with great devotion, and it was always well-worshipped. Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, and other gods used to come there daily at night to worship Gajanana, the bestower of success. Ever since the slaying of Gajasura, they would arrive filled with devotion, bringing offerings of celestial flowers, their faces blooming with joy.

Once, a royal sage named Virabhadra, who was fond of hunting, arrived there surrounded by his army and ministers. Having arrived, he worshipped Ganesha with devotion and stayed there for the night, filled with great fame. At dawn, he came to worship Gajanana and saw flowers there that had originated from heaven. The king was astonished to see the fragrant Mandara and other celestial flowers, and he remained there. The king closed the temple, stationed guards, and stayed with his army, longing to see the gods.

Then, at night, the gods led by Indra arrived. Seeing them, he worshipped Gajanana in secret. After worshipping, they all departed. The king, who had observed the worship, was filled with repeated astonishment. He then went to his guru, and with folded hands and great humility, asked him, as he was longing to see the gods.

Virabhadra said: O Lord, the hosts of gods come here daily to worship Gajanana. I wish to see them; please tell me the reason.

Shivasharma said: O King, cover the flowers that have originated from the Nirmalya here and there; then you will be able to see them.

Virabhadra did as instructed and returned to the temple. The gods arrived at night and acted out of ignorance. By merely touching the Nirmalya, they lost their merit. After performing the worship, the lords of the gods wished to leave but could not move. Then, the gods, led by Indra, realized through contemplation the reason for their lack of movement. They realized that they had touched the Nirmalya of Ganesha with their feet, and thus, being devoid of movement, they wondered what they should do.

Then the king arrived to worship in the presence of the deity. After clearing the Nirmalya, he saw the principal gods. He bowed to them with great devotion and praised them with various hymns. The gods, including Shiva, were pleased and spoke to the king.

Shiva and the others said: O King, the action you performed out of self-interest is painful. Now, provide a remedy for the gods so they may regain their movement.

Then the king, anxious with fear, bowed to his guru and asked for the supreme, merit-bestowing remedy for their departure. Instructed by his guru, the king meditated on Gajanana in his heart and offered the merit-bestowing flowers that had been sanctified by the worship of Ganesha. Through the power of this immense merit, the gods regained their movement. Vishnu and the others said to him, "O King, ask for a boon." They told him that their appearance is infallible and approved by the scriptures, so they would undoubtedly grant him his heart's desire.

Then the king, with folded hands, said to Brahma and the others, "May I have firm devotion to Ganesha by your grace." They were all astonished, and after praying to Gajanana, they granted him devotion to Ganesha, which bestows the state of Brahman. The gods returned to heaven, and the king remained there. After entrusting his kingdom to his young son and ministers, he worshipped Gajanana daily with single-minded devotion. In the end, he attained the abode of Svananda and continues to worship the Lord of the Ganas.

Thus, the glory of the Nirmalya has been described in brief. Its reading and hearing bestow the fruits of worldly enjoyment and liberation upon men.

Thus ends the forty-ninth chapter of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which describes the glory of Ganesha’s Nirmalya.

Chapter 50 : Arrival of the Demon Vighnasura at the Ashrama

Mudgala said: Previously, the great sage Parshva, along with his wife Deepavatsala, worshipped the Lord through supreme truth and penance. After ten thousand years, Ganesha, the bestower of boons, became pleased and, having been praised and worshipped by him with various hymns, asked him to choose a boon. Parshva and his wife requested, "O Lord, may you become our son." The Lord of Ganas said "So be it" and returned to his abode.

Thereafter, the divine sage Narada once visited Indra and, having been worshipped by the Lord of the Gods, spoke beneficial words. Narada said: "In the foothills of the Himalayas, a king named Abhinandana, blinded by pride, is performing a sacrifice. He gathered the Brahmins and, filled with the arrogance of power, said, 'Perform a sacrifice for me without Indra. If you do not, I will kill you all in my rage.' Hearing this, the terrified Brahmins agreed, saying, 'We will perform the sacrifice tomorrow or the day after; do as you wish.'"

Having said this, the great yogi Narada departed and went to Vaikuntha to see Lord Vishnu. Indra, filled with anger, remembered the Lord who takes the form of Time (Kala). The Lord, possessing a massive form, went to Indra. Indra bowed to Him, praised Him, narrated his sorrowful plight, and prayed for the destruction of the sacrifice. Kala, pleased, said "So be it" and descended to the earth.

Kala, the Lord of all who measure time, reflected: "The entire world is subject to time and is always consumed by it. Yogis conquer time through the power of yoga. Those who are pure in action become established in yoga; therefore, I shall strive specifically to destroy the fruits of action. Without action, men become impure; they do not desire the supreme yoga and become filled with ego. All are subject to birth and death, and there is no doubt they are under my control. I shall not perform deception just because Indra prompted me."

Thinking thus, Kala assumed a demonic form. In the form of Vighna (Obstacle), he went to the king, filled with rage. He broke the sacrifice with great speed, beating the Brahmins. Then, the wicked one, assuming the form of Vighna, went to various places. Deluded by power, he wandered about, disrupting all actions and tormenting humans to ensure the cessation of all deeds. He deluded the people, Brahmins, and yogis by assuming various forms. He turned morning into midnight and afternoon into evening, creating constant confusion. He turned night into day and day into night, such that it could not be described. He made all the worlds confused and devoid of action. Kala covered the moon, the sun, and the stars.

Then, the gods, filled with great fear and confusion, became devoted to fasting due to the cessation of all actions. They were unable to move the Lord of Time, as all gods, including those of creation, sustenance, and dissolution, are subject to time. Then, Vashistha and other sages took refuge in Brahma. Together with him, they went to Vaikuntha, fearful of Time. From Vishnu, the terrified Brahmins went to Shankara. Having praised Him with various hymns, they reported the actions of Time. Together with Shiva, they all praised the Lord Vighnesha. They worshipped Him with devotion and meditative penance.

After a hundred years had passed, the Supreme Lord Gajanana, the bestower of boons, went to those gods and enlightened the sages. Praised by them and requested to destroy the obstacle, He said "So be it" and became the son of Parshva. Parshva, the knower of supreme yoga, along with Deepavatsala, had previously performed severe penance, remembering the Lord of Obstacles. After a thousand years had passed, the Lord of Ganas was pleased and appeared before him. Parshva worshipped and praised Him, and asked, "O Lord, may you be my son. Out of parental affection, I will worship you in this world along with my wife. I will worship you, O Dhundhi, in three ways: through your divine form, through the path of yoga in my heart, and everywhere else. I am engaged in various actions, O Gajanana; by becoming your father, you will be my savior."

The Lord of Obstacles said "So be it" and disappeared. He took a body to destroy the demon Vighnasura. He entered the womb of Deepavatsala and, being highly pleased, accepted her milk and care. When the child reached the age of five in the sage's hermitage, all the divine sages arrived there with devotion. Seeing Shambhu and the other gods, as well as Vashistha and the other great sages, Parshva, filled with excitement, worshipped them according to the rites. He said with love, "My birth and actions are blessed, as all the divine hosts and sages have come to my home. Give me your commands, O Lords of the Gods; I am your servant, O Brahmins. I will surely fulfill them by the power of your remembrance."

The divine sages said: "By the power of your fortune, Gajanana Himself has become your son, who dwells in supreme bliss. We all pray to Him for the welfare of the world. The wicked demon Vighnasura has ruined the world, O Sage. For his destruction, you must make an effort, O wise one." Hearing their harsh words, the sage became filled with sorrow. In the meantime, Gajanana arrived there. Seeing Him, all the Lords of the Gods and sages stood up suddenly, bowed, praised the Lord, and prayed to Him with reverence. Knowing what was in their hearts, the four-armed Lord, holding weapons and mounted on a mouse, went to slay the great demon.

Thus ends the fiftieth chapter named the arrival of the demon Vighnasura at the Ashrama, in the Gajanana Charitra of the fourth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 51 : The Description of the Incarnation of Vighnaraja

Mudgala said: No one could ever know the location of the demon Vighna. The Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) went to his hermitage in the form of Time (Kala). Ganesha, assuming a hidden form, brought the demon Vighna near with his goad. Seeing this, all the gods were filled with great wonder. Then, the great Time (Mahakala) bowed to the elephant-faced Lord and, making himself invisible, became the form of fire. Knowing that the demon had taken refuge in the essence of brilliance, the elephant-faced Lord, in anger, became the destroyer wind. Then, Time himself became the sky element and began to pull the Lord of the Ganas, who was stationed in the wind. Knowing that Mahakala was in the sky, the elephant-faced Lord, the controller of the dissolution, became the ego-principle and pulled him in anger. Then, the demon Vighna took refuge in the Mahat-tattva (the great principle) and struck the elephant-faced Lord, who is the master of all beings. Then, the elephant-faced Lord, assuming the form of the Pradhana (the primordial nature), pulled the fierce demon who was stationed in the Mahat-tattva. Then, abandoning the Mahat-tattva, the demon became the lord of the Gunas and, as the invincible Time, began to destroy the Pradhana. Then, the elephant-faced Lord became the Bindu-Brahma and threw the four-footed Shakti (power) to destroy the end-bringer. There is no time beyond the Lord of the Gunas; thus, his prowess was shattered. Seeing the four-footed Maya, Time became terrified. Seeing the most formidable and supreme Shakti, which destroys the pride of Time, the demon Time took refuge in the Lord of the Ganas. Seeing the great demon Vighna surrendered, the gods and sages were astonished and sang, "Victory to the Lord of the Ganas!"

The demon Vighna, having bowed down, first worshipped the elephant-faced Lord. With the knowledge born from that vision, he praised Him with folded hands. Vighna said: Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, the elephant-faced one, the Lord of Yoga, and the giver of Yoga to the yogis. Salutations to You, who are both formless and possess a form; salutations to the Lord of all distinctions, who is devoid of all distinctions. Salutations to the Lord of obstacles, the Supreme Lord, who removes the fear of Time; salutations to Heramba, the Supreme of the supreme. Salutations to the beginningless one, the Lord of the helpless, the primordial image of all, the Lord of devotees, and the granter of all desires. Salutations to You, who dwell in Svananda and have a mouse as Your emblem; salutations to the Dhundhiraja who sits upon a mouse. Salutations to the elephant-faced one, who is without beginning, middle, or end, yet is the embodiment of the beginning, middle, and end, and who is worthy of worship by all. Salutations to the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the giver of Siddhi and Buddhi, and the great-bellied one. Salutations to You, whose form is beyond the reach of immeasurable Maya, who are the embodiment of perfect peace, who are beyond mind and speech, yet are the essence of all arts. O Heramba, please forgive the offenses committed by me through the ignorance of my intellect. Protect me, who am filled with devotion; O ocean of mercy, O protector of the supreme, grant me the best boon. Now I have understood Your true form, O elephant-faced Lord; You are the embodiment of Yoga, and that is why I have been conquered by You. Who else but You would be capable of conquering Time, the master of all? I am blessed, O Lord, by the sight of Your lotus feet.

Having praised Him thus, the great Vighna bowed to the elephant-faced Lord. The Lord, who grants all, lifted him up and spoke these words. Sri Gajanana said: The hymn you have composed for me shall grant all desires. Those who read or hear it shall have no fear of Time. I shall grant whatever is desired through the recitation of this hymn; may this hymn, which increases my joy, be the bestower of great Yoga. I came here filled with anger to destroy you, there is no doubt; ask for what you desire, for I do not kill one who has sought refuge. Mahakala said: Grant me Your firm devotion, O elephant-faced Lord. Protect me as Your servant, O Lord, as I remain near You. O Lord of Brahma, tell me my duty, and I shall perform it under Your command. Give me a place with food, where I may dwell eternally. O elephant-faced Lord, make my name precede Yours; may it be the grantor of all success and supreme Yoga. The Lord of the Ganas, knowing the mind of that best of devotees to be free from doubt, spoke to him. Sri Gajanana said: My devotion shall be steady in you, O sinless one. Whatever you desire shall always be fulfilled. Remain near me as a Gana, filled with strength, surrounded by various hosts of obstacles in the form of the great Vighna. My name, "Vighnaraja," shall be the bestower of great success, the grantor of the state of Brahman, and the fulfiller of all desires. Wherever I am not remembered, and where there is no worship at the beginning of actions, you shall consume all those who are arrogant, O Vighna. If gods like Shiva and Vishnu, or humans like sages, or Nagas like Shesha, or any other beings become filled with ego, O demon, then destroy them and enjoy the pleasures you desire. Those who are my devotees should be especially protected by you; be the one who ensures their path is free of obstacles.

Having said this, the glorious elephant-faced Lord disappeared. Vighna, along with his obstacles, became peaceful. The gods and sages then went to their respective abodes. O Prajapati, the parents (of Ganesha) were left there, unconscious. The Lord of the Ganas appeared in their hearts and spoke: "O parents, see me as the Chintamani, residing in your hearts. By merely worshipping the image and meditating upon me in the heart, you will always be satisfied, and there will be no doubt." "Whenever you remember me during great tasks, you will see me without doubt and will attain the peace of Yoga." Having said this, the Lord, who dwells in the heart, disappeared. They, being at peace, established the image and worshipped it constantly. Thus, the great story of the Lord of the Ganas has been told to you. These are known as the partial incarnations of Gajanana, O Vidhi. Gajanana appeared as the son of Varenya to kill Sindura, there is no doubt. According to the different Kalpas, the Lord assumes various forms; it is impossible to describe the elephant-faced Lord fully. These are the many forms of Gajanana that grant success; who can know or describe them all, O Prajapati? I have told you in brief the story of Vighnaraja, which destroys sins and grants both worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Chapter 52 : Description of the Glory of the Character

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Daksha said: O Lord of sages, please tell me where the sacred site of Gajanana (Ganesha) is established. Briefly describe its glory, which bestows great success.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, the Lord of Ganas, who assumed the form of an elephant-faced incarnation, was established with great reverence by the gods and sages in the southwest corner of the direction. In their respective divisions of the continents, the Lord, residing in the southwest corner, is worshipped by all humans, gods, and sages alike.

On the fourth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Bhadrapada, at midday, the Lord of Ganas was established by them, which enhances the joy of festivals. Devotees from the three worlds perform a pilgrimage there, especially for the attainment of various accomplishments. The Lord resides in the center of a site spanning ten yojanas, and the mouse sits in front of the Lord in the square area. Siddhi (Success) stands on His left side, and Buddhi (Intellect) on His right.

In the eight directions, the eight Siddhis are established. Behind Him are the eight Matrikas, followed by the eight Bhairavas. The guardians of the ten directions, armed with weapons, are stationed around the Lord. Gods like Shiva and Vishnu are positioned on His left. Sacred sites like Kashi and rivers like the Ganga are in the north. Sages like Shesha and others are positioned behind Him. Devotees, led by Mudgala, praise Him from the front. All the prominent beings of the three worlds reside there, performing their duties for the service of the Lord.

Those who bathe in the sacred waters and the ocean there with devotion, even if they possess small bodies, speak to one another about the character of Gajanana. They dance and laugh, absorbed in divine play. O Prajapati, those men who bathe in the Ganesha Tirtha, which grants great success to Gajanana, undoubtedly attain their desired fruits and become fulfilled. Those who perform a pilgrimage there attain the four-fold fruits of life.

Blessed are those men in this world who are fulfilled and live anywhere as devotees of Gajanana. They are said to have attained the state of Brahman, and their sins are destroyed just by His sight. If devotees of other deities die in this sacred site, they attain the realm of their respective deities and enjoy eternal pleasures, and at the end, they go to the Lord of Ganas for the attainment of Brahman.

O Vidhi, many men and women have attained success there; it is impossible for me to describe their complete character. By merely performing a pilgrimage there, one obtains the four-fold fruits, and at the time of death, the person undoubtedly becomes one with Brahman. I have told you briefly about the greatness of this sacred site; no one can describe its glory in full detail.

This character of Gajanana grants all successes and will be a source of all happiness, O fortunate one. I have told you this, filled with various stories, O Prajapati. Any person who listens to this with devotion or reads it will attain success and all happiness. There is nothing in all the scriptures as purifying or as capable of granting both worldly enjoyment and liberation as this.

He who gives away thousands of loads of gold daily, the mortal who listens to this attains that same merit, without a doubt. The person who gives away cows with devotion daily attains the eternal fruit of a thousand such acts, O Daksha. Those who give various gifts to Brahmins according to the rules enjoy the fruits of that merit and then fall back to earth. But by merely listening to this, one enjoys all desired pleasures and, in the end, becomes one with Brahman, residing in the joy of the Self.

Even if a man performs various sacrifices and rituals, they do not equal even a fraction of the fruit obtained by merely listening to this. All other actions lead to the cycle of birth and death, but this is said to grant everything and, in the end, bestows Brahman. I have told you briefly about the supreme happiness; what more do you wish to hear about the glory of Gajanana?

Suta said: Having said this, the great yogi Mudgala became silent. Hearing this, Daksha was also filled with joy, O best of sages. I have also told you all the glory that grants complete success, just as it was spoken by Mudgala to the assembly of sages. I am blessed and fulfilled by your company, having been engaged in the nectar-stream of Ganesha with all my heart. There is nothing higher than Ganesha; hence He is remembered first. He is worshipped by all, the Lord of Brahman, and the bestower of Brahman. You have heard this, O fortunate one, along with the Brahmins learned in scriptures. Now, tell me, what else do you wish to hear?

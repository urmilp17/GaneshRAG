Chapter 1 : The Description of Diti’s Grief

Shounaka said: O wise Suta, having heard the character of Vikata, I am filled with immense joy, for it reveals the nature of the Supreme Brahman. O disciple of Vyasa, whatever actions are performed by the Lord of the Ganas are undoubtedly the givers of liberation. Blessed is the fortune of the sages and my own, O Brahmana, that I have had the vision of one who is virtuous and the bestower of Yoga. Having drunk the nectar of the story of Yoga that flowed from your cloud-like mouth, I am not satisfied, just as a person is never satisfied after drinking nectar. Now, please tell me, out of your grace, the deeds of the Lord of Obstacles, which describe the destruction of the demons and are the supreme givers of Brahman-realization.

Suta said: Having heard this holy story from the mouth of Mudgala, Prajapati (Daksha), filled with joy, spoke again to him. Daksha said: Blessed is my birth and my deeds, through which I have attained your company. O you who are worshipped by the best of Yogis, please tell me this complete story that bestows Yoga. You are born in the lineage of Angiras as the embodiment of Yoga; there is no doubt that you are the savior of your clan. Now, tell me the greatness of the Lord of Obstacles, for even after hearing the greatness of Vikata, I am not satisfied, O Mudgala. What is the nature of his Brahman-form? Why does he assume a body? What are his deeds, and where does the Lord of Obstacles reside? Whose destroyer is he? Tell me his entire character, for you are a great soul who contemplates everything regarding the Lord of Obstacles.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, you are worthy of being worshipped by Yogis, and there is no doubt that you will become one, for your love for the story of Ganesha continues to grow. I shall tell you briefly the character of the Lord of Obstacles, which brings happiness and grants all accomplishments; listen to it with devotion. Here, I will tell you an ancient, supreme history regarding the dialogue between Kashyapa and Diti. The great demons, the sons of Aditi, Hiranyakashipu and Hiranyakaksha, were previously killed by Lord Vishnu through His Maya. Thereafter, Diti, filled with extreme grief, approached Kashyapa and remained engaged in his service, devoted to him daily. After a long time had passed, Kashyapa was pleased and told her to ask for a boon, to which she replied.

Diti said: My sons were killed by that Vishnu for the sake of Indra. Therefore, O Kashyapa, if you are pleased, grant me a son who will kill Indra. Hearing her words, he became filled with grief, thinking: "Alas, even a woman of virtuous character cannot be trusted by anyone. What shall I do now? How can I protect Indra, who is a protector of Dharma and a virtuous, quality-filled son?" Therefore, remembering the Lord of Obstacles, Prajapati said to her: "Perform a vow with great effort for one year, O great lady. Then, without doubt, you will have a son who will be the killer of Indra. Do not create any flaw in the vow, even if you encounter obstacles." Having said this, he meditated on Ganesha and enjoyed her company. Having placed his seed in her, he granted her the vow that would result in a noble son.

Having bowed, she returned to her home filled with joy and performed the vow with effort, following the rules of Prajapati. Then, Indra came to know of this incident through the words of Narada. Meditating on Ganesha in his mind, he heaved a sigh. Then, the Lord of Obstacles, residing in his heart, gave him wisdom. Indra went to his mother, Diti, eager to serve her. He served Diti daily, bowing to her as she performed her vow. Being a seeker of flaws, the powerful Lord of the Gods remained full of devotion. Nine months passed, yet he saw no flaw; the Lord of the Gods became distressed. Meditating on the Lord of Obstacles in his heart, the Lord of the Gods, filled with anxiety, served her with devotion, his mind always absorbed in her.

When nearly a year had passed, ten days short of the full term, he became extremely distressed, wondering what would happen. Then, the Lord of Obstacles created a great and wonderful disturbance. The Goddess herself was deluded and became confused, yet filled with joy. She thought in her mind: "A year has passed in the vow. My desire is almost fulfilled; there is no doubt." She thought that her son would leave the kingdom of the three worlds and make it Indra’s, and that she was now fulfilled as he was born from her womb. Then, the Goddess, overcome by extreme sleep, happily placed her head between her knees and fell asleep, deluded.

Due to her sleep during the day, her vow was broken. Because her head was between her knees, two openings were created. Seeing her in that state, Indra was delighted. Remembering the elephant-faced Lord, he took his thunderbolt (Vajra) and entered her womb through Maya. There, the Lord of the Gods saw the radiant fetus. Out of enmity, he cut it into seven pieces with his thunderbolt. Even then, due to the severe penance of Diti, it did not die. The fetus, the best of the radiant ones, cried while residing in the seven bodies. Seeing this, he was astonished and again remembered the Lord of Obstacles. He cut each of the seven pieces into seven more with his thunderbolt. Even then, the supreme, holy fetus did not die. It assumed a body and cried, trembling with fear.

The fetus said to Indra: "O Lord of the Gods, why are you killing us? Are we not all your brothers? There is no doubt." Hearing their words, Indra said to them: "Abandoning enmity, you shall become the dwellers of heaven." They agreed, and Indra came outside. Thereafter, Diti woke up and lamented. In the meantime, she gave birth to the sons. Seeing forty-nine of them, she spoke to Indra.

Diti said: "O Indra, why are there so many sons? Only one was intended. Tell me the truth, O great one, if you know, O Lord of the Gods." Indra said: "I knew of your intention, which was destructive to me. O Mother, I came for that reason, to serve you, there is no doubt. I saw a flaw in your vow, Mother; that is why I did this. I entered your womb with the thunderbolt, cut the fetus, and came out. By the power of your penance, these sons did not die. They have become living beings themselves; let them be the Maruts (winds)." Taking them, Indra went to heaven. She did not curse him; she was pleased with Indra for speaking the truth. Once, seeing the demons miserable and filled with grief, the chaste woman again approached Kashyapa and bowed to him. Filled with great sorrow, she spoke to Prajapati with humility, driven by her own selfish delusion.

Thus ends the first chapter of the seventh section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of Diti’s Grief."

Chapter 2 : The Meeting of Shiva and Parvati

Diti said: O Lord, my sons were dead, but through your blessing, a son was born to me who became a friend of Mahendra and assumed various forms. O Lord, the Daityas are filled with sorrow; what should I do? My efforts have constantly been in vain. O Lord, when you are capable and present, why have I received such sorrow? Tell me. The world is known to be filled with sons, grandsons, and relatives, but even that has become fruitless for me while you are here.

Mudgala said: Hearing these words from the distressed Diti, the Prajapati, the lord of yogis who knows the ultimate truth, spoke with a smile.

Kashyapa said: Do not grieve, O auspicious one; such is the nature of karma. The scriptures describe the world as being filled with sorrow and delusion. Daityas are known for sinful conduct, while Devas are considered the most virtuous. According to the Vedas, there is no eternal happiness for the sinful. What is stated in the Vedic texts is the absolute truth; have no doubt about it, and do not harbor attachment to these things. The sons you have borne, and their sons thereafter—this lineage will continue forever. Your lineage will never be severed. The Daityas are destined for mortality; why do you grieve in vain?

Hearing the words of Kashyapa, Diti, her body scorched by grief, spoke again to her husband, the best of the radiant ones.

Diti said: You are the creator himself, capable and beyond doubt. You are the Lord who can make the impossible possible and change what is destined. O best of yogis, you have renounced attachment to all things and remain established in the truth of the Vedas. I am filled with delusion and constantly afflicted by the three types of suffering. I am burning; tell me the path to renounce attachment, O giver of honor. By attaining peace through that path, I shall remain free from burning. Whatever is to happen, make it so that it happens with happiness.

Hearing her words, Kashyapa, filled with compassion, spoke to her with joy to show her the path of peace.

Kashyapa said: I will tell you an ancient story. By knowing it, you will become peaceful and worship Gajanana (Ganesha). Parvati, the daughter of the Himalayas, is your sister. She worshipped the Supreme Mahamaya through penance. Shiva, residing on the Himalayas, was absorbed in meditation and performed severe penance, always contemplating Ganesha. Parvati came there, eager to serve Maheshana (Shiva). Knowing him to be her husband, she served him with great effort. Mahadeva, realizing this, felt anger in his mind, thinking that she had ignored him to go to Daksha’s sacrifice. A wife who acts without the husband’s command is not considered worthy in the Vedas. Therefore, remaining without a wife, I shall always worship the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha). Thinking this, Maheshwara did not acknowledge her. Even when she arrived, he did not speak to her.

Then, the beautiful Parvati, filled with great sorrow, served Maheshwara with devotion and discipline. Seeing her daily service and her gestures of devotion, Shiva left the mountain. Seeing Sadashiva depart, Parvati was filled with grief and began to contemplate leaving her body, remembering the elephant-faced Lord (Ganesha). By merely remembering him, a divine inspiration arose in her heart. Parvati, filled with sorrow, thought: "Shankara has left me, filled with pride. He will never accept me; this is certain." She thought, "The Vedas say that which is difficult to attain through penance can be achieved; I shall worship that Lord (Ganesha) through penance."

Having decided this, the Goddess performed excellent penance, meditating on Sadashiva daily while living in the forest with her companions. She pleased Shankara through the Panchakshara mantra, devoted to the worship of the Linga and various penances. As much time passed, Sadashiva was not pleased; being angry by nature, he never acknowledged her. Then, the Goddess, devoted to daily worship, pleased Vighneshwara (Ganesha) by meditating on his single-syllable mantra. Abandoning Sadashiva, the Goddess became absorbed in the worship of Ganesha. Soon, Shiva’s resolve began to waver.

On the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Shravana, she made an earthen image of Ganesha and worshipped it with devotion daily. Living only on air, the Goddess worshipped that image with deep feeling, meditated, and chanted the supreme mantra. She chanted the mantra daily through the path of Purashcharana. Then, Lord Shankara became mentally inclined toward her. Shiva thought: "The Goddess is engaged in penance. Now I shall go to her, as she is following the rules for my sake. A human being sometimes commits offenses. Therefore, I shall apologize and accept her; there is no doubt."

Thinking this, the Lord of Parvati went to her. Seeing the Goddess exhausted by her efforts, he was astonished and enchanted. In the form of a Brahmachari, he criticized Shankara. Filled with anger, she rebuked him and left the forest. Then, Shankara, filled with great joy, assumed his own form. Seeing him, the Mother of the World bowed down. She praised Mahadeva, and he became the bestower of boons. The Lord of Lords, filled with affection, spoke to Parvati.

Shiva said: Ask for a boon, O noble one; you have conquered me through your penance. I will grant it; there is no doubt. I am under your control out of respect. I shall always remain under your command. Be my wife, O auspicious one; I am your ancient husband.

Parvati said: If you are pleased and will grant me a boon, O Shankara, then go to my father and ask for me in marriage. Grant me the dharma of a chaste wife, free from obstacles. Satisfied by that, O Mahadeva, I shall always be devoted to you.

Shiva said: Everything you have said will be fulfilled; there is no doubt. I will certainly ask your father and then marry you. Oh, what penance! Oh, what patience! Therefore, your bodily impurities are gone. You will be worshipped by all, O Great Goddess; there is no doubt.

Kashyapa said: On that day, which was the third day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, the Goddess, along with Shiva, worshipped Ganesha. On the fourth day, she performed a Homa with joy. On the fifth day, the Mother of the World broke her fast along with the Brahmins. Filled with joy and celebration, the Goddess took that image and immersed it in water, praying to Vighnesha with great effort. Then Shiva returned to his abode with joy. Parvati went to her father, bowed to him, and stayed there.

Chapter 3 : Granting of Boons to Mamasura

Kashyapa said: Lord Shiva, enchanted by the merit of the Ganesha vow, became absorbed in Him and appeared in the guise of a Brahmin. He went to the Himalayas, remembered the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha), and by displaying various arts, enchanted the mountain (Himachala) in an instant. Himachala said, "Ask for any boon you desire; if it is within my power, I will grant it without hesitation." The Brahmin replied, "O Lord of Mountains, give me Uma; I seek no other boon." Hearing this, the mountain became filled with sorrow and remained silent. Seeing him so distressed, the Mother of the World, Uma, said, "Father, give me to him; there is no doubt that he is Shiva himself."

Delighted, the mountain gave his daughter to Him. Shiva returned to his abode, honored by the gods. Observing an auspicious time and having worshipped Ganesha, Shiva, accompanied by the gods, went to the mountain king. Himachala, with a joyful heart and devotion, performed the marriage of the divine couple, attended by Vedic Brahmins. Taking Shiva, the Great Lord went to Kailasa. There, the great yogi enjoyed her company, and subsequently, Skanda was born. By worshipping Ganesha, Skanda defeated the demon leader Taraka and brought happiness to the world.

Shiva, united with Shakti, was deeply enchanted by her Maya. The great goddess, by her nature, remained under his influence. Once, Goddess Parvati was sitting with her companions and thought to herself, "I am blessed in every way. Leaving Shiva, I went to Daksha’s house out of pride, abandoned my body, and was reborn as the daughter of the Himalayas. Then, the Lord of Gods, Shiva, left me and went to the mountains, only to return and ask my father for my hand. Why did Shambhu leave me, and why did he ask for me again? Now, he is under my control; there is no doubt about this. My pride is fully satisfied, and I have preserved the secret of proud women."

Thinking this, the daughter of the mountain felt blessed. Her companions said, "You are blessed among the proud, for you have conquered Shiva through your pride. O Goddess, through your penance, Shiva has become under your control." Hearing this, the Mother of the World became filled with pride and laughed. From that laughter, a man appeared, resembling Kama, named Mama, a great being as vast as a mountain. Seeing him, the surprised Goddess asked her companions, "Who are you, why have you come here, and what do you wish to do?"

The man said, "Know me as your son, born from your laughter, O giver of pride. Command me, and I will fulfill your will in every way." Parvati said, "I was filled with pride, and from that, you have emerged. Be named Mama, and be a son devoted to pride. Worship Ganesha with devotion; He will grant you everything, and through Him, you will become famous throughout the universe." The Great Goddess then gave her son the six-syllable mantra of Ganesha with joy.

He bowed to the Goddess and went to the forest for penance. There, the demon king Shambara arrived, driven by time. He asked the son of Parvati, "Who are you, and why have you come here? Tell me the great reason." Shambara said, "I have come with respect to give you knowledge, O great one. With it, you will always be capable, without doubt." Having said this, the great demon gave him various kinds of knowledge. He practiced the Asuri (demonic) knowledge of Mama with effort. Having mastered the knowledge, he became capable of assuming any form and, filled with various powers, became joyful.

Bowing to Shambara, he spoke with a humble and devoted nature, "O great one, you have done a great service for me. Now, as your disciple, instruct me on what I should do." Shambara said, "Worship Ganesha with the mantra given by Shakti, O giver of pride, and ask the Great Lord for the sovereignty of the universe. May my death not come from elements born of elements, O Lord. Ask for this with all your heart, and no other boon. After obtaining these boons, come to my house, and then do as you wish." Mama agreed, and Shambara returned home joyful.

Mama sat there and performed severe penance, subsisting only on air, meditating on Ganesha in his heart. After a thousand divine years, the Lord of Ganas, pleased, went to grant boons to Mama, who was near death. The great demon did not realize the Lord of Ganas had arrived. The Lord of Ganas, filled with compassion, spoke to him while he was in meditation.

Shri Ganesha said, "Ask for the boons you desire, O great one, which are in your heart. Pleased by your penance and the chanting of the mantra, I will grant them." Hearing Ganesha's voice, Mama opened his eyes and, seeing the Lord of Obstacles, became filled with existence. He rose, bowed, worshipped Him with devotion, and began to praise Him.

Mamasura said, "Salutations to the Lord of Ganas, the Lord of the hosts. Salutations to the giver of positions and the one who takes the form of the Ganas. Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, the form of obstacles, the destroyer of obstacles for devotees, and the striker of others. Salutations to the Lord of the helpless, the giver of refuge, the form of the Lord of Lords. Salutations to the Lord of Brahmins, the giver of Brahman, the protector of Brahmins. Salutations to the one with immeasurable power, the form of power, the giver of power. Salutations to the Supreme Lord, the giver of the supreme state, the Supreme of the Supreme. Salutations to the King of the Elders, the giver of the supreme state, the Father and Mother of all. O Lord of Obstacles, who performs infinite pastimes, who gives self-bliss, who protects all, O Supreme Soul, Heramba, salutations to you everywhere. How can I praise the one who grants yoga, the single-tusked, the form of yoga, the ultimate truth? Even the Vedas, Shiva, and other yogis are unable to praise you, O Dhundhi. I am blessed in every way to see the Lord Gajanana, who is inaccessible to yogis. I am fulfilled. O Lord of Ganas, grant me the boon that I may not die from elements born of elements. May whatever I desire be successful, O Lord. Make me healthy and grant me the sovereignty of the universe, O fulfiller of desires. May there be no equal to me in battle, O Lord of Obstacles. Make me always victorious, invincible to Shiva and others, and the wielder of infallible weapons."

Shri Ganesha said, "You have asked for difficult things, O leader of demons. Still, I am pleased and will grant what you have asked. The hymn you have composed will fulfill the desires of those who seek it; there is no doubt it will grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation." Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, disappeared. Mamasura, with a joyful heart, went to Shambara's house.

Chapter 4 : The Meeting of Shiva and Mamasura

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Kashyapa said: Having first bowed to Shambara, he narrated the entire account in detail. This made Shambara very happy. Shambara gave his daughter Mohini to him. Mamasura, having attained her, lived happily with his beloved. After a short time, Shambara went to Shukracharya. Having bowed to that great soul, he narrated the entire story. Then, urged by Shukra, all the demon lords arrived. Shambara himself went along with Mamasura to meet Shukra. Seeing Shukra arrived, the valiant Mamasura bowed to that great soul and stood before him with folded hands. He worshipped the sage with a joyful heart, and after bowing, he sat on a seat at his command. Then the hosts of demons arrived there, filled with joy. Mamasura honored them all according to the prescribed rites. 

Then, taking the consensus of all, Shukra, the expert in statecraft, along with the Brahmins, anointed him and made him the king of the demons. He had five powerful ministers: Preta, Kala, Kalapa, Kalajit, and Dharmaha. The demons, the kings of the Danavas, and the Rakshasas were all filled with joy and then returned to their respective homes. To remove his anxieties, Mamasura built a magnificent city on earth, adorned with all beauties and surrounded by moats. People of various kinds, fond of worldly life, went there to reside. Everyone, belonging to different varnas and ashramas, lived there happily. The demons and others shone there as residents, and King Mamasura protected them joyfully. Two sons of equal character, possessing great brilliance and increasing the demon lineage, were born to Mohini and Mamasura. The Brahmins and others named them Dharma and Adharma. Mamasura shone greatly, endowed with brilliance, because of them.

Once, having summoned the great sage Ushanas (Shukra) through his father-in-law, and having bowed to him, he spoke excellent words. Mamasura said: By your grace, O Lord, I am capable, there is no doubt. I shall conquer the entire universe filled with worlds. O great yogi, command me, your servant who is subject to your orders. O best of Brahmins, instruct me as to what your desire is. 

Kashyapa said: Hearing the words of Mamasura, Shukra, the best of those who know the Vedas, looked at the handsome and devout demon and spoke. Shukra said: By the boon of Ganesha, you are capable, O great demon. Devoted to Ganesha, conquer the world easily. O lord of demons, never harbor enmity toward Vighneshwara (Ganesha); only then will you be able to rule effectively. Saying "So be it," the chief of the demons spoke to him with joy. Then, calling the demons, he narrated the great plan. They were all filled with joy and praised his words. Having consulted the Brahmins for an auspicious time, Mamasura set out. 

Accompanied by an infinite army of the four-fold forces, the demon lords riding various vehicles shone brilliantly. The heroic chiefs, fully armed, gathered around the great demon. They were of great strength and valor, capable of uprooting mountains. Accompanied by his ministers, who were experts in the ways of war, and his two sons, Mamasura shone on his chariot, endowed with brilliance. The great heroes went to conquer the earth, which is composed of islands. Having defeated all the Kshatriyas, they brought them under their control. Then, all of them went to the netherworld, defeated the powerful Shesha, and then, filled with anger, the demon lords went to the heavens to confront Indra. 

Indra, along with the gods, prepared to fight. Mounted on Airavata, he went to the battlefield. The demon lords, having crushed the gods with a barrage of weapons, captured Indra and went to Mamasura, filled with joy. Mamasura, the valiant one, sat on Indra's throne and shone, being served by the fierce demon lords. Seeing the messenger who had come from Satyaloka and knowing that he was prepared to fight, Brahma fled in fear. The Creator went to Vishnu and said, "Protect me, protect me!" Vishnu took him and went to seek refuge with Shambhu (Shiva). Knowing this turn of events, the valiant Mamasura went to Kailasa to fight with Maheshwara. 

When he arrived at the realm of Shiva, the Sun, the Shakti, and others fled in fear. Having deliberated, all the gods, surrounded by their forces, went to confront the demon lord. Having resolved to die, the gods led by Shambhu, fully armed, marched toward Mamasura. Mamasura's army, fully prepared and filled with anger, had come to fight the gods, protected by various heroes.

Thus ends the fourth chapter entitled "The Meeting of Shiva and Mamasura" in the seventh section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 5 : Description of the Reign of Mamasura

Kashyapa said: Seeing the gods approaching, the demons, filled with rage, struck them with various weapons and missiles that pierced their vital parts. The gods, equally enraged, struck back at the foremost demons, and as they clashed, they became intermingled. Blinded by the darkness of passion (rajas), they lost the ability to distinguish between friend and foe, striking down their own kin and allies in their delusion. A fierce and terrifying battle raged for a full day, and a dreadful river of blood began to flow there.

Then, the darkness subsided, and clarity returned. The demons and gods, now acting with discernment, fought with renewed intensity. The massive army of the gods was broken by the demon forces and fled in terror, while the jubilant demons pursued them. Then, the Bhairavas, Vishwaksena, and other powerful entities, along with the Adityas and the Shaktis led by Kali, arrived, filled with anger. With their great weapons, they crushed the demon army, forcing them to flee in all ten directions in fear.

Seeing this, the chief commanders of Mamasura, filled with wrath, attacked the gods with their weapons. The Bhairavas and the demons fought with great fervor, and a horrific battle ensued for the entire day as both sides sought victory. The leading demon commanders fell unconscious, and the demon army fled in all directions. Realizing this, the two sons of Mamasura arrived, bowed to their father, and went to battle with great devotion.

Then, revived by the sage Shukracharya, the great demon warriors rose again and fought the gods with extreme ferocity. The demons Dharma and Adharma, raining down weapons, rendered the gods unconscious in the fray. No one in the divine army could withstand their power, and the gods fled in terror. Then, Lord Shiva himself, mounted on his bull, arrived in anger, followed by the Goddess, the Sun, and Vishnu.

A terrifying battle ensued, so horrific that it is impossible to describe, causing the entire universe to tremble. Adharma fought Vishnu, Dharma fought Shankara, Kalajit fought the Sun, and the powerful Preta fought the Shakti. Kapala fought Kalrudra, and Dharmaghna fought Kali; all fought with weapons that pierced the heart. After a day of fierce combat, Kali killed the wicked Dharmaghna. Kapala was rendered unconscious by Shiva’s trident, and Preta was killed by the Shakti’s sword. Dharma was struck by Shiva’s trident and fell unconscious, and Adharma was similarly incapacitated by Vishnu’s discus.

The demon army was decimated by the divine weapons, and cries of agony filled the air. Rivers of blood flowed, and the demons, broken and severed, fled in all directions. Many died from the sheer force of the weapons. Seeing this, Mamasura himself, filled with rage, went to the battlefield. Seeing Shiva standing before him, the powerful and destructive demon spoke.

Mamasura said: "Stand, stand, O Mahadeva! Witness my supreme prowess. I have seen your strength, which surpasses all the gods." Having said this, the demon king readied his bow and released a powerful arrow. Shiva then hurled his trident to destroy him, but upon reaching Mamasura, it became ineffective. Struck by the demon's arrow, Shiva fell to the ground, overcome by sudden unconsciousness.

Then, Mamasura, in his rage, pierced Lord Vishnu in the chest with a single arrow, rendering him unconscious as well. The Sun and the Shakti, enraged, hurled their weapons, but all their arrows fell uselessly. Mamasura then pierced the Sun and the Shakti in the chest with single arrows, causing them both to fall to the ground. He then unleashed a fierce rain of arrows, crushing the gods in the battle. Seeing the gods dead or fleeing in terror, the demon king rejoiced.

He captured the gods, led by Shiva, and took them to his own magnificent realm. Having revived the demons and his sons through Shukracharya’s power, he joyfully appointed Dharma as the lord of Kailasa, Adharma as the master of Vishnu’s realm, Preta as the lord of the Shakti’s city, Kapala as the ruler of the Sun’s realm, and Dharmaghna as the lord of Brahma’s realm. He appointed Kalajit as the lord of Indra’s realm and others as rulers of the various worlds.

After returning to his own city, he threw the gods into prison and ruled with great power. The wicked Mamasura became the lord of the universe and felt his mission was accomplished. Consumed by lust and indulgence, he lived a life of excess, believing himself to be immortal despite having a physical body. Filled with arrogance, he forcibly abducted the wives of the gods, human maidens, and Naga princesses, enjoying them with his demon chiefs.

As time passed, he sent his cruel demon commanders to destroy the performance of righteous duties (Varnashrama-dharma) and established demonic laws everywhere by force. No offerings were made to the gods, and the sacred rituals ceased due to the mixing of castes. Sages were beaten or imprisoned, and all temples were destroyed by the demons. They desecrated holy places and sacred trees, forcing all humans to perform only the works of Mamasura.

Statues of the demon king were installed in every temple, and the destruction of all virtue spread across the earth. Everyone was filled with despair, and the people, stripped of their social and religious order, suffered greatly. Mamasura ruled happily, though the natural order, including the rains, became chaotic and fearful. With sin rampant, the gods remained in prison, fasting and suffering in deep sorrow.

Chapter 6 : The Manifestation of Vighnaraja

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Kashyapa said: Thereafter, the gods, led by Shambhu (Shiva), were filled with sorrow and fear, and began to deliberate on how to destroy Mamasura. Then, the wise Vishnu spoke excellent words, reassuring all the gods while meditating on Vighnaraja in his heart.

Vishnu said: We have been thrown into prison and are being subjected to fasting and various hardships by Mamasura. Great Lords, immense sin has been committed in the kingdom of this demon; therefore, devoid of merit, he will undoubtedly die. The Vedas describe Ganesha as the sustainer of all existence, and because he is the protector of Dharma, he is known as Vighnaraja (the Lord of Obstacles). By following the prescribed path of worship in times of distress, we shall propitiate him, and he will surely destroy this demon. He is the revealer of truth, yet he does not hold the form of the material elements; being beyond the elements, O Lords of gods, he will slay this demon. Mamasura, deluded by the demon kings and attached to worldly pleasures, has abandoned the Lord of Ganesha. Furthermore, O wise ones, listen: all the idols of Vighnaraja in the sacred regions have been broken by these wicked ones, and their own images have been installed in their place. In the past, the worship and remembrance of Ganapati were prescribed as the first act in all rituals, but Mamasura has destroyed this practice on earth. Therefore, Vighneshwara will certainly kill him; worship him with devotion, O gods.

Hearing the words of Vishnu, the gods, led by Shambhu, praised him, saying, "Well said, O Keshava." Then, the gods, contemplating Ganesha for their deliverance from distress, performed mental bathing and internal rituals (nyasa). Meditating on Ganapati, they all worshipped him mentally with various offerings and chanted his supreme mantra in their hearts. Following the single-syllable (Ekakshara) rule, they performed great penance, remaining hidden, fearful, and fasting.

When one hundred years had passed in this manner, the Giver of Boons, the Lord of Hosts, appeared to them at midday on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada. Seeing him, the gods were filled with joy; they rose quickly, bowed down, and worshipped him with mental offerings. Again, bowing to Vighneshwara, the gods, with tearful eyes and necks bent in devotion, praised him with folded hands.

The King of Gods said: Salutations to you, Vighnaraja, the destroyer of the obstacles of your devotees, and salutations again and again to you who specifically create obstacles for those who are not devoted. Salutations to Ganesha, the Supreme Lord, Heramba; salutations to the four-armed one who bears the banner of the serpent. You are immeasurable, beyond reasoning, and always dwell in your own bliss; you are the Lord of the Vedas and the Giver of knowledge, salutations to you. You are the essence of Sankhya, the embodiment of wisdom, the Supreme Person (Purusha), the Supreme Soul; salutations to Ganesha, the primordial nature (Prakriti), and the one who transcends the three Gunas. You are the Creator, the Sustainer, and the Destroyer; you are the embodiment of light, the deluder, and the Lord of the three worlds, salutations to you. You are the mover of various illusions, the Lord of those who possess Maya, and the one who bestows delusion, salutations to you. The Brahman, which is full of various differences, is undoubtedly filled with obstacles; salutations to you, who are full of bliss and equal in all states. That which is the supreme, nectar-like Brahman, devoid of all differences, is, according to the scriptures, associated with the obstacle of non-difference. O Lord of Hosts, you are the creator of both the differentiated and the undifferentiated duality; salutations to you, the Lord of both. You are the supreme bliss, the Brahman, considered the remover of obstacles; you are the giver of the truth itself, O Vighnaraja, salutations to you. We behold the King of Vighnaraja with our physical eyes; we are blessed, O Lord of Hosts, by the sight of your feet.

Having spoken thus, the gods, led by Shambhu, bowed down. Bound by their devotion and hymns, Vighneshwara said to them:

Shri Vighnaraja said: Ask for the boons you desire in your hearts, O great gods. Pleased by your hymns and your penance and meditation, I shall grant them. This hymn composed by you will grant all successes; those who read or hear it, O gods, will have their desires fulfilled.

Hearing the words of Ganesha, the gods were filled with joy; bowing to him again, they spoke with voices choked with emotion.

The gods said: Grant us, O Vighneshwara, unwavering devotion to your feet, through which the great yogis, free from obstacles, have attained peace. O Lord, slay Mamasura, who causes all suffering, destroys Dharma, and causes the mixing of castes. We have been thrown into prison by the power of his boons and are now being subjected to fasting by that demon.

Hearing their words, the valiant Vighnaraja said to them, "So be it; I shall fulfill this beneficial request." Meanwhile, O Devi, a strange event occurred there. The demons who had been previously stationed to guard the gods saw the activities of the gods and the arrival of Ganesha; they went and reported the entire matter with great concern.

The guards said: O Lord, the Lord of Obstacles has arrived, propitiated by the gods. Maheshwara and others have bowed to him and are praising him for your destruction. The Lord of Hosts has agreed to everything, without a doubt. Now, O King of Demons, do what you must.

Hearing their words, Mamasura was filled with rage; he summoned everyone and explained the situation in detail. Hearing this, they were filled with fear and agitation; bowing to him, they spoke words for their own welfare.

The demon kings said: How did the gods, while in prison, manage to propitiate the Lord of Hosts and make him a giver of boons? The demons have seen the Lord of Hosts as the embodiment of truth; he is never seen by those who are devoid of truth. Therefore, we shall undoubtedly kill him along with the gods. What will Vighneshwara do to you, who are destined to be free from death?

Having said this, the great demons made a terrifying sound and prepared themselves against the gods and others. Meanwhile, another strange event occurred there. Narada, filled with joy, went to Vighnaraja. Bowing and praising Vighneshwara, he sang with joy and said to the great yogi, "O Lord, slay Mamasura." Vighnaraja then sent the sage to the demon king to convey a message, and he went to the demon leader.

Thus ends the sixth chapter entitled "The Manifestation of Vighnaraja" in the Vighnaraja Charita of the seventh section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 7 : The Removal of Mamasura's Pride

Kashyapa said: When the demon Mamasura approached Narada, who was being worshipped by the demons, and was about to ask him a question, Narada spoke to him.

Narada said: O King, Ganesha, the bestower of boons and provider of success, is currently shining forth, having been propitiated by the gods through the discernment of duty in times of distress. He has been requested by the leaders of the gods for your destruction, and having agreed to this, the Lord is now stationed there. He has sent me to you in his presence. Listen to my words, for they will be for your benefit.

Obstacles are said to be of the nature of existence, and existence is considered to be of three types: that which is of the form of true resolve, and that which is dependent on others. The holders of the power of true resolve are those known as Brahman, the Supreme Lord, who are independent and self-existent. Other beings, such as humans, are undoubtedly possessors of existence, but in them, destiny is prominent, and thus they are known as being subject to destiny.

You are capable through a boon, but you are not possessed of your own inherent power; O demon, you are undoubtedly dependent, being possessed of the power of a boon. Humans who are dependent are always considered to be afflicted by obstacles. Filled with vain pride, they eventually become devoid of power. Deluded by the ego of "I am Brahman," the lords of the world said, "This entire world moves based on me." When they became devoid of power, thinking there was nothing beyond them, the Lord of Obstacles took away the success born of their true resolve.

Therefore, know that everything associated with obstacles is of two types: true and false, deluded by the illusion of duality. The Supreme Brahman is bliss, devoid of duality, and self-existent. That which is called the Lord of Obstacles is the Brahman revealed by the Vedas. Having been worshipped by the gods, He has now taken on a physical form. O King of demons, He has now arrived in the abode of the gods. Go and take refuge in Him, otherwise, you will undoubtedly die.

Let the entire world be filled with its own respective duties, O King of demons. Remain in your own place, devoted to your own duty. Let the gods be the enjoyers of heaven, let humans rely on the earth, and let the demons dwell in the netherworld, remaining full of happiness. Let the people, following the social order and stages of life, remain with respect. By doing this, you shall remain filled with enjoyment.

In the meantime, a disembodied voice spoke there: "Do not be proud, O King of demons; be subject to Narada's command. Otherwise, the Lord of Obstacles, being angry, will surely destroy you. Therefore, take refuge in Him, the giver of all success."

Hearing this, Mamasura became filled with sorrow. He summoned Shukra and held a deep consultation. Even after Shukra spoke in a similar vein, he remained fearful. Then, bowing to Narada, he spoke words for his own benefit.

Mamasura said: O Narada, what has been said by you and confirmed by Shukra is highly beneficial to the gods, but I will not go to the Elephant-faced one. O great sage, I worshipped Him specifically in the beginning, but the Lord of the gods abandoned me, and for that reason, I will not go to Him. When my death arrives, it will be glorious. O Brahmin, taking refuge in enemies is never appropriate for the proud.

Hearing his words, Narada said again: Do not worry, O King of demons; He is not on the side of the gods. The gods, filled with pride, killed the demons while deluded. In the past, they entered the netherworld to destroy the root of the demons. At that time, the Lord of Obstacles, becoming the bestower of boons, made the gods devoid of pride through your power. Yet you, deluded, have abandoned the supreme Ganapati and are prepared to kill all the gods by destroying their duties. Through the power of the destruction of duty, the gods perish, and the world becomes entirely devoid of divinity. Therefore, the Lord of Obstacles is prepared to kill you, O great demon. He does not kill those who are devoted to their own duties.

He is the one who plays with success and intellect, encompassing both gods and demons. Tell me, what will you do without success and intellect? Tell me, who is capable of creating an obstacle for the Lord of Obstacles? You will die because of your foolishness, being constantly filled with obstacles. The Brahman of bliss, described in the Vedas, is the essence of both existence and non-existence. There is nothing devoid of that essence, O demon. He is the holder of duality, present everywhere through the state of yoga. That very Lord of Obstacles will undoubtedly kill you.

By His yoga-maya, He created everything in three forms. He made the world, filled with its own duties, for the sake of play. When living beings and lords, filled with pride, abandon their duties due to delusion and become attached to objects, and having attained invincibility, they do as they please, then He takes on a physical form to destroy their pride. The body of Ganesha is of the nature of non-existence, while His head is of the nature of existence. That is why He became Elephant-faced. Go and take refuge in Him.

Hearing the words of Narada and Shukra, the King of demons, Mamasura, pondered that heavenly voice in his heart. "Ganesha is the form of Brahman, the cause of dwelling in one's own bliss. I shall go and take refuge in Him; it will be for my own benefit. Who, having disregarded the Lord of Obstacles, the master of success and intellect, could remain in the entire universe without obstacles?"

Mamasura then spoke beneficial words to the demons, his heart pleased, and he felt affection for Ganesha.

Mamasura said: O Kings of demons, headed by Kala, listen to my words, my dear ones. I shall do what was said by Shukra and Narada through the heavenly voice.

Hearing his words, Dharma and Adharma said: "It is not appropriate to take refuge in the Lord of the world who is on the side of the gods. Death in the midst of battle brings heaven and fame. Therefore, O father, let us fight, especially against the Lord of Obstacles. Tell me, what will the embodied Ganesha do to me? Father, the gods are showing you a delusion through His maya."

"How can you, a great thinker, consider the words of Narada, who is a devotee of the Lord of Ganas, to be acceptable to the demon kings? Shukra speaks that way to protect Narada's state. The voice created by the gods in the sky is false. Do not be afraid, father. We will kill the Elephant-faced one, who is full of essence, with your valor, O protector of demons."

Having said this, the two great warriors mounted their chariots. Filled with pride and anger, they went to kill the gods. Abandoning the words of the King of demons, the two fools, Dharma and Adharma, went to kill Ganesha. Then the Brahmin Narada went to the Lord of Ganas and told him the news. He too became filled with anger. Seeing the gods filled with fear, the Lord of Ganas said: "Do not fear, O wise ones; I will kill the Mamasuras. Even if the powerful Mamasura, equipped with the strength of infallible weapons, has arrived, I will undoubtedly kill him."

Having said this, the Lord of Ganas, filled with anger, released a lotus from his hand, a great weapon of the form of Brahman. In an instant, the lotus fell upon the demon army. From it, a sweet fragrance arose and spread across the earth. The fragrance reached those who were hostile to Ganesha and killed them. It was as if it were a miracle that it spared those who were devoid of malice.

Taking the fragrance, the two great demons, Dharma and Adharma, died in the battle. Five of their leaders also died there. Then, raising a cry of distress, the demons entered the netherworld. The lotus, filled with anger, went to Mamasura. Upon inhaling the sweet fragrance, Mamasura became unconscious. After half a watch, the King of demons regained consciousness.

Thus ends the seventh chapter named the removal of Mamasura's pride in the seventh section of the Ganesha Purana.

Chapter 8 : Describing the Pacification of Mamasura

Kashyapa said: Then Mamasura became cautious. Seeing the lotus standing nearby, he became filled with fear. Abandoning his pride, the great demon bowed to the lotus. With folded hands, he became frightened and prepared to offer prayers.

Mamasura said: Salutations to you, O Lotus, the primary weapon of Ganesha. Salutations to the Supreme Lord of all desires, the one who possesses the fragrance of existence. Those who are deluded by the fragrance of worldly objects wander in confusion; you have made them bound by sorrow and happiness in this world. Those who are united with the fragrance of Yoga become one with Brahman; what can I say of the many varieties of desires? Salutations again and again to you, who are the form of Brahman and the player of these games. They say the universe is born from a lotus and is lotus-shaped. Therefore, who is capable of praising you, O best of all weapons? Salutations to you again and again; be pleased and protect me from fear.

Seeing the lord of demons praying, Ganesha became peaceful. The lotus appeared in the hand of the Lord of Ganas. Mamasura, with a pleased heart, approached the remover of obstacles, bowed to him, and worshipped him with devotion. Again bowing to the Lord of Obstacles and folding his hands, he praised him with joy, knowing him to be the Lord of Brahman.

Mamasura said: Salutations to you, O Lord of Obstacles, the mover of all existence. Salutations to the beginningless, the Supreme Lord, the Lord of Brahman. Salutations to Ganesha, the leader of the Ganas, the Supreme Soul, the giver of eternal bliss. Salutations to Heramba, the single-tusked, the elephant-eared, the seeker, the pot-bellied one, the protector of devotees. Salutations to you, the Lord of Svananda, the embodiment of Yoga, the giver of peace, the one who resides in peace. Salutations to the eldest king, the worshipable one, the lord of all, the remover of obstacles, the protector of gods and demons. Salutations to you, the first to be worshipped, the one who remains at the end, the lord of success and wisdom, the four-armed one. Salutations to you, who reside upon Shesha, the bearer of the serpent-king banner, the elephant-faced one, the Lord of gods, who has assumed the form of the demon-lord. Salutations to you, the destroyer of obstacles for devotees and the destroyer of non-devotees, the one who is equal to yogis and resides in their hearts.

From the perspective of the Vedas, Brahman is described as having many forms and yet being formless. You are the one who effortlessly reconciles these two. Who can truly know the Lord who resides in his own bliss? I see the Lord of Obstacles directly as the Lord of Brahman. I am now fulfilled; there is no doubt. My birth and actions are blessed, for I have seen the elephant-faced one. How can I praise you, O Lord of Ganesha, when even the Vedas and others cannot? Salutations to you, whom the yogis have attained with great peace.

Having praised him thus, Mamasura danced, being very fortunate. His body was thrilled, and his eyes were filled with tears. The Lord of Obstacles, pleased by his prayers, said to him, "O mighty-armed one, ask for a boon; I will grant it. This hymn composed by you will be beneficial to all. For those with desires, it will grant various wishes, and for those without desires, it will grant great liberation. It will always grant my devotion and remove the ego."

Hearing the words of the Lord of Obstacles, Mamasura bowed and said with folded hands, "O Lord of Obstacles, if you are pleased, grant me this boon: give me steady devotion to you, which destroys illusion. O Lord, grant me constant residence in your Gana-abode. Tell me where I should stay and what I should eat, and I will remain there under your command. I am your servant, O Lord of Ganas; I ask for no other boon. By your mere sight, I have become enlightened."

The Lord of Obstacles said: "Mamasura, your devotion to me will be intense. You will become dear to the Ganas and united with Yoga. Remain here in your place, fearless and devoted to me. You shall consume the fruits of actions performed by people who are devoid of my worship or remembrance. By my influence, rule over those who are deluded and remain in your heart. Protect my devotees like servants with affection, and bring those who are devoid of my essence into my fold."

Hearing these excellent words of the Lord of Obstacles, Mamasura said, "So be it," and was overjoyed. Bowing to him, the demon went to his place. Holding onto peace, he worshipped the Lord of Ganesha with all his heart. All the other demons, filled with fear, abandoned him and immediately entered the netherworld, while he himself became full of joy. Thus, the Lord of Obstacles pacified Mamasura, brought him under his control, and established him in his place.

Thus ends the eighth chapter in the seventh section of the great Mudgala Purana, which describes the pacification of Mamasura.

Chapter 9 : Description of the Sacred Region

Kashyapa said: Seeing the demon Mamāsura pacified, the gods were filled with great joy. Accompanied by the sages, they bowed down and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles. Having worshipped Him with hair standing on end due to devotion, they bowed again and, with folded hands, praised the King of Obstacles with great reverence.

The divine sages said: We constantly worship the King of Obstacles, who is the embodiment of equilibrium, existing in the duality of the world, yet devoid of pairs of opposites, the ultimate truth, the Lord of Bliss, and the sustainer of His devotees. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles, who created the entire universe as a reflection, and through that, created duality; He is the embodiment of truth and the ultimate reality, yet also the unmanifest. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles, who is devoid of distinctions, the supreme object of knowledge, the witness of all, and who, by assuming the form of Vinayaka, remains eternally established in equanimity.

O Dhundhi, You created the second, the world of duality, which is the supreme Brahman and the source of divine play; You Yourself exist in the form of the rising consciousness. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. O Supreme Lord, You remain unaffected by the delusion caused by Your own Maya, even when the dualities meet and manifest. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. You are the one who inspired the duality and the 'I-am' (ego), and You created the cosmic point (Bindu); remaining established in that, You are the one who plays with perfect equanimity. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles.

O Ganesha, by uniting those two, You created this ancient four-footed world; Your light shines within it. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. O Elephant-faced Lord, having created this through the power of Your own rising, You play in the form of equilibrium, yet You are devoid of those very states. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. O Lord of the Supreme, You are the one created by the Lord of Lords, yet You appear as such to us; the Vedas describe You as the embodiment of Sankhya (knowledge). We constantly worship the King of Obstacles.

The Sankhya philosophy describes the state devoid of faults; therefore, O Lord of Obstacles, You are established in that Sankhya. You are never devoid of the rising consciousness. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. The Vedas say You dwell in Svananda (Your own bliss) and are the embodiment of bliss; You are the light of duality, yet devoid of its essence. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. You are beyond the reach of mind and speech, yet through the perfect yoga of equilibrium, You are perceived as the dense light of eternal bliss. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles.

The Vedas are unable to praise You; we bow to You, the primordial Heramba. May You be pleased with this, O God of Gods. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. Everyone, including the greatest yogis who have become Your servants, calls You the soul of all; Your meditation is infinite. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. You assume a form to protect the devotion and sentiments of their hearts; the true devotee finds devotion in You alone. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. Your body is both the unreal and the Brahman, and Your trunk represents the elephant; You shine as the Divine in the union of both. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles.

The saints call You the one with the elephant face, and thus You always reside in the heart as the embodiment of yoga; You are the giver of success and the awakener of intellect. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. Those whose minds are deluded by Your Maya say, "I am Brahma," and "The universe is under my control." We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. O Great One, You deprive those who are devoid of essence of their power by bestowing obstacles upon them; those who are devoid of selfishness become complete. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. Those who take refuge at Your feet become free from obstacles and attain greatness; thus, You are known as Vighnesha. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles. How can we, who are caught in the world of duality, praise such a Lord of Obstacles? Even the Vedas cannot reach You with the mind. We constantly worship the King of Obstacles.

Kashyapa said: Having praised the Lord of the Ganas in this way, the divine sages bowed down. The Lord, Vighnesha, the Master of the Vedas, spoke to them, being pleased.

Vighnesha said: O divine sages, ask for the boons you desire in your hearts. I am pleased by this hymn and, being won over by your devotion, I shall grant them. This hymn composed by you will grant all desires. Whoever reads or hears it will attain the supreme goal. I shall grant knowledge, longevity, wealth, grain, fame, righteousness, prosperity, desire, liberation, and the state of Brahman through the recitation of this hymn. A person will attain happiness, health, and victory in times of conflict through the recitation of this hymn. By this, the destruction of enemies and the removal of obstacles will be accomplished. It will also pacify the evil acts of others. The suffering caused by imprisonment and other such things will certainly be destroyed by this, O gods and sages, there is no doubt.

Whoever reads or hears this twenty-one times for twenty-one days will attain their heart's desire. This will constantly increase devotion to Me and will always destroy one's sins. Having heard the words of Ganapati, the divine sages bowed to Him and spoke with devotion.

The divine sages said: If You are pleased, O Vighnesha, then grant us devotion at Your lotus feet; we do not seek any other boon. O Lord, the great demon Mamāsura has been pacified by You; because of this, all the gods will return to their abodes. The sages will perform their duties, and the people will be established in their own righteousness. O Lord of the Ganas, what more could we ask for? You have accomplished the task of removing obstacles. Saying "So be it," the Lord of the gods disappeared.

The gods, along with the sages, bowed to Him and, with reverence, established the Lord of the Ganas. That supreme place of the Lord of Obstacles is in the middle of the Himalayas, in the north, and is the bestower of all success. On the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, at midday, Vighnesha was established by the sages and gods, bringing happiness to all. In the center is the Lord of Obstacles, on the left is Siddhi (Success), on the right is Buddhi (Intellect), and the Lord of Serpents is in front. The sacred regions like Prayaga are situated behind Him to serve the King of Obstacles daily. On the left are the gods led by Shiva, and on the right are the sages. They serve the Lord of the Ganas in front.

Demons, serpents, Gandharvas, Apsaras, and various Siddhas live there, eager to serve. The rivers like the Ganga and others dwell there with devotion. Other people also live there with devotion, O Goddess. The region extends for ten yojanas and is square in shape, known to be the bestower of happiness. The Ganesha Tirtha there is the bestower of all success; by merely bathing in it, a creature attains both worldly enjoyment and liberation. The holy places of Vishnu and other yogis are also situated there, granting union with the respective deities upon bathing.

Those who are devotees of other gods and die in this region attain the state of Brahman through the path of light. Devotees perform a pilgrimage there in the month of Bhadrapada, on the fourth day of the bright fortnight, with great celebration. All the residents of the region, along with the divine sages, remind each other of Gajanana. They narrate the stories of the King of Obstacles to each other, their eyes filled with tears of devotion for Vighnesha. O Goddess, on whatever day humans perform this pilgrimage, they enjoy all pleasures here and attain liberation in the end. Those who bow to the King of Obstacles there with their heart's desire attain happiness; there is no doubt about this.

O Goddess, wherever the devotees of Vighnesha may be, they become one with Brahman; there is no doubt that the mere sight of them purifies people. Having attained a human body, if one does not see the Lord of Obstacles, he should be considered like an animal, and his birth is in vain. Thus, in all the continents and islands, this supreme region of the King of Obstacles was created in the north by the sages. It is a region that grants the state of Brahman and is four-footed; what more can I say about it, when even the Vedas are amazed by it? I have described the greatness of this region in brief; no one is capable of describing it in detail.

Thus, the Lord Vighnesha, who assumes various incarnations for the protection of devotees and the destruction of the non-devotees, has infinite incarnations. I am not capable of describing the character of the great Vighnesha in full. I have narrated the pacification of Mamāsura and the character of the King of Obstacles, which is the bestower of all success. Whoever hears or reads this will have no fear of death, and will attain all their desires and the eternal Brahman.

Chapter 10 : Description of the Incarnation of Vishnu

Diti said: O Lord of sages, having heard the complete story of the King of Obstacles (Ganesha), which grants all accomplishments and bestows knowledge of Brahman, I am not yet satisfied. O Lord, I have now understood from your words that the Lord of Obstacles is undoubtedly the embodiment of Brahman itself. O Master, please tell me about the incarnations of the great-souled Ganesha, so that you may help me cross this ocean of worldly existence.

Mudgala said: Being asked thus, the great yogi Kashyapa, filled with joy, embraced his beloved Diti and spoke these beneficial words. O daughter of Daksha, you are blessed, for your devotion to the Lord of Obstacles has awakened; there is no doubt that you will accomplish everything. O great Goddess, the Lord of Obstacles created the universe, which is essentially non-existent, yet he resides within it as the Lord, manifesting through the reflection of his own Maya. By uniting these two, he created the supreme twofold reality: the rise from the self and the other, and from that, the point (Bindu). From that, he specifically created the fourfold universe, consisting of the gross and the subtle, encompassing both the singular and the plural.

Previously, this diverse universe, being devoid of bliss, appeared inactive. The beings performed their respective duties without delusion, yet they were not devoid of sorrow. They performed severe penance for the Lord of Obstacles, meditating upon his Brahman-form through the chanting of the single-syllable mantra. After a thousand divine years had passed, the Lord of Obstacles became pleased and appeared in their hearts. He revealed his form, which possessed the face of an elephant, and seeing him, the Brahmani (creative energies) were filled with joy and bowed down. By the grace of the Lord of Obstacles, they attained pure knowledge of his form, and thus satisfied, they began to worship him.

Then, the Lord of Obstacles appeared in the form of Vishnu, the bestower of boons, and said, "Ask for your boons." Seeing him, the Brahmani were astonished, and by his grace, they gained knowledge of his true nature. This glorious Lord of Obstacles, marked by the elephant face, resides everywhere as the reflection of the supreme, established in his own majesty. Filled with joy and devotion, they bowed down, worshipped the great Vishnu, and praised him.

The Brahmani said: Salutations to you, O Lord of Obstacles, O bearer of the Garuda banner, O four-armed Lord, O Master of all, salutations to you. Salutations to you, O wielder of the discus, O Lord of Lakshmi, O supreme of the supreme, O Heramba, O Lord of Vaikuntha. Salutations to you, O dweller of Svananda, O elephant-faced one, O Dhundhi, O infinite one, O son of Maheshwara. O son of Kashyapa, O son of Shesha, O lover of devotees, O destroyer of demons, O bearer of the Sharnga bow, salutations to you. O Lord, bearer of the mace, O Vasudeva, O protector of gods, O establisher of Dharma, O Brahman, salutations to you. Salutations to you, who are the form of existence and non-existence, the holder of the state of bliss, the eternal one who is equal in all beings. Salutations to you, O Vishnu, who are the form of speech, the indweller of all bodies, the embodiment of knowledge, and the formless one. How can we praise you, O Lord of Lords, before whom even the Vedas are silenced? O great-souled one, O yogi, be pleased with us, salutations to you.

Having praised him thus, the Brahmani bowed to Janardana. Vishnu, filled with joy, raised them and spoke. Vishnu said: This hymn composed by you will grant worldly enjoyment and liberation; it will bestow knowledge of Brahman upon those who hear or recite it. O Brahmani, ask for whatever you desire, and I shall grant it, being pleased by your penance and this hymn.

The Brahmani said: O Lord, grant us the natural, supreme bliss of the soul, and the capacity to perform our respective duties with the power of true resolve. Especially, grant us devotion to your lotus feet, and the happiness born of yogic peace, which leads to the state of Brahman.

Vishnu said: Everything you have requested shall come to pass. Perform your excellent duties with full dedication. I shall now explain the yogic peace that leads to the state of Brahman; listen, O Brahmani, with devotion. Know that the intellect, which is composed of five types of consciousness, creates illusion, and the Lord of both is the elephant-faced one. The body is composed of knowledge, and the head is the embodiment of knowledge; the Lord assumed the elephant form to unite the two. The syllable 'Ga' represents union, and 'Na' is known as the path of yoga; there is no doubt that his name 'Ganesha' is derived from the union of these two. Through self-realized yoga, one attains the supreme vision, and thus he is remembered as the dweller of Svananda. The wise say that Svananda is the union of the four Brahman-principles: the power of non-existence, the light of existence, the delusion, and the negation. Svananda, when combined with Maya, is considered the union of Brahman; without Maya, it is the state of non-union. In the union of both, Ganesha stands as the embodiment of peace. Worship him, for he is the mover of the heart and mind. By doing so, you will undoubtedly become one with Brahman. O Brahmani, apply this yoga for the sake of devotion. Whatever you do in the creation of the universe, offer all those actions to Ganesha.

Having said this, the great Vishnu disappeared. The Brahmani bowed to him and proceeded with their work. Gradually, they attained the state of Brahman. By the yoga of Ganesha, O daughter of Daksha, they became filled with bliss and created all that is moving and non-moving. In that state of bliss, they all played together. O Goddess, I have told you this beautiful story of the Lord of Obstacles, who assumed the form of Vishnu and grants all desires. Whoever hears or reads this will always be dear to him; it grants enjoyment, liberation, and complete happiness.

Chapter 11 : Description of the Story of Lakshmi-Vinayaka

Kashyapa said: Once, when Lord Narayana was seated comfortably and free from all afflictions, the highly fortunate Vishvaksena approached Him, bowed down, and spoke.

Vishvaksena said: O Keshava, please tell me the yoga that grants peace and leads to Your abode. By practicing it, I wish to become one with the form of Vishnu through supreme devotion.

Shri Narayana said: Know that everything consisting of name and form is unreal according to the teachings of the Vedas. Understand that the Brahman, which is associated with birth and death, is of that same nature. Know through the practice of yoga that the Supreme Brahman, which is devoid of all distinctions, is the only Truth, immortal and singular. Understand through the spirit of devotion that the Brahman, which is the union of these two, is eternal, full of bliss, and is known as the form of Vishnu. That same Brahman, established in the Vedas, is known as Vighnaraja (Ganesha). Worship Him according to the prescribed rites, and you shall attain peace.

Vishvaksena said: O Lord, knower of all scriptures, please tell me for the removal of my doubts, why is Vighneshvara described as having the form of Vishnu?

Shri Vishnu said: Know that worldly pleasure is particularly associated with obstacles (vighna), while the wise have always described the bliss of the Self as being free from obstacles. The union of these two is called Ganesha or Vighnaraja. He is full of eternal bliss and is worshipped by the yogis. The word 'Gana' means a group, and groups are indicators of Brahman; through the union of the external and internal, they are certainly known by the yogis. The image of Ganesha, which appears associated with Maya, is the very source that manifests as Shiva, Vishnu, and others. Therefore, we are all parts of the form of Ganesha. Worship Him according to the rites, and you shall attain peace.

Kashyapa said: O daughter of Daksha, hearing the words of Vishnu, Vishvaksena bowed to Him and spoke with devotion.

Vishvaksena said: I do not worship Vighnesha, as I am devoted to You. There is nothing greater than You; why do You say this, O Janardana? Having said this, he bowed to Lord Vishnu and went to the forest to attain the success of yoga, performing penance with great reverence. Holding Lord Vishnu, who is the embodiment of bliss in all states, within his heart, he chanted the king of mantras, which is the Rig-Vedic mantra of Vishnu. Becoming one who abstains from food, the glorious Vishvaksena, the crest-jewel of the Vaishnavas, performed penance with great devotion.

Then, after a hundred years had passed, the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha), being pleased, granted him the knowledge of yoga. Seeing Him in the form of a union, he was filled with joy. Then, he saw Ganesha in his heart, accompanied by Lakshmi. Seeing this, he became troubled and, thinking of Vishnu, he chanted the mantra while observing Ganesha. For a moment, he saw Narayana accompanied by Lakshmi in his mind, and the next moment, he saw Vighneshvara, and he became astonished. Through the great power of his penance, his mind became pure. Casting aside the delusion born of ego, he reflected in his heart: "The body is made of Maya, but the head is free from it. In the union of the two, the Lord of the Ganas bears the form of an elephant. This image is certainly always associated with delusion, O Devi. Vishnu is of a balanced nature and is especially accompanied by Lakshmi. The Lord, who is established in His own glory and accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, is the one who plays the game of Maya and is called the Lord of Lakshmi."

"I was instructed by Vishnu in the past due to my ego. Like a foolish scholar, I did not understand His true form. Oh, I have neither seen nor heard of anything equal to this yoga, by the mere service of which ego is destroyed." Having reflected thus, he meditated upon the Lord of the Devas, Ganesha, and worshipped Him with mental offerings. Then, Ganesha, who is fond of His devotees, became pleased and came to the hermitage of Vishvaksena to grant him boons. Being absorbed in meditation, Vishvaksena did not realize that Ganesha had arrived. Then, the Lord of Obstacles performed a wonder; He caused Himself to vanish from Vishvaksena’s heart. Then, feeling extremely distressed, he saw Him standing outside. Seeing the Lord of the Ganas accompanied by Lakshmi, he was filled with joy. Rising, he bowed to Him first and worshipped Him. After worshipping Him, he bowed again with tearful eyes and praised the Elephant-faced Lord with folded hands.

Vishvaksena said: Salutations to You, O Vighnesha, destroyer of the obstacles of Your devotees. Salutations to You, the giver of obstacles, the Lord of the noble, and salutations to those who are not Your devotees. Salutations to You, O Lord of Lakshmi, the bestower of Siddhi and Buddhi. Salutations to You, O Lord who dwells in Your own bliss, who resides in Vaikuntha. Salutations to You, O Keshava, Supreme Lord, holder of the discus and the mace. Salutations to You, O Heramba, the bearer of the battle-axe. Salutations to You, O son of Dasharatha, O Vasudeva. Salutations to You, O son of Shiva, O Lord of the Devas, O son of the most excellent. Salutations to You, O Ganesha, the embodiment of yoga, the Lord, the giver of peace to yogis, the Creator, the Destroyer, and the eternal vessel. Salutations to You, who recline on Shesha, salutations to You whose navel is the source of all. Salutations to You, the eternal one with a large belly, wearing a serpent as a sacred thread. Salutations to You, the player of many games, the mover of many illusions, the one who is beyond play, the helper of the Devas. Salutations to You, O Dhundhiraja, who destroys the demons and grants boons to them, the Lord of devotees, who is fond of devotion. Salutations to You, O Ganesha, the leader of the Ganas, the Supreme of the Supreme, O Narayana, O Krishna, dark as a cloud. Because You are associated with Maya, O Lord, You are always in the form of Vishnu. You are established in Your own glory, O Ganesha, salutations to You. Therefore, O Ganesha, Your name is called Vishnu in the Vedas. He who is Vishnu is indeed the Lord of Obstacles; there is never any difference between them.

Having praised Ganesha thus, the wise one bowed down. Then the Lord of Obstacles spoke to Vishvaksena.

Shri Vighnesha said: O wise Vishvaksena, ask for boons from Me. I am pleased with your hymn, and I shall grant them to you out of devotion. This hymn composed by you shall grant worldly enjoyment and liberation. Whoever reads or hears it shall attain all success.

Kashyapa said: Hearing the words of Ganesha, Vishvaksena spoke to Him, bowing his head with devotion and reverence.

Vishvaksena said: If You are pleased, O Elephant-faced Lord, then grant me devotion to Your lotus feet and bestow upon me the yoga of peace.

Hearing this, the Lord of the Ganas spoke, as if to gladden him: There is no doubt that your devotion to Me will become intense. Through the union with Vishnu, you shall attain the yoga of peace. Whatever you desire, may it always be fulfilled.

Having said this, Ganesha, bound by devotion, vanished. Vishvaksena went to Vaikuntha and bowed to Vishnu. He narrated the entire incident, and hearing it, Vishnu was filled with joy. Keshava then taught him the supreme yoga. Having practiced it according to the rules, he became worthy of being worshipped by yogis. Vishvaksena worshipped Ganesha with devotion, holding the great Vishnu in the form of the Guru and Ganesha in the form of the Lord within his heart, and he became devoted to the meditation of yoga. This story of Lakshmi-Ganesha, which I have told you, grants all success and becomes complete through reading and hearing.

Thus ends the eleventh chapter of the seventh section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled the "Description of the Story of Lakshmi-Vinayaka."

Chapter 12 : The Description of the Incarnation of Shurpakarna

Kashyapa said: In ancient times, Lord Vishnu worshipped Ganesha with devotion and yogic practices to destroy the demons Madhu and Kaitabha. Pleased by this, Ganesha granted him boons, using which Vishnu slew those two formidable demons. O Devi, since Janardana (Vishnu) attained glory through Ganesha’s grace, who else among your sons could possibly defeat Him? Therefore, abandon your attachment to your sons and worship Ganesha with a pure heart; by doing so, you too shall become worthy of being worshipped by the yogis. Everything in this world—the gods, demons, and humans—exists exactly as it was created by the Lord of the Ganas in the past.

Hearing the words of Kashyapa, Diti, filled with joy, spoke again to the sage for her own well-being. Diti said: O chief of sages, please tell me again with reverence the story of the incarnation of the Lord of Obstacles (Vighnaraja) and the supreme knowledge regarding Him.

Kashyapa said: There was a demon named Yajnadhrut, the son of Yajnahna, who was determined to commit sins. He performed severe penance for Shiva, following the rules of the Panchakshara mantra for thousands of divine years. Pleased by his penance, Shiva appeared and offered him boons. The demon asked, "May I never die at the hands of the three gunas, and grant me the kingdom of the entire universe, free from disease. Whatever I desire, O Lord of Gods, may it be fulfilled." Shiva said "So be it" and disappeared.

Returning home, the demon king Yajnadhrut conquered the universe with his might. He displaced the gods, led by Shambhu (Shiva), from their positions and installed demons in their places. He then strove specifically to destroy all sacrifices and struck down all the sages. Terrified, the sages abandoned their rituals, and the demon created chaos across the entire moving and non-moving world. He then sought to destroy Vishnu and the very roots of the gods. Since Vishnu is known as the embodiment of Yajna (sacrifice) in the Vedas, the demon reasoned that by destroying the sacrifices, Vishnu himself would be destroyed. Thus, he broke all sacrifices and left the three worlds devoid of ritual.

Hungry and weakened, Lord Vishnu, along with the gods, remembered the Lord of the Ganas. He prayed, "O Lord of the Ganas, protect me from this formidable demon; without the sacrifices, I shall surely perish." Along with the gods, Vishnu performed supreme penance, worshipping Ganesha with the Ekakshara mantra. After a hundred years, Ganesha appeared, pleased by their worship. Surrounded by the gods, he went to slay the demon Yajnadhrut. After a fierce battle, Ganesha killed him with his goad and sent the other demons to the netherworld.

Then, Vishnu, along with the gods and sages, stood with folded hands and praised Ganesha. Vishnu said: Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, the remover of all impediments, and the pacifier of great troubles. Salutations to the beginningless Supreme Lord, who is worshipped first of all. Salutations to the bestower of boons, the Lord of all, the one who is sought after by the Vedas. Salutations to the Supreme Soul, who grants happiness to all, the immeasurable Brahman. Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, the master of Siddhi and Buddhi. You are the form of Brahma, Vishnu, Shakti, Shiva, and the gods like Indra. You are the sage, the merchant, the shudra, the animal, and the bird; you are the one who upholds the social order. You are the serpent, the demon, and the one who pervades all moving and non-moving things; salutations to you, the Shurpakarna (the one with winnowing-basket ears). Salutations to the three-eyed one, the wielder of the trident, the discus, the noose, and the goad—O Heramba, salutations to you.

O Lord, you are the Supreme Soul, the embodiment of knowledge, pure and beyond the reach of worldly attachments. How can I praise you, when even the Vedas and yogis are lost in wonder at your peace? By killing the demon, you have protected us, the sages and gods. O Giver of Life, salutations to you. Grant me pure devotion to your lotus feet, so that we may be liberated from all bonds.

Having said this, the gods and sages bowed to him. Ganesha, the Shurpakarna, said "So be it" and disappeared. The gods established the image of Shurpakarna there, worshipped him, and returned to their abodes, free from all distress. The world returned to its natural order. Thus, Ganesha destroyed many demons. The incarnations of the Lord of Obstacles are countless, and even Shiva and others are unable to recount all his deeds. I have told you his supreme incarnations in brief; worship him according to the scriptures, and you shall attain peace.

Just as a winnowing basket (shurpa) separates the grain from the chaff, making it fit for consumption, so too the Brahman, when covered by the illusions of Maya, is not attained. O beautiful one, abandon the impurities of the heart and take refuge in Shurpakarna. He is called Shurpakarna because he is the Brahman who dwells within the human form, acting like a winnowing basket to remove the impurities of the heart. Worship him, and you shall attain peace. This is the story of the Shurpakarna incarnation, who took a physical form to slay the demon Yajnahna. Whoever hears, reads, or teaches this story shall attain their heart's desires and remain eternally happy.

Chapter 13 : The Description of Dattagita

Kashyapa said: Once, in the month of Magha, when the sun was in Capricorn, the gods, sages, and others gathered at Prayaga with great reverence. Everyone was filled with joy, devoted to the sacred bath of Magha, and worshipped the Lord of Ganas, the Omkara, with deep devotion. Then, O beloved, they worshipped Lord Shiva and Madhava (Vishnu) who were present there, and with hearts full of emotion, they discussed ancient tales.

At that time, Dattatreya, the best of the great yogis and the revealer of the Avadhuta path, arrived there while wandering. He bowed to the Lord of Ganas, the Omkara, who is the Supreme Brahman and the giver of all boons, and began to chant a hymn. Seeing this, the gods, including Shiva, and the yogis, including Kashyapa, were filled with great wonder and approached him with respect. After bowing to the great yogi who was absorbed in the Brahman, they respectfully asked him, seeing his devotion to Ganesha.

Shiva and the others said: O Lord, knower of all truths, devoted to the Brahman, you have arrived here by chance, being the embodiment of Brahman itself. You have taken a body specifically to reveal the path of the Avadhuta for the purification of all embodied beings. By your mere sight, we are fulfilled and blessed. O Master, please enlighten the sages and gods.

Thus praised and questioned by them with humility, the great sage, filled with immense joy, spoke to those fortunate ones.

Dattatreya said: O Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Angira, and other pure sages, and O Nagas like Shesha, listen to my beneficial words. This entire universe appears as Brahman; worship Him for your own benefit. You will undoubtedly become capable and established in the Brahman.

Hearing his words, they again spoke to him with folded hands, filled with joy, as he was worthy of being worshipped by yogis.

Shiva and the others said: You are established in the Brahman, so why do you worship Ganesha, a deity who possesses name, form, and the shape of Omkara? He is our family deity, the root cause of those who possess the three gunas; how can he be the object of worship for yogis?

Hearing their words, the glorious Datta smiled and spoke with joy, desiring to bestow knowledge.

Datta said: Ganesha is described in the Vedas as the Lord of all Brahmans. Brahman is described in various forms, associated with Maya. O gods and sages, listen: "Food is Brahman," and in that context, the Maya in the form of food appears as the deluder. "Prana (life-force) is Brahman," and in that context, the Maya in the form of Prana is the deluder. Similarly, the mind is Brahman, and the Maya in the form of the mind resides in that Brahman. Knowledge is Brahman, and the Maya in the form of knowledge is that. Bliss is Brahman, and it is associated with the Maya of bliss. Consciousness is Brahman, and the Maya is in the form of consciousness. The Bindu (point) is Brahman, and the Maya appears in the form of the Bindu. "I am Brahman" is the state where the Maya reveals the "I am." Knowledge is Brahman, and the Maya of knowledge is the deluder there.

"Bodyless" is Brahman, and the Maya is in the form of the body. Because of this, O great gods, those who consider themselves Brahman are deluded. "Action is Brahman," and the Maya of action is the deluder there. Similarly, "Knowledge is Brahman," and it appears associated with the Maya of knowledge. "Equanimity is Brahman," and the Maya is in the form of equanimity. "Natural state is Brahman," and it gives rise to natural modifications. "Non-existence is Brahman," and it is associated with the Maya of non-existence. "Existence is Brahman," and it is described as being associated with the Maya of existence.

O sages, Brahman is described as having the nature of both existence and non-existence; it should be known as being associated with the modifications of both. "Unmanifest is Brahman," and it is associated with the Maya of the unmanifest. The Supreme Brahman, which is self-evident, is associated with the Maya of self-bliss. Words like "Self," "Non-dual," and "All" are described as revealing the respective Mayas through the great Vedic statements. Words like Shiva and Vishnu are also names for Brahman; know them to be associated with their respective modifications. The elements like earth and water are also indicators of Brahman; know them to be associated with their respective modifications.

Thus, various types of Brahmans have been described in the Vedas and other scriptures, all associated with the delusion of Maya. The Lord of Brahmans (Brahmanaspati) stands as the master of all these. Because He is the protector and sustainer of all these Brahmans, He is the one described in the Vedas as the Lord of Ganas. I worship Him, who is the direct embodiment of the Brahman.

He who is called Ganesha is the Brahman that is devoid of birth and death. He is remembered as Gajanana (Elephant-faced), the head of the Sankhya philosophy. By the means of the earth, one becomes absorbed in the Brahman. His body is called "Yoga" because it holds various planes of existence. In the union of these, Ganesha became the one who took a body. He is the direct embodiment of Yoga in Sankhya and the giver of complete peace. In union, he is called "Ga," and in separation, he is called "Na." The Lord of both is Ganesha, as per the Vedic tradition.

The intellect, consisting of the five states of consciousness, became his right side. The Siddhi (perfection), which causes the five types of illusions, is always his left side. The Lord of Ganas is the master of both and plays with these two Mayas. The Chintamani (wish-fulfilling gem) in the heart is the direct source of light for both. A person becomes a great yogi by abandoning the ego-centered mind. I worship Him specifically, as He is the mover of the mind.

All these Brahmans are created by Siddhi and Buddhi. By abandoning them, how can humans attain the Supreme Brahman? Therefore, He has taken a body to be the guide for the path of devotion, so that He may be easily accessible to the gods and others.

Hearing his words, Vashistha, prompted by all, asked him to destroy their doubts.

Vashistha said: Brahman alone is described in the Vedas as the only truth. How can there be a "Lord of Brahmans" (Brahmanaspati)? The great Maya in the form of a "Lord" resides in Ganesha. Just as Brahman is present in the modifications of food, etc., is He considered in the same way?

Hearing Vashistha's words, Datta, the great yogi and knower of the ultimate truth, spoke with joy.

Datta said: The Brahmans associated with food, Prana, etc., were created by Ganesha; that is why He is called their Lord. He is the Lord of all the Brahmans described. Even though He is Brahman Himself, He is called the Lord because of His role as the protector. Those who abandon the modifications of food, Prana, etc., will become established in the Brahman by worshipping Him. If the Brahmans have merged into the Brahman, O great sage, then tell me quickly, of whom is Ganesha the Lord? Therefore, this Lord of Ganas is devoid of the modifications of Maya. He is Brahman itself, and His lordship is merely a description for the sake of worship.

Hearing this, Narada again spoke to the pure Avadhuta and great yogi to understand the essence.

Narada said: O great yogi, you are Ganesha himself, there is no doubt. If I, Datta, and Ganesha are not different, then whom are you worshipping? O wise one, you are omniscient, please remove this doubt of mine.

Datta said: This body, similar to a human's, performs actions daily, specifically through the modifications of name and form. Brahman is everywhere, O sage, and everything is filled with Him; there is no doubt in this. The existence appears to be more or less based on one's contemplation. The term "Lord of Brahmans" (Brahmanaspati) refers to the existence present in Ganapati, not elsewhere in Shiva, Vishnu, or others. Therefore, seeing the Brahman present in the heart as the Lord of Obstacles (Vighnesha), who would not worship Him? How can one be devoted to the Brahman otherwise?

Therefore, I, who have taken a body, am the Lord of Ganas in the form of Brahman. I worship Him specifically while being engaged in bodily activities. The body is said to be the performer of actions, and the mind is the thinker. Just as one naturally performs thinking, etc., my body and mind worship the Lord of Ganas. How can the body and mind worship the Brahman, who is the illuminator of both? Brahman itself has become my form. My body and mind worship Him, who is the source of self-illumination. Through this path of Yoga, I worship the Lord of Ganas. I am not different from Ganesha; think about whom you are asking.

Just as I perform daily activities like eating with reverence, in the same way, O dear one, I worship the Lord of Ganas with devotion. I am not the enjoyer, but I am present in the body; know me as such. The dharma of the body does not apply to the devotee of the Lord of Ganas. One who is established in the Brahman but does not worship the Lord of Ganas is not considered the chief among yogi-devotees. Therefore, I worship Ganesha with joy. He who, having attained the Brahman present in the body and heart without effort, does not worship Him upon seeing Him, is called a killer of Brahman. Who among the yogis would not feel immediate love upon seeing Ganesha, the Lord of Yoga and giver of peace, in the form of an idol?

Hearing his words, Shiva, Narada, and the others were filled with joy and bowed to him, amazed by his actions. They said with joy, "We are now free from doubt. O great soul, you have made us follow the single path. People wander, deluded by various conflicting opinions, having abandoned Ganesha, who is complete and worthy of worship by all. To destroy this delusion, you have been created by the Lord of Obstacles as a knower of the path of Yoga and a giver of all peace."

Thus praised by them, Datta laughed and departed. Shiva, Vashistha, and the others also returned to their respective abodes. This Gita, which is the essence of all and has emerged from the mouth of Datta, grants peace and all perfections to those who read or hear it. Hearing the words of Kashyapa, Diti, who was devoted to the Lord of Obstacles, was filled with joy, bowed to him, and spoke with deep emotion.

Chapter 14 : The Granting of the Boon to Diti

Diti said: O Lord of great fortune, please tell me the method of worshiping Ganesha, so that I may constantly worship the Lord of Obstacles with devotion.

Mudgala said: Being asked thus, the great yogi, who knew the proper procedures, gave her the ten-syllable mantra of Ganesha along with the prescribed rituals. Having bowed to that best of yogis, Diti went to Kashyapa and then proceeded to a forest filled with trees and other vegetation, where she performed severe penance. Remaining without food and intent on the meditation of Ganesha, she kept her gaze fixed on the tip of her nose and chanted the mantra. Abandoning all desires and absorbed in the Lord of Obstacles, Diti became free from delusion through the power of her penance. She lost interest in worldly attachments like sons and grandsons, and desiring yoga, she worshiped the elephant-faced Lord with complete devotion.

Thus, after a thousand years of being absorbed in the fruits of yoga, she attained the state of peace and became worthy of being praised by yogis. Diti kept in her mind the path given to her and never ceased her devotion to the Lord of Obstacles. Then, pleased with her, the Lord of the Ganas appeared before her and said, "Ask for the boons you desire in your heart while you are in meditation." Hearing the words of Ganesha, Diti was filled with joy; she stood up, bowed to Him, and worshiped Him with devotion.

Again bowing to the Lord of Obstacles, Diti, the daughter of Daksha, with tears in her lotus-like eyes, praised Ganesha with folded hands. Diti said: Salutations to the King of Obstacles, the destroyer of the obstacles of His devotees, and salutations again and again to the One who provides great obstacles to those who are not His devotees. Salutations to You, the Lord of Kashyapa, the Supreme Lord, the Remover of Obstacles, the Dhundhi, the One with a large belly, and the enjoyer of all offerings. Salutations to You, the Lord of the helpless, the One who is helpless for the sake of the helpless, the protector of the helpless, and the Heramba. Salutations to You, the Supreme Lord, the Lord of the Ganas, the One who is greater than the greatest, the Creator of those who are greater than the greatest, and the One with a large belly. Salutations to the Beginningless One, the One who bears the primordial form, the One who is worshiped first by all, and the One who resides within all. Salutations to the son of Shiva, the son of Vishnu, the Supreme Soul, the most excellent son, and the son of Shesha. Salutations to the One worshiped by all, the eldest, the form of the eldest among the eldest, the King of the eldest, and the Mother and Father of all. Salutations to the Lord of the Vedas, the Giver of Brahman to the Brahmins, the form of Brahman, and the One with a single tusk. Salutations to the elephant-faced God, who is equal to both gods and demons, the form of yoga, and the Giver of yoga to the yogis. How can I praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, the embodiment of peace, in whom even the Vedas and Shiva find peace? Therefore, I bow to You, who are the essence of all, O Lord of Obstacles. May You be satisfied with this small devotion of mine; salutations to You, O Lord. If You are pleased to grant me a boon, then be my son out of Your grace, O Lord, O ocean of mercy, so that I may be delivered by You. O elephant-faced Lord, the demonic nature is described in the Vedas; with what great devotion shall I constantly worship You? When You, O Lord, become my son and the bestower of success, how can there ever be any illusion-based bondage for Your mother? O Dhundhi, grant me devotion to Your feet always, and make me, who am free from demonic bondage, dear to Your feet.

Having said this, the daughter of Daksha bowed to the Lord of the Ganas. The King of the Ganas, filled with joy, said to Diti: The hymn you have composed for Me will be dear to Me. Whoever reads or hears it, O Devi, will be granted all successes. I will become your son and the destroyer of the demonic nature. You will worship Me daily with devotion and a focused heart. Having said this, the Lord of Obstacles, the Lord of Brahman, disappeared. Diti returned to her home and bowed to her husband. She narrated the entire event, and Kashyapa was filled with joy. Hearing this, he embraced her and said with tearful eyes: You are blessed, your purpose is fulfilled, and your life is considered successful, for you have seen the Lord of the Ganas, the giver of peace to all. O Devi, your son will be the Lord of the Ganas, who will destroy the demonic nature; it is a great fortune that you are now worthy of being praised by yogis. I have truly been delivered by you, a virtuous wife devoted to the Lord of the Ganas. My birth and actions are blessed, as you, though of demonic origin, are devoted to your own welfare. Having said this to Diti, the sage Kashyapa became silent. Diti bowed to the sage and remained engaged in his service.

Chapter 15 : The Description of the Exploits of the Four-Armed Incarnation

Mudgala said: Then, she always remembered the Lord of the Ganas in her heart, and she perceived this entire universe as being filled with Him. Once, in the month of Magha, during the bright fortnight, when the sun was at its zenith on the fourth day, she worshipped the supreme Gajanana who resides in the form of an idol. From the midst of the idol, the Lord of the Ganas appeared as a four-armed deity, possessing a brilliance equal to infinite suns, O Prajapati. Seeing Him, she became terrified and, filled with devotion, remembered Kashyapa, her guru, and the Lord Ganesha. By the mere remembrance of the guru, Ganesha assumed a gentle form, and like a child, spoke to Mother Diti with a voice like the rumbling of clouds.

Shri Ganesha said: I am your son, born through the power of the boon. Do not fear, O auspicious one; look upon me in my gentle form. Then, opening her eyes, she, who was trembling with fear, beheld the child, the four-armed God, holding a tusk, adorned with great weapons like the battle-axe, with a moon on His forehead, a large belly, a single tusk, and the serpent Shesha as His navel. Seeing Him with fan-like ears, three eyes, decorated with various ornaments, wearing priceless garments, and the Chintamani gem around His neck, she bowed to Him along with Kashyapa. She worshipped the Lord, the remover of obstacles, and praised Him with the Atharvashirsha.

Then Kashyapa bowed to Him and prayed to the Lord who bestows peace: O Lord, please assume the form of a child so that we may nurture You. I am blessed and fulfilled; there is no one like me, for You, the Lord of the Ganas, have become my son. You, in whose pores exist crores of universes, how have You come to be my son? This is indeed a divine play. Being thus prayed to by him and Diti, the Lord became as requested and instantly transformed into a four-armed child. Kashyapa, filled with joy, bowed to the Lord of the Ganas and, along with Diti, performed the rituals and invited the Brahmanas. Then, the four-armed Lord cast His Maya upon their hearts so that they would behave as parents. Diti bathed the child and fed Him, and Kashyapa performed the birth rituals with great joy.

On the twelfth day, the Brahmanas arrived for the naming ceremony. Worshipped by Kashyapa, they spoke with great joy: This child will establish the gods in heaven, humans on earth, and the Asuras, Nagas, and Kurmas in the netherworlds. Because He moves the elements, O Brahmana, He shall be known as Chaturbhuja (the Four-Armed), and the wise consider Him the establisher of the four diverse realms.

Mudgala said: Then, in the second year, Prahlad and others were being struck by the gods led by Vishnu. Fearing for their lives, the Asura lords abandoned everything and fled to the netherworld. Lord Vishnu himself became filled with anger against the Asuras. Accompanied by Indra and others, He, the mighty one, armed with the discus, went to the netherworld to destroy the Daityas. The Lord of the gods released His discus, and the Daityas were killed in battle; some, severely wounded, fell with mangled bodies. Some sought refuge in Keshava, yet the great Vishnu struck them down in His rage. As He prepared to rid the world of Asuras, the Daitya lords, terrified, sought refuge in Diti, the giver of shelter. Seeing them with their limbs severed and broken, Diti grieved. Then, the Lord of the Ganas became filled with anger.

The four-armed Lord released His sharp battle-axe to slay the gods. The radiance of the axe pervaded the entire netherworld. The weapons of the gods, like the discus, became useless in battle. The gods, scorched by the heat, fled in all ten directions. Then Brahma arrived there and enlightened Keshava. Terrified, they all abandoned the netherworld and fled to heaven. The gods, scorched by the radiance of the battle-axe, found no peace and were filled with cries of distress. Then Shambhu, Ravi, Brahma, and Keshava, along with the sages and gods, prayed to the battle-axe in fear. O Prajapati, that great weapon then became peaceful.

Then, all the gods, led by Devendra, went to the four-armed Lord. Bowing to Him in fear, the leaders of the gods and the sages worshipped Ganesha with folded hands. The divine sages said: Salutations to the four-armed Dhundhi, the son of Kashyapa. Salutations to the beginningless Ganesha, the Lord of obstacles. Salutations to the Supreme Lord, the master of all, the Paramatman, the Vinayaka, and the bestower of boons. Salutations to You, the Lambodara (large-bellied), who holds everyone within Your belly. Salutations to the Mahodara (great-bellied), the eldest, the Brahmana. Salutations to You, who are the Kshatriya-dharma for the Kshatriyas, the Vaishya-form for the Vaishyas, the Shudra-dharma for the Shudras, and beyond all varnas. Salutations to You, who are the householder for the householders, the celibate for the celibates, and the forest-dweller for the forest-dwellers. Salutations to You, who are the renouncer for the renouncers, and beyond all ashramas. Salutations to You, the fifth beyond the four, the Heramba. Salutations to You, the dweller of Svananda, the bestower of Siddhi and Buddhi, the single-tusked God, the Lord of the Asuras. Salutations to You, the protector of the helpless and the Lord of the protected, the Maheshvara of the Ishvaras, and the Vishnu. Salutations to You, the Brahma, the Sun, the Shakti, the form of all, and the bestower of positions to the gods. How can we praise You, who move the four realms with Your own arms? Therefore, we bow to You, O Four-Armed One, please be pleased with us.

Having said this, the gods and sages bowed to Him. Pleased, the Lord, who loves His devotees, spoke to them. The Four-Armed One said: Ask for the boons you desire from Me, O great gods and sages. I will grant them, as I am pleased by your prayers; be free from fear. This prayer composed by you, O gods, shall grant all successes. It shall destroy the fears of those who read or hear it. In particular, a person shall attain sons, grandsons, wealth, grain, and ultimately My abode by reading this. Hearing His words, the gods and sages were freed from fear and bowed to Him, singing His praises with joy.

The divine sages said: If You are pleased and are a granter of boons, O Four-Armed One, then may we have constant devotion to Your lotus feet. Command us, O Lord, and we shall act accordingly, for we have been constantly tormented by the fear of the Daityas. The Four-Armed One said: From today, O lords of the gods, do not harm the great Asuras residing in the netherworld, for they were established by Me long ago. Let them remain in their own dharma—the gods in heaven, humans on earth, and the Danavas in the netherworld. If they become deluded by pride and deviate from the path, they shall not attain success; there is no doubt about this, by My word. Saying "So be it," the gods bowed to Him and returned to their abodes. From that day on, the gods did not slay the Danavas residing in their own realms. The four-armed Lord also commanded all the Daitya lords, and they, saying "So be it," returned to their own realms with joy.

Then, the four-armed God disappeared, O Prajapati. Kashyapa, along with Diti, grieved while remembering Him. A voice from the sky spoke to the parents: Establish an idol of Ganesha and worship Him daily with reverence. Diti and Kashyapa, along with the Brahmanas, did exactly that. They established the idol of the four-armed Lord forever. Meditating on the Chintamani in their hearts and worshipping the four-armed Lord externally, Diti and Kashyapa remained filled with devotion. Abandoning their Asuric nature, Diti, filled with devotion, worshipped the four-armed Lord with the nature of a Gana. I have thus narrated this story of the four-armed Lord. This is known as the incarnation of the aspect of Vighnaraja. Any mortal who hears, recites, or causes this to be heard shall attain worldly enjoyment, liberation, and supreme devotion to Vighneshvara.

Chapter 16 : The Description of the Glory of the Narrative

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Mudgala said: After a long time had passed, the powerful demon kings, filled with anger, prepared themselves to conquer the gods. Led by Prahlada, who was deluded by a curse from Brahma, they approached Diti, who was with Kashyapa, and bowed to her. Diti, filled with anger, spoke to them herself: "Do not go, you wicked ones; abandon unrighteousness, O lords of demons. The Lord of gods, who has four arms, has established the gods in the heavenly realms, while you have been granted the kingdom of the netherworlds." Ignoring her advice, the demon kings, driven by greed and unrighteousness, bowed to her and departed to conquer the gods. Diti, leaving the side of the wicked demons and Kashyapa, worshipped Lord Ganesha with great devotion. Similarly, Aditi, leaving the side of the gods and the sages, worshipped the supreme Lord Vinayaka. Abandoning divine nature, she worshipped the clay-formed Vinayaka with love, devotion, and a yogic state of mind. Vinata and others, instructed by Kashyapa, also worshipped Lord Ganesha with peace and love.

Daksha asked: O great sage, tell me, how do humans worship the Lord of Ganas with devotion, having abandoned both divine and demonic natures?

Mudgala replied: If a person performs rituals according to the scriptures with desire, it is called demonic, as it is meant for bodily comfort. If a person performs rituals without desire, it is called divine; the wise consider such scriptural actions as those that grant liberation. People established in their own duties perform various supreme actions; knowing the nature of the body, this is remembered as a form of yoga. That action performed for the pleasure of the Lord is considered filled with devotion. The sage, along with his wife, performed this with joy and devotion. I have told you all this history of the King of Obstacles, which grants all accomplishments, is complete, brings peace, and is the cause of devotion.

O respected one, there are many such incarnations of the King of Obstacles; no one can describe all their stories. This Lord of Obstacles is the one who dwells in universal bliss; the Lord plays with His illusion, creating both truth and falsehood. This King of Obstacles is described as being full of complete yoga. Listen with focus to the meaning of this, which illuminates the knowledge of Brahman. It is undoubtedly filled with both known and unknown Brahman, and is associated with great obstacles. That which is free from illusion is also free from obstacles; when one becomes one with Brahman, neither duality exists. This King of Obstacles is the creator of obstacles for all, including the creator Brahma, and is Himself the remover of obstacles for the gods constantly. He is neither inherently filled with obstacles nor free from them; knowing this, a person becomes one with Brahman and the Lord of Obstacles.

Whoever listens to or reads this wonderful story of the Lord of Obstacles will always have the Lord pleased with them. When the King of Obstacles is pleased, what could possibly be unattainable? Such a person, endowed with all accomplishments, eventually becomes Brahman Himself. There is nothing in this world as purifying as this; you have heard a narrative that grants both worldly enjoyment and liberation. One who listens to this gains more merit than one who performs various austerities like the five-fire penance. One gains more merit from listening to this section than from visiting various holy places. O Daksha, one gains more fruit from listening to this than from performing all complete sacrifices. There is no doubt that one who listens to this gains more than one who performs all charitable and pious acts. By listening to this, a person becomes endowed with children, grandchildren, wealth, grain, and health. If a person is suffering from afflictions like infertility, they will be freed from all pain simply by listening to this.

In the house where this section, which inspires devotion to the Lord of Obstacles, resides, there is never any fear of ghosts, thieves, or others. There is no fear of fire, spirits, the king, planetary positions, or malevolent entities. This narrative is said to be the bestower of righteousness, wealth, desire, and liberation; there is no doubt that listening to it grants the state of Brahman. What more can be said? Where the Lord of Obstacles Himself resides, can anything be ordinary? I have told you all the deeds of the King of Obstacles; worship Ganesha with all your heart, and you will attain happiness. This narrative, which grants all accomplishments, should not be told to the wicked; it should be told with effort to a virtuous devotee of Ganesha.

Suta said: Having said this, the great yogi Mudgala became silent. Daksha, with a joyful heart, bowed to him with folded hands. I have told you the complete story of the Lord of Obstacles, exactly as it emerged from the mouth of Mudgala. I am purified by your association, there is no doubt. O Shaunaka, tell me, what else do you wish to hear that is beneficial to you? O Brahmin, there is nothing equal to the Lord of Obstacles, and this story of the King of Obstacles is the bestower of all accomplishments.

Thus ends the sixteenth chapter of the seventh section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of the Glory of the Narrative."

Dedicated to Lord Gajanana.
Thus ends the seventh section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

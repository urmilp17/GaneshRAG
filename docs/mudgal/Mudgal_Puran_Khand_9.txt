Chapter 1 : Description of the Self-Evident Nature

Salutations to Shri Ganesha. Salutations to Shri Ganesha, Sharada, and the Guru. Shaunaka said: O wise one, having heard the excellent deeds of the Dhumravarna incarnation, which are endowed with various stories, I am not yet satisfied. Now, please tell me the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala. What did Daksha ask the great sage Mudgala again? Suta said: Having heard the supreme greatness of the Dhumravarna form, the Lord of creatures (Daksha), with his hair standing on end in joy, asked the sage again. Daksha said: I am blessed in every way to have heard from you the most wonderful deeds of Dhumravarna, which bestow all accomplishments. Now, O best of sages, tell me about the Yoga that grants the supreme state. You have spoken of the eight Vinayakas; tell me about their form in Yoga. What is the nature of Svananda Yoga and what is non-Yoga? Tell me in detail about their form and the perfect Yoga that grants great peace. Suta said: Being asked thus by Daksha, the great Yogi, filled with joy, said to him: Listen, O Shaunaka, to that which brings happiness to the twice-born.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, from Svananda (the bliss of the Self), by the will of the Lord and through His Maya, four Brahmas emerged, known as Asat, Sat, Samaneti, and the fourth. Through the dependence of each, happiness was created. From that came awareness, then the sense of "I am," and then the Bindu (point), O wise one. Then the fourfold universe—gross, subtle, and causal—arose. These Brahmas, being merged with Nada, were devoid of their own inherent power due to divine destiny and could never attain the enjoyment born of their own activities. Then, they performed severe penance with a single-minded heart, bowing to the primordial source of all. After a thousand divine years, the Lord of Ganas (Ganesha) became pleased and revealed His own single-syllable mantra residing in their hearts. Seeing Him, they were filled with joy and chanted the mantra thereafter. When a hundred years were completed, He revealed His own form. Seeing the form of the man-elephant, they were struck with wonder, and by His grace, they immediately attained the knowledge that reveals His true nature. Knowing Him as the Lord of Svananda, the Brahmas, with great joy, meditated upon Him in their hearts and chanted the mantra for supreme success.

After one year, the Lord of Obstacles became very pleased and appeared before them to grant boons, full of majesty. Seeing Him, the Brahmas praised the venerable Lord of Obstacles, folding their hands for the accomplishment of their respective powers. The Brahmas said: Salutations to You, the Supreme Lord, the Master of obstacles, the eternal giver of happiness to others, the benefactor. Salutations to You, O Dhundhi, who are of self-evident form, the giver of bliss, and the Yoga of self-bliss. Salutations to You, O Lord of the helpless, the soul of all, the ever-free from delusion, the one who holds the negation (Neti). Salutations to You, O Shiva, the giver of the threefold state, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, O Lord of Maya, the manifestation of duality, the soul of all, O Vishnu. Salutations to You, O form of great bliss, the one who transcends all, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, the eternal Truth, the soul of all, devoid of modifications, the Sun, the Infinite. Salutations to You, the limitless, free from distinctions, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, of infinite form, of many manifestations, attached to divine play, the perfect soul. Salutations to You, the great power, the form of Shakti, the enchantress, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, who dwell in the intellect of restraint, the giver of the illusion of self-bliss, the form of accomplishment. Salutations to You, who enter the fourth state as the image of the Self, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, who create the Sankhya form, the form of the fourth state, the awakener. Salutations to You, ever established in the Self, the destroyer of resolve, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, who create the form of awareness, the form of knowledge, the abode of Maya. Salutations to You, the eternal player, the one who manifests in many ways, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, who create the embodied form, the ever-awakened, the remover of illusion. Salutations to You, free from illusion, the giver of the "I am" (So'ham), the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, who create the Bindu, the fourfold player in the four-legged form. Salutations to You, of infinite form, the one who manifests in bodies, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, who create the Nada, the form of sound, the dweller within, the giver of consciousness. Salutations to You, the unborn, residing in the threefold body, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, who dwell within and without, creating the deep sleep state, O Supreme Soul. Salutations to You, the giver of bliss in that form, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, the eternal dream-state, residing in all, the subtle, the soul of existence, the player. Salutations to You, who create the subtle in three ways, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, O Lord, the gross form, creating the waking state in that form. Salutations to You, the food-being, the external soul, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. O Lord of Brahma, protect these Ganas created in Your own form, these servants of Yours. Make them ever devoted to Your lotus feet, O Dhundhi, the Yoga of self-bliss. Salutations to You, with the elephant face, the large belly, four arms, and winnowing-basket ears. Salutations to You, the three-eyed one, holding the noose and goad, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, the human-formed below the neck, the single-tusked, clad in red. Salutations to You, the one with the mouse-banner and mouse-mount, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, the infinite creation on Your left, and the great accomplishment on Your right. Salutations to You, the holder of perfect Maya, the God of gods, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. Salutations to You, residing in the city named Svanvedya, served by joy and others. Salutations to You, the one who plays in the ocean of sugarcane juice, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. We praise Ganesha, the form of Samadhi, the one who is the Brahma of Brahmas, O Great Soul. Still, by our own intellect and proofs, we praise You, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi. We are blessed, O Lord of Ganas, by Your praise and worship, O friend of the lowly. Salutations, salutations, be pleased quickly, O Infinite One, the Yoga of self-bliss, O Dhundhi.

Mudgala said: Having praised the Lord of Ganas thus, they bowed to Him, O Prajapati. Then the elephant-faced Lord, being pleased, spoke to those Brahmas. Shri Ganesha said: Ask for the boons you desire in your hearts, O Brahmas; I am pleased. Through this hymn and penance, I shall grant even those things that are difficult to attain. This hymn composed by you will be the cause of My satisfaction; there is no doubt it will grant all accomplishments. Whoever reads, causes to be read, or listens to this, that best of men, after enjoying all worldly pleasures, will eventually attain Svananda (the bliss of the Self). This will grant victory, sons, and grandsons. It will be the giver of wealth, grain, and cattle. For those lacking limbs, it will grant completeness and destroy diseases. It will burn away the misfortunes and troubles of those who recite it. By reading or hearing this, one will certainly attain knowledge, wisdom, and everything else, including worldly enjoyment, liberation, and the state of Brahma. It is effective in the six acts of magic, destroys the evil deeds of others, and removes the bondage of kings for those who serve it well. Whoever reads it twenty-one times for twenty-one days will attain all their desires; there is no doubt about this. Hearing these words of the Lord of Ganas, the Brahmas bowed to the Lord of Obstacles and, filled with joy, spoke to Him, O Prajapati. The Brahmas said: You are the giver of boons, O Lord of Ganas; grant us that supreme devotion by which great delusion is destroyed; there is no doubt of this. Tell us of the knowledge born of Your own activity, O Lord of all. We shall do Your work, for we are subject to Your command. O Lord, always stay near us for the sake of Your devotees. Make everything free of obstacles, O Elephant-faced One, salutations to You. Make us like Yourself, we who have taken refuge at Your feet. Salutations to Your grace, which grants everything to those who worship You. Whatever we desire, O Lord, let that be fruitful. Grant us the happiness born of Your true resolve, O Leader of Ganas. Hearing their words, pleased by their devotion, He said "So be it" and disappeared. The eight Vinayakas were declared, and the Elephant-faced Lord, in that form, stayed near them for the accomplishment of all purposes. All this has been told regarding the deeds of the Self-Evident One. By hearing or reading it, men attain all accomplishments.

Thus ends the first chapter of the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the essence of the Upanishads, titled "Description of the Self-Evident Nature."

Chapter 2 : The Discourse on the State of Non-Attachment (Ayogacharita)

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: O Daksha, now listen to the story of the Lord of the Ganas, who embodies the state of Nivritti (cessation/renunciation) and grants the status of Brahman. He, who is Svananda (Self-Bliss), the foundation of all, resides within various Brahmas and, through the union of Yoga, becomes one with the worlds. Endowed with various illusions (Maya) and inclined toward diverse states, He shines eternally, being free from the duality of existence and non-existence. After an infinite cycle of time had passed, He sought contentment. Being wise, He desired Nivritti, as He was devoid of Maya. Thus, He worshipped Ganesha along with the various Brahmas, filled with devotion, to destroy the state of union (Samyoga). He worshipped the Lord through the Ekakshara (single-syllable) mantra, meditating on the Elephant-faced Lord within his heart.

Daksha asked: You have spoken of the great single-syllable mantra as being self-evident. Tell me, how does it grant satisfaction to the Lord of the Ganas in His state of non-attachment (Ayoga)?

Mudgala said: The mantra of Ganapati, consisting of both sound and meaning, is considered by the wise to be self-evident and is accepted by all scriptures. Because it represents only a part of the Name, it is the indicator of the state of Ayoga (non-attachment). In that state, there is neither sound nor meaning, nor any union between them. After a hundred years had passed, the Lord of the Ganas, pleased, appeared to grant him boons, for He is the giver of happiness to His devotees. Seeing the Elephant-faced Lord, Svananda was overjoyed and bowed down, worshipping Him with devotion.

Daksha asked: His body is Saguna (with attributes), yet His head is Nirguna (without attributes). In the non-dual state of these two, He is the Elephant-faced Lord, the Self. In the state of Ayoga, there is neither Saguna nor Nirguna. O best of Yogis, how can there be a union of the two? How did the Elephant-faced Lord become the indicator of the state of Ayoga? Remove my doubt with your words of wisdom.

Mudgala said: O Vidhi (Creator), the power of Siddhi became His Maya. Abandoning all illusions, it dissolved into His body. The power of Buddhi (intellect) became His head. It dissolved into Him, abandoning the state of holding illusions. In the union of these two, the self-evident Brahman, which holds the union, became manifest in the form of the Elephant-faced Lord through the state of dissolution. Devoid of Siddhi and Buddhi, He became the indicator of Ayoga. There is no doubt that Ganesha is marked by the elephant face and other signs. Devoid of the three—sound, meaning, and their union—the Elephant-faced Lord shines in the state of Ayoga, forever free from Maya. Now, listen to the peaceful story. Svananda, filled with joy, praised Ganesha.

Svananda said: I bow to the unborn, ancient, eternal, and immutable Lord, who is pure Nivritti, established in deep meditation, the primordial Lord of the Ganas, who is in the state of Ayoga, devoid of Maya, and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not born of the womb, sweat, egg, or earth; who is neither stationary nor moving; who is without beginning, middle, or end; who is the unfailing form, devoid of Maya, and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not earth, water, light, air, or ether; who is not Rajas, Sattva, or Tamas; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not in the waking, dream, deep sleep, or Turiya states; who is not even a point, nor 'I am He'; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not knowledge, nor ignorance, nor delusion, nor free from delusion; who is neither Nirguna nor Saguna; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not action, nor knowledge, nor equal, nor dependent; who is not of the Self, devoid of all modifications; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is neither non-existent nor existent, neither equal nor beyond description; who is not found in various forms of the Self; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not infinite, nor single, nor equal, nor fourth, nor fifth; who always bears the form of Ganesha; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who neither comes nor goes; whom the Vedas call the state of Ayoga; who is always full of Nivritti; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who cannot be described by words or reached by the mind; who is the Lord I speak of; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not Siddhi, not Buddhi, not Maya, but the Supreme Brahman; who is infinite, bearing the elephant face; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who has three eyes, an elephant face, four arms, one tusk, a great belly, and no vehicle; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not in Yoga, not in play, not in His own city, not in His own ocean; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is not fond of devotee or devotion, yet grants Nivritti through Yoga; who destroys the infinite bonds of Maya; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is the Lord of Ganesha, the great soul, whom I, deluded by modifications, wander seeking among the various Brahmas; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is the form of Samadhi, unthinkable, the essence of all, and devoid of all; protect me, O Giver of Nivritti; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is the giver of Siddhi and Buddhi, whom I, deluded, have divided into various forms; I wander in the four goals of life; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is the source of all play and happiness, yet I have found no peace or joy in other yogas; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. I bow to the One who is the beloved of His feet; protect me, O Ganesha, from this infinite delusion; grant me Nivritti, the supreme goal; who is devoid of Maya and immeasurable. Salutations to Ganesha, the embodiment of Nivritti; salutations to the Supreme Lord, the ocean of bliss; salutations to Heramba, the great-bellied; salutations to the Lord of Brahman, the most peaceful. I praise the Lord of the Ganas in the state of Ayoga, who is beyond entry; save me now, O Lord of Obstacles, salutations to You.

Mudgala said: Having praised Ganesha thus, Svananda bowed to Him. Pleased, the Elephant-faced Lord spoke to him.

Shri Ganesha said: Ask for a boon, O great one; I will grant all that you desire. You are surely suffering due to the distinctions of Maya. This hymn composed by you will grant all peace. One who reads it will not suffer from the sorrows born of Maya. I will grant whatever is desired through the reading of this hymn. A mortal can attain the unattainable by listening to it.

Hearing these words of Ganesha, Svananda bowed and spoke with folded hands.

Svananda said: If You are pleased, O Lord of Obstacles, then grant me the happiness born of Nivritti. I desire no other boon; remove this delusion and Maya.

Saying "So be it," Ganesha disappeared. Svananda, feeling dejected, remained in his place. He meditated on the Lord of Ayoga, and then a wonder occurred. All the worlds, led by Brahma, dissolved into Svananda. Then, Svananda himself vanished. Through the power of being devoid of Maya, he shone with joy in the state of Ayoga. Devoid of the Maya of union, he saw nothing—neither himself, nor others, nor the Lord of the Ganas in the state of Ayoga. He became free from all bonds and attained his true nature. This is the entire story of Ayoga. O Daksha, in the state of Ayoga, there is no "you," "I," or eternal Brahman. There is no Ganesha, only the self-evident; through this, one attains Nivritti. Whoever reads or hears this story of Ayoga will enjoy all pleasures and, in the end, attain Nivritti.

Chapter 3 : Description of the Form of Ganesha

Mudgala said: O Daksha, listen now to the character of the Lord of the Ganas, who is the source of complete peace and the master of various yogas. He is eternally beyond the influence of Maya, free from all bonds, and always immersed in the bliss of Brahman. The Supreme Brahman, known as Svananda, was lost due to the Ganesha-yoga, but was recreated by Ganesha, who is the embodiment of complete yoga. In this way, O Prajapati, it was destroyed and recreated many times over many aeons.

Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, always established in the state of Ayoga (non-union), pondered in his heart upon seeing the illusion arising from Svananda. He thought: "Alas, by the influence of Maya, the Brahman, which is the holder of its own bliss, takes on various forms, is born, and dies. I am always free from delusion and Maya, established in unbroken yoga and devoted to Brahman. Who has given me this imperishable state? There is no Brahman that is not imperishable."

Daksha asked: "You previously described the imperishable as having a body, and it is certain that it was created by its own rising. If the Supreme Brahman is described as the form of yoga, how can you, O Lord of Yogis, call it perishable?"

Mudgala replied: "The distinctions of destruction and non-destruction arise from Svananda. That which is beyond difference and non-difference is called self-evident. Yogis speak of it to instruct their disciples, though it is impossible to describe it without some distinction. I will now speak of the Ayoga, which is called imperishable, and the perishable form, which is self-evident, for the sake of understanding. 

The Ayoga, filled with contemplation and free from bonds, considered the happiness arising from detachment to be trivial. He thought: 'I am always free from Maya, and there is no doubt about it; the absence of Maya does not exist in Brahman.' Thinking this, he became filled with sorrow and devoted himself to the path of yoga. He performed severe penance. After ten years, the Lord of the Ganas, pleased, appeared in his heart and revealed his single-syllable mantra. The yogi, overjoyed, chanted the mantra and meditated on Ganesha, the giver of peace. Soon, he saw the auspicious form of Ganapati, the great Lord with the elephant face and other marks. 

Astonished, he wondered: 'Who is this elephant-faced deity shining in the heart?' By the grace of Ganesha, he attained eternal knowledge and understood the play of Vighnesha. Filled with joy, he meditated on the elephant-faced deity and chanted the mantra with devotion to attain peace. Soon, the elephant-faced Lord appeared before him, granting boons out of love for his devotee.

Daksha said: "O Brahmin, you speak of wonders that I do not understand. The mystery of your words, woven with yoga, is profound. Eight Vinayakas are spoken of, all with elephant faces and marked with the single tusk. When those with single-minded devotion worship Ganesha, he appears in their hearts. Yet, when they see him, they are astonished and do not know his true form. How can the wise, who are always elephant-faced, see the elephant-faced one? Why do they not know the secret of the single syllable? How does Ganesha, established in the form of union, not know the mantra-bearing Lord? This Ayoga-Ganatha, bearing the elephant form, does not recognize the Lord of the Ganas who is with the mantra. What kind of Lord is this, the speaker of complete yoga, whom even the Vighneshas do not recognize despite bearing his form? In union, Ganesha is endowed with Siddhi and Buddhi, but in non-union, he is described as devoid of them. How can Ganesha, who is beyond union and non-union, be said to be joined with Maya? Tell me this mystery."

Mudgala replied: "You have asked well, O Daksha. This is a supreme mystery, blissful to yogis and peaceful to those of contemplative mind. Listen to his character with a focused mind, or you will remain in constant delusion. Just as this world is always functioning as the form of Brahman, yet does not know the Brahman form situated within it, so it is. Because he is devoid of name and form, he has no mantra. Through the power of penance, the Lord perceives the Pranava (Om). Seeing the great seed, the form of Pranava, he ponders in his heart. Through penance and yoga, he knows the four-footed, world-pervading Lord. By the power of yoga, he sees the deity who is the meaning of Pranava, the one who resides in the essence of all, as the world itself. 

If the Pranava is the body, the meaning of the Pranava is the elephant form. The union of the two is Ganesha, the wearer of the yoga form. Seeing this great form of yoga, the world, filled with devotion, desires to worship him. He wondered how this Brahman, which is in such great peace, could have a body, and resolved to serve the great soul. The Lord, knowing the thought in his heart, assumed the elephant-faced form, and the devotee was again astonished. By the grace of yoga-service, he knows the Lord. 

Thus, the eight Ganeshas are spoken of as speakers of yoga. The Brahman situated in their hearts is self-evident, without doubt. Endowed with great majesty, they never know it; they consider themselves to be the form of Brahman. Thinking there is nothing higher than themselves, they become deluded and engage in play within the world. After playing in various ways through many aeons, they desire eternal peace. Then, abandoning play, they remain in their own glory. Yet, they themselves create the world again. Then, filled with sorrow, they wonder: 'By whom are we driven to create this diverse world? We have no independence. We are not the form of Brahman, but are always joined with the delusion of Maya.' Thinking this, they performed penance and became united with yoga. 

Gradually, all the Ganas attained dissolution in the form of complete yoga, O Prajapati. I will now tell you the method that destroys doubt. Yoga is described as eightfold: Shama (calmness), Dama (restraint), Asana (posture), Pranayama (breath control), Pratyahara (withdrawal of senses), Dharana (concentration), Dhyana (meditation), and Samadhi (absorption). 

Restraining the mind with effort while following one's own duty to attain Brahman is called Shama. Restraining the body for the sake of peace while established in one's duty is called Dama. Practicing postures like Svastika for the sake of yoga is called Asana. Practicing breath control gradually for yoga is called Pranayama. Withdrawing the senses from various objects and engaging them in yoga is Pratyahara. Holding the Brahman, described by the great sentences, in the mind is called Dharana. The realization of Brahman in the heart through unity is called Dhyana. When one becomes absorbed in the Supreme Brahman, where neither Brahman nor the self is perceived as separate, that is called Samadhi. 

This eightfold yoga is spoken of for the attainment of yoga. For those who practice it, it will surely grant Brahman. The souls joined with Maya are considered to have different bodies; for them, this eightfold path is the means. The various elements are said to be forms of Brahman. The yoga of these is described as threefold. Listen to it, for it grants happiness. Dharana, Dhyana, and Samadhi are the means to worship through yoga and attain peace. Abandoning the enjoyments of the world and holding onto Brahman is called penance. If one desires complete peace, the wise should know that abandoning enjoyments is their penance. 

Brahman is described in the Vedas as neither devoid of mind and speech nor consisting of mind and speech; that is the Lord of the Ganas. There is neither Saguna (with attributes) nor Nirguna (without attributes) in Ganesha; see him in the Vedas, for he is remembered as the form of yoga. When the Brahmas (souls) were filled with sorrow due to dependence, they performed penance to attain Brahman. By the power of severe penance, inspiration arises in the heart. Know that to be the king of mantras of the Brahmas. Then, filled with joy, they remembered the experience; that is their chanting. They always saw the yoga situated in the heart; that is described as the form of the Lord of the Ganas. By being absorbed in that, they remain as Brahmas. That is to be known as the grace of Ganesha. 

If this yoga-Brahman were to have a body, they desired to worship it. The Lord, moved by their devotion, appeared before them as the elephant-faced one to grant them boons. The marks of the Brahman form that I described earlier were seen by them in the form of Ganesha. Then they bowed to him and worshipped him daily. In the end, they became filled with yoga. I have told you the character of the Brahmas; now listen to the other practices of penance and yoga, O Prajapati. 

If Ganesha, the Vakatunda (curved-trunk), is filled with sorrow due to dependence, he becomes devoted to the yoga of peace. Then, filled with penance, he strives for yoga in three ways; by this, he sees the Ekadanta (single-tusked) situated in his heart. Vakatunda was situated in the bodies with the feeling of Brahman; therefore, the embodied one is considered superior and of a different form. Thus, Vakatunda does not know the Ekadanta, the highest of the high; I will tell you the difference. The body of three qualities is the form, and the head is considered the fourth (Turiya). Vakatunda is the speaker of the Bindu-Brahman in the union of the two. That which is known as being joined with delusion in the four types of bodies is called the Jiva (individual soul), which is never devoid of difference. That which is free from delusion, the witness-form, the holder of the Supreme Self, is called Ekadanta; in the union of the two, it is known as 'I am That'. 

The Jiva is situated in the body-form, and the Supreme Self is the head. There is no doubt that Ekadanta is always in the elephant form. Seeing the Jiva and the Supreme Self as devoid of difference, the Vakatunda-Gajanana becomes astonished. I have said that Vakatunda is joined with various differences; seeing the difference-less Ekadanta, he asked him again. The 'I am That' situated in the heart, which is called the Brahman-form, is the Ekadanta of the Bindu, never joined with the body. The Brahman that was requested by Vakatunda for the sake of service became the Ekadanta. Just as this whole world is described as the form of Brahman, so Vakatunda should be known as the wearer of the Ekadanta form. The Saguna is known as the human form, and the Nirguna as the elephant form; in the union of the two, the Supreme Brahman is called Gajanana. 

Requested by all for the sake of service, he became embodied. Ganesha, known as the Brahman-form, is situated in various Brahmas. Therefore, O Daksha, the Lord of the Ganas does not know the Lord of the Ganas; joined with various delusions, he does not know the giver of peace due to confusion. In the state of self-rising, the body is called Bindu-maya. The 'I am That' is the head, the elephant form; in the union of the two is the Mahodara (great-bellied one). In this way, the Lord of the Ganas is situated in them, marked by the elephant face and other signs, devoted to various Brahmas. 

In the union of the Lord of the Ganas, the body is the self-rising one; the head is the rising from the other, there is no doubt. In non-union, the head is devoid of intelligence and Siddhi. Ganesha is described as the union of the two. In complete yoga, the body is considered the sign of union, and the head is the elephant form, the sign of non-union. The word 'Gaja' (elephant) is described as the meaning of imperishability. There is no Brahman higher than Ayoga, which is called imperishable. The confusion of union and non-union is read everywhere. O Daksha, the restraint of the mind is called Siddhi. The state of union and non-union is considered to be the mind holding confusion. The master of both is the Lord of the Ganas, the speaker of complete yoga. 

If I am the form of Ganesha, and union and non-union are mine, why are they called the influence of Maya? Through this, a man attains peace. The elephant-faced one, the soul of union, stands everywhere without difference; there is no doubt he is joined with the Maya of union. The elephant-faced one, the form of yoga, stands everywhere without difference, joined with the Maya of non-union in a distinct way. If I call the Lord of the Ganas the yoga that is the soul of all, then he is known as the 'Anvaya' (inherent) situated in various Brahmas. If the elephant-faced one has never come or gone, they call him the 'Vyatireka' (transcendent) liberated in various Brahmas. 

Therefore, I am Ganesha through the dissolution of the mind, there is no doubt. By abandoning the five states of the mind, one attains complete yoga. I will tell you of the happiness born of Samadhi through reasoning. If I become that form in Ganesha through yoga, then the experience born of union is spoken of in the hearts of yogis. When I am the Lord of the Ganas, who has never come or gone, then the experience born of non-union is spoken of, which is the giver of peace. Therefore, how can the yoga of non-union function there? The mind is of various kinds, O Daksha, holding both union and non-union. When I am the soul of yoga in yoga, then peace is attained. That yoga-peace is the peace of peace, O Prajapati. There is no Brahman higher than that, which is certain through yoga. Therefore, this Lord of the Ganas is called the Lord of the Brahmas, because he is the protector of the Brahmas of union and non-union. I have told you all this supreme mystery. Now listen to the character of Gajanana as it is, O Daksha.

Chapter 4 : The Description of the Life of Purnayoga

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Seeing Gajanana arrive, moved by devotion and penance, Ayoga was filled with immense joy; he rose and bowed down to Him. Then, having worshipped Lord Ganesha with various offerings and bowing again, he praised the Lord of Obstacles with folded hands.

Ayoga said: Salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga, who are the embodiment of peace, the giver of supreme tranquility, and beyond the reach of speech and mind. O Dhundhi, You are the one who contemplates through speech and mind; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. I cannot describe You as being composed of speech and mind, nor can I praise You as the Universal Form. O Great Soul, salutations to You, the eternal Ganesha; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. How can I call You devoid of speech and mind, or sometimes composed of union? You are not merely the form of Yoga; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. From You, the great universes and the supreme creations are born, and into You, they dissolve in non-dual essence; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the eternal one, the giver of liberation, the one devoid of illusion, the Lord of the Ganas, who is easily attained by transcending speech and mind; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the embodiment of self-bliss, the giver of peace, who plays along with spiritual power and intellect, attainable only by the self beyond speech and mind; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the unmanifest, the spontaneous, the one devoid of delusion, the ever-auspicious, the Lord of the three-fold existence, the one who transcends all negations; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the common form, the essence of the three Vedas, the light of duality, the Vishnu, the Lord of Bliss, the playful one; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the essence of the Self, the sun attained through eternal nectar-like yoga, the one devoid of divisions, the life-force; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, who are the essence of all in various manifestations, the power behind all, the one who bestows supreme delusion through infinite qualities; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the witness, the one attained through Sankhya-yoga, established in the Self, devoid of mere intellectual knowledge, the Supreme; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the embodiment of knowledge, the player of numbers, the protector, the Lord of the three-fold existence, the large-bellied one; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the 'I am' essence, the consciousness, the one associated with delusion yet devoid of a body, the one who plays in the form of the embodied soul; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the source of the Bindu, the four-footed one, the origin of the four-fold creation, the infinite principle, the enjoyer of the body; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the Turiya-bodied, the essence of dissolution, the form of time, the Lord of Ganas, the essence of sound, the one who dwells in the three bodies; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, who reside in the sheath of bliss, the Prajna, the deep-sleep state, the eternal Lord, the mover of both; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, who dwell within all, the subtle body, the dream state, the Taijasa, the Hiranyagarbha, the mover of both; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, who reside externally, the food-sheath, the Vishva-Vaishvanara, the mover of both, the gross form, the waking state; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. Salutations to You, the duality of the universe, the player in the form of various cosmic eggs, the power, the creator, the preserver, the destroyer; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. These Vighneshvaras are but partial rays of You, the Lord of Ganas; You are never limited by them; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga. How can I praise You, O Lord, who are Ganesha, beyond union and beyond separation? Be pleased with this identity; salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga.

Mudgala said: Having praised Ganesha in this way, Ayoga bowed with folded hands. The Lord of Ganas spoke to him with a deep, resonant voice.

Ganesha said: O great soul, ask for the boons you desire in your heart. I am pleased with your devotion and this hymn, O Lord of Yoga. This hymn composed by you grants the status of Yoga and peace. Those who recite or hear it daily will attain various spiritual perfections. I will grant whatever you desire through the recitation of this hymn. May it bring me joy and bestow the spirit of devotion. If one recites it twenty-one times for twenty-one days, I will grant even that which is difficult to attain. Having said this, the Lord of Ganesha embraced the leader of the Ganas, filling Ayoga with joy. Then, Ayoga, bowing repeatedly to the Lord of Ganas, spoke with devotion, his hair standing on end with delight.

Ayoga said: If, O Dhundhi, You are pleased by my devotion and will grant me boons, then grant me firm and supreme devotion to Your lotus feet. Make me established in the form of Purnayoga, O Gajanana. I do not desire the happiness of liberation, O Lord; salutations to You.

Mudgala said: Hearing the excellent words of Ayoga, Ganesha said "So be it" and disappeared. Ayoga kept Him in his heart and worshipped Him with a single-minded focus. In the end, he too became one with Yoga and a master of liberation. Thus, O Prajapati, I have briefly told you the life of Ganesha, the bearer of Purnayoga. Therefore, worship the Lord of Ganas with devotion. By doing so, you will surely complete this sacrifice. In the end, you will be united with Yoga and become one with Brahman. Give me permission to leave, as I am satisfied by your hospitality. I have told you the entire secret of the Lord of Ganas; there is nothing else beyond this that is accepted by the Vedas. Whoever hears or recites this will attain all auspiciousness. In the end, becoming one with Yoga, they will abide in the form of Ganesha.

Chapter 5 : Sarvabhauma Yoga

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: I am blessed and fulfilled, O Great Sage, as you have enabled me to hear the glory of Ganesha, which bestows Yoga. You have explained the essence of the Ganesha Gita and the complete knowledge that provides Yoga in a concise manner. However, I have not fully grasped it; therefore, please explain it to me simply, along with the systematic path of Yoga, by which a person becomes a Yogi.

Suta said: Being asked thus, the great Yogi Mudgala, filled with great joy, spoke to him as follows.

Mudgala said: You are blessed and fulfilled, for you are truly the devotee of Ganapati. It is no wonder that you ask about His manifestations. O fortunate one, what you have asked is beneficial to the world and provides the path of Yoga and peace to all in an easy way. I will tell you with affection the secret of the Vedas. Listen with a focused mind, O Daksha, to the Yoga that bestows peace.

The mind is said to be of five types, and there are five types of essences within them. Abandoning the mind filled with these essences, a human becomes a Yogi. Know that the five types of the mind, which exist in the worlds and in Brahman, are: Kshipra (distracted), Mudha (deluded), Vikshipta (scattered), Ekagra (one-pointed), and Nirodhaka (restrained).

The various creatures that exist in the individual state, born in the eighty-four lakh species, are skilled in the paths of worldly enjoyment. They are devoted to various tasks within their own spheres. For the sake of accomplishing these tasks, their mind is always engaged in actions, and through the manifestation of those tasks, it illuminates them in the heart. Because of this, they become efficient in their work; know this, O giver of honor, as the Kshipra (distracted) mind.

Among them, some are ignorant, some are considered knowledgeable, and some are mediocre; thus, people are divided into various categories. They exist in three-fold divisions with various distinctions, and their forms, endowed with various types of knowledge, cannot be described. Those creatures in the individual state who are devoid of knowledge in all their actions are called Mudha (deluded) by the world, though they are always clever in worldly matters. Their mind, which illuminates them, is of a deluded nature; this should be understood as the Mudha mind.

Those people, O Daksha, who are always devoted to their own duties and desire eternal liberation through various austerities, even while being detached from worldly objects and devoted to Brahman, do not see Brahman in their hearts. Their minds never find pleasure in worldly happiness. Therefore, they are considered by Yogis of old to have a Vikshipta (scattered) mind. They perform all actions as an offering to Brahman, both internally and externally.

The mind that is especially distracted by Maya in the world, and which seeks to manifest the feeling of liberation through various means, is called the Vikshipta mind. Bewildered by the power of various actions, one thinks the deity is pleased with them. Sometimes, taking the grace of the deity, one performs actions without a foundation, following the will of the gods in vain. One sees various states in dreams and, with a mind absorbed in them, treats the dream as truth. Thus, deluded by the Vikshipta state, one performs various austerities without a foundation.

The mind that resides in the collective and individual, which is the supreme Virat, permeates the individual and enjoys various pleasures. The action of the Virat is said to be pervasive, as accepted by the scriptures. In that, the mind is considered the illuminator of the pervasive state. In the various elements, whether gross or subtle, the mind is considered to be situated due to its pervasive nature.

In the union of the collective and individual, the food is the supreme gross Brahman. Similarly, the dream state is subtle, and the deep sleep state is balanced. The consciousness is considered complete, and the sound-form is also considered as such. The supreme Brahman known as Asmita (ego) is said to be the mind in these. Know the Ekagra (one-pointed) mind to be the one that illuminates the unity of the collective and individual parts. Because it does not become different in the collective and individual states, it is called Ekagra, the creator of knowledge.

The body is said to be four-fold: gross, subtle, causal, and the fourth, known as Asmita; the embodied soul is considered to reside in these. The embodied soul, deluded by the bodies, is called Jiva (the individual soul). The same soul, when devoid of delusion, is called Shiva by the wise. The terms Jiva and Shiva are merely due to the illusion of the embodied soul; because of their identical nature, there is no difference between them. The Ekagra mind, which illuminates the nature of Jiva and Shiva, resides within the embodied soul.

There is no mind beyond the Asmita state other than the Ekagra mind. In the opinion of Yogis, the Ekagra mind consists of the body and the embodied soul. Where the union of the four bodies arises in the form of Brahman, that point-like form should be known as the all-pervading Brahman. It is said by the wise to be four-footed, yet always devoid of the four; the Brahman of the bodies is known as the one that pervades the nature of all bodies. The mind resides in that which is devoid of the four, illuminating the form of the four-footed.

Because of the all-pervading nature of the Self, it does not become different in the bodies. The mind that illuminates the nature of the point (Bindu) is called Nirodha (restrained). It is the Brahman that is devoid of delusion, situated in the nature of Jiva and Shiva, known as "I am He," and always devoid of illusion. The mind that illuminates the delusion-free state of Jiva and Shiva, residing in the nature of the Self, is called Nirodha by the wise.

In the union of the body and the embodied soul, there is a spontaneous rising, known as the knowledge of the duality of Brahman, which is always playing. I will tell you the form of the Nirodha mind; listen with a focused heart, O Lord of the people. The external is called gross, the internal is considered subtle, and the one that resides in the unity of both is the supreme Bliss-body. Devoid of the three, and the driver of the three, is the Asmita-form; this should be known in the heart of the Yogi through the experience of "I."

In the union of these four, the supreme Brahman, which is all-encompassing, is the fifth, known as the Bindu. The four-fold imagination of man resides in the heart; beyond this, there is no difference by which this imagination could exist. Upon abandoning the Bindu, the mind becomes ever-supportless. That is constantly called Brahman, devoid of the meditator. Where nothing is known due to the absence of difference, that mind, which is the essence of "I am He," bestows the supportless Samadhi.

The mind that relies on support is four-fold, residing in the embodied soul. That which is devoid of support becomes the consciousness residing in the soul. Upon abandoning the supportless state, there is greater restraint, as the mind is devoid of both body and soul; there is no doubt about this. That mind should be known with reverence as being devoid of mind and speech, as it is devoid of existence and non-existence, due to the union of the support and the supported.

The union of support does not exist in five ways in the heart of the Yogi; therefore, the supportless state is devoid of mind and speech. Everything is known through knowledge in the supportless Samadhi; there is no knowledge of the support in that state. Thus, the knowledge-form Brahman bestows Samadhi on the body and the embodied soul, and the mind that illuminates the union of the body and the embodied soul is spoken of.

One knows the happiness of the body and the happiness of the soul continuously. The happiness that arises in their union is known as the Brahman of knowledge. Through the knowledge of words and the knowledge of objects, a rising occurs; therefore, it is called spontaneous rising. He who, having known happiness, performs the rising himself, and having created the body and the soul, plays, is devoted to Brahman. The mind that illuminates that rising is called Nirodha; there is no doubt that this should be known in the heart of the Yogi.

Upon abandoning knowledge, the Brahman is called Sankhya, O Lord of the people. Who can count it, due to the effect of the destruction of knowledge? The Brahman devoid of rising is Sankhya, which is of the nature of the witness. O Lord of the people, how can there be a rising from that which is devoid of knowledge?

The knowledge born of Prakriti and the Purusha born of them—the Sankhya does not know the happiness of Yoga due to the absence of knowledge. Just as the rising of a person occurs after knowing happiness, O Lord of the people, so too does the rising of Brahman occur after knowing the happiness of Maya. Thus, the rising from the other is always established in Sankhya. I will tell you the Yoga by which a person becomes a Yogi.

If it is accompanied by rising, then it is so; but the rising-devoid state, O Daksha, is the primordial seed. Sankhya always shines in the state devoid of rising, and the spontaneous rising itself has emerged from that effect. This was not done by Sankhya, yet due to the absence of rising, the spontaneous rising occurs; there is no doubt about this.

When there is untruth, O Daksha, then truth is proclaimed. When one Brahman remains, who can speak of it as truth? He who has not seen untruth does not know the truth. Because of its constant nature and the demonstration of a single state, it is held to be devoid of rising, and the spontaneous rising arises by itself. It was not created by Sankhya; it emerged by itself. Therefore, the rising from the other is attained there; there is no doubt.

The mind that illuminates the count-less state operates there; it is known as Nirodha through the illumination devoid of knowledge. Brahman endowed with rising is called knowledge-form; Sankhya is devoid of rising, and in their union, it is of its own nature. There is no rising from the other there, O Lord of the people. In the union of the worlds and Brahman, it becomes its own nature.

There is no union beyond its own nature; it is always one with it. That is called Samadhi, which is the inherent nature of Brahman. The mind that illuminates the state of non-difference of the Self resides there; there is no doubt. The union is five-fold, O Daksha; listen to its form. I will tell you the difference for the benefit of the people.

These differences are described as having merged through Yoga. Through self-realization, the doubt is removed, and by that, they are created and sustained. Taking the form of these, it plays in its own nature. This is described by the knowers of the Vedas as the performer of various plays. These differences, associated with birth and death, are called the happiness of the unreal because of their own nature.

That Brahman, known as the form of Shakti, should be known through the holding of differences. The mind remains fixed there through the manifestation of the unreal. The nectar-like Brahman, which is devoid of these differences, is called the life of these, and that is the nature of the real. That Brahman is called Solar due to its difference-less effect. The mind that illuminates the eternal is considered Nirodha.

Unreal and real are said to be two-fold; in their union, the supreme Brahman, which is self-realizable, is Vishnu due to the entry into duality. The mind that illuminates bliss is called Nirodha; the great Yogis attain that through Samadhi. Devoid of the three, endowed with the three, the unmanifest, the "not this"—the fourth Brahman is called so due to the effect of being devoid of delusion.

That should be known as Shiva by the service of Yoga. The mind that illuminates the "not this" resides there. Three-fold associated with delusion, the fourth is devoid of delusion. In the union of these, it is called self-realizable. The fifth Brahman, which is the state of complete union, is remembered as such. There is no union beyond that, as always said by the Yogis.

That very Lord of the Ganas is called the Self-Bliss. The mind that illuminates its own nature resides there, all-pervading. Therefore, the Brahman beyond this Yoga is devoid of Maya. That is what the Yogis call the bestower of the happiness of liberation. The union of the worlds and Brahman is not spoken of there. Because of that, it is called non-Yoga.

The union of the Self with Brahman is called being one with it. Therefore, liberation is attained; hence it is called the state of liberation. Through the destruction of the self-realizable, it is attained in Samadhi. Where has the Brahman, which has neither come nor gone, found union? The mind that illuminates non-Yoga resides in the refutations, bestowing the happiness of liberation.

This five-fold mind has been told to you, O Lord of the people. It is the knower of the worlds and Brahman, the illuminator of various lights. The five planes of the mind of various natures are well-known. Upon abandoning the planes, the mind becomes Brahman-like. Therefore, abandon the five-fold mind through the service of Yoga. You will become a Yogi, O Daksha; there is no doubt in this wish-fulfilling gem.

The state of union and non-union is considered Nirodha by the wise. There is no Nirodha beyond this, as accepted by all. By the nature of the Sarvabhauma (all-pervading) Self, a human becomes a Yogi. There, the mind attains peace, abandoning the delusion born of the planes. The happiness that exists in the heart of the Yogi in the form of Yoga can never be described, O Daksha.

The mind is said to be four-fold, consisting of mind and speech; the Nirodha state is considered devoid of mind and speech. Therefore, Yoga is described as not being of the nature of mind and speech. Even the state devoid of mind and speech is not described by the Yogis. How is Brahman there, and by what kind of Yoga? A person becomes Brahman-like and attains peace from these.

When a person becomes a Yogi in Yoga, it is never known. Is Brahman a means, or is it the cause of the Brahman-like state? There is no being one with Brahman, nor is there the abandonment of being one with it. Therefore, take hold of peace and be devoted to the Yoga of peace.

In the five-fold minds, the supreme of the worlds is seen. One should not hold the essence of the divine happiness of Brahman there. O Lord of the people, having made the mind devoid of various essences, and abandoning them, one becomes a Yogi and attains peace through peace. Just as rivers go to the ocean even without being urged, so too does the best of Yogis enjoy desires due to past karma.

In the various plays of duality, devoted to equanimity, the Yogi becomes peaceful and is always absorbed in the happiness of Yoga.

Thus ends the fifth chapter named Sarvabhauma Yoga, in the dialogue between Mudgala and Daksha, in the Yoga-charita section of the ninth part of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 6 : The Description of the Peace and Happiness Experienced by the Yogi’s Mind

Daksa said: O Great Sage, please describe the experience of Yoga through which the mind attains peace, and by renouncing all worldly tastes, remains established in supreme tranquility.

Mudgala said: O Daksa, in that state, the happiness born of the world or Brahman is not seen as separate, for seeing it as illusory, one becomes established in liberation. I am not the body, nor is the body mine; I am not associated with various delusions. I am not the manifest or unmanifest, nor am I devoid of them. I am not the collective, nor am I ever the individual. I am Brahman, existing in all, yet devoid of those specific attributes. I am not the enjoyer of external pleasures, nor am I devoted to internal states. I am not moving in equality, nor do I hold onto the perception of the Self. I am not gross, nor am I subtle, nor am I ever in the state of bliss. I am not situated in the sound-body, yet I am considered to be of all forms always.

If I have four feet, they are sustained by me through Maya; by the influence of being devoid of Maya, I am instantly without feet. The state of being with or without Maya is not established in me; the duality established by Maya is indeed a great wonder. Through this experience alone, the Yogi becomes one with the point (Bindu). By the nature of being absorbed there, he always acts in peace. Now, I am devoid of difference, and no difference exists in me. I am constantly established here in a continuous state. I do not create, nor do I destroy, nor do I sustain—this is certain. The world shines by itself, just as it is held within me. It arises by its own nature, remains, and is destroyed; the world does not cause me any loss or gain at any time.

I am not the Jiva (individual soul) in the world, nor am I Shiva, the witness. Through delusion, the non-Yogis always call me associated with or devoid of Maya. Just as a cloud bank born of the sky stays everywhere, can the sun ever be covered by it, O Daksa? When the cloud is destroyed, is the sun directly devoid of covering? Similarly, those who speak of delusion and non-delusion in me do so out of ignorance. In me, who is always of the nature of the one Self, how can the two states of delusion and non-delusion arise? See, they are born of the influence of Maya. Nevertheless, I am undoubtedly deluded when associated with the world. Having known the Self, I am the undeluded, eternal witness, without doubt. By Maya, I am called the Jiva, and likewise, I am Shiva, without doubt. In me, who is Brahman, the world is seen and yet not seen. Knowing this, the Yogi always remains in peace. Abandoning the sense of duality, he remains established in "I am Brahman."

Beyond this, I shall speak of the form of the experience of Brahman. I am never the body, nor am I ever situated in the body. The world does not exist in me, nor am I in the world. Being devoid of difference and non-difference, I am Brahman, delighted in Brahman. I am the form of the world; everything is established in me. There is no doubt that I am without the world, always holding one form. I do not create, sustain, or destroy the world at any time. The world is always created, destroyed, and sustained by me. The continuous state is not seen in me, nor is the world of various forms and bodies. I am always of the nature of consciousness, and I am also the body and the embodied. O Daksa, for me, there is neither bondage nor liberation.

Associated with Maya, I am considered the body and the embodied; by the influence of being devoid of Maya, I am devoid of both. Where is the state of being with or without Maya seen in me? I am always of the nature of consciousness, Brahman, and Brahman-realized. Thus, O Prajapati, through this experience of the nature of consciousness, the Yogi, endowed with Yoga, always remains in peace. Beyond this, I shall speak of the Brahman-state called "Knowledge." Nature (Prakriti) is not that, and I am certainly not the Purusha (the Person). Through the union of the two, I am Knowledge; I am Brahman, devoid of enjoyment. I am always established in Brahman, due to the influence of being devoid of play.

The entire world is of a false nature, and the world-soul is also considered as such. Brahman, which brings about the union of the two, is the illuminator of the root of falsehood. In me, who is Brahman, there is neither Nature nor the Person anywhere. The union of the two is a form of delusion, always intent on play. The state of arising from consciousness always occurs from Brahman. How can that which is false exist in me? "Brahman is Truth," it is said by those who know the Vedas. How can the play, which is of an unreal form and gives rise to delusion, exist there? Always attached to the bliss of Brahman, and established in Brahman as Brahman, there is no difference there, nor is there ever the state of male or female.

Everything created by Maya is undoubtedly called unreal. That Maya does not exist in me, O Daksa; it is baseless and full of delusion. Seeing me as the form of Brahman, it destroys itself. Then that Maya itself directly becomes the form of Truth. If I am associated with Maya, and if I create the world of male and female, then how does Truth exist in me? Where the unreal form does not exist at all, according to the Vedic view, that alone is called Truth; hence, knowledge is not seen. The entire world is unreal, consisting of Nature and the Person. Therefore, I am distinct, the one and only, the form of Truth, without doubt. Through such Yoga, a man becomes endowed with peace. Abandoning the various counts, he becomes established in the nature of Brahman.

Now, I shall speak of the Yoga of Self-Bliss for your benefit. Listen to it, O Lord of the people, with an attentive mind. Truth is devoid of arising, and the unreal is associated with arising. In Brahman, the two-fold does not exist; it is always in its own nature. Knowledge is the arising of the Self, and Sankhya is the state devoid of arising. In the union of the two, the supreme Brahman becomes the bliss of the Self, which is non-existent. I am devoid of arising, and I am not associated with arising; I am devoid of both, see my supreme Yoga. In me, who is Brahman, everything becomes of the nature of the Self. There, neither association with arising nor the state of being devoid of it is seen.

Alas, deluded by the influence of Maya, they call me associated with or devoid of arising, while I am established in my own nature. Brahman is called associated with arising and devoid of arising. In Brahman, everything is of that nature, due to the state of union and non-union. When the union of the Yogi occurs in me, who is Brahman, who then sees the associated or the devoid? Those who are not Yogis always see the diverse due to the state of being devoid of Samadhi; there is no doubt that this does not exist for the Yogis. "Everything is Brahman," it is said by those who know the Vedas. Therefore, abandoning Maya, a man becomes of the form of Brahman. By this method, he who practices Yoga through Samadhi becomes one with Brahman and attains eternal peace.

Now, O Sir, I shall speak of the method of the true nature of the Self. Knowing this, a human becomes a Yogi through the path of Yoga. "Everything is Brahman," it is said in the Vedas; there is no doubt about this. That same Brahman is associated with limiting adjuncts due to the influence of Maya. Brahman, which is one and without a second, according to the Vedic debate—how does everything appear there, intent on various states? I am not associated with arising, nor am I devoid of arising. I am the union of the two, and I am not established in this non-dual state always. There is no Truth in me, nor is there the unreal; the two are associated as the Supreme. There is no giver of union in Brahman; I am always called Brahman. I have not created the form of Truth, nor the form of the unreal. I am not the union of the two, nor am I immortal by the nature of the Self. Where do the states of beginning, middle, and end come from in me? I am always established in the immortal, the Self, realized as Brahman.

All are associated with Maya, and the life of all is the Supreme. They speak of me specifically, but everything does not exist in me. The Supreme Brahman, who is the soul of all, becomes endowed with various plays. It also becomes devoid of play, and that is beyond both. I have not come there, O Daksa, nor have I ever gone. See, life happens by itself due to my influence. Where is the transformation of the soul of all seen in me at any time? Those who are deluded speak of me as the life beyond the Brahmas. The life of the Brahmas and the worlds is not sustained by me. It is seen in me in vain by its own influence. I am always established in Brahman, devoid of difference and non-difference. By the influence of non-duality, I am the Self, of a complete nature. By this chief Yoga, a man becomes a Yogi. By being one with Brahman, he always becomes endowed with peace.

Beyond this, I shall speak of the form of the experience of Brahman, which is called the same, with great affection, by which a man becomes a Yogi. I am not the soul of all, O Daksa, nor am I ever the nature of the Self. I am Bliss, in the equality of the two, in Brahman, by the nature of Brahman. By the power of Maya, the soul of all is established. I am established in the equality of the two, which is one and without a second. In Brahman, where does the state of all and the state of being devoid of state come from? The immortal and the root of the dead do not exist in me at any time. It is endowed with infinite play, and there is no state devoid of play. See my greatness, in which the endowed and the devoid of play exist in me.

I am always established in the form of the undivided, pure. Being devoid of various transformations, I am easily devoid of both. There is no doubt that the state of all exists in Brahman. The state of the Self is always there; hence, equality is remembered in the Vedas. Whatever is spoken of in the Vedas, Brahman is considered to be established in them. It is not described there as such, nor is it of the form of Brahman. Therefore, Brahman is called equal according to the Vedic debate. Consider the bliss from the bliss of both, all around. I am Bliss, without doubt, established in Brahman. Through this experience alone, a man becomes endowed with peace.

Therefore, O Prajapati, I shall tell you of the state of the manifest, the greatness of the experience of Brahman, by which a man becomes a Yogi. I am of the unmanifest form, always considered devoid of the three. I am devoid of delusion in the three, and I am the Supreme, the doer of "not this, not this." The soul of all does not exist in me, nor is it of the nature of the immortal. The equality is in the state of independence, and in Brahman, it is of the nature of bliss. The delusion called "devoid of arising" is established in the all-pervading. The delusion of the immortal is always spoken of in the undivided Brahman. The bliss in all states is of the form of delusion due to equality. How can independence exist in those three? I am extremely associated with the three, yet I am also beyond the three delusions. I am always distinct from the three, and I am the doer of "not this" easily.

Beyond that, which is associated with various states and devoid of states, is the equality that goes to both; the unmanifest is remembered as beyond them. Having created the three-fold Maya, I play there with joy. I am devoid of delusion, even then, by the influence of Maya. By my command, the three always move here, without doubt. Associated with their own activities, I am independent by influence. I am the consciousness within the three, and I stay here, without doubt. There is no one who is the motivator within me, O virtuous one. The motivator is always established in the three, in their own natures. Nevertheless, I have no delusion, nor am I called the motivator. I am of the unmanifest form, as stated in the Vedas. There is no diversity here; I am always devoid of the three-fold diversity.

There is no diversity here by any proof; the unmanifest Brahman is eternal. I am that, attached to bliss, always through Yoga in Brahman. I am the doer of "not this" for all, the Lord holding the great staff. There is no doer of "not this" for me; who is that? I am the unmanifest easily. I am devoid of mind and speech, the unmanifest, considered the Supreme. Through this experience alone, a man attains peace. Now, listen to the experience of the full, own-bliss, which gives happiness, by which you will be endowed with the bliss of the Self, O great-minded one.

I am Brahman, directly; in me, there is no delusion. How can Brahman, associated with delusion, ever be in its own nature? When the Vedas, endowed with great sentences, are absorbed in Samadhi, then they are called the bliss of the Self; see this, O great-souled one, in the Vedas. The creature endowed with the sense of "I," by Maya of the fourth state, becomes devoid of delusion and associated with it due to the state of delusion. I have created the four-fold, intent on various states. It is devoid of state, equal, and immutable, by being established in them. That which is devoid of the fault of Brahman is called endowed with fault. The equality, which is of the form of "not this," becomes beyond the four in the Vedas.

The Yogi who becomes one with the bliss of the Self through Samadhi does not see again here among the four deluded souls. The Yogi, endowed with Samadhi, has gone to dissolution in his own nature. There is no seeing of him again, nor do they act there. What kind of person am I, who, having abandoned this Maya and being endowed with Yoga, becomes one with Brahman in his own nature? When, through Yoga in Brahman, he becomes one with it by himself, then speak again, O Lord, of the "self" and "other." The "I am Brahman" that is spoken of—see this in the Vedas through reflection. By that alone, he becomes fulfilled through the service of Yoga. Thus, through the Yoga of union, he becomes one with Brahman. Endowed with peace, he directly exists as the holder of peace.

Beyond this, I shall speak of the supreme Yoga. By the mere experience of that, a man becomes endowed with peace. The "I am Brahman" that is spoken of—how can this be there? That oneness, O Lord of the people, is Brahman established in Brahman. Brahman is devoid of speech and devoid of the movement of the mind. That has not come anywhere, nor has it gone into Brahman again. When it becomes attached to play, it is distinct and Supreme. Then, through Yoga, it becomes endowed with the bliss of the Self, not otherwise. Brahman is established in Brahman; it is considered as neither having come nor gone. How does a man become one with it through Yoga? I am always of the form of Brahman, never associated with Maya. Through delusion, they do not know me, who am established in my own glory, the Supreme.

The Maya called the Yoga of all unions is of the form of various delusions. Where does it stay in me, who am Brahman, devoid of Brahman? Just as the water in the mirage gives rise to vain delusion, so is the great Maya in me, the holder of vain union. Brahman, devoid of fault, was spoken of in the Vedas before. How can that, associated with Maya, be deluded? How can that which is associated with delusion become devoid of delusion through good Yoga? Fools, devoid of Brahman, speak of Brahman associated with Maya. Therefore, see, I have not come into the worlds of Brahman, the Lord. Nor have I gone into Brahman there, again through the service of Yoga. Various forms of Maya do not stay in Brahman. That Brahman does not stay in Maya, due to the influence of refutation.

Yoga is called three-fold: mild, medium, and intense. I shall speak of their difference, which cuts the root of Maya. Having abandoned the delusion of self-arising, "I am Brahman," through refutation—the man who stays in this is considered to be endowed with mild Yoga. Having abandoned the delusion born of external arising, O Daksa, through non-Yoga, he stays in the middle; he is also considered to be endowed with the service of Yoga. Brahman, which is devoid of arising from self or other, and is the holder of union—having abandoned the delusion of that, he who stays in this is of the intense state.

When I have not come, wandering in the worlds of Brahman through delusion, then where is my going? It will happen through Yoga alone. By the nature of being one with Brahman, that best of men stays. Taking refuge in the Yoga of renunciation, he always acts in peace. Now, I tell you the experience of the full Yoga, by which a man, having become a Yogi, stays, O beloved of Ganapati. "I am not, nor is Brahman," it is said in the Vedas, giving the delusion of difference. O Daksa, how can the two-fold in Brahman be in the mind? I am of the nature of the mind, and Brahman is also of the nature of the mind. Having abandoned the five-fold mind, a man becomes a Yogi.

The great mind is of the form of the world, and it is also considered of the form of Brahman. The mind is called the union and non-union of the worlds and Brahman. The mind becomes associated with delusion, deluded by various Mayas. The mind intent on the service of Yoga becomes devoid of delusion. How am I, and how is Brahman, and how is the union of the two? The mind, which is non-different and all-pervading, plays here, without doubt. I am not everywhere through Yoga, due to the state of non-difference of union. I stay; the non-Yogis speak in vain due to the delusion of the mind. Brahman is established in Brahman, which has never come or gone. I am not the holder of non-Yoga; the mind is considered as such.

The mind desires duty, and it is also associated with delusion and meaning. Desire, liberation, and the Brahman-state are due to the influence of the five planes. Having abandoned the mind of five forms, the great Yogi, who has attained supreme peace, certainly becomes one with Brahman; he is called the wish-fulfilling gem. The great intellect, which is of the form of the mind, is the giver of delusion there. The Siddhi (perfection), the lord of the two, directly plays as the other, according to his own will. Knowing that, he becomes one with Brahman; there is no doubt about this. Having abandoned everything consisting of Siddhi and Buddhi, O Prajapati, the Yogi... There is never union or non-union in Brahman. The lord of that desires the Supreme only due to the delusion of Siddhi and Buddhi.

I am Ganesha, without doubt; how can this be in me? Everything is done by Siddhi and Buddhi; by that, he becomes a Yogi. Endowed with Siddhi and Buddhi, a man becomes one with Brahman. Through the Yoga of peace, he stays in peace, always with respect. By the state of Yoga in Brahman, there is the state of being devoid of association with Maya. Having attained peace, he himself, having abandoned Yoga, becomes a Yogi. Through such an experience, the Supreme, endowed with the mind born of delusion, having abandoned all taste, stays well.

In Yoga, it is always intent on being endowed with taste. It is motionless, endowed with peace, and devoid of the impurity of Maya. This is all that I have told you, the happiness born of experience. Knowing which, he stays as one with Brahman; there is no doubt about this. He who hears or reads this, which is of the form of the experience of Yoga, attains all greatness, all that is desired, and attains peace.

Thus ends the sixth chapter, named "The Description of the Peace and Happiness Experienced by the Yogi’s Mind," in the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 7 : Description of the State of Yoga

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: O Great Yogi, please describe the state of one who is liberated while living (Jivanmukta) and the happiness they experience. Where do the auspicious and inauspicious deeds performed by them go?

What happens to the actions performed by the Yogi—whether physical, verbal, mental, or social—and what is their ultimate destination?

Mudgala said: A Jivanmukta Yogi is always free to act according to his own will, yet he remains constantly and respectfully guided by his Prarabdha (past karma).

Established in Brahman, the image of the Supreme resides in his heart. Therefore, nothing done by the Yogi ever becomes a cause for bondage.

Although he acts like an ordinary person in all respects, he is devoid of faults. Because he has become one with Brahman, he is beyond both auspicious and inauspicious outcomes.

Driven by his Prarabdha, the Yogi may perform virtuous acts, such as pilgrimages, sacrifices, and the worship of deities.

He remains pure and devoted to righteousness, yet he is certainly not bound by the fruits of such virtuous deeds.

Alternatively, controlled by the Prarabdha acquired in previous births, he may joyfully perform various sinful acts.

He might commit acts like killing, adultery, drinking, or theft due to the play of destiny.

Even if a person lives such a sinful life, the Yogi never experiences the fruits of those sins as an ordinary man would.

O Lord of the people, because these actions are forced upon the Yogi by the Prarabdha earned in previous births, he is not the true doer of those actions.

One attains the state corresponding to the thoughts held at the time of death. A person who is not a Yogi attains a corresponding birth, but a Yogi becomes Brahman itself.

O son of Brahma, I have told you this to dispel your doubts, explaining the greatness of the Yogi's actions in brief.

A Yogi, established in Yoga, never commits sins; he is undoubtedly devoted to righteousness.

All Yogis are known to be saviors of the world. They never commit sins and are always endowed with virtues like non-violence.

The Yogi performs his duties without attachment to fruits, always devoted to Brahman for the benefit of the world.

If, due to past impressions, he happens to commit a sin, he is not tainted by it, as has been described before.

Why would a Yogi cause suffering to living beings? He is certainly not tainted by sin.

He sees the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) everywhere with a non-dual vision. He moves through a world filled with the happiness of virtue.

O Daksha, the Prarabdha of Yogis does not cause sin; their Prarabdha is naturally aligned with virtuous actions.

A wise person strives for Yoga through many births, performing duties as an offering to Brahman, and thus becomes a Yogi.

How could the Prarabdha of such a person lead to evil? A great Yogi is always established in righteousness.

Now, I will describe the state of Yogis so that the Yogi may never become confused.

Having mastered the great Yoga that restrains the mind, the Yogi abandons the five-fold distractions and becomes established in the Self.

Knowing the mind to be the wish-fulfilling gem, he merges into it. He becomes that form, absorbed in Brahman.

According to the scriptures, the Prarabdha of a Yogi does not yield fruits of bondage. When a Yogi is stubborn, he becomes the driver of his Prarabdha.

If he leaves his body, he does not incur the sin of suicide; he leaves the body at the destined time.

Yet, the Yogi who is truly united with the Lord does not abandon his body. He does not act by transgressing his Prarabdha.

He remains in a state of peace, without a doubt. Whatever is to happen will happen; he is devoid of illusion and attachment.

What is there to gain by the falling of the body or by protecting it? Let it be as ordained by the Lord of the Ganas.

Deciding this in his heart, with a peaceful nature, the Yogi performs his duties while remaining established in his own Dharma.

If a Yogi is within the Varna and Ashrama system, he performs his duties with a detached mind.

The body is said to be composed of actions; how can one remain without action while in it? It is impossible to exist without performing some form of action.

If he were to abandon all action and remain only in Yoga, he would undoubtedly be subject to the falling of the body.

How can one live while abandoning all actions, including eating, waking, and sleeping?

Whatever is done is called action. Therefore, the body is action-oriented, and through it, he performs his duty.

If a Yogi is eager to abandon his body, he is not considered capable of enduring the dualities of life.

Such a person, lacking peace, is like a non-Yogi; he is unable to endure dualities.

Whatever happens by the will of Ganesha, let it be so; he endures everything, remaining in supreme peace.

Such a Yogi is never devoid of peace. He is a great soul, one with Brahman, and a purifier of all beings.

Therefore, a Yogi who is in the Varna and Ashrama system should perform his duties as an offering to Brahman, meditating on the Elephant-faced Lord in his heart.

If he does not perform his prescribed duties, others, seeing him inactive, might abandon their own duties.

Therefore, for the benefit of the world, the Yogi performs his duties, leading others to follow the path of righteousness under his influence.

A Yogi, being superior to all, performs virtuous deeds, and others follow his example.

O Lord of the people, the actions performed by him with joy are considered authoritative and are accepted by all the worlds.

Actions performed as an offering to Brahman never cause bondage. The wise say that such actions lead to the attainment of Brahman.

Actions performed by Yogis for the sake of the world, with a detached mind, do not cause bondage.

Because he is always one with Brahman, he does not "do" anything. The nature of the body continues to function.

I will now describe another path: those who criticize or praise the Yogi become consumers of his sins or virtues respectively.

The sins committed by a Yogi cannot touch him; they always cling to those who criticize him.

The virtues earned by a Yogi do not touch him; they always cling to those who praise him.

Therefore, a wise person should never criticize a Yogi; instead, one should serve them to attain happiness.

If a Yogi lives in the forest, controlled by his Prarabdha, he eats fruits and remains in peace.

There, he has no critics or servants. The great Yogi alone experiences the auspicious and inauspicious.

At the time of death, the Yogi's life ends, and the auspicious and inauspicious deeds are left without a foundation.

I have told you the behavior of one who has become Brahman. This knowledge of Yoga is auspicious to hear.

Now, I will describe the supreme Yoga of knowledge, which is full of peace and happiness for Yogis.

The body is action-oriented; one should practice Yoga within it, performing actions as an offering to Brahman while remaining peaceful.

Knowledge is said to be the manifestation of consciousness, residing in the heart. One should practice the Yoga of knowledge based on discrimination.

One should abandon all knowledge that arises in the heart regarding worldly objects or various desires.

Knowledge that is subject to birth and death is merely worldly. The knowledge that grants the bliss of Brahman is born from the influence of the state of Yoga.

There is knowledge with attributes and knowledge without attributes. Abandoning the former, the Yogi becomes glorious.

Among all forms of knowledge, the one in Yoga is called the knowledge of the Self. In that state, the Yogi dissolves into the nature of Brahman.

"I am Brahman"—when this is realized, where is the room for other knowledge? Abandoning the confusion in the heart, one should become devoted to Brahman.

By this method, abandoning all knowledge residing in the heart, the Yogi remains peaceful and devoted to Brahman.

"I am never the doer, nor the one who causes action." Those deluded by Maya call me the doer of actions.

I am the witness of the body. Whatever is done by the body is not done by me. I am Brahman, beyond the beyond.

Whatever thoughts arise in me are the play of Maya and illusion. The soul, being a witness, is not established in them.

I am not bound by delusion, nor am I ever devoid of it. Only the non-Yogis, due to Maya, say these things about me.

Thus, the great Yogi always remains like a witness, established in the Yoga of knowledge in the heart, having abandoned the illusion of thoughts.

For that great soul, the mind is devoid of worldly taste, always peaceful, and established in Brahman.

This is the Yoga of knowledge I have described to you. I reside in the body like a witness.

Now, listen to the Yoga of Equanimity. Knowing this, one becomes Brahman and never loses peace.

The body is composed of three types of actions; one should practice Karma Yoga. The mind should be the witness, practicing discrimination.

O Daksha, when I am Brahman, where is there room for duality? I will describe its nature.

The body is action-oriented, while discrimination is devoid of action. Knowing this duality, the great Yogi practices the Yoga of Equanimity.

"In the body, I am the doer, but in the heart, I am the witness, pure." Through the union of both, something happens naturally.

If there is no discrimination, what will the body do? Similarly, without the body, who would receive the knowledge of the mind?

I am established in the state of bliss. Both the body and the mind perform their respective functions.

In the body, I am the doer; in the heart, I am the witness. I am always in the state of bliss, creating a two-fold Maya.

I am established in the equal state of both, without a doubt. I am the one called Brahman, devoid of attributes and non-attributes.

Therefore, by taking refuge in equanimity, internally and externally, one should act as Brahman acts.

Becoming a doer in the body and a witness in the heart, one abandons both and attains the state of bliss.

Whatever is of the nature of duality, the Yogi becomes one with Yoga and remains in bliss.

The union of all blisses is called the Bliss of Brahman. The wise person should act accordingly.

Through my association, both function. He acts through both, yet he is devoid of both.

This state of bliss was described by the Yogis of old. One who has become Brahman remains in this state with peace.

Now, O Lord of the people, listen to the state of "Sahaja" (natural state), which is especially practiced by the great Yogi.

The body is action-oriented; the best of Yogis practices Yoga in it, offering all actions to Brahman.

In the heart, he remains as a witness, beyond both. By taking refuge in equanimity, the three-fold state is described.

If, due to forgetfulness or illusion, one forgets or acts contrary, one should not feel sorrow.

The three-fold state is said to be dependent on Maya. The fourth state (Turiya) is always described in the scriptures as self-dependent.

The three-fold state moves by the will of the Turiya, without a doubt. It always remains as if deluded, joined with its own Prarabdha.

There is no ego in the Turiya state. Whatever action is performed, the corresponding fruit is experienced.

Therefore, due to Prarabdha, I may forget the supreme state, driven by the Turiya state.

Or, due to the delusion of knowledge, I may act in reverse. Abandoning the full state of Yoga, Prarabdha becomes primary.

The Turiya state is the holder of Prarabdha, there is no doubt. Whatever happens by its will is the giver of Yoga.

I am completely dependent, joined with the will of the Turiya. Whether I act, witness, or remain in bliss, there is no doubt.

Brahman always remains in the Turiya state, self-dependent. What bondage can there be for one who plays through the Turiya?

Whatever happens is done by the Turiya, there is no doubt. Where am I? The Lord plays by His own will.

This is the Sahaja state described to you, O Lord of the people. Whatever happens is said to be "done," yet in reality, nothing is done.

Whatever is done happens by itself with the Sahaja state. This Yoga is called Sahaja and Turiya.

Now, listen to the state of "Svananda" (Self-Bliss), which is the state of being Brahman.

The Turiya state is self-dependent, while the three-fold state is dependent. In their union, the Yogi remains in his own Self.

The three-fold state is the play, and the Turiya is the player. In their union, one remains in the bliss of the Self.

For a Yogi who has become Brahman, where is the question of being self-dependent or dependent? It is all the play of Maya.

Taking refuge in the state of Yoga, the wise person practices all four. The great soul remains in their union.

Taking refuge in Karma Yoga, he performs bodily actions. Taking refuge in Jnana Yoga, the wise person acts as a witness.

Taking refuge in Sama Yoga, he experiences supreme bliss. Taking refuge in Sahaja, he avoids sorrow.

Being joined with the four and yet devoid of the four, the Yogi always remains established in the state of Yoga.

In the state of the Self, where is the holder of the state? In their union, the Yoga-state is described.

The state of Yoga is described as being four-fold and yet devoid of four. Taking refuge in this, the Yogi never loses peace.

Now, listen to the second supreme state, which is devoid of Yoga, full, and always granting liberation.

A Yogi who has become Brahman takes refuge in this supreme state. When he is joined with this renunciation, he never loses peace.

Established in Brahman, it neither comes nor goes. For one who has become Brahman in Maya, where is the holder of the state?

Whatever he does or causes to be done, he does not look at it. Knowing it to be Maya, he sees the five-fold in the five-fold.

If I am not in the five-fold, then the actions performed by it—why would they appear in me? He becomes established in renunciation.

Life, death, birth, Yoga—these are all states of the Self. How can they be tainted by Maya and delusion?

Now, O giver of honor, listen to the full state of Yoga, which is devoid of union and separation, by which one never loses peace.

I am Brahman. In me, the five-fold Maya appears, but it is devoid of Maya and Yoga.

The mind is always joined with injunctions and prohibitions. But it becomes useless when it is devoid of them.

Through the body, Karma Yoga occurs; in the heart, knowledge arises. In both, he is equal; in the Turiya, he is Sahaja.

In all unions, the bliss of the Self prevails. In renunciation, it is called "Ayoga" (devoid of Yoga), and in Yoga, it is the Yoga of peace.

Thus, having mastered various types of Yoga, the wise Yogi remains in peace, having become Brahman.

The state of Yoga has been described, which grants the happiness of being Brahman. By this method, the life of the Yogi is sustained.

A Yogi in the Varna and Ashrama system practices this supreme state, performing actions for the sake of the world without attachment to fruits.

When he abandons the Varna and Ashrama system and becomes a "Fifth Ashrama" holder, there is no state for him, there is no doubt.

He is always devoid of injunctions and prohibitions. Whatever he does, it is always in the form of Yoga.

If he remains in the state according to the Vedas, he is joined with injunctions.

When he remains without the state, he is joined with the prohibitions of the Vedas.

Therefore, the Yogi, whether with the state or without it, acts according to his will, abandoning Varna and Ashrama, like a Vinayaka (Ganesha).

He performs everything with peace, according to his heart's desire, devoid of injunctions and prohibitions, tasting the essence of Brahman.

All this has been described—the great behavior of Yogis. One who has become Brahman should always act in this way.

Whoever hears or reads this will attain all their desires. In the end, becoming one with Yoga, they remain absorbed in the Lord of the Ganas.

Chapter 8 : Description of the Conduct of One Who Has Fallen from Yoga

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Daksha said: O Sage, having taken refuge in the peace of Yoga and practicing it daily, how can one who has become one with Brahman fall from Yoga? This world is full of illusion and various delusions; tell me, O Sage, how one who has realized the truth can fall back into this curiosity.

Mudgala said: O Prajapati, when a Yogi who has become one with Brahman is established in that state, delusion arises in his intellect due to ignorance. Seeing various types of worldly enjoyments, he becomes enchanted by that very delusion. Becoming attached to those pleasures, he constantly desires them. At that moment, discrimination remains in his heart, and I shall describe its nature; listen, O wise one. He thinks: "I have become one with Brahman through Yoga, there is no doubt. Therefore, virtue and sin do not affect my actions." He further thinks: "When I renounce enjoyment and remain in constant restraint, my Yoga does not grow. But when I enjoy various pleasures daily, ignoring virtue and sin, my Yoga does not diminish."

Thinking thus, the Yogi, longing for enjoyment, gradually becomes attached to various pleasures. This is called the delusion that brings confusion to the Yogi's heart. Abandoning peace, he becomes obsessed with virtue and sin. Then, taking refuge in desire, which provides bodily comfort, he seeks various enjoyments. As the Yogi strives for these pleasures, if someone creates an obstacle, he becomes filled with anger. He perceives the one who created the obstacle as his enemy. From this, supreme ignorance arises, and filled with it, he constantly strives to destroy that enemy. To teach the enemy a lesson, he acquires wealth through various means, defeats him, and then enjoys the pleasures with great effort.

O Prajapati, he loses the distinction between self and others; this is the manifestation of ignorance that creates enemies and friends. Then, he gradually forgets his inner peace. Influenced by this, he considers the body to be the ultimate truth. When the body is satisfied and feels accomplished, he considers himself fortunate, but when it is otherwise, he considers himself unfortunate. Thus, abandoning Yoga, he becomes identified with the reflection (the body). There is no doubt that he will become absorbed in that reflection.

In this way, the Yogi, lacking peace, always remains in that state and never takes refuge in Yoga again. He becomes happy when the body is comfortable and miserable when it is in pain, becoming subject to various states of mind. He never attains even a trace of peace and, filled with dissatisfaction, wanders constantly in search of enjoyment. For the sake of pleasure, he performs various sins and abandons all virtues, disregarding all rules and prohibitions. Whether he belongs to a particular caste or stage of life, he abandons his duty and engages in sin or virtue, whichever suits his desires.

Constantly attached to enjoyment and filled with delusion, he is called a "Yogabhrashta" (one fallen from Yoga). In his heart, the discrimination remains, but it only brings more delusion, as he thinks: "I am beyond virtue and sin; I am Brahman, established in the nature of Brahman." At the end of his life, he goes to hells that cause dualistic suffering, and after enduring terrible torments, he is reborn on earth.

Daksha said: O King of Yogis, kindly tell me the ultimate fate of one who has fallen from Yoga. How does he go to hell and suffer? How can one who has renounced the fruits of action be reborn? This is a great wonder; tell me about the actions of such a Yogi.

Mudgala said: For one who has become one with Brahman, if he returns to the service of worldly objects and loses devotion to Brahman, he will certainly have no place at the end. O Daksha, through immense merit, a person sometimes attains the state of becoming one with Brahman. But due to the connection with worldly objects and the rise of dualistic tendencies, that merit is diminished by various sins. Upon death, he may fall into the realms of Shiva, Vaikuntha, the Sun, Shakti, Brahma, or Indra. The world, which gives rise to joy and sorrow and is full of dualities, is indeed a hell for Yogis; there is no doubt about this, as agreed upon by the scriptures.

Having abandoned the cycle of birth and death and become established in Brahman, if he returns to duality, he will constantly experience the dualities of the heavens and other realms, remaining attached to various enjoyments. Therefore, O Lord of the people, the Yogi, after experiencing these dualistic "hells" (heavens), is reborn on this earth. He is born into a family of Yogis or ascetics. There, due to his past impressions, he will practice Yoga again. Because of his previous संस्कार (impressions), he will never find joy in worldly pleasures. Through even a little effort, his mind will become established in Yoga. He will not fall from Yoga again, O son of Brahma. With a peaceful nature, he will nourish his body with joy. In the end, that Yogi becomes one with Brahman, having attained stability through the merit of his past Yoga.

I have told you the complete account of the conduct of one who has fallen from Yoga. What else do you wish to hear? Tell me, and I shall speak.

Chapter 9 : Description of the Gradual Yoga for the Ignorant

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: O best of sages, the mind is extremely restless and deeply attached to sense objects. It is difficult to control and does not attain peace. Therefore, O Lord, please tell me an easy method to conquer the mind or any other means to attain Yoga.

Mudgala said: One should worship the reflection of the Chintamani (the wish-fulfilling gem) that resides within the five-fold mind, tainted by delusion. O King, I see no other way for a Yogi to conquer the mind; without conquering the mind, there is no doubt that Yoga cannot be attained.

Daksha said: Tell me an easy path, one that grants Brahman to all, by following which all beings may become one with Brahman.

Mudgala said: A person full of ignorance, addicted to sense objects and obsessed with the belly and genitals, never desires Yoga. For such a person, one should teach them that desire-driven actions can be pleasant and heart-warming, leading to the attainment of the unattainable, if performed with greed. By abandoning sin out of a desire to attain the unattainable, such a person, becoming devoted to righteous deeds, should constantly perform virtuous acts. Through this, their sins will be destroyed, and the purified person may then perform higher, desire-based actions. If they attain the desired object, they will perform further actions with even greater faith. If they do not attain it, one should instruct them to perform even more virtuous deeds.

Without a doubt, you have committed sins over infinite births, and these create endless obstacles. Thus, even a person engaged in action may suffer, but by performing actions to destroy sin with joy, their inner self becomes pure. Through the great power of these righteous, desire-driven actions, O Daksha, their intellect will eventually turn toward desirelessness.

Enjoyments that lead to birth and death have been experienced in various lives, but the Brahman that grants infinite bliss has not been attained by those dwelling in bodies. Thinking, "I have not attained the unattainable due to my past karma, and no one is capable of changing their destiny," one should respectfully perform desireless actions. "I shall become free from birth and death, there is no doubt." By attaining the infinite bliss of Brahman, one becomes devoted to Brahman and strives to become one with it.

Abandoning desires, one should serve the five deities, with Ganesha as the foremost, and become devoted to the path of liberation. By desiring the happiness of Salokya (residing in the same realm) and other states, a human can gradually attain greatness through penance. The various forms of Brahman’s majesty are described, and one’s mind should delight in them, recognizing them as manifestations of the Supreme Brahman. By contemplating this everywhere, one becomes devoted to their worship and, through their grace, attains purity of mind.

By realizing Ganesha as the complete Lord through contemplation, one should become devoted to Him, the best of all. Recognizing Him as the Lord of Self-Bliss, one should worship the Lord of Ganas with desireless devotion. Then, day and night, one should worship Him with penance, detached from sense objects for the sake of liberation. Through Ganesha’s grace, the mind becomes perfectly pure, and with that, one worships Him with their whole being. One should know the entire moving and non-moving universe as His form and worship the Elephant-faced Lord for the welfare of all.

One should not cause suffering to anyone, but endure the pairs of opposites with sincerity. A virtuous person should never cut leaves or plants unless it is for divine service, remaining devoted to the Universal Form. Sitting in a solitary place, meditating on the Elephant-faced Lord, one should worship Him with devotion and perform deep contemplation. With a focused mind, one should worship the Lord, practicing restraint of the senses and control of the mind.

Knowing the mind is difficult to conquer, one should conquer it through the control of breath (Pranayama). I shall now tell you the excellent method of breath control, by which a person abandons sin and becomes pure-minded. One should fill the left nostril with air, hold it in the stomach for four times that duration, and then exhale through the right nostril, twice the duration of the inhalation. Then, fill again through the right, perform Kumbhaka (retention), and exhale through the left. A wise person should practice this continuously to become sinless.

One should perform this Pranayama at the three junctures of the day. Do not use excessive force, as forced breath can lead to ailments. When the breath is blocked by the path of the Ida and Pingala nadis, the Sushumna becomes clear. The Apana breath resides at the root of the navel and moves downward, while the Prana moves upward. By blocking the paths, the breath is forced to the root. When the breath is held there for a moment, the Sushumna nadi is pierced. Once the Prana and Apana are united there, the person becomes a knower of the three times, a Siddha, and full of radiance.

Then, becoming devoted to meditation and the piercing of the six chakras, the Yogi sees their own true form. Gradually, the Yogi reaches the Sahasrara and sees the Elephant-faced Lord there. By establishing the breath there through deep Samadhi, one remains united with the Lord. Through the power of the breath, the mind becomes pure, and one sees the eternal Brahman everywhere. Then, possessing self-mastery, one becomes powerful through one's own radiance; whatever one desires, it is immediately fulfilled. If the Yogi desires nothing, they attain Yoga; otherwise, they fall back into sense enjoyment.

There are two types of practice: the external, consisting of righteous action and penance, and the internal, consisting of breath control, which grants all Siddhis. By these, one brings the mind under control. The internal path is considered superior to the external.

I shall now tell you a third method, by which a human becomes worthy of being served by the greatest of Yogis. For the sake of mind purification, action is said to be of two types. Once the mind is pure, one should perceive the eternal Brahman, which is full, unchanging, and devoid of parts, and become absorbed in it.

To conquer the mind, one should practice Shama (tranquility) and Dama (self-restraint). By performing one's own duty to sustain the body, one practices Dama. When the mind makes a resolve for sense objects, one should withdraw it and turn it toward Brahman; this is called Shama. By this, the mind becomes fully under one's control.

Gradually, the great-souled one should withdraw from sense objects and, holding onto Yogic firmness, become a Yogi. Abandoning the two paths of action, one should remain devoted to Dhyana Yoga (meditation). One should meditate on the Brahman that is beyond mind and speech, abandoning all resolves regarding sense objects. When one is unable to hold the mind, one should remain like a statue, not moving or performing any action. One should accept whatever food or drink is offered by others, or if nothing is given, one should not strive for it, remaining content with destiny. Even if tormented by snakes, scorpions, tigers, or people, one should not move from their place, remaining like a stone, attributing it to past karma and feeling no anger. Similarly, if worshipped by others, one should remain unmoved, knowing it is the result of past deeds.

By this method, O King, one will conquer the mind and attain perfect peace without a doubt. One should constantly contemplate Brahman: "What is the Brahman within me, as described by the Vedas?" By crossing the five planes and seeing Brahman everywhere, one attains peace.

Ganesha’s worship is the easiest and most supreme path. By this, one surely becomes a Yogi. The chanting, hearing, and worship of the Lord of Ganas grants oneness with Brahman. Even a person deeply involved in worldly affairs, if they remember Ganesha day and night, will eventually become absorbed in Him. By remembering the Lord of Ganas with respect, one attains the memory of Him at the time of death.

Those who remember Shiva, Vishnu, and others will be remembered by them at the end, and they will attain their realms. However, they will eventually fall back into the mortal world. Therefore, the worship of Ganesha is the essence, for it turns the ignorant into Brahman. This is the path to Yoga for the sake of the ignorant, granting them the state of Brahman.

Chapter 10 : The Yoga of Divine Glories

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Daksha said: O Lord of Yogis, please describe the divine glories of the great-souled Ganesha, by knowing and worshipping which a person becomes a knower of Ganesha.

Mudgala said: There is no doubt that the glories of the Lord of Ganas are infinite; even Shankara and others are unable to describe them fully. Moment by moment, the Lord of Ganesha, who is beyond the beyond, engages in various divine plays and manifests the entire universe through His glories. In all the worlds, among the primary elements and the Brahmas, whoever is supreme is a bearer of His divine glory. O Daksha, there are countless incarnations of His glories in every Kalpa; who is capable of describing them all? Therefore, I will tell you briefly; listen to the glories of the Lord of Ganas, which bestow various Siddhis.

How can I describe the Lord, who is Gajanana in the form of Yoga, existing in the worlds and Brahmas in different forms? Even He, associated with Maya, assumes various forms; this truth is always realized in the hearts of those who are not yet Yogis. Through the influence of Maya, what is impossible for Gajanana? I will describe them by taking refuge in that Maya. Those who are parts of parts, or possess a fraction of His energy, are all bearers of His glory. In this very way, when asked by Narada, the Lord of Ganesha spoke; listen to those most wonderful glories.

Lord Ganesha said: I am the Supreme, the controller of the mind, complete in all Yogas, and I am the non-dual reality beyond the influence of Maya. I am the Self in all beings, existing in the state of Turiya, the unmanifest, and the bliss in those who are equal in joy. I am the Brahman in various forms, the sustainer of the unreal, the Sankhya-form in the truthful, and the wisdom in those who play. O wise one, I am always the One in those identified with the body; I am the Bindu in bodies and the Lord of Gunas in consciousness. I am the Bliss-sheath among the sheaths, the knowledge in subtle beings, the mind in the discerning, and the wind in those who move.

I am the food-form in the gross, the Lord in the collective, the Pragna in the individual, and the great Virat in the atoms. I am the mind among the senses, the space among the elements, the sound among the subtle elements, and the knower among the deities. Among the various principles, I am the supreme Mahat-tattva; among the qualities, I am the quality-sustainer, and among the protectors, I am Janardana. I am Hara among the destroyers, the great-grandfather among the creators, the soul of Aryama among actions, and the power among those who enchant.

I am Maghava among the gods, Vinayaka among the Adityas, the Sun among the radiant, and the Moon among the immortals. I am Agni in the valiant, Dharma in those who act righteously, Yama among those who restrain, and Nirriti among the demons. I am Varuna among the waters, the wind among the strong, the giver of wealth among the treasures, and Shankara among the Rudras. I am Kama among the archers, Rati among the enjoyers, Shesha among the kings of Nagas, and Takshaka among the serpents.

I am Vasuki among the Nagas, the provider in the Jambu-dvipa, the sweet water among the oceans, and Meru among the mountains. I am Yama among the ancestors, the Shami tree among trees, the Mandara among divine trees, and the fulfiller of desires among the plants. I am Kamadhenu among cows, Durva among herbs, the peacock among birds, and the lion among beasts. I am Shravana among the months, the mouse-lord among thieves, the horse-rider among vehicles, and the giver of prosperity among the Siddhis.

I am the intellect among the sciences, the consciousness in those who illuminate, the mind among the swift, and the ego among those who cause delusion. I am the king among men, the Brahmana among the castes, the Turiya among the stages of life, and the householder among those who sustain. I am the Avadhuta among the renouncers, the fourth day among the lunar days, Daksha among the Prajapatis, and Bhrigu among the Brahmarshis.

I am Narada among the divine sages, Dattatreya among the Avadhutas, Kapila among the knowers of truth, and Shuka among the Yogis. I am Janaka among the liberated, Sanaka and others among the knowers, and Badarayana among the wise. I am Vashistha among the performers of duty, Mudgala among the Ganapatyas, Bhrushundi among the single-minded, and Brihaspati among the knowers of Brahman.

I am the sesame among grains, ghee among dairy products, honey among all tastes, and the Makara among aquatic creatures. I am the Kaumandali among rivers, the Ganapa-tirthas among holy places, Mayura-kshetra among sacred regions, and the King of Kashi among the intelligent. I am Mayuresha among incarnations, Gargya among the worshippers, and Gritsamada among those established in meditation.

I am the wisdom among the demoniac, the enemy of knowledge among the hateful, the mind among the unconquerable, and the Omkara among the letters. I am Shukra among the emotional, Trishira among the pure-hearted, and the powerful Yajnavalkya among the teachers. I am Suta among the Puranikas, Shaunaka among the listeners, Skanda among the commanders, and Vishnu among the glorious.

I am the form of falsehood among sins, conflict among those who divide, greed among the attainments, and the fulfiller of desires among gems. I am the sacred knowledge among the elements, the Sama-singer among the righteous, the Atharva among the Vedas, and the Parashu (axe) among weapons. I am the witness among the detached, the elephant among the royal, the object of delusion among the confused, and Rambha among the Apsaras.

I am Chitraratha among the Gandharvas, the lord of physicians among the healers, Dhanvantari among the doctors, and the ocean among the essence-filled. I am Jahnavi among the flowing, the single syllable among mantras, the Brahmanaspati among the hymns, and the knowledge-form among the sacrifices. I am the Samana among the vital breaths, the power among the powerful, and the Brihadaranyaka among the Upanishads.

I am the Yoga-shastra among the scriptures, the Mudgala-purana among the Puranas, and the Dandaka forest among the great forests. I am the rod among the controllers, the Maya among those who create states, and the Smriti of Yajnavalkya among the Smritis. I am the Gayatri among the meters, the bliss of the Self among the cities, and the grammar among the limbs of the Vedas.

I am Kavya among the knowers of policy, the bull among those who support the earth, Nandikesha among the cattle, and the naked Bhairava among those who contemplate. Thus, these are the various forms of the glories of Ganapa; they cannot be fully described, but I have told you the essence. O Brahmana, whatever is most excellent, know that to be His glory; contemplate especially upon that of the Lord of Ganas in the worlds.

Mudgala said: Having said this, the Lord of Ganesha became silent. Narada bowed to Him and departed, becoming transformed. O Daksha, whoever listens to this or causes others to listen with effort, will never fall into delusion, having known the power born of His glory. All living beings and the supreme lords, endowed with infinite majesty, are all bearers of the glory of the Lord of Obstacles. Knowing this, those wise ones who worship the Lord of Ganesha will all become Brahman-realized, O Prajapati.

Chapter 11 : Yoga of Inquiry into Truth (Tattva-vichara Yoga)

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Daksha said: O learned one, you have previously spoken of the inquiry into the Supreme Brahman. Please explain that which is beneficial and leads one to attain Yoga by practicing it. By what method does a person, whose mind is purified by action and who is devoted to Brahman, perceive the form of Brahman everywhere?

Mudgala said: O Lord of creatures, a person of excellent character, having performed external and internal actions and purified their mind, perceives the eternal Brahman through the path of inquiry into Truth. I shall tell you that knowledge of Truth, which is the primary means for yogis to attain Yoga.

The body is composed of the five elements—earth, water, fire, air, and ether—and Nature (Prakriti) is also fivefold. This Nature, which is of the form of action and possesses many divisions, manifests itself in various ways and plays in diverse forms. The one essence of the five elements, which is of the form of knowledge, resides in the heart as Nature, bearing the state of the individual soul (Jiva). Through it, all knowledge arises, providing discernment of the many. The entire universe is based upon it, and it is declared to be of one form.

O Lord of creatures, in the union of these two (action and knowledge), Brahman is described as being beyond duality, the supreme of the supreme, and of the nature of both action and knowledge. Hear how that which is fivefold becomes the giver of Yoga: it is known as gross, subtle, equal, the realization of the Self, and the Bindu (point). Brahman is said to be twofold through the states of collective (Samashti) and individual (Vyashti). It shines by itself in five ways through the five elements.

The gross is the waking state of Brahman, and it is considered twofold: the individual is called Vishva, and the collective is called Vaishvanara. In the cosmic eggs, various forms of living beings are visible; all those who bear the waking state are called the Vishva-form. I shall explain its understanding to make the meaning clear: in bodies characterized by action, they are of the form of action; in bodies characterized by knowledge, they are considered to be of the form of knowledge. Through the union of both, they are declared to be Vishva.

O Daksha, just as the span of the external body is measured, the knowledge-body resides in the heart by that same measure. Virat, situated in the waking state, is known as Vaishvanara. It exists in all beings through its all-pervading body. Its body is also twofold, divided into action and knowledge; in their union, it is known as the all-pervading Vaishvanara. The union of Vishva and Vaishvanara is declared to be Brahman, which bears the waking state, is full, and reveals the gross elements.

This is the essence of the gross told to you briefly. Now hear the different methods of Truth that grant the samadhi of Yoga. O Lord of creatures, know that which is subject to birth and death and is of many states as the 'Tvam' (Thou) principle, which pervades all states. Know that which is devoid of birth and death and is declared to be of one form as the 'Tat' (That) principle, which is supreme and supports all souls. The union of these two becomes the Truth, which is the supreme 'Asi' (Art) principle; it is devoid of duality and reveals the form of duality. The 'Tvam' principle is associated with action, and the 'Tat' principle is known as knowledge. There is no doubt that the union of these two becomes the universal Truth. Thus, know the Vaishvanara as the Truth, which manifests through the union of action and knowledge.

O Daksha, hear of another ancient form of Truth. All living beings, known as Vishva, are considered by the wise to be of many states. They are said to be subject to birth and death and are considered to be holders of the 'Tvam' principle. Vaishvanara and 'Tvam' are considered to be one and the same, all-pervading. The Brahman-truth, which is declared in the non-dual state of these two, is full, bears the waking state, and is devoted to the gross. Thus, the form of Truth should be known in various divisions, such as the subtle, O Lord of creatures, as it grants the bliss of Brahman.

Now I shall speak of the subtle for the sake of the path of Yoga. Through the power of a purified mind, that knowledge becomes one with it. Know, O respectful one, that the external gross is especially the waking state, consisting of food, while the internal is the subtle, known as the dream state. The vital breath (Prana) is said to be tenfold and resides within as the subtle form. Therefore, the mind is said to be subtle, situated within the Prana. More subtle than that is the intellect (Vijnana), which is excellent and knows the duality of the body. It resides within the mind, is full, and nothing exists beyond it.

The dream state is subtle, consisting of three sheaths. Brahman, which resides within all and moves the illusion of duality, is the subtle form. The body of action is remembered as consisting of subtle elements, residing within all and causing the enjoyment of objects. The body of knowledge is declared to be the holder of the eternal state. Everything should be known as before, as the revealer of subtle illusion. All beings who are in the dream state are of different natures; they are considered in the scriptures as Taijasa, the revealers of action and knowledge. Virat is there declared to be Hiranyagarbha, the all-pervading in dream states, moving all various creatures. The Brahman that is of the nature of the dream state in the non-dual condition of these two is called subtle by the wise because it reveals subtle illusion. The state of Truth should be known as before, as the one who goes into the subtle; through the power of a purified mind, one realizes it.

Beyond this, I shall speak with great affection of the Brahman known as Sushupti (deep sleep), which is beneficial to all people. O Daksha, the external body is everywhere called the waking state. The internal is called the dream state, and the union of the two is the state of the Self. It is called the state of the oneness of external and internal, devoid of both. It is the state of bliss, made of the elements and qualities. When a person leaves the waking state and goes to the dream state, the deep sleep (Sushupti) arises by relying on the junction of the two. Similarly, leaving the dream state and going to the waking state, one stays in the junction of the two; that is the state of Sushupti.

O Daksha, when a person is asleep, they become endowed with knowledge. They do not know themselves or others, and they do not see dreams. That is called Sushupti, which remains by relying on sleep. It is the Sushupti that creates the knowledge of waking and dreaming. It is twofold, associated with illusion, and is of the nature of the Lord of Wisdom (Prajna). For those who bear different bodies, it is declared to be the state of wisdom. It is eternal, bearing the form of Virat, and is called the Lord. In the union of these two, it is the form of Brahman, as the Truth defined before.

Now I shall briefly tell you of the Turiya (the fourth) state. It bears the form of Nada (sound) and is immediately identical with the subtle elements. It is established in order, is full, and is spoken of as the witness of the three. I shall tell you of the greatness of its experience for your benefit. He who says, "I experienced the waking state," "I saw the dream," and "I knew nothing in sleep," is the one who is the witness. When he is in the waking state, he is in the Vishva; then he is devoid of the other two. When he sleeps, he remains definitely devoid of the other two. When he is in Sushupti, he is devoid of the other two. Thus, through these types of differences, he is considered to be associated with the order.

I shall speak of its Truth, which is the Lord, bearing the form of Nada, known as the 'Tvam' principle, residing in the Vaishvanara, Taijasa, and Prajna. He is the one who is the three-fold Virat, declared to be the 'Tat' principle. In the non-dual state of these two, he is the form of Truth, consisting of 'Asmita' (I-am-ness). The union of the states is the Nada, the holder of the Turiya. He is always associated with the eternal state and shines everywhere. He is not external, not internal, and not both. He is the Supreme Lord, the soul of the sheaths, known as 'Asmita' (I-am).

Beyond this, I shall speak of the Bindu-truth, which grants great happiness. It is the fifth, called Brahman, the supreme that goes into the bodies of bodies. That which is established in order is called the 'Tvam' principle. The body known as Turiya is declared to be the 'Tat' principle. The Truth in the non-dual state of these two is the supreme, consisting of Yoga; it is full, residing in the four, and always devoid of the four.

Thus, through the inquiry into Truth, one who is devoted to tranquility and self-control, having abandoned the desires born of intention, becomes of the nature of Yoga. There is no doubt that one should see that which resides as Brahman everywhere. It is full, bearing the form of Truth, always through the inquiry into Truth.

Now I shall tell you of another path of Truth that is always beneficial. The Bindu is in the 'Tvam' principle, and 'So'ham' (I am He) is considered to be in the 'Tat' principle. O Lord of creatures, the knowledge in the union of these two is remembered as the Truth. Because it is devoid of mind and speech, it is seen by the yogis as the Supreme. The knowledge is the form of 'Tvam', and the realization is the form of 'Tat'. In the union of these two, the Truth is declared in the Vedas to be self-evident. The 'Tvam' principle is non-existence, and the 'Tat' principle is said to be existence. The union of these two is the equal Brahman, the Truth that grants Yoga. The 'Tvam' principle is equal, and the 'Tat' principle is the unmanifest. The Truth in the union of these two is declared to be self-bliss. Self-bliss is the 'Tvam' principle, and the union is called the 'Tat' principle. The Truth in the union of these two is considered to be the full Yoga. Success is said to be in the 'Tvam' principle, and intellect is said to be in the 'Tat' principle. In the union of these two, the Lord of Ganesha is declared to be the form of Truth.

Thus, those who are expert in the meaning of Vedanta, associated with various opinions, speak of the Truth. O Daksha, know this, there is no doubt. Always know the 'Tvam' principle as associated with infinite states. The 'Tat' principle is uncreated, and the union of the two becomes the Supreme. This essence, the secret that grants Yoga, has been told to you, by which one may see Yoga everywhere with the eye of knowledge.

Chapter 12 : Yoga of the Bright and Dark Paths

Daksa said: O Yogi, you who are the savior of all, please tell me with compassion about the bright and dark paths by which one attains liberation, as well as the cycle of worldly existence.

Mudgala said: Know that action is of three types: Karma (prescribed duty), Akarma (action without desire), and Vikarma (prohibited action). All actions performed devoid of one's own duty are termed Vikarma. Actions performed according to one's duty but driven by the desire for enjoyment are termed Karma, which leads to the dark path. Actions performed as an offering to the Supreme, remaining within one's duty, are known as Akarma; know this to be the bright path that grants liberation.

The path of fire, light, the northern direction, and the daytime—know all of these to be the bright path, which is the giver of liberation. The path of the moon, smoke, night, and the southern direction—know this to be the nature of the dark path, which leads to birth and death. I shall explain the difference between these two for the welfare of the people, so that by abandoning duality, one may become absorbed in Brahman.

When a man dies after performing desireless actions, if it is at night or during the sun's southern course, the gods do not seize him. Having rejected divine enjoyments and being devoted to offering everything to Brahman, what can the gods who grant heavenly pleasures do to such a man? Therefore, O Daksa, upon cremation, he remains in the light of the fire. When the daytime arrives, he is taken by the deities of the day. Upon the sun's northern course, he reaches the solar sphere. At the time of the dissolution of that sphere, he reaches the Purusa (the Supreme). At the time of the great dissolution, when both Prakriti and Purusa dissolve, he enters Brahman and becomes one with Brahman.

When a man dies during the day in the sun's northern course after performing actions with desire, the sun does not seize him because he is bound by desire. When night arrives, he remains in the smoke. O Prajapati, he stays there, and upon seeing the southern course, he immediately ascends a celestial chariot and goes to heaven through the sky. Relying on the lunar light, he enjoys the fruits of his own actions according to the measure of his karma, devoted to various heavens.

I shall explain the process of the moon's digits. The nectar in the divine realms is the supreme giver of enjoyment. The actions performed by humans here, which are full of merit, become nectar-like and go to the moon, by which one becomes endowed with its digits. In the bright fortnight, the Lord (the moon) grows digit by digit until he is full on the full moon day. In the dark fortnight, all the gods, who are consumers of nectar, drink that nectar residing in the moon, which brings satisfaction. By that, they remain satisfied month by month. On the new moon, the moon falls into the medicinal herbs; there is no doubt about this. Therefore, it is certain that medicinal herbs should not be cut by men on the new moon, as they are united with the moon and are sources of happiness. Thus, all nectar born of the moon is in the divine realms; that is why the followers of the Vedas call it the lunar heaven.

Having performed various actions with desire for the gods and having worshipped the presiding deity of those actions, the man goes there after offering the fruits of his deeds. Having enjoyed the pleasures of those respective worlds, at the end of his enjoyment, he enters the cloud sphere. Becoming water-like, he falls to the earth with the rain. Then, entering the medicinal herbs, he becomes food. Then, taking refuge in the seed, he enters the womb of a woman. There, O Daksa, the elements undoubtedly enter him. The soul (Kshetrajna) residing there creates the body with the elements. I shall explain the process as it is in the Garbha Upanishad.

In one day, the man becomes a mixture of the woman's seed and blood. In three days, he becomes a foam-like form. In five days, a bubble; in seven days, a mass. In fourteen days, he becomes a sprout-like form. In twenty-five days, he becomes flesh. In a month, he becomes firm. In two months, O Prajapati, he develops a head. In three months, a neck; in four months, skin. In five months, the creature gradually develops senses. In six months, he is endowed with limbs, nails, hair, and a mouth; there is no doubt. Then, knowledge born of the senses arises gradually. In six full months, the form becomes complete. According to his previous karma, he takes a form and senses corresponding to the womb he enters. In seven months, the creature is endowed with consciousness; he moves in the mother's womb, suffering. Lacking space and constantly pressed by creatures, the delicate human faints and regains consciousness. In eight months, the man becomes endowed with intelligence. Becoming devoted to Sankhya-Yoga, he meditates on the eternal Brahman. Pressed by the contraction and the mother's food, he suffers. Realizing the misery of the womb, he becomes devoted to the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) to attain liberation. Then, supreme knowledge arises, and seeing the misery of infinite births, he seeks liberation. With a focused mind, the wise one praises the Lord to end the stay in the womb and to become Brahman.

The fetus said: Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, to Brahman, who is the form of Brahman. Salutations to the Lord of the helpless, to the remover of obstacles. Salutations to the eldest, the divine, the form of the Lord of gods, the beginningless, the supreme, and the one who is worshipped first. Salutations to the one worshipped by all, the form of all, the beginning of all, the Supreme Brahman, the Lord of all. Salutations to the one with the elephant form, the one who is the elephant form, the one who bears the elephant head, the Lord of elephants. Salutations to the Lord of self-bliss, who is the beginning, middle, and end. Salutations to the one who is beyond beginning, middle, and end. Salutations to the giver of success and intelligence, the one who dwells in success and intelligence, the one who is the form of success and intelligence, the Lord of Brahman. Salutations to the one who is Shiva, Shakti, Vishnu, and the sun-form, the one who causes delusion through the Maya of the deluded. O Lord of the Ganas, what can I praise you with, when the Vedas and others, and the yogis who have attained peace, bow to you? Protect me from the misery of the womb; I have taken refuge in you. Make me free from birth and death, and dear to your feet.

After he praised Him thus, after nine months, the great wind of birth pushes the man out in an instant. Pushed by the wind, he faints with pain. With cramped limbs, the creature falls to the earth through the birth canal. He becomes cautious here, having forgotten the supreme knowledge. Being a child, he cries on the earth. All the great knowledge gained in the womb becomes fruitless; therefore, one should attain knowledge here on earth. O Prajapati, if a man does not attain knowledge here in the land of action, he suffers the misery of the womb again.

Thus, the dark path has been explained to you, which causes birth and death and gives pleasure and pain to the creature. The human who reads this hymn composed in the womb will enjoy all pleasures and finally attain self-bliss. O Prajapati, he will never again have to stay in a womb. Therefore, the creature should constantly chant this.

Now I will tell you another matter that gives happiness, regarding the bright and dark paths, by which one gains knowledge of both. For the sake of enjoyment and liberation, a man who knows Yoga should constantly perform actions according to his duty and worship various gods. If one dies during the sun's northern course or during the day, he reaches the world of fire and remains ever-devoted to penance. There, burning the desire for enjoyment, the wise one attains power. Abandoning ego there, he goes to the sun. With deep meditation, he contemplates the Self. Abandoning the inner self, he goes to Vaikuntha. Abandoning the bliss there, he goes to Shiva; there is no doubt. Abandoning the state of being a witness there, he easily goes to self-bliss. Seeing the Lord of the Ganas there, the man becomes Brahman. O Prajapati, this has been told to you through the influence of the bright path.

When one dies during the sun's southern course or at night, that man, even if he is seeking enjoyment and liberation, goes to heaven. Having enjoyed the fruits of various heavens created by his own actions, the man falls again to the earth, where he develops a taste for liberation. Then, relying on the bright path, the yogi attains liberation. Know this, O Daksa, as the ancient path of the two.

When one relies on a single deity and is always devoted to Him, worshipping Him according to the rules of his own duty, he dies and goes to the world of his chosen deity; there is no doubt. In that case, the paths of the moon and sun are not mentioned. Enjoying pleasures there, he definitely falls back here. O Prajapati, the gods are made of the three gunas; upon their destruction, he falls. When a man worships the Lord of the Ganas here, he becomes absorbed in self-bliss at the end and becomes Brahman. Self-bliss is without destruction and always gives the bliss of Brahman. Therefore, relying on self-bliss, he becomes Brahman.

At the time of death, if he is without desire, he relies on the element of light, or if he relies on the element of smoke, he is dependent on the bright or dark path. Everything has been told to you as before, O Prajapati. Thus, the paths have been explained to you in detail. Therefore, O Daksa, take refuge in the Lord of the Ganas, who is beneficial. He will always provide for the welfare of those who are single-minded. You will never have the bright or dark path; you will attain the state of Brahman; there is no doubt about this.

Chapter 13 : Yoga of Knowledge of the Field and the Knower of the Field

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: O highly fortunate one, please explain the Field, the Knower of the Field, and that which is beyond both. Explain what is called the all-pervading Brahman, and by what means the knowledge of both is attained.

Mudgala said: Know the body to be the Field and the embodied soul to be the Knower of the Field. In the union of these two, the Great Brahman arises, which is known as self-manifestation. The five elements, the five subtle elements, the five organs of perception, the five organs of action, and the five vital airs (pranas) are the constituents. The ten senses, aversion, desire, fortitude, the unmanifest, consciousness, sorrow, happiness, and delusion—these, along with the mind, intellect, the gross, the subtle, the causal, and the fourth state (Turiya), are what the wise call the Field when they are in a state of union.

The Supreme is always of the nature of pure existence, devoid of distinctions. The Knower of the Field, abiding in the Field, holds the position of the indweller of all. In the union of these two, the Supreme Brahman exists, devoid of the seen and the unseen, yet full of the seen and the unseen, of the nature of pure consciousness, O Lord of creatures.

Through the states of the gross, subtle, and causal, Maya specifically and constantly deludes the Knower of the Field, making it dependent on her. Deluded by these four types of distinctions, the soul constantly and foolishly considers itself to be the body. Then, for the sake of enjoying the Field, it becomes devoted to various actions, wandering in the three gunas, identified with "I" and "mine."

A person should abandon the Tamasic quality and adopt the Rajasic; then, abandoning the Rajasic, one should gradually become Sattvic. Then, taking refuge in the fourth state, one should worship the Supreme Ganesha. By attaining the all-pervading fifth state, the person becomes one with Brahman.

Actions that always provide worldly happiness are known as sinful, for through them, one never realizes the truth. Know that which is full of bodily enjoyment and devoted to the belly and genitals as Tamasic, which leads to hell in the end. Those attached to the Tamasic, after experiencing great suffering, are born again in low species, afflicted with diseases and deformities, poor, and devoid of enjoyments. If such people continue to perform sinful deeds, they dwell in the house of Yama, in hell, again and again.

Therefore, humans should abandon the Tamasic nature and, endowed with righteousness and fortitude, strive through divine grace. Know the Rajasic state as that of people on earth who are full of desire; they constantly perform actions that are righteous but yield worldly fruits. Actions performed with desire while remaining in one's own duty are Rajasic, leading to various states. Rajasic people, after enjoying all pleasures, fall again; as is the action performed here, so is the fruit. Or, due to small Rajasic actions, people are born again on earth instantly, or they are born in various high and low species, subject to birth and death. For the Rajasic, O Lord of creatures, there is no hell; they wander through stationary and moving forms, subject to birth. There, they experience various joys and sorrows; there is no doubt about this. Thus, the Rajasic state has been briefly explained to you.

Now I will explain the Sattvic, which is devoid of desire. Know that those who remain in their own duty perform this constantly. Whatever righteous action is performed as an offering to Brahman, know that to be Sattvic, O giver of honor. By this, they follow the path of light and attain liberation easily. Thus, humans become endowed with the Sattva guna.

Now I will briefly explain the fourth state (Turiya). When the mind, along with the body, is devoid of passion, and the fourth state arises, then the action performed is supreme. The entire diverse world is undoubtedly of the form of Brahman. Whatever action is performed by such a person becomes a giver of Brahman; praise and blame are not attributed to it. O Daksha, devotion to deities and the like are not established in the Turiya state; one always remains in Brahman with an equal vision. One sees the Universal Form within all states; then the action of the Turiya state occurs, and not otherwise.

Thus, that which happens through equality in all is the Turiya state, occurring through the fruition of past actions and the essence of Brahman. In the end, the human becomes absorbed in that state of 'I-am-ness.' Through the great causal state, one becomes the mover of the three gunas.

Now I will tell you about the action of liberation (Kaivalya). Know that action which is devoid of injunctions and prohibitions, performed by one's own will, as the fifth state, accepted by the Yogis. The body is the doer, there is no doubt; the Turiya state is considered the motivator. Where does the Turiya state exist in the Supreme, by which this entire fourfold creation was created in play? He himself, as the doer and the one who causes to be done, exists there; where is the "I" and where is the inquiry into Brahman? I am neither human nor Brahman; what is to be attained by me constantly? Without doubt, everything is seen in me through the delusion of Maya. Brahman plays at will in its fourfold form; there is no auspicious or inauspicious for Him; a person should abandon useless ego.

Through this experience, a person attains peace. Abandoning the essence everywhere, one becomes the holder of the essence of Brahman. Thus, having established the embodied soul in the fourfold state and having become free from the delusion of the body, one becomes identified with "That I am." Thus, by degrees, the best of men becomes the best of Yogis. Everything regarding the means of the Field and the Knower of the Field has been explained in detail.

Now hear the conduct of the Varnas and Ashramas, which grants all happiness, by which the best of men attains righteousness, wealth, desire, and liberation. Where the senses are controlled, where there is forgiveness, simplicity, penance, purity, and where the knowledge of the Vedas and Smritis is established—that is the path. One who never acts without the basis of the Vedas and is always devoted to Brahman is remembered as a Brahmana.

Valour, skill, firmness, facing the battle, protecting those who seek refuge, lordship, and the wielding of the rod of justice—these five duties belong to the Kshatriya. Buying and selling, cultivation of land, and the tending of cows—these three are the duties of the Vaishya. Service to the three Varnas, charity, chanting the name and mantra, and listening to the Puranas—this is the Shudra's duty according to the Puranas. The duties of the outcaste are those that deviate from the Puranic path; there is nothing equal to the Shudra, and there is untouchability among the twice-born.

The Brahmana, Kshatriya, or Vaishya who has been initiated with the sacred thread should study the Vedas and remain in celibacy. One should live on alms, be obedient to the Guru, and chant the Gayatri daily at the three junctures. One should perform the daily fire sacrifice morning and evening, being self-controlled. One should practice celibacy for sixteen or twelve years. Devoted to the five daily sacrifices, one should perform occasional duties. Having taken a wife for the sake of progeny, the householder should approach his wife during the proper season.

Abandoning attachment to women, one should live in the forest with one's wife. One should perform all daily duties with forest products and engage in supreme penance. With matted hair and nails, one should not enter villages. Thus, the Vanaprastha should live, eating only air and the like. Even in the mind, one should not remember attachment to women, being endowed with renunciation. Constantly self-controlled, the person should live on alms. One should never touch even a shell. One should always strive for the state of being Brahman in Brahman.

Like a witness, even while in the body, one should hold the staff of silence or, if without a physical staff, be a holder of the staff of knowledge. Thus, having taken to renunciation, one should always remain self-controlled. One should touch only the scriptures that lead to the path of liberation, and nothing else.

A Yogi who is devoid of Varna and Ashrama is called the fifth. He is devoid of injunctions and prohibitions, like Ganesha. All this has been told to you regarding the practice of one's own duty, by which a person attains the Yoga that grants Brahman.

Daksha said: O giver of honor, explain the nature of righteousness, wealth, desire, and liberation. To me, the state of being Brahman seems different; please destroy my delusion. Some say righteousness is the greatest, others say wealth, desire, or liberation, and still others say the state of being Brahman is the highest of the high.

Mudgala said: Through righteousness, the heavenly realm is attained by humans; Brahman is equal to all, and this is called one's own duty. Wealth is the state of capability, which grants the realm of the Lord; there is no one more capable than Ishvara in the entire universe. Desire is said to be of the nature of enjoyment, granting the realm of Vishnu; He is known as the Lord of Lakshmi, the Lord who grants various desires. Liberation is said to be of the nature of the Sun; the Sun is called the Purusha. The one who follows the path of light is the full, direct witness, the radiant one.

The state of being Brahman is said to be one's own bliss, granting full Yoga. The knowers of the Vedas do not speak of any realm beyond one's own bliss. Hear another thing, O Lord of creatures; these five are the givers of Yoga. All are indicators of Yoga; I will tell you the distinction among them. Through the Yoga of righteousness, supreme Yoga is attained by humans. There is nothing higher than Yoga; therefore, this wealth is declared. Through the Yoga of desire, this Yoga is attained in all states. Abandoning the veil of Maya, the Yoga of liberation is declared.

Brahman is described in the Vedas as Brahman; there is no doubt about this. The Yogi who, through Yoga, becomes one with Brahman is remembered as such. Righteousness, wealth, desire, and liberation, especially in the Yoga scriptures, are said to be different and of the nature of Maya; the state of being Brahman is the fifth. The Yogi who is established in Brahman is called Brahman himself. He is not different in any way from that Brahman; he is the one who has gone to Brahman.

Thus ends the thirteenth chapter, titled the Yoga of Knowledge of the Field and the Knower of the Field, in the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 14 : The Yoga of Unity of Diverse Opinions

Mudgala said: I shall now describe a single realization that is consistent with the meaning of the Vedas and scriptures. By abandoning the fear of diverse opinions, a person becomes a yogi through this understanding. Logicians of the Nyaya school declare that consciousness is the primary essence of Brahman, and all beings, endowed with consciousness, are devoted to their respective duties. Because it illuminates everything, this consciousness is termed Brahman; when abandoned by consciousness, everything meets with destruction. There is no doubt that through the union of the five types of consciousness (panchachitta), that which is called Brahman is attained, regardless of the various interpretations of words.

Logicians argue that the ultimate view is the non-duality of all, a concept supported by the Vedanta and the scriptures. The distinctions made by yogis between the five types of consciousness are actually non-distinct; this yoga, known as the realization of a single essence, is established by the evidence of its meaning. Experts in the Dharma-shastras believe that there is no state higher than knowledge (bodha), and that the supreme Brahman is essentially knowledge, devoid of both existence and non-existence. The dharma associated with the five types of consciousness must be abandoned by the yogi; the true Dharma is the Lord who upholds the nature of Brahman.

The Sankhya philosophers say that the Sankhya is that Brahman which is devoid of numbers; there is no Brahman higher than pure knowledge, which is beyond the influence of play. In that state, the five-fold consciousness, through the yoga of counting, becomes devoid of itself and is known as Yoga according to the testimony of words. Mimamsakas argue that the ultimate view is self-evident; Brahman is the cause, and there is nothing else established within Brahman. By abandoning the five-fold consciousness, the yogi attains the form of Brahman; through Mimamsa, the yogi himself becomes the supreme reality known as Yoga.

Thus, those who follow various opinions speak according to their respective scriptures, yet Brahman is manifold and becomes accessible through Yoga. The Shaktas say that Shakti is Brahman and there is nothing higher; where there is a union of the two (Shiva and Shakti), that is what becomes accessible through Yoga. Shakti, residing in the fifth consciousness, merges into Brahman; by adopting the consciousness known as Nirodha (restraint), she is remembered as the Goddess.

The Saura (sun-worshippers) say that the Sun is the supreme Brahman, the highest of the high, and there is nothing beyond it, as it is the foundation of all. By living in the realization of the non-dual nature of the Sun, one becomes filled with Yoga, without a doubt. The Vaishnavas declare that Vishnu is the supreme Brahman, and there is no state higher than that, as He is the embodiment of eternal bliss. When the fifth consciousness is freed from delusion and becomes one with that, it results in the Yoga of Brahman, which is pure bliss.

The Shaivas declare that Shiva is the supreme Brahman, and in the Vedas, there is nothing higher or devoid of delusion than Him. The three-fold restraint (nirodha) is dependent on the Lord, and when one is established in that, it becomes the Yoga that is under one's own control. Thus, those who follow various opinions and are devoted to their respective deities speak of their truth based on the meanings of words. Since words have multiple meanings, what is not possible? All words, when interpreted through Yoga, lead the yogi to the goal. Words like 'food' and 'prana' are also indicators of Brahman; when understood through the lens of Yoga, they all lead to the realization of Brahman.

I shall now describe the Vedanta, which is especially honored by all, and the scriptures are its limbs and eternal proofs. He who knows all things in all states as being united by Maya, through the influence of being free from Maya, remains in Yoga eternally. That is the primary Brahman, there is no doubt. From Brahman, the Rig Veda and others have emerged, and this knowledge is known as Yoga. He who knows that everything arises from the five-fold consciousness is a master, and Yoga is the state of being free from the attachments associated with it.

The statement 'I am Brahman' refers to that Brahman which is devoid of all faults; it is proclaimed in the Yajur Veda as the supreme of the supreme. Know, O King, that the distinctions arising from the five-fold consciousness are actually devoid of distinction when viewed through the evidence of Yoga-shastra. The union of that which is with Maya-delusion and that which is without it is what is described in the Sama Veda as the supreme Brahman. Brahman with Maya-delusion is the source of its own bliss, and that which is beyond it, devoid of Maya, is the state of Yoga when the two are united.

That which is established in Brahman is not composed of mind or speech; the Atharva Veda declares it to be beyond mind and speech. That which is composed of mind and speech is called Samprajnata (conscious) Yoga, and the Asamprajnata (super-conscious) is that which is beyond both. Thus, O noble one, one should understand these great Vedic statements through the practice of Yoga. Words like 'Karma' are described by the wise as being full of Brahman, and in the view of yogis, they all reside in the state of Yoga. The unity of all scriptural opinions has been told to you through the understanding of words; knowing this, a human being becomes forever free from delusion.

Chapter 15 : Yoga of the Mystery of Ganesha

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Now I shall explain the dissolution (Laya) by which the mind attains dissolution. Having established yoga in Brahman and knowing the yoga, O Prajapati, listen. Dissolution is said to be of four types: Nitya (constant), Naimittika (occasional), the third is Prakrita (natural), and the fourth is Sarasvata.

The wise have previously described the Nitya (constant) dissolution as being of the nature of Karma, Akarma, and Vikarma. Through the influence of Karma, the birth and existence of people occur. From the beginning of moments up to the end of one's lifespan, the manifestation of stages like conception is described. At the end of the day of Brahma, it is undoubtedly obstructed by the Naimittika dissolution, which causes the dissolution of the three worlds. All creatures, whether endowed with merits or demerits, undergo dissolution. Due to the obstruction of Karma, they become devoid of the fruits of merit and demerit. The three worlds, up to the heavenly realm, go into dissolution. By that measure, it is called Nitya by the wise.

Now, O Daksha, I shall describe the Naimittika dissolution briefly. A thousand cycles of four ages are called a day of Brahma. The dissolution that occurs at the end of that day is called Naimittika by those skilled in all meanings. Now I shall speak of the Prakrita dissolution, which is accompanied by arrangements. That which goes into the Great Cause is called Prakrita. Because it is the state of the elements—both gross and subtle—becoming one, it is called Prakrita by the wise, as it remains in Prakriti (Nature).

Listen, O Lord, to the Sarasvata dissolution, which is supreme, complete, and bestows yoga. It is the dissolution of the five states of the mind through the state of restraint. The entire world is composed of mind and speech and follows various natures. The wise know that which is devoid of mind and speech as Asamprajnata (unconscious/transcendental) Samadhi. Through speech, a thought is expressed, and the mind follows it. O Lord, this is because all living beings are differentiated by various objects. That which is called Brahman, which is within the heart and devoid of speech—the mind always remains in that state through Samadhi.

Speech is said to be of two types: Brahma-like and world-like. The wise say that the dissolution of both is the supreme Sarasvata dissolution. Where one attains the dissolution of Sarasvati (speech) through the state of yoga, combined with the Great Sayings (Mahavakyas), that is called Sarasvata. Through the Sarasvata dissolution, the mind becomes fivefold, O Lord. Having dissolved the mind into the Chintamani (the wish-fulfilling gem of consciousness), remain as one accepted by the yogis.

I have told you all this briefly, which bestows yoga. Keep this great yoga secret, and then you will become a yogi. Do not tell this yoga to anyone, O Prajapati. Because of the influence of an unworthy recipient, it will be lost. Instruct those who are devoid of desires and possess a longing for the knowledge of Ganesha, especially; then it will be auspicious.

I shall tell you the reason for this briefly, O Daksha. By merely hearing it, doubts will dissolve. When Ganesha, the embodiment of yoga, was engaged in play, then Maya, in the form of Siddhi and Buddhi, said these words to Him.

Siddhi and Buddhi said: If You engage in play, O Ganesha, we shall cover You by displaying various phenomena. Through the Vedas and other scriptures, the speech-based narrative, O Gajanan, will describe Your true form to humans. By that, humans and others will know You as the Lord of Brahman. By merely worshipping You, they will become Brahman-like. Speech, which resides in You, will not be able to cover You, O Ganesha. Therefore, for that purpose, O Gajanan, do something joyfully.

Hearing their words, Gajanan said to them: Do not be sad, O Goddesses. I shall delude speech. That speech, deluded by Me, will describe Me in a way that creates confusion, filled with hidden meanings and yet speaking the truth. The masters of yoga, the seers of truth, will not know the hidden meaning, and therefore, all will not worship Me with devotion. Speech will reveal Brahman endowed with Siddhi and Buddhi; it will not tell the story of Ganesha that bestows yoga.

He who serves the Brahman of the world, which is full of Siddhi and Buddhi, when that person becomes pure, O beloved, then he will know Me. The Brahman endowed with supreme Siddhi is called the giver of Siddhi. That very Ganatha is the Lord of Siddhi by evidence. The Brahman endowed with supreme Buddhi is called beyond Buddhi. Ganesha Himself is declared the Lord of Buddhi by evidence. Thus, speech will always speak of Me in this threefold way. The Brahman that gives Siddhi and is in Buddhi—these three are considered in the scriptures.

Having said this, Ganesha became silent again. Then Bhakti, being extremely sorrowful, bowed and spoke.

Bhakti said: You have given a boon to Siddhi and Buddhi, O Lord Gajanan. Because of that, humans will always be devoid of devotion. Therefore, I am filled with sorrow and will wander constantly. Protect me, O Lord of the Ganas, and make people endowed with devotion.

Being thus addressed, the Lord of the Ganas, who is fond of devotion, smiled and said to the sorrowful Bhakti, O Prajapati.

Shri Ganesha said: Do not grieve, O auspicious one. Humans will become My devotees. There is no doubt that gods, Nagas, and demons will also become so. Those who are devoted to various gods, birth after birth, will be satisfied, and the gods will joyfully give them a liking for fire. Then, constantly worshipping the fire with devotion, they will satisfy Him, being devoted to various sacrifices. Then, through the grace of His yoga, humans will become endowed with devotion to the Goddess, and all of them will worship Her with restraint.

After a long time, She, being satisfied, will give them supreme solar devotion, and through that, they will worship the Sun. Then, after a long time, the Sun, being satisfied, will respectfully give them intense Vaishnava devotion. Endowed with that, they will worship Vishnu with devotion. Then, after a long time, He will be satisfied. Vishnu Himself will give them the best devotion. Through that, they will worship Sadashiva. Then, after a long time, Shambhu, being satisfied, will give them Ganesha-devotion, and through that, they will worship Me. Humans, having attained peace and being devoted to yoga from Me, will become Brahman-like, and even then, they will worship Me.

Thus, in this order, humans and others will become My devotees. Endowed with you, they will worship Me, and I shall become subservient to them. There will be nothing as dear to Me as My devotee. Wherever devotion goes, I shall remain there like a servant. I shall give them My own bliss, the attainment of goals, Siddhi, Buddhi, and My own self; there is no doubt about this. This body of Mine will be subject to devotion. I shall always remain before the devotee, in the form of a devotee, like a servant.

Devotion is greater than actions; it is a supreme penance that is difficult to perform. Knowledge is greater than penance, as it bestows the state of non-attachment. Yoga is described as greater than knowledge, as it bestows peace. Therefore, O great devotee, you will become more dear to Me. There is nothing greater than devotion in My eyes, as accepted by the scriptures. Do not worry at all, O great one.

As for the other speech that will describe My hidden form, I shall do what is dear to you, O devotee, listen to My words. This Mudgala, who resides in My arm and destroys all pride, will become a Brahmana and wander constantly. He has composed My Purana, which is augmented by the Vedas. It will reveal the hidden meaning and bestow devotion upon all. In it, O devotee, those who look will see My form revealed, and those who listen will undoubtedly become complete.

Even so, My devotion will be difficult for you to attain. Therefore, O Goddess, those with little knowledge will never worship Me. I shall create confusion in the Mudgala scripture. Due to lack of faith, it will be destroyed at times. I have created the Mudgala scripture to protect your state of mind. Therefore, O great devotee, tell me what else I can do for you.

Hearing His words, She was delighted and bowed to Him. Ganesha, deep in emotion, did not say anything further.

I have told you this secret of Vighneshvara, O Daksha. Therefore, keep My scripture strictly secret. You are Daksha (expert) in all matters; there is no one more expert than Daksha. Therefore, I have told you this entire supreme scripture. Inspired by Ganesha who resides in my heart, O Prajapati, by His command, I have told this supreme scripture that was in my heart. By this method, O Daksha, worship the Lord of the Ganas. By that, having attained your desires, you will become a follower of Ganesha.

Thus ends the fifteenth chapter named the Yoga of the Mystery of Ganesha, in the Mudgala Mahapurana, in the ninth section, in the dialogue between Mudgala and Daksha.

Chapter 16 : Description of the Mystery of Devotion

Daksa said: O great-minded one, I am blessed and my purpose is fulfilled today. Being under your command, O Lord, I shall perform the secret scripture for you. Now, tell me about the devotion to Ganesha, so that I may worship Him with special devotion, always being intent on that devotion.

Mudgala said: O Lord of the people, listen as I tell you of the Sankhya and Yoga. Being endowed with these, you will perform the supreme devotion with joy. The mind is said to be fivefold, devoid of the number five. With proper effort, fix your mind in the Cintamani (Ganesha) with a dissolved state of being. This is called Sankhya, which bestows non-difference with Ganesha. By this, a yogi immediately becomes peaceful. You are well-established in the enjoyments arising from the five minds; enjoy them in the form of the image, filled with the essence of peace. In the minds, which are the source of the mind, let all actions, knowledge, etc., be offered to Ganesha with constant reverence. O Daksa, always keep your body established in your own duty and your mind filled with peace, intent on the state of Brahman. This yoga is declared in the scriptures and is accepted by all; in the Vedas and Puranas, it is the giver of the state of Brahman.

Having worshipped the Sankhya and Yoga, worship the Lord of the Ganas. Meditating on the form of Yoga, be intent on Him and devoted to Him. Perform the listening to the qualities of the Lord of the Ganas daily with reverence. Likewise, perform the chanting of His names, filled with these essences. Do not forget the state of Ganesha, O Lord. Hold the delusion born of Ganesha’s feet with a truthful heart. Perform everything related to the Lord of the Ganas with special care. There is nothing greater than Ganesha; therefore, be intent on Him. Always serve the path of Ganapatya. Knowing Ganesha to be the witness residing in your heart, worship Him. Constantly contemplate that you are not different from Ganesha. In this way, worship Him with the nine types of devotion. Perform all external and internal actions for the sake of Ganesha with reverence. By these nine divisions, you will become a complete devotee.

Now, listen as I tell you of the fourfold devotion. Perform the bodily, verbal, complete, and mental devotion daily. Also, O Daksa, I shall tell you the signs of social devotion. O great-minded one, always perform bodily labor for the sake of Ganesha. Do not speak any words that are devoid of the state of Ganesha. Similarly, contemplate Ganesha or those who are devoted to Ganesha. Always keep the company of those who are endowed with devotion to Ganesha. Worship with devotion in this way, and you will become the king of devotees.

Devotion is of two types, O Daksa, practiced by humans: that which is mixed with hypocrisy and that which is complete, intended solely for the pleasure of the Divine. Worship performed for the sake of bodily enjoyment is that of hypocrites, O Daksa, which is meant to delude the world. I shall tell you the main sign of hypocritical worship. Listen to it for the benefit of the people, O savior of the world. A hypocrite appears to people as if he is devoid of all ego, by which people become deluded and perform worship. Wherever and whatever the people like, he performs it with effort, displaying all kinds of emotions. Whatever leads to the attainment of worldly objects, that becomes his constant worship. Or, he becomes one-pointed and devoted to the deity, but he is a wicked person greedy for wealth, deluding the devotees of his own deity. People attached to the worship of their own deity are many, though their intellect is small; their worship happens according to their tastes. O Prajapati, I have told you of the devotion that deludes people. It is always practiced by hypocrites who are full of desire. Those who always perform devotion mixed with hypocrisy are considered untouchables in the scriptures and are the cause of suffering to the gods. Those wicked people, having gathered the supreme deity who is attained by the power of true resolve, treat Him like a beggar and go to the lower realms.

Now, listen with a focused mind to the devotion that is dear to the Divine. One performs devotion for the pleasure of the Divine after contemplating it in the heart. Always becoming intent on the Divine, one worships with a single mind. Whether people have a taste for it or not, or whether people criticize or praise that devotee, he ignores them and always worships for the pleasure of the Divine. He also always contemplates in his own heart; the devotion that he performs is known by the Divine who resides in his heart. When people insult or disrespect the path of the Divine, he abandons them and always worships. Sometimes his worship is according to the taste of the people, and sometimes it is against them, but it is always for the pleasure of the Divine. By this method, O Daksa, worship the Lord of the Ganas. There is no doubt that you will become a Ganapatya, supreme among the supreme.

Know that the knowledge in the five minds is related to objects. Be devoid of taste for that and worship the Lord of the Ganas. O Daksa, when the essence of Ganesha is constantly generated in the mind, and not in other states, he is called a devotee. This mystery of devotion, which bestows devotion, has been told to you. Worship Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, in summary.

Whoever listens to this yoga or makes others listen, whether they are yogis or other beings, shall attain their desired goals. There is nothing equal to this that gives yoga and is accepted by the scriptures. By merely listening to this, a person becomes Brahman-like. One is freed from great sins and minor sins by reading it. By listening to this yoga, one attains the supreme Brahman at the end. Endowed with sons, grandsons, wealth, and grains, and endowed with health, one attains the supreme state at the end. One who has desires will definitely attain enjoyments by reading this. There is no doubt that by listening, one will become Brahman-like at the end. Whoever reads this Yoga Gita without desire, or listens to it, will become free from obstacles and become Brahman-like. Whoever reads this Yoga Gita will become peaceful. Whoever listens to it becomes Brahman-realized, wise, and steady. O Prajapati, this should be served by those intent on action and those intent on penance. By being endowed with those respective accomplishments, one attains Brahman here at the end. One who reads this Gita daily is Ganesha on earth; by his sight, he is a purifier of men and a giver of all accomplishments. Whoever reads this once, twice, or three times a day, having made Ganesha the focus, becomes a giver of everything. A noble person should read this on the Shukla and Krishna Chaturthi; having enjoyed all pleasures, he attains the supreme Brahman at the end. Or, one who reads it on the Shukla Chaturthi of Bhadrapada or Magha, or on the Krishna Chaturthi of Jyeshtha or Magha, attains his desired goals. Or, having gone to a pilgrimage site of the Lord of the Ganas, one who reads this with a pure heart attains his desired goals. Whatever essence has been declared in the Vedas, scriptures, and Puranas, this Yoga Gita is that, bestowing all accomplishments upon you.

Chapter 17 : Describing the Attainment of Success by Daksha

Mudgala said: O Lord of subjects, I have narrated to you this entire supreme secret of Ganesha, which is auspicious and concise. Therefore, worship the Lord of the Ganas, and you shall attain all your desires. Do not grieve, O Daksha, over the destruction of your sacrifice.

Suta said: Having said this, the sage Mudgala gave Daksha the supreme single-syllable mantra according to the prescribed rites. As the yogi was worshipped by him and prepared to depart, the auspicious Lord of the Ganas manifested. He was seen mounted on a mouse, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, holding a noose, a goad, and a boon-granting mudra. He had an elephant face, was adorned with red garments and ornaments, was surrounded by Ganas like Pramoda, and was served by them. Seeing the three-eyed, single-tusked, large-bellied, and supreme Lord arrive, Daksha and Mudgala were filled with joy. Both Daksha and the sage Mudgala stood up and bowed down to the elephant-faced Lord, prostrating like a staff upon the earth.

Then, filled with immense joy, devotion, horripilation, and tears, Daksha spoke to Ganesha. Daksha said: I am blessed in every way by your vision, O Elephant-faced One. You are beyond the reach of Vedanta, the very form of Brahman, and yet you have come here. Blessed is this yogi Mudgala, and blessed is this supreme Purana, through which I have attained the Lord of Obstacles, who is supreme. What Mudgala said—that Ganesha is subservient to devotion—I have now realized to be the absolute truth. You were moved by the devotion of Mudgala and appeared suddenly. There is no doubt that Mudgala is greater than you, O Lord of Ganesha.

As the noble Daksha spoke thus, the essence of devotion arose in him, and he danced in ecstasy. Overwhelmed, he forgot his own identity and cried out, "Victory to Heramba, Lord of Obstacles, Ganesha, whose banner bears a mouse! Victory to the Elephant-faced One, the Supreme Lord, the Pot-bellied One!" As he chanted, with tear-filled eyes, he seemed to transcend his physical body.

Meanwhile, all the lords of the gods, led by Shiva and Vishnu, arrived upon knowing of Ganesha's presence. Sages like Bhrigu, Narada, Shuka, Sanaka, and Kapila, along with the Vasus, Sadhyas, Rudras, Gandharvas, Apsaras, the Ashvins, Nagas led by Shesha, mountain kings, Siddhas, Charanas, Asuras, protectors of Dharma, Vidyadharas, and many cows and Brahmins arrived. They bowed to the Lord of the Ganas, praised him with joy, and felt themselves blessed by their devotion.

Then, the yogi Mudgala was amazed to see the infinite fruit of hearing the Purana. He enlightened Daksha, who, filled with joy, controlled his emotions and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles according to the rites. After worshipping the gods and the sages, Daksha bowed again to the Elephant-faced Lord. With tearful eyes and a neck bent in devotion, he praised the Supreme Lord, and the gods and sages followed suit.

Daksha said: Salutations to you, O Ganesha, the soul of all, who becomes manifest through the illusion of devotion. Salutations to you, who are praised by Shiva and other gods, and by sages like Shuka; you are the one who brings union to those who are not united. Salutations to you, O Elephant-faced One, who holds the axe, the mace, and the conch. Salutations to you, O Lord of Obstacles, Supreme Lord, destroyer of great obstacles, who gives refuge to the shelterless, O Heramba. Salutations to you, O Large-bellied One, devoted to the protection of your devotees; you destroy the desires of the non-devotees and always grant peace. Salutations to you, O Lord of Brahma and Shiva, who dwells in your own bliss, who grants success and wisdom, and whose banner bears a mouse. Salutations to you, who wears the head of the demon, who is the son of Diti, and who is the Lord of gods and demons. You are the essence of Yoga, worshipped by all, the deluder of those who practice illusion, the mover of Maya, the granter of success, and the protector of Dharma. O Lord, you play your infinite pastimes through your own Maya; what can I describe? I simply offer my salutations again and again. You are the embodiment of Yoga, the granter of devotion, the lover of devotees, and the perfect one. Be pleased and protect me, your servant who longs for your devotion. Protect me, the disciple of Mudgala, the king of devotees.

Having said this, the Prajapati bowed to Ganesha, and the gods and sages followed him. The Lord of the Ganas lifted him up and spoke excellent words to Daksha and Mudgala. Ganesha said: O Lord of subjects, I am pleased; ask for boons. Through the hearing of this Purana and this hymn, I shall grant your heart's desires. This hymn composed by you shall grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation. I shall grant whatever is desired by the one who recites this hymn. It will grant knowledge of Brahman, peace, and increase devotion; it will be a source of great pleasure to me.

Hearing Ganesha's words, Daksha was filled with joy and, bowing down, said: O Lord of the Ganas, you are the granter of boons; grant me supreme devotion. Tell me what I should do, and I shall perform it. Hearing this, the Lord of the Ganas, knowing Daksha's love for devotion, said: O noble one, you shall have immense devotion for me. From this day forward, O wise one, you shall be truly enlightened. Perform your sacrifice, and I shall be with you, along with Maheshwara. May you be happy, despite the curses of the followers of Shiva. Always remain in the peace of Yoga and worship me. You shall be honored by all and shall be the foremost among the Prajapatis.

Having said this, Ganesha became silent. Daksha, with a joyful heart, performed the excellent sacrifice. First, he worshipped the Lord of the Ganas, then the gods led by Shambhu, and the sages and Nagas, and then he sent them off with great joy. Then, worshipping the sage Mudgala with devotion, Daksha said: O great yogi, I am your disciple; I bow to you. My body is dedicated to your lotus feet, O Lord. Then, having blessed Daksha, Mudgala departed to his hermitage, served by the followers of Ganesha.

This is the greatness of Daksha and the excellence of Mudgala, which grants all success, as narrated to you in brief. The entire Mudgala Purana, filled with the essence of Yoga, has been narrated; it is honored by all and destroys all doubts. I have also told you the fruit of hearing this Purana. By hearing it, the Lord of subjects, Daksha, became truly wise. Now, O leader of Brahmins, I shall depart for my hermitage. Tell me, O Lord, what else I should do for your benefit?

Chapter 18 : Description of the Order of Ganesha’s Forms

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O Suta, you are blessed and considered more knowledgeable than all others. In these three worlds, there is no one who can dispel doubts like you. I am now free from all doubts and feel blessed by you; having attained the company of sages, I feel my purpose is fulfilled. I have not known anything higher than the Mudgala Purana, which is why it is said that the Mudgala is equal to the Supreme. It is the destroyer of various conflicting opinions found in the Vedas, Shastras, and Puranas; it is the form of Mudgala and the revealer of the essence of all things.

In the three worlds, the Yogi Mudgala is considered worthy of honor by all because he is free from all ego and acts according to the truth. The Purana created by Mudgala is in the form of Mudgala himself; one who insults it is born in hell. The messengers of Yama, holding clubs, will surely destroy such a person. There is no speaker or yogi anywhere who can be compared to Mudgala. There has never been, nor will there ever be, anyone like him. The speech of Mudgala is devoid of ego. All other Puranas, while focused on their own respective deities, often hide their own faults due to ego. However, the speech of the Puranas is indeed true, and I reside within the sages and deities as well. Because of this, the light of different opinions shines through them, but the Mudgala is honored by all due to its egoless nature.

Ego is terrible and difficult for even gods and yogis to conquer, but Mudgala has crushed it and made it lifeless. Therefore, the sages have declared this Mudgala to be unique. O Suta, it is no wonder that it is honored by all. Behold, the Mudgala, which was in the hands of Ganesha, has fallen to the earth and is protected by Angirasa in human form. That same Yogi Mudgala is the revealer of the form of Yoga; what wonder is there in that, as he is devoid of ego? I have heard the Purana spoken by Angirasa, which contains the dialogue between Mudgala and Daksha and grants peace.

Now, O Suta, tell me in detail about this Purana. What is the greatness of listening to it, and how should people listen to it? By what method should it be performed, and who has done so in the past? Who has attained perfection by listening to it? By whom should the recitation of this Purana be performed, and who should listen to its different sections? You have mentioned four Puranas of Ganesha; does one get the same fruit by listening to all of them, or are the fruits different? Tell me this so that we, free from doubt, may worship Gajanana.

Suta said: The Lord of Brahman, Ganesha, plays in twenty-one forms, appearing as the Pranava (Om) within the Puranas of the universe. In the Brahma Purana, He plays in twelve forms as the mover of intellect, being both Nirguna (formless) and Saguna (with attributes), and the driver of the Pranava. In the Ganesha Purana, He plays as the primary embodied form in a dualistic manner; He is the synthesis of the intellect and the Pranava. The worship of the Pranava aspect is called the Upasana Kanda, and the play of the One who sports in the heart is called the Krida Kanda. In the Mudgala, He is described in eight forms as the holder of Yoga, full of peace and the synthesis of union and separation.

I will tell you the fruit of these, which leads to the knowledge of Ganesha. Hearing this, all sages will be free from doubt. There are five deities described as having bodies made of three qualities: Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Surya, and Shakti. Some scriptures say that Brahma is not to be worshipped, and those who are indifferent go to the realm of Brahma. Those attached to the worship of the four, starting with Shiva, go to their respective realms according to their devotion. Those who worship the Saguna forms of Shiva and others attain their realms and enjoy great pleasures. Then, they fall back to earth, and through their past merits, they develop a taste for the Pranava.

Then they worship the Lord of Ganas in the form of the Pranava, striving for the attainment of Brahman without doubt. Through His grace, they become endowed with knowledge, and leaving the Pranava, they worship the One who holds the intellect. They worship the Lord of the intellect in their hearts with effort, being peaceful and self-controlled. Having worshipped Him, they attain the Lord of Ganas, adorned with the trunk. With single-minded devotion and respect, they strive for Yoga and peace. Being engaged in the worship of Ganesha and the meditation of mantras, they become holders of the Gaanapatya ego. They never consider anything higher than Ganesha after contemplating the Vedas, Shastras, and Puranas. Then, through the power of His grace, they worship the One in the Mudgala and become masters of Yoga.

Thus, O best of Brahmins, one becomes dear to Ganesha. The worshippers of the Puranas are considered to have different fruits. Those who worship the Pranava consider Gajanana as the embodied one in a general sense. How can the Lord of Ganas, who is the true form of Pranava, exist with limbs and other features? Everything is indeed Omkara; there is no difference. By this method, their worship occurs. After that, those who worship the One in the intellect consider Ganesha as the same in all. Beyond the intellect is the One who resides in the intellect and gives light to it; where does Brahman shine there, as the Pranava is connected to meaning? Brahman, which is beyond mind and speech, is the Lord of Ganas; others who think otherwise are born of delusion.

Know, O best of sages, that those who worship the Lord of Obstacles in this way, devoid of the Pranava, are those in the Brahma Purana. Those who worship the Lord of Ganesha in the Ganesha Purana know the secret of Ganesha's meaning and are filled with faith. The Pranava is the form of Ganesha, and He is remembered as Saguna. The One devoid of Omkara, residing in the intellect, is remembered as Nirguna. The Pranava residing in the intellect becomes the giver of Brahman. For the sake of their joy, He became embodied. The 'Ga' is the Pranava, and the 'Na' is the mover of intellect; the Lord of both is Ganesha, marked by the elephant face.

By the nature of Gaanapatya, they worship Him constantly. With single-minded devotion, they become detached. Then they worship the One who is full in the Mudgala with devotion; He is Gajanana, the indicator of the three, in the union of Yoga. This Ganesha, the form of Yoga, became the holder of three forms for the sake of work, without doubt. The Pranava is the form of the world, and the consciousness is the form of the intellect; He is the soul of both, marked by the elephant face. Knowing this, they worship Ganesha who resides in the three. Those who know the Mudgala, O Brahmin, are always yogis and in the form of Yoga.

I will tell you the five-fold worship, by which you, O King, will become wise. One should worship Ganesha in the image through methods like puja, mantras, and hymns, which provide comfort to the embodied. One should worship the Pranava, the form of the world, as the Lord who is the life-force, residing everywhere, doing good to all. That best of men should not do anything inauspicious to anyone. By this method, he should please the Lord of Ganas. Observing the dualistic nature, one should please the Lord of Obstacles, who is fond of devotion, like a witness, devoid of feelings.

The wise man, leaving behind the delusion of dharma and adharma, and abandoning the sense of intellect, should always be detached. "I am not the doer, nor the one who makes others do; I am not in the body, nor in the intellect; I am the pure Brahman, full of joy." This is called devotion devoid of attachment. By this, the Lord of Ganas is pleased, and the soul becomes pure. "I am not different from Ganesha; the body functions." The one who is devoted to Ganesha and resides in the Mudgala worships the Supreme. Becoming Ganesha himself, one should worship Gajanana, who resides in the three, through three paths, with peace and devotion.

The one who is a yogi and knows the Mudgala, being of great fame, should worship Ganesha with three feelings. Thus, O giver of honor, perform the worship of the Lord of Ganas. By this, you will be worshipped by the kings of yogis and become a Gaanapatya. First, by serving the embodied ones like Shambhu, then the ones in the form of life (Pranava), then the ones in the intellect, one attains the highest devotion. He then serves Ganesha, who is in the body, with single-mindedness. Then, knowing the Mudgala, the yogi who is devoted to Yoga worships Ganesha, who resides in the three, and becomes dear to the Ganas. This is all the worship of Ganesha told to you, the three-fold form, and the three paths, O sage.

Shaunaka said: Eighteen Puranas have been told, and other Upapuranas by the wise Vyasa. Just as there are Vedas and Upavedas, the Brahmins do not consider them equal to the Vedas. Similarly, among the Upapuranas, the Gaaneshwara and the Mudgala are mentioned; how can they be superior?

Suta said: Just as Indra is called the Lord of Gods everywhere, and Upendra (Vishnu) is called the first, so is Vinayaka. Vishnu and Vinayaka are considered greater than Indra. Similarly, the Upapuranas grant union with Brahman. After hearing the Puranas, one becomes a fit vessel for the Upapuranas. Know this everywhere regarding the Puranas; it is not like the Veda and Upaveda regarding the determination of Brahman. For the sake of deluding people and revealing the state of illusion, the form of Ganesha is described as very secret. Therefore, the devotion to the Lord of Ganas is very rare and is attained by yogis who are rich in the power of complete merit. Through the Mudgala, the form of Ganesha is known by people as complete; there is no doubt, otherwise, there will be delusion.

Chapter 19 : Description of the Rules for Listening to the Purana

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: A person should remain pure, devoted to their own duty, eat only once a day, observe celibacy, and sleep on the ground at night. Having abandoned falsehood and other such vices, one should be composed and, after eating food offered in sacrifice, listen to this excellent Purana. Whether in the presence of Ganesha, in a forest, or at one's own home, one should listen to the complete Mudgala Purana with devotion to Yoga. Whether a person has worldly desires or is desireless, if they listen while observing these rules, they will immediately attain the fruit.

One should go to where the narrator is or invite a learned Brahmin to listen to this excellent Purana. A Brahmin, Kshatriya, or Vaishya may listen to it; if a Shudra wishes to listen, they should do so with focus, keeping a Brahmin as the presiding authority. Starting from the first day of the bright fortnight of the month of Bhadrapada, one should listen to the complete recitation of the Mudgala Purana for four days while fasting. Similarly, in the months of Magha and Jyeshtha, the recitation should be heard for the attainment of all success. Alternatively, one may begin on the first day of Bhadrapada and conclude the Purana on the full moon of Ashwina. In the same manner, in Magha and Jyeshtha, one should listen to the complete Mudgala Purana for a month and a half with full devotion.

Alternatively, one may begin on the first day of Bhadrapada and listen to the Mudgala Purana for an entire year. Upon reaching the next Bhadrapada, one should eat only once a day, and from the first day to the fourth, one should remain without food. On the fifth day, one should conclude the complete Mudgala Purana. By listening in this way, one immediately attains their desired fruits.

I shall now describe the procedure: one should have a pavilion constructed, decorated with arches and various ornaments. First, one should worship Lord Ganesha, then the book, and then the learned narrator who is an expert in Yoga. When the narrator reads the Mudgala Purana, one should worship the peaceful narrator with devotion. Then, honoring the audience, one should request them with affection to listen to this excellent and meritorious Purana. A person who listens to the entire Mudgala Purana with its meaning becomes skilled in all matters and attains all success.

When it is heard over four days, it is called a Parayana (complete recitation), which grants all success, even if the deep meanings are not elaborated upon. One should remain festive and devoted to righteousness, and by engaging in various acts of charity, one should feed Brahmins and others. A wise person should continuously perform all these acts for the pleasure of Ganesha, considering themselves blessed while listening to this excellent text.

At the end, one should worship Lord Ganesha and the Purana with royal offerings and deep devotion. Then, honoring the narrator, one should satisfy him with wealth, gems, and other gifts, and with a bowed head, pray: "O Lord, I have been liberated by you, the narrator of the Purana. By the sight of your feet, my purpose is fulfilled." Having prayed and bowed, one should satisfy all those who have gathered with offerings and then bid them farewell. The noble person should then invite the narrator again, along with other listeners and Brahmins, and feed them sweets and other food. After bowing to them and concluding the observance, one attains their desires and, at the end of life, becomes one with Brahman.

Alternatively, O Brahmin, one may listen to the Mudgala Purana daily with reverence, according to one's own convenience and diet. Even without strict celibacy, if one listens with devotion and joy for the pleasure of Ganesha, one attains the highest success. Even a destitute person, if they listen with devotion and perform worship according to their capacity, attains the fruit of all rituals and charity. Whether one worships with Shami leaves, Mandara flowers, or Durva grass, one receives the full fruit of worship and charity. One should perform according to their own means, and by that, the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) becomes pleased and grants everything.

He who listens to even half a verse, a quarter of a verse, or even a single word of the Mudgala Purana is blessed; there is no doubt about this. What then to say of one who listens to the entire Purana with faith? Their birth becomes fruitful. One who listens daily with devotion attains everything, and by their very sight, they become a savior to all beings. They are no different from Lord Ganesha himself; there is no doubt about this. Such a wise person dwells on this earth as one who has attained the state of Brahman. I have thus told you the entire procedure for listening to and reading the excellent Mudgala Purana.

Thus ends the nineteenth chapter of the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which describes the rules for listening to the Purana.

Chapter 20 : Glory and Praise of the Purana

Shaunaka said: O Suta, please tell me briefly about those who attained perfection after hearing the complete Mudgala Purana and describe their characters to me.

Suta said: First, the highly intelligent Daksha heard it from the great yogi Mudgala. Having heard it, he became free from all faults and established peace within himself. Thereafter, Daksha made a vow to Lord Ganesha to recite the Mudgala Purana daily without interruption. He did not set a strict rule regarding the number of chapters to be read daily, but recited it with devotion whenever he found time. Beginning his fast on the first day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, he completed the full recitation on the fourth day. In the same way, the wise Daksha performed the annual recitation in the months of Magha and Jyeshtha, accompanied by gods and sages. Thus, following this sequence with devotion, he remains a devotee of Gajanana and continues to study the Mudgala Purana even today, embodying the nature of a true follower of Ganesha.

Instructed by him, the great sages like Vashistha and the Prajapatis also began to study the Mudgala Purana with great reverence. They, in turn, taught other sages who were devoted to the path of yoga, and these sages, with various states of mind, also began to study this excellent scripture. Thus, this tradition was passed down, and all living beings, with various dispositions, came to know and study the supreme Mudgala Purana.

When the gods, including Shiva and Vishnu, arrived at the sacrifice of Daksha, they learned that the complete Mudgala Purana is the giver of yoga and all perfections. After concluding the sacrifice, they all returned to their respective abodes with joy. Thereafter, Shiva requested the great yogi Mudgala to recite the Purana. Mudgala, having bowed and worshipped him with devotion, was pleased and went to Shiva's abode to recite it. Upon learning of the glory of this Purana, Vishnu and other gods went to Mount Kailasa to hear it. There, along with Shiva, Brahma and the other gods listened to the complete Mudgala Purana as it was narrated by the ocean of yoga, Mudgala. After listening for a year, Shiva and the others worshipped him, and feeling fulfilled, they returned to their homes with their consorts.

A portion of Ganesha, the son of Shiva, also resided there, and he, being dear to Ganesha, recited the Mudgala Purana daily. He worshipped the Lord of Ganesha in his image form, and remaining in his presence, he recited this excellent scripture. Lord Vishwanatha (Shiva) himself, residing in Kashi, daily recited this in the presence of Dhundhiraja (Ganesha) while strictly following his vows. He would begin his fast on the first day of the bright fortnight of Magha and complete the recitation on the fourth day. Similarly, in the month of Bhadrapada, Mudgala, accompanied by Shuka, joyfully performed the recitation in the presence of Mayuresha. He recited it daily with deep feeling, joined by other followers of Ganesha; he is the great devotee, the savior of all, and the embodiment of the savior.

In the month of Jyeshtha, Shesha himself performed the recitation in the presence of the Lord who rides the mouse, accompanied by the Nagas and Asuras. He recited it daily with all his heart, being a great devotee and a benefactor to all. Likewise, Dattatreya himself, for the satisfaction of Jnyanesha, completed the recitation in the month of Jyeshtha with great devotion. He remained dedicated to the daily recitation of this excellent scripture, accompanied by various yogis and Lord Shankara.

Vishnu, the Sun, Brahma, Shakti, Indra, Agni, and other gods daily study this supreme Mudgala Purana in their own homes. Sages like Kashyapa, other great seers, and those who have attained perfection, such as Kapila, study it daily with devotion. Sanaka and the other nine yogis also joyfully study the complete Mudgala Purana daily with great dedication. Narada, singing with his music, wandered through the worlds, chanting this Mudgala Purana, which bestows yoga and peace.

Thus, various people, Manus, and great sages study this scripture daily, as it grants all perfections. Vyasa himself, the highly fortunate one, studies the Ganesha Mudgala daily with devotion. O best of sages, it is not possible for me to describe everything; those who are fortunate and wise always study the Mudgala Purana.

Thus ends the twentieth chapter of the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which describes the glory and praise of the Purana, in the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala.

Chapter 21 : Description of the Recitation

Suta said: Now I shall briefly describe the fruits of listening to the Purana for those who are full of desires, as it grants all accomplishments. There lived a Brahmin of the Atri lineage who was well-versed in all knowledge, but he abandoned his prescribed duties and became addicted to sinful acts. His parents, Sushila and Sumukha, were filled with grief upon seeing their son, Sumati, behaving in such a wicked manner. Despite their constant efforts to instruct him, the wicked son never listened to their words.

Once, while they were living in their hermitage, filled with sorrow, the great sage Bharadwaja arrived there. After worshipping the great sage, the grieving parents asked him for a way to make their son virtuous and happy. Moved by compassion, the wise sage told them that their son would become virtuous after listening to the Mudgala Purana. Having said this, the great sage Bharadwaja departed. Sumukha, along with his wife and son, went to the sage Atri. After bowing to that great soul, the king of yogis who bestows yoga, Sumukha asked him where the Mudgala Purana could be heard. Hearing his request, the great sage Atri, pleased, spoke words of benefit to the Brahmin.

Atri said: Do not worry, my son; stay in my hermitage. I will perform the recitation of the Purana in the month of Bhadrapada with devotion. You should listen to the Mudgala Purana there and then return to your own hermitage. Sumukha agreed with joy, bowed to him, and did as instructed. After a short time, the month of Bhadrapada arrived, and many sages gathered there to listen. Seeing the Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, and Shudras present, Sumukha listened to the recitation with proper rituals and devotion. By observing a fast along with the Brahmins on the fifth day, he performed the concluding rites with a heart full of devotion.

Consequently, his son Sumati became virtuous, righteous, and established in good conduct. Seeing this miracle, Sumukha, along with his wife and son, became devoted to the worship of Ganesha and became revered even by yogis. In the end, the Brahmin attained the Lord of Ganas and became one with Brahman. O Brahmin, I have told you this; now listen to another wonderful story. During that same recitation, an ox, suffering greatly, stood at a distance due to its weakness but listened to a little of the recitation with devotion. When it died, the attendants of Ganesha arrived to take it. Seeing them, everyone was astonished and asked them with reverence.

The Brahmins asked: By what merit are you, O immortals, taking this ox? Tell us, O best of Ganesha’s attendants, this wonderful mystery. The attendants of Ganesha replied: This ox was lying near you, suffering, and it listened to the Mudgala Purana, O great sages. It died in the midst of its suffering, and now we are taking it to become one with Brahman; there is no doubt about this. If one dies while listening to the words of the Mudgala, there is no doubt that they will not be reborn. Hearing their words, the sages were filled with joy and asked them another question, full of curiosity.

The Brahmins asked: This being is in the form of an ox, and we are born in human bodies. Everything we say is not understood by the ox. What is said by an ox is not understood by humans due to the difference in their species. How can this ox, being in the form of an ox, listen to the Purana and enjoy the fruits of its greatness? The attendants of Ganesha replied: The listening done by the ox was devoid of knowledge, yet it was certain, even without faith, like the nature of fire to burn. Just as a spark of fire burns regardless of intent, the Mudgala Purana, when heard, acts in the same way. The Mudgala is full of the nectar of yoga, universally accepted, grants all accomplishments, and leads to the state of Brahman.

Having said this, all the attendants of Ganesha departed for the abode of Ganesha, taking the ox with them, while the Brahmins spoke in astonishment. Thus, O best of sages, is the wonderful greatness of the Mudgala; who is capable of describing the brilliance born of its influence?

Thus ends the twenty-first chapter of the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "Description of the Recitation."

Chapter 22 : Story of the Magha Parayana

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Suta said: There was a Kshatriya named Madhava who lived in Malwa. He was the ruler of the city of Avanti and was always devoted to his own duty. A king named Kamboja Karna came there to wage war. Having defeated the king, he happily ruled over the kingdom of Avanti. The king, along with his wife, sons, ministers, and brave warriors, wandered in the forest here and there, feeling very miserable. Then, the brave king saw a great forest filled with tigers and various animals, and he entered it in great distress. Seeing the great sage Kashyapa, who grants all desires, he bowed down with his whole body and wept in his helplessness. Seeing him filled with such sorrow, Kashyapa asked him the reason, and the king, overwhelmed with grief, narrated his story as it had happened.

Then Kashyapa said to him, "O King, do not worry. Listen to what you must do to regain your kingdom. The month of Magha is approaching; listen to this. I am performing the complete Parayana (recitation) of the Mudgala Purana, which grants success." Hearing these words, Madhava was filled with joy and stayed there with everyone to listen to the Purana. Seeing the groups of sages and Kshatriyas who had gathered to listen, he became very happy. On the first day of the bright fortnight, the great sage, having performed the worship of Vinayaka and observing a fast along with the women, began the Parayana of the Mudgala Purana in the pavilion, according to the prescribed rites, with devotion and being a great yogi who knew all truths.

O great sage, listen to the miracle that happened there. A mongoose living on the ground heard it, filled with fear. Suddenly, that mongoose, filled with sorrow, died. Seeing the attendants of Ganesha arrive, the sage Kashyapa asked them. Kashyapa said, "O masters, why have you come? Tell me your noble purpose. We are servants of Ganesha and will perform your task with zeal." Hearing the words of Kashyapa, the beloved attendants of Ganesha said, "We have come to take the mongoose that was lying on the ground and has now died. O best of Brahmins, the mongoose had heard the complete chapter of the Mudgala Parayana, though unknowingly. When the second chapter began, it suffered greatly from a fever and has now died. We are going to make it attain the state of Brahman. Know, O best of sages, that by the power of hearing the Mudgala Purana, which grants success, it has attained this state."

Hearing their words, Kashyapa, along with everyone else, was astonished and, after bowing to them, continued with the Parayana. The attendants of Ganesha took the mongoose and went to their city, Svananda. Seeing the Lord of Ganas there, it attained the state of Brahman. The great-minded yogi completed the Parayana in four days and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles with great effort. Then, on the fifth day, he performed the concluding ceremony with everyone. On the sixth day, the Brahmins and others went to their respective places, O great sage.

Madhava, having bowed to him first, surrounded by his ministers and others, departed and arrived in Maharashtra. There, his friend, the powerful King Bhadraka, was ruling his own country. Together with him, Madhava, bearing weapons and accompanied by an army, became enraged and marched for battle. Remembering Ganesha in his mind and his supreme guru Kashyapa, he defeated the King of Kamboj, Karna, after a fierce battle. Karna, filled with fear, was defeated, and Madhava regained his kingdom and lived with his friends. The king enjoyed various pleasures with his sons and grandsons, worshipped Ganesha daily, and listened to the Mudgala Purana. In the end, he went to the Lord of Obstacles and attained the state of Brahman through the greatness of listening to the Parayana, that best of men.

Thus, O Brahmin, many people have attained success; it is impossible for anyone to describe how much can be achieved by listening to the Mudgala Purana.

Thus ends the twenty-second chapter named "The Description of the Magha Parayana" in the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 23 : Description of the Glory of the Parayana (Recitation) in the Month of Jyeshtha

Suta said: There was a Brahmin named Sumati who suffered from leprosy. Born into the Galava lineage, he endured intense agony. His entire body was consumed by worms, he was always foul-smelling, and he was covered in pus, blood, and sweat, O Brahmin. Desperate, he decided to end his life and, with devotion, remembered Lord Ganesha in his heart to remove his obstacles.

At that moment, the excellent Brahmin Dhaumya arrived there, a knower of all truths, appearing like another fire incarnate. Seeing him, Sumati bowed down and wept in his misery. Observing Sumati, Dhaumya spoke words that brought comfort. Dhaumya said: Do not worry, and do not destroy your body, O wise one. Listen to the Mudgala Purana with devotion, and you will be free from disease. It grants all accomplishments, is complete, and bestows the state of Brahman. By merely listening to the Mudgala Purana, you will be healed. Come quickly with me.

Having said this, the great sage took him, and Dhaumya went to Pulaha, surrounded by many sages. There, the month of Jyeshtha arrived, which grants the fulfillment of all desires. In a short time, the lord of yogis, Pulaha, became joyful. On the first day of the bright fortnight, the wise one began the recitation of the Mudgala Purana, joined by many people. They were all engaged in fasting, O great sage, and were devoted to listening, as was Pulaha. Sumati, accompanied by Dhaumya, also became wise; meditating on Ganesha in his heart, he listened to the Mudgala Purana intently.

In four days, he completed it. On the fifth day, devoted to Ganesha, everyone performed the concluding rites. Sumati was instantly freed from leprosy and filled with joy. Seeing this, the others were amazed and joyful. Dhaumya bowed to Pulaha and returned to his ashram. There, after worshipping Dhaumya, Sumati spoke: O great sage, you are the creator of this body, my father and mother. There is no doubt; tell me what you wish for me to do. My body is now forever under your command, O Brahmin. Instruct me, and I will always do as you say.

Dhaumya said: Worship Ganesha with devotion, along with the Mudgala Purana. By doing so, you will make me feel fulfilled; there is no doubt. Having said this, the great yogi Dhaumya went to his ashram. Sumati always worshipped the Lord of Ganas along with the Mudgala Purana. At the end of his life, having enjoyed all desired pleasures, he attained the state of Brahman. Such is the supreme fruit of the recitation in the month of Jyeshtha.

Now hear another story that occurred there, O knower of Dharma. Pulaha, filled with joy, performed the supreme recitation. A Chandala happened to arrive there by chance. Standing at a distance, he watched this great spectacle. From afar, he heard one excellent verse, fully and with devotion, dedicated to the Protector of devotees. Then, filled with wonder, he went on his way. Being a sinner, he continued to commit various sins, O sage. When he died, the messengers of Yama took him and, in haste, went to the Lord, bowed down, and presented the sinner.

There, the wise Chitragupta said to Yama: This Chandala is a great sinner and a wicked person. He has never performed even a trace of merit, O Lord. However, he did hear one verse of the Mudgala Purana. I have told you all of his actions. Do what is righteous and determine his punishment, O wise one. Hearing the words of Chitragupta, Dharma meditated on Gajanana and pondered in his heart what to do that would be auspicious.

At that moment, the Truthful Word spoke to him: Show the hells to the Chandala, O wise one, and after rebuking him, place him in the heavenly realms so he may be happy. The powerful Dharmaraja did exactly that with joy. Having placed him in his own abode with all enjoyments, the son of the Sun stood there, and a truly wonderful miracle occurred. The attendants of Ganesha took him and went to their own city of Svananda. Dharma and the others were amazed.

Thus, the fruit of hearing even one verse of the Mudgala Purana is such that I cannot fully describe it. In this way, people who hear the recitation in the month of Jyeshtha enjoy all pleasures here and, in the end, attain Svananda. It is impossible for me to describe it fully, O Brahmin, even in ten thousand years; I have told you only a fraction of it.

Thus ends the twenty-third chapter of the Mudgala Purana, titled "Description of the Glory of the Parayana in the Month of Jyeshtha."

Chapter 24 : "The Victory Over Doubt" in the Dialogue Between Daksha and Mudgala

Shaunaka said: O Suta, you speak of a wonder that I have never heard of anywhere; a man became one with Brahman just by hearing a single verse. Alas, a Chandala of sinful conduct became established in Brahman upon hearing a verse from the Mudgala Purana. Therefore, O ocean of compassion, tell me, what is the nature of the complete greatness of the Mudgala Purana, which destroys all doubts?

Suta said: Having taken the Chandala, the Ganesha-attendants departed for their city. Seeing this, Yama, filled with astonishment, went to Brahma. Bowing to the Creator, he spoke excellent words, and being full of humility, he was honored by Him. Dharma said: I see the greatness of the Mudgala as something wonderful; I have never seen or heard of anything like it, O Lord, which grants all accomplishments. Who is this Chandala of sinful deeds who, having heard just one verse, became established in Brahman? What is this curiosity, O Creator? In this way, O Brahmin, all people are filled with ignorance; those endowed with knowledge will attain their own bliss in various forms. If the entire mobile and immobile world hears this, it will immediately become diminished (in its worldly attachment). Therefore, strive to protect this world, which grants happiness. What is the nature of the greatness of this Mudgala that you have created? I do not understand it, O Lord; tell me its boundless essence.

Hearing the words of Dharma, the Grandfather sighed and said: Listen, O best of Brahmins, to these words that protect the gods. Brahma said: The supreme Mudgala was composed by Ganesha for the sake of His devotees. When prayed to with devotion, it grants all accomplishments. Who knows the essence of its greatness, O son of the Sun (Yama)? I do not know how to describe the Mudgala. Having said this, Brahma himself remembered the chief gods. The best of Brahmins arrived and asked for the supreme reason. He narrated to them in detail what had happened, the account of the Mudgala as told by Dharma. Hearing this, they became filled with sorrow, O best of sages. Then Vishnu, having considered what was beneficial, said to them: O Creator, place the Mudgala on a scale to see what it equals; in this way, everything will be beneficial. Hearing this, He took various spiritual practices and placed them on one side, and the supreme Mudgala on the other. All those practices were not equal to the Mudgala, O fortunate one, because of its singular focus.

Then, all of them, filled with sadness, deliberated with respect: "Everything has been conquered by the Mudgala; what shall we do now?" By merely serving it, beings of various kinds will enjoy pleasures and then attain the state of Brahman, just like the yogis. In a short time, the entire world will be destroyed, and we will be left without any purpose. What obstacle should be placed in the way of the Mudgala so that humans, abandoning the Purana, will become distracted? Then Shambhu himself said: "Whatever is destined will happen. Do not create a great obstacle for the Mudgala, O wise ones. If you create an obstacle for this, the elephant-faced Lord, filled with anger, will burn the entire world along with its gods; there is no doubt about this."

Hearing the words of Shiva, the sages and gods became fearful. Then a voice from the sky spoke to them, remembering the Lord of the Ganas. Brihaspati said: "There is no doubt that this supreme Mudgala, which contains the world in its various natures, was created by Ganesha for Shiva and others. The words of the Lord of the Ganas exist within the Mudgala; it shines continuously until the final dissolution of the world. Moreover, how can there ever be an obstacle to the Mudgala? O best of sages, that word cannot be false. Therefore, pray to the Lord of the Ganas and create an obstacle; Ganesha will not be agitated by this."

Hearing the words of Brihaspati, the gods and sages were filled with joy and praised him, saying, "Well done, well done." Then they praised the Lord of Obstacles with folded hands and, through various hymns, respectfully pleased the Lord of the Ganas. The Lord Himself, assuming the form of a voice from the sky, said to them: "By creating 'Vikalpa' (doubt), O lords of the gods, you will become happy." Then Brahma, remembering Ganesha, immediately created Vikalpa—a form of doubt, filled with great intensity and malice. Seeing him, the chief gods, Brahmins, and others were immediately filled with doubt. "Will the Lord of Obstacles be pleased by the Mudgala? How did Ganesha, the Lord of Brahma, take on a physical body?"

"Everything in the Vedas and other scriptures was said for the sake of taste; there is no doubt. Brahman is established in Brahman; that is especially worthy of respect by all." Having said this, they all went to their respective places, O great sage, deluded by the great wonder of that Vikalpa (doubt). They did not remember why they had come or what they had done; they became confused, filled with doubt. Then, they all abandoned the Mudgala and, being of various minds, returned to their own activities, O Brahmin. Seeing them, Vikalpa became joyful and spread throughout the three worlds, causing various doubts. Because of him, all people abandoned the Mudgala, O Brahmin. In this way, that Purana gradually became lost. Other Puranas and all the chief Vedas were also lost, or existed only in a diminished state.

Then the entire world, mobile and immobile, became devoid of action. All the gods, filled with sorrow, went to fight Vikalpa. They were all defeated by Vikalpa and lost their strength. Then they praised the elephant-faced Lord for his destruction. Again, the elephant-faced Lord spoke from the sky: "Become subject to the command of Mudgala and perform your duties without delay. Vikalpa will be under your control; there is no doubt." Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas became silent, O Shaunaka. Then the gods, led by Shiva, Vishnu, and Kashyapa, filled with joy, went to Mudgala, the best of yogis. Bowing with devotion, they asked with folded hands for the complete method to conquer doubt, which would grant them their own welfare. Hearing their words, the yogi Mudgala smiled, honored them, and spoke to them, who were still bound by illusion.

Thus ends the twenty-fourth chapter of the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Victory Over Doubt."

Chapter 25 : The Destruction of Vikalpa’s Glory

Mudgala said: O highly fortunate ones, having first remembered the Lord of the Ganas and having created the powerful Vikalpa, why do you not desire him again? I shall do what is dear to you who have sought my refuge; stay here in my presence, what will Vikalpa do? The great sages, Kashyapa and others, and Shiva and others, having been reassured by Mudgala, stayed there and became filled with happiness. O highly fortunate one, Vikalpa could not enter the ashram of Mudgala; therefore, they remained free from doubt. Vikalpa came to know of the events concerning the divine sages and, filled with anger, came himself to Mudgala’s ashram to kill them. Seeing him arrive, the terrifying Vikalpa, the Indras and the gods, filled with fear, spoke to Mudgala there.

The Indras said: O wise one, Vikalpa has arrived filled with anger; do now in your ashram whatever you intend to do. Hearing their words, Mudgala said to them: Do not worry, O wise ones, I shall destroy Vikalpa. Having said this, the yogi Mudgala himself approached Vikalpa, who was full of radiance and the cause of all doubts. The great yogi Mudgala, the knower of the ultimate truth, being naturally compassionate and inspired by the Lord of the Ganas, spoke to him.

Mudgala said: Tell me now, Vikalpa, why have you come? Go where you wish, otherwise you will be reduced to ashes. Hearing the words of Mudgala, Vikalpa, filled with anger, spoke to the great sage, as if burning him with his radiance.

Vikalpa said: Seeing me, Vishnu, Shambhu, and others, and the great sages like Vashistha, became filled with doubt in that very moment, O sage. I do not know you, O king of yogis, where do you reside, O Brahmin? You speak like a fool, calling me a conqueror of all. Indeed, I have brought all the moving and non-moving world under my control. Now I shall kill you, who are the cause of hatred towards me. Tell me, why have you made the gods, led by Indras, and the sages devoid of doubt? You have come to Mudgala’s ashram to destroy my pride; know this and leave everything, otherwise you will be reduced to ashes.

Hearing the words of Vikalpa, the great sage, filled with anger, remembered the Lord of the Ganas in his heart for the destruction of Vikalpa. By the mere remembrance of him, a fire of dissolution emerged from the heart of the yogi Mudgala, burning everything. The fire, filled with great anger, went towards the cruel Vikalpa and, by its mere sight, caused him to burn. Then, O Shaunaka, he made great efforts for peace, and Vikalpa became filled with intense heat. Being scorched and filled with sorrow, Vikalpa abandoned his pride and bowed before the great Brahmin Mudgala.

He said, trembling with fear: Protect me, O best of sages, I am being scorched by your radiance, O lover of those who seek refuge. O yogi, there is no doubt that I have easily conquered all, including Shiva, Vishnu, and the Brahmins led by Vashistha. Are you the Lord Ganesha himself who has come? There is no doubt. I am unable to conquer you, therefore I have sought your refuge. Protect me, O ocean of mercy, I am especially scorched; forgive me for the offense I have committed against you, as I have been destroyed by your radiance.

Seeing Vikalpa distressed, Mudgala became compassionate and extinguished the fire born from the Lord of the Ganas. Then, regaining his senses, Vikalpa, with a calm mind, bowed first to Mudgala, the best of yogis, and spoke to him.

Vikalpa said: I have been conquered by you, O Brahmin, and protected from the fear of fire. Now I shall remain under your control, staying near you. Give me a place, O highly fortunate one, and I shall stay there motionless; becoming subject to your command, I am your servant.

Hearing his words, he became absorbed in meditation. Knowing it to be the work of the Lord of Obstacles, he spoke to him with joy.

Mudgala said: The base humans do not know Ganesha in his yogic form; those who consider him to be like a mortal being, born of qualities, you should always stay in their hearts and outside, filling them with various doubts. By my command, make those who are hostile to the Lord of the Ganas, and who are full of doubt, reside in the hearts of the yogis. Those who do not honor Ganesha in his yogic form, whether they are yogis or non-yogis, should always be abandoned by you. Those who are filled with devotion to the Mudgala-form will always be happy; know by my word that they are the true yogis of Ganesha and no others.

Having said this, the great yogi Mudgala became silent. Vikalpa said "so be it," bowed to him, and went to his own place. Then the gods and sages, filled with joy, bowed to him and went to their own places, and the people engaged in their duties shone. From that day on, the Mudgala-form is not served by living beings, especially those who are full of faults and accompanied by Vikalpa. In the Kali age, due to intense ignorance, the Mudgala-form will be destroyed, and faulty people will be found in every house. Yogis always serve the Mudgala-form with devotion, being clever and purifying all by the knowledge of the form of Ganesha. No other hypocrites, greedy people, or those wearing various disguises on earth, who are filled with delusion, will be able to attain the Mudgala-form. Thus, the obstacle created by the gods, inspired by the Lord of the Ganas, caused men to be born in the Mudgala-form. I have told you all this most wonderful secret; whoever hears it or causes it to be heard shall become free from doubt.

Chapter 26 : The Glory of Listening to the Mudgala Purana

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: There was a Vaishya named Bhima, who was devoid of all righteousness and a great sinner in the land of Anga, whose very sight destroyed one's merit. Once, he was hiding in the forest to commit theft. At that time, the King of Vidarbha, a royal sage, arrived there with his army. Seeing him, the sinful Bhima dropped his weapons and fled deep into the forest, fearing for his life. There, on the first day of the bright fortnight of the month of Magha, a Brahmana named Medhatithi, accompanied by other sages, began to recite the Mudgala Purana. Seeing them, the sinful Vaishya approached out of fear, having discarded his weapons. The King of Vidarbha arrived there, bowed to the eminent sage Medhatithi and all the other sages with devotion. After bowing again, the King, who was like the sun, returned to his place. The Vaishya, feeling humble, slept in that forest of sages.

In the morning, the Brahmanas, having bathed and performed their daily rituals, arrived with joy, eager to listen to the Mudgala Purana. Along with them, Bhima the Vaishya also came, fearing the King. He listened to the Mudgala Purana there and was filled with wonder. By listening to the Mudgala Purana, the wicked man's intellect was transformed, and he remained there. The sinful Bhima thought in his mind, "Alas, I am a wicked Vaishya; what have I been doing? Sinners surely go to hell, there is no doubt about this. Having attained a human body, a person should perform righteous deeds." Thinking thus, he remained there, devoted to listening to the Mudgala Purana. Abandoning his attachment to his wife, children, and others, he became devoted to Ganesha.

On the full moon day of Phalguna, the recitation of the Purana was completed. Medhatithi, filled with joy, fed the Brahmanas on the first day of the next month. After performing the concluding rites and dismissing everyone, he remained devoted to Ganesha, worshipping the Lord of the Ganas daily with devotion. Bhima returned to his home, abandoned his sinful nature, performed his own righteous duties, and became devoted to Ganesha. By merely listening to the Mudgala Purana, he was cleansed of all sins, became righteous, and attained great fame as a devotee of Ganesha. At the end of his life, the Vaishya attained the supreme abode of Ganesha. Having beheld the Lord of Obstacles, he became one with the Brahman. O Sage, many other people who constantly listened to the Mudgala Purana also became righteous and absorbed in the Brahman. How many can I tell you? No one is capable of describing it. Know that there is nothing equal to this.

Thus ends the twenty-sixth chapter of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Glory of Listening to the Mudgala Purana," during the discourse between Daksha and Mudgala.

Chapter 27 : The Description of the Glory of Listening to the Mudgala Purana for One and a Half Months Starting from Bhadrapada.

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: The great yogi Pulastya, devoted to Ganesha, began the recitation of the Mudgala Purana in the month of Bhadrapada, starting from the first day (Pratipat) after worshipping Ganesha. O Brahmana, people including sages gathered there to listen, and all of them listened with devotion according to the prescribed rites. The great sage, well-versed in the meaning of Ganesha, narrated the Mudgala Purana with great joy, always explaining its profound significance.

Once, a snake living in a hole nearby heard the recitation, driven by destiny. One night, when the sages were asleep, the snake came out and moved along the path at its own will. A mongoose living in the forest saw the snake and, filled with rage, attacked it to kill it. A fierce battle ensued between them. The powerful mongoose killed the snake in the forest. The devotees of Ganesha immediately took the dead snake and transformed it into a divine being (Brahma-bhuta).

Seeing this, the gods, including Indra and the great sages, were astonished. With tearful eyes, they said to one another: "Alas, the glory of the Mudgala Purana cannot be described by anyone. This snake, having heard only a little of it out of ignorance, has now become one with the Divine. If those who listen to the entire Mudgala Purana attain such a state, then what more can be said? It is beyond comprehension. Those who have heard the entire Mudgala Purana should be known as the lords of the Ganas; they exist to purify the world.

If a person born as a human does not listen to the Mudgala Purana, he is completely deluded by Maya; his life is in vain, and shame upon his existence. O Lord of Obstacles, we bow to you; grant us birth on this earth and make us humans who are always devoted to the Mudgala Purana. Shame upon the heaven of the gods where there is no fruit of action; if the gods do not perform actions, their existence becomes futile. Alas, even if one is born in the form of an insect or a moth, if one is born in Bharatavarsha, one can become one with the Divine through the company of the righteous. This snake, devoid of human speech, did not understand anything, yet by hearing the Mudgala Purana, which is composed in human language, it became one with the Divine and attained the city of Svananda. What then can be said of a human being who attains the Elephant-faced Lord?"

Having said this, the gods returned to their respective abodes. Now I shall tell you the rest, which brings supreme happiness. The recitation concluded on the full moon day of Ashvina. The listeners, filled with joy, worshipped the Mudgala Purana. The great yogi performed the breaking of the fast (Parana) along with everyone, and on the first day of the next month, he bade farewell to all. By his command, they all returned to their respective hermitages, becoming fully devoted to the worship of Ganesha. Having enjoyed all worldly pleasures here, they finally attained Svananda, becoming one with the Divine after beholding the Lord of the Ganas.

Thus, I have told you all about the glory born from listening to the Mudgala Purana for one and a half months, starting from the month of Bhadrapada. In this way, countless beings attained the Divine; it is impossible to describe the greatness of listening to the Mudgala Purana.

Thus ends the twenty-seventh chapter of the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the essence of the Upanishads, dealing with the conduct of yoga and the nectar of yoga, in the dialogue between Mudgala and Daksha, titled "The Description of the Glory of Listening to the Mudgala Purana for One and a Half Months Starting from Bhadrapada."

Chapter 28 : The Description of the Glory of Mahodara’s Deeds

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: Long ago, there was a Brahmana born in the lineage of Garga, known by the name Shambhu, who was afflicted by the condition of being childless. He performed many remedies to remove the defect of infertility, yet he remained without a son or a daughter. Filled with great sorrow, he went with his wife to the great sage Gautama and bowed to him, desiring a son. The great sage Gautama welcomed Shambhu, who then stayed there, diligently serving his guru.

One day, while Gautama was sitting comfortably, Shambhu spoke to him with devotion, seeking advice for his own welfare regarding the attainment of a son. Shambhu said: O Master, I have performed many efforts with great care to obtain a son, yet I have not received any offspring. Tell me the reason for this. O wise one, tell me the method by which I may have a son, and I shall follow your command. You are my supreme guru; there is nothing beyond you. O fortunate one, please deliver me from this state of infertility.

Hearing his words, the all-knowing sage Gautama spoke to the sorrowful Shambhu. Gautama said: Do not worry in vain, O best of Brahmanas. You shall have three wise sons, who will be learned in the Vedas and dear to Ganesha. Listen from me to the Maudgala (Purana), which grants all accomplishments. By hearing it, the sins accumulated over infinite births will be destroyed instantly. You will become a reservoir of merit and a savior yourself; there is no doubt about this, and you will be endowed with all perfections. In the end, you will attain the Lord of the Ganas and become one with Brahman. Stay here; in the month of Jyeshtha, I will recite the Maudgala.

Hearing these words, Shambhu was filled with joy and stayed there with his virtuous wife. After a short time, the month of Jyeshtha arrived, and other sages also gathered there to listen. Seeing them, Shambhu and his wife were overjoyed. All the listeners prepared themselves according to the proper rites. On the first day of the bright fortnight of Jyeshtha, Gautama, the best of yogis, joyfully began the recitation of the Maudgala, which grants all accomplishments. The all-knowing sage explained the true meaning of the Maudgala, and hearing it, all were amazed and became absorbed in listening.

They listened daily with devotion and discipline. One day, O Brahmana, a great miracle occurred. A donkey, suffering greatly from a fever, arrived there by fate and stood at a distance. Though devoid of knowledge, the donkey heard a verse of the Maudgala and was instantly relieved of its pain. Filled with devotion, it stood there, longing to hear more. Then, the sage Gautama joyfully concluded the recitation. Everyone performed their daily duties and returned the next morning. The donkey, now free from suffering, wandered off at will and eventually died when its time came. At that moment, the attendants of Ganesha arrived joyfully to take it. Because of the merit of hearing the scripture, the donkey attained the state of Brahman. Seeing this, the gods and sages in heaven were filled with wonder.

O fortunate one, listen further. Gautama, filled with joy, concluded the recitation on the full moon day of Ashadha. The Brahmanas and others performed the breaking of their fast on the first day. Filled with joy, they bowed to him and returned to their respective hermitages. Shambhu, along with his virtuous wife, returned to his home and worshipped Ganesha daily, knowing him to be the embodiment of Yoga.

After a short time, Sushila became pregnant, and seeing this, Shambhu was filled with great wonder. At an auspicious moment, she gave birth to an excellent son, endowed with all auspicious marks. Eventually, they had three such sons, all learned in the Vedas, respected by all, and devoted to Ganesha. Seeing them, Shambhu was amazed and remembered his guru, Gautama. He remained devoted to the Maudgala with constant respect. In the end, he attained the Lord of Obstacles and became absorbed in Him. Many people attained the grace of Ganesha through the hearing and reading of the Maudgala; how many can I describe, O sage? Thus, I have told you the glory of hearing the Maudgala, which lasted for a month and a half, starting from the month of Jyeshtha.

Thus ends the twenty-eighth chapter of the Maudgala Mahapurana, which describes the glory of the deeds of Mahodara.

Chapter 29 : The Glory of Listening to the Mudgala Purana Annually

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: There was a king named Sugriva in the Pundra country, who was well-versed in all scriptures, righteous, and wise. A severe drought occurred in his kingdom, causing great suffering, as the clouds did not rain for a long time. The king became deeply distressed and performed rituals to propitiate Indra and Varuna, and he constantly pleased the Lord of the clouds through the Brahmins. However, due to past sins, the clouds did not rain for nine years, which left King Sugriva in great agony.

The king left his kingdom and went to Sage Vishwamitra, whom he bowed to and asked about the means to bring rain. The sage advised him to listen to the Mudgala Purana, assuring him that the rain would surely return to his land. The king returned to his city, had the Mudgala Purana written down, and listened to it with great joy along with his subjects. As soon as the recitation began, rain fell, and the king was amazed. All the citizens of Pundra were filled with joy, and the king began to worship Lord Ganesha with great devotion.

Starting from the first day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, he listened to this Purana, which grants all accomplishments, with great delight. He concluded the recitation on the fifth day of the bright fortnight. Overjoyed, the king began the recitation again on the first day of the bright fortnight of Shravana and concluded it on the new moon day of Ashadha. Thus, Sugriva, along with the noble citizens, began to observe this annual tradition of listening to the Purana. Every year, all the citizens listened to it with great devotion, and free from sorrow, they worshipped Lord Ganesha.

During the reign of King Sugriva, no one in the kingdom suffered from infertility, disease, or grief. The people were endowed with all kinds of wealth and were free from fear and ailments. Eventually, the king, along with all his citizens, attained the state of Brahman and reached the abode of Lord Ganesha, shining with supreme radiance. As they traveled to their divine destination, the wind that touched their bodies relieved those suffering in the Raurava hell from their torments. The attendants of Ganesha took them and transformed them into divine beings, leaving Yama and others amazed, who then praised the glory of the Mudgala Purana.

Thus, I have told you the glory of listening to the Mudgala Purana annually, which grants all success. Once, a man suffering from leprosy heard this wonderful story and went to that land to listen to the Purana. The king, a tiger among men, began the annual recitation with devotion, and the leper listened to it daily to be cured. Upon hearing it, he was freed from leprosy and, filled with joy, returned home to his duties. After enjoying worldly pleasures, he eventually attained the abode of the Elephant-faced Lord, becoming one with Brahman through the merit of listening to the Purana.

In this way, various people who listened to the Mudgala Purana even once enjoyed all pleasures here and attained supreme bliss in the end. How many such stories can I tell you? Even a hundred years would not be enough. Therefore, I have briefly described the fruits of listening to this Purana annually.

Thus ends the twenty-ninth chapter of the Mudgala Purana, which describes the glory of listening to the Mudgala Purana annually.

Chapter 30 : The Glory of Daily Listening to the Mudgala Purana

Suta said: In the Chakshusha Manvantara, there was a king named Sumedha. He was a righteous ruler, a performer of sacrifices, a generous donor, and a man of great strength and honor. Well-versed in the essence of all scriptures and devoted to the gods, Brahmins, and guests, he ruled over the seven continents with excellence. Despite being a virtuous king who cared for the welfare of all, he was deeply distressed because he was childless. He tried many ways to obtain a son, but failing to find joy, he became sorrowful.

Consulting with his wife, the king left his kingdom and, accompanied by his ministers, went into a dense forest, overwhelmed by grief. Distraught, Sumedha wandered aimlessly until he reached a terrifying part of the forest. Resolved to end his life, he entered deeper, where he saw wild beasts and tigers, but they did not harm him. As he wandered like a madman, he encountered the great sage Kratu, the best of yogis.

The king and his wife bowed before the sage with folded hands and praised him, the son of Brahma, who was radiant and divine. Pleased, the sage comforted the king and asked, "O King, why are you sad, and why have you come here?" The king narrated his story in detail. Hearing this, the sage entered into meditation. Then, to gladden the king, Kratu said, "O King, listen to the Mudgala Purana; you shall be blessed with a son."

The overjoyed king bowed to the sage and, following his instructions, returned to his city. He told his priest, Shandilya, a master of all scriptures, what had happened. Hearing the account, Shandilya was filled with joy and said, "I will bring the Mudgala Purana, which grants all accomplishments, and I will recite it for you, O King. Do not worry."

Having said this, Shandilya went to the sage Kratu, paid his respects, and learned the great Mudgala Purana. After writing it down, the wise Shandilya returned to King Sumedha and gave him this beneficial advice: "O King, there is nothing greater than the Mudgala Purana. It is a scripture revered by the greatest yogis and grants all desires. By the grace of Kratu, I have learned this supreme knowledge. Listen to the Mudgala Purana daily, O Great King."

The Brahmin then began the recitation with joy. Before starting, the king bowed and asked, "O Master of Brahmins, please identify an auspicious moment for me to hear this scripture that grants all success." Shandilya replied, "O King, this body is fleeting and cannot be trusted. Remember Lord Ganesha, the remover of obstacles, and that time will be auspicious. Whether you look for an auspicious moment or simply remember Ganesha, you may begin without doubt."

Following this advice, the king, along with his wife, listened to the Mudgala Purana daily from Shandilya with devotion. After a short time, the king saw that his wife was pregnant and was filled with joy. The virtuous queen gave birth to a son endowed with all auspicious signs. The king continued to listen to the Mudgala Purana daily with great delight. He became single-mindedly devoted to it, and at the end of his life, he went to the realm of Ganesha. Upon seeing Lord Ganesha, he attained the state of Brahman.

Sumedha, along with his people, attained this through the hearing of the Purana. Shandilya also went to the forest and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles, reciting the Mudgala Purana before Ganesha. Always filled with devotion, he remained absorbed in the Mudgala Purana and, attaining peace, served the Lord of the Ganas. He became the foremost among Brahmins, honored by all, and at the end, he attained the Lord of the Ganas, becoming revered by yogis and highly renowned.

His hermitage became a place of accomplishment, granting all happiness to those who visit it. Thus, those who are devoted to the daily recitation of the Mudgala Purana enjoy worldly pleasures and eventually attain the state of Brahman.

Thus ends the thirtieth chapter of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Glory of Daily Listening to the Mudgala Purana."

Chapter 31 : The Description of the Greatness of Hearing the Story of Vakratunda

Suta said: Kutsa was a great Brahmin who performed various penances. Through his austerities, he became pure-hearted and devoted to the faith of Brahman. Being self-controlled and calm, he behaved like one who is dull or intoxicated, becoming completely absorbed in the Brahman. He realized that the world of names and forms is considered illusory, and knowing this, he became restless and filled with sorrow. He then bowed to the glorious and great Yogi Yajnavalkya, who had arrived there. Having worshipped him with joy, he humbly asked, "O great Yogi, tell me the yoga that brings peace, which is accepted by all yogis."

Yajnavalkya said: The intellect is composed of five states of mind, which are forms of union and separation. In it, the delusions of success appear, displaying various powers. Within that, the image of Ganesha falls, which causes attachment. By abandoning that image-consciousness, you will become one with Brahman. For that purpose, always worship Vakratunda; by doing so, you will undoubtedly attain peace. The image is said to be filled with Maya, deluding those who seek worldly success and intelligence. Because He destroys that state with His trunk, He is called Vakratunda. The joy of Maya is human-like, and therefore the crooked face is considered supreme. When both are united with Brahman, He is called Vakratunda. Below the neck, it is associated with Maya, but the head is devoid of it; O best of Brahmins, because of that crookedness, He is called Vakratunda. Worship Vakratunda, who is endowed with this meaning, and you will become peaceful and attain the state of Ganesha.

Having said this, the glorious Yogi Yajnavalkya departed. Kutsa, filled with joy, became devoted to Vakratunda. Soon after, the great sage attained peace and, with the nature of a Ganesha-devotee, worshipped Vakratunda. The great Yogi Kutsa daily read the story of Vakratunda from the Mudgala Purana with great devotion and joy. Once, a sinful Brahmin born in the Angirasa lineage, suffering from severe pain and anal fistula, came to his house. Filled with sorrow, he bowed to Kutsa, the best of those who know yoga. After bathing, he lay down near him in great agony. Kutsa worshipped Ganesha and read the story of Vakratunda. The Brahmin, while staying in the house, listened to it effortlessly. Upon hearing the entire story, he became free from disease. The Angirasa bowed to him and returned to his own place. After enjoying various pleasures, he finally attained the Lord of the Ganas. O Shaunaka, see the wonder of the one born in the Angirasa lineage, salutations to you. In this way, many people attained success just by hearing the story of Vakratunda from the Mudgala Purana, O giver of honor. How many such stories should I tell you? It is not possible for me to narrate them all. Therefore, I have briefly described the greatness that comes from hearing it.

Thus ends the thirty-first chapter titled "The Description of the Greatness of Hearing the Story of Vakratunda" in the Yoga-charita section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 32 : Description of the Glory of the Story of Ekadanta.

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: In ancient times, there was a supreme Brahmana named Pippalada. He was devoted to the path of Shiva and always worshipped Lord Shankara. Residing on the banks of the Gautami River, he gradually became established in Yoga, attained a natural state of being, and became dedicated to self-mastery. Then, having understood the Yoga born of the meaning of the Vedas, which is the supreme peace-bestower, he became sorrowful upon seeing that the natural state is devoid of delusion. The Brahmana went to Gautama, bowed to him with devotion, and asked for the supreme means that grants Brahman for the sake of attaining the peace of Yoga.

When asked, Gautama spoke to the Brahmana about Ekadanta, saying, "By devotion to Him alone, you will attain the Yoga of peace. O best of sages, know that whatever is associated with the transformations of Maya, along with Maya itself, is denoted by the word 'Eka' (One). And know that which remains devoid of Maya, or that which is illusory, as 'Danta' (the tooth/the manifestation), O wise one, behold the Vedas. In the union of these two, the Lord of Ganas is known as Ekadanta. Worship Him according to the prescribed rites, and you will become peaceful."

Having said this, the great Yogi Gautama became silent. Pippalada bowed and went to his own supreme hermitage. Staying there, he worshipped Ekadanta with devotion, and by the grace of Ganesha, the Brahmana attained the peace of Yoga. Thereafter, the great Brahmana daily recited the story of Ekadanta, which is established in the Mudgala Purana and is of the nature of Ganapatya.

At one time, the Yogi, having worshipped Ekadanta, recited His story in His presence with great devotion. A tribal woman who was nearby heard the story; O fortunate one, she was suffering from a fever and became free from it. At the end of her life, through the hearing of the story, she went to the Lord of Ganas. Seeing her, she became one with Brahman, which was a wonderful miracle. Thus, by serving and listening to the story of Ekadanta alone, both men and women have become one with Brahman. O sage, it is not possible for anyone to describe their stories fully; therefore, it has been told in brief, as it grants all accomplishments.

Thus ends the thirty-second chapter, entitled the Description of the Glory of the Story of Ekadanta, in the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the essence of the Upanishads, the Yoga-charita, the scripture of the nectar of Yoga, in the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala. Om Tat Sat.

Chapter 33 : Description of the Glory of Mahodara's Life

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: Madhyandina, a highly fortunate sage, was well-versed in all meanings, constantly devoted to his own duties, and endowed with various powers and penance. For the sake of attaining peace, he went to the omniscient and supreme Vyasa. Having bowed to that great soul, he asked with humility.

Madhyandina said: O great-minded one, tell me what men should do for the sake of peace, by which all those who are united with Yoga have become one with Brahman.

Vyasa said: A man becomes one with Brahman through the Yoga of union and separation. By that, one becomes endowed with peace, and that peace is accepted by all. That Lord of the Ganas is known as Mahodara. Worship Him according to the prescribed rites, and then you shall attain peace. Being in the form of union and separation, He consumes all the enjoyments that reside within them; hence, He is remembered as Mahodara. The happiness of Brahman, which is described as the form of enjoyment, is the very belly where the consumed enjoyment resides. O Brahmin, the worlds and the Brahmas are always situated in the belly of Ganesha; He is the enjoyer of enjoyments, and there is nothing else within His belly. Therefore, He is called Mahodara, the leader of the Ganas, in the Vedas. Worship Him alone with devotion, and by that, you shall attain peace.

Having said this, the great Yogi Vyasa, the best among the masters of Yoga, became silent. Madhyandina bowed to him and went to his own hermitage. He specifically worshipped Mahodara, becoming devoted to the Yoga of service. Pleased with him, the Lord of the Ganas granted him the Yoga that leads to supreme peace. Thereafter, he constantly worshipped the leader of the Ganas, endowed with peace.

He who reads the life of Mahodara daily with reverence, the great Yogi himself establishes the remover of obstacles there. Once, having worshipped the King of Ganas, he read that glory of Mahodara, and a miracle happened there. A frog, by destiny, heard it with ignorance and remained in the remnants of the offerings, fearful on all sides. After a long time, when it died, the attendants of Ganesha came with joy to take the frog to some place. Having gathered it, they made it one with Brahman, beyond the reach of the senses. O Lord of Brahmins, behold the supreme and wonderful glory of this reading. Thus, O Brahmin, many people, having served the life of Mahodara, enjoyed their pleasures and became immortal, filled with Brahman.

Thus ends the thirty-third chapter named "Description of the Glory of Mahodara's Life" in the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 34 : Description of the Greatness of Hearing and Reading the Gajanan Charitra

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: Long ago, there was a Brahmin named Suruchi, born in the lineage of Ashvalayana. He was devoted to his own dharma and was intent on the knowledge of Brahman. Being attached to various yogic practices and dedicated to self-control and tranquility, he practiced yoga and eventually attained a state of natural absorption. Seeing his own self-mastery, he was astonished and went to his own Guru, and after bowing to him, he asked about the supreme yoga.

Suruchi said: O Great Yogi, please tell me of the yoga that grants peace in every way. You are our ultimate refuge, our Guru, the upholder of our lineage, and our Lord.

Ashvalayana said: The yoga that grants supreme peace was told to me by Paila. I shall tell you that which bestows the state of Brahman. By abandoning the five-fold mind and realizing "I am Brahman," you will surely attain peace. Know, O seeker of honor, that the essence which remains at the end, from which the mind and all that is perceived arises, is the Gaja (the Supreme Principle). Worship Him who has that face (Anana) according to the scriptures. By doing so, O sage, you will now attain supreme peace.

Having said this, the great Brahmin Ashvalayana remained silent. Suruchi bowed to him and returned to his ashram. He became devoted to Gajanan and worshipped Him daily with reverence, reading the Gajanan Charitra as contained in the Mudgala Purana. Pleased by this, the Lord of the Ganas, the fulfiller of devotees' desires, granted the yoga of supreme peace to his devotee Suruchi.

Thereafter, he understood the mystery arising from the four Vedas, which is contained in the name Gajanan and spoken of by Brahman. That into which all universes, which are manifestations of Brahman, merge—He who exists in them in the form of yoga is called Gachchhati (the mover). The all-pervading Brahman, declared as the essence in the Rig Veda, is to be known as 'Ga' in the name Gajanan. Various universes and manifestations of Brahman are born from Him; therefore, He is known as 'Ja', the unique and non-dual one. That which remains the same in the beginning, middle, and end is declared as Brahman in the Yajur Veda, and is remembered as 'A' in Gajanan. Everything consisting of the five-fold mind is undoubtedly perishable; know that 'Na' represents the destruction of this, and 'A' represents the all-encompassing presence. He who remains in the state of destruction, encompassing everything, is the Brahman described in the Sama Veda, and is the 'Na' in Gajanan. Everything consisting of the five-fold mind is known to be perishable; through the state of yoga, it is declared to be the form of Brahman. The Atharva Veda declares "I am the Atman" without doubt; the destruction caused by ignorance is not attributed to Him in Gajanan.

Thus, the essence from the four mouths of Brahman has been spoken. The great Brahmin Gajanan is remembered as the embodiment of the four Vedas. Having known Gajanan in this way, Suruchi worshipped Him always and read His greatness, and a miracle occurred there. A pigeon sitting on a tree heard that story; it was diseased and sinful, but it became free from disease. At the end, it went to Gajanan and became one with Brahman with joy. What more can I tell you of the fruit obtained even by hearing it unknowingly? Thus, many people, having heard and read this section named Gajanan, enjoyed worldly pleasures and ultimately attained Brahman.

Thus ends the thirty-fourth chapter of the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, the Upanishad of the Puranas, titled "The Description of the Greatness of Hearing and Reading the Gajanan Charitra," in the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala. Om Tat Sat.

Chapter 35 : Description of the Deeds of Lambodara

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: There was a sage named Kapila, a descendant of Bhrigu, who was well-versed in all scriptures. By the boon of Kapila, a person who knew the essence of righteousness was born. He became endowed with various austerities and was established in Yoga. For the sake of this knowledge, he went to Shaunaka with great humility. Bowing to that great soul, he stood there with sincerity. Once, he joyfully asked his father, who was highly fortunate.

Kapila said: O Lord, tell me about the Brahman that bestows the peace of Yoga. You are declared as our Guru; please deliver us from the ocean of worldly existence.

Shaunaka said: Know that Yoga to be the Brahman from which the entire universe has emerged, upon which it rests, and into which it merges. That Brahman, which is the giver of Yoga, is described as Lambodara. The entire universe resides within His belly; worship Him always. By doing so, you will become endowed with peace and become one with Brahman. O fortunate one, there is no other way, for you are deluded by the intellect of worldly achievements.

Having said this, the great Yogi Shaunaka became silent. Bowing to him, Kapila also returned to his hermitage with great joy. He chanted the deeds of Lambodara as described in the Mudgala Purana and worshipped Ganesha daily with devotion. Pleased by this, the Lord of the Ganas, out of affection for His devotee, bestowed the Yoga that grants supreme peace upon Kapila. Being peaceful, the Yogi himself chanted that narrative, remaining devoted to daily worship and staying near Lambodara.

O Shaunaka, listen to a miraculous event that happened there once. An ox, afflicted by disease, fell down. Even without understanding, it heard that entire narrative. Immediately, it became free from disease and was filled with joy. The ox never knew of that great merit. In time, when it died there, it became one with Brahman. In this way, whether knowingly or unknowingly, various people have become one with Brahman; it is impossible for me to describe it all.

Thus ends the thirty-fifth chapter entitled Description of the Deeds of Lambodara, in the Yoga-charita section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 36 : The Description of the Glory of the Vikata Khanda

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: Ashwatthama, the glorious and valiant son of Drona, went to an excellent forest along with his maternal uncle Kripa. Knowing from the mouth of Krishna that the essence of the Mahabharata had departed, he became eager to attain the Supreme Brahman, which transcends the intellect. Desiring to know how knowledge could fully reside in the heart of Ganesha, the Lord of the intellect, he sought refuge in Sage Gautama. Kripa, a knower of the ultimate truth, accompanied his nephew and, having bowed to the sage with humility, stayed at the ashram to serve him.

Once, when Gautama was seated among the yogis, he spoke of the yoga that bestows peace through the practice of devotion. Ashwatthama, filled with joy and humility, and accompanied by Kripa, asked him a question to dispel his doubts. Ashwatthama said: O Lord of yogis, O omniscient one, to whom should one offer worship with joy to attain yoga? Please tell me, salutations to you.

Gautama replied: The worship of Ganesha is supreme and bestows complete peace. In the Vedas, He is described as the embodiment of yoga, appearing in the form of an elephant among men. Due to the delusion of the five-fold mind, the world is bewildered and does not recognize the Supreme Being, the Lord of the Ganas, who bestows yoga. By abandoning the deluded mind, one becomes like the Chintamani (wish-fulfilling gem). Therefore, by worshipping the Vikata (the formidable/transformed) form with devotion, one attains the Lord. Whatever is composed of the five-fold mind, abandon that illusion and worship the elephant-faced Lord who resides within your own heart.

Hearing this, all the sages were astonished; they bowed to him, returned to their respective ashrams, and became absorbed in the worship of Vikata. Ashwatthama and Kripa also bowed to the wise sage, returned to their ashrams, and remained devoted to the worship of Vikata. They recited the character of the great Vikata from the Mudgala Purana with great devotion, worshipping the Lord of the Ganas daily. In a short time, Vikata was pleased and bestowed upon them the yoga that grants complete peace. Nevertheless, those two great souls continued their worship with reverence, daily honoring the Vikata form and reciting the Vikata Khanda.

Once, while the two excellent Brahmanas were engaged in this recitation, a dog listened to it while standing before Kripa, and a cat listened while standing near Ashwatthama. Through the merit of hearing this, they were freed from diseases and ignorance. At the end of their lives, they attained the bliss of the Self due to the merit of that single hearing. How can I describe the infinite glory of this? Ashwatthama and Kripa, having attained their own divine essence, remained joyfully absorbed in the worship of Vikata until the end.

Shaunaka asked: O Suta, how did they become united with their own divine essence, and how did they take the form of Ganesha? Tell me this great wonder.

Suta replied: Gods, through their partial incarnations, take birth as humans endowed with various powers to alleviate the burden of the earth. When humans commit sins, they go to the realm of Yama after death and fall into hells, like Dasharatha and others. Or, having performed great meritorious deeds, they go to heaven and enjoy pleasures, like Rama in the realm of Yama. When they become united with yoga and return to their own divine essence, they attain the form of Ganesha and merge with Him at the end of the Kalpa. Just as Viduraka, who was in the body of Yama among the Kurus, worshipped Ganesha with devotion in the form of Yama. At the end, Yama, purified and united with yoga, attained the Lord of the Ganas and became one with Brahman by nature.

I have told you the complete story of the Vikata Khanda, which is worth hearing and bestows all accomplishments. Thus, many people, having heard this wonderful Khanda of Vikata, enjoyed happiness and became one with Brahman.

Thus ends the thirty-sixth chapter of the ninth Khanda of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Description of the Glory of the Vikata Khanda."

Chapter 37 : Description of the Greatness of Hearing and Reading the Vighnaraja Khanda

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: Vibhishana, the highly fortunate one, having reached Lanka and being surrounded by his friends, always worshipped the venerable Rama with great devotion. There, he pondered upon the Vedic injunction of worshipping the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) first, and he became filled with wonder and sorrow. He contemplated Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, in his mind—the one who is worshipped by all, and whose remnants are consumed by the gods and others.

Once, the great yogi Narada arrived there at his own will. Vibhishana bowed to him, worshipped him, and asked what was in his heart. Vibhishana asked: O Sage, the gods, including Vishnu, consume the remnants of Ganesha. Since he is the one worshipped first, is this practice in accordance with the Vedas?

Narada replied: O King of demons, the wise have stated in the Vedas that Brahma-like beings, such as Shiva and others who practice various yogas, are essentially manifestations of Brahman. Ganesha is their master, known as Brahmanaspati, because he is the protector and sustainer of all forms of Brahman. To grant them yoga, he assumed a physical form, adorned with the signs of an elephant face and others, which are symbolic of the Brahman form. The obstacles are said to be the essence of existence, and Ganesha is their master. He plays as the embodiment of yoga, crushing all obstacles.

O King of demons, when actions are filled with obstacles and lack the power of true resolve, one must worship Him. Knowing that all things are inherently dependent, Ganesha becomes pleased. He respectfully grants them his supreme yoga, and then they become free of obstacles and full of vigor. Therefore, the Lord of the Ganas is complete, having attained the fullness of all, and is the eldest king according to the Vedas. Thus, you should always worship the Lord of Obstacles, who bestows peace. Rama himself is that Lord of Obstacles, through a portion of his divine essence, there is no doubt. There is no deviation in worshipping Ganesha, for he is the Lord of all groups, including those headed by Vishnu.

Having said this, the great yogi Narada, remembering the Lord of the Ganas, departed. Vibhishana, of great intellect, pondered this and remained devoted to the Lord of the Ganas. He read the Mudgala-based section of the Vighnaraja Khanda daily, worshipping the Lord of the Ganas with devotion. Consequently, Vibhishana became filled with peace and worshipped Ganesha daily, seeing him as one with Rama.

Once, the King of demons went to the bridge (Setubandha). Having worshipped Mahodara, he recited the text there. A frog in the water heard this excellent section of the Vighnaraja Khanda and became filled with happiness. In the meantime, it was caught by a snake. As it was being swallowed, it died and was taken by the attendants of Ganesha to the city of Svananda. The attendants, filled with joy, took the frog and made it one with Brahman. Thus, even through ignorance, the merit born from the story of the Lord of Obstacles leads to liberation.

O Brahmin, many people, having heard this section, have joyfully attained Svananda and enjoyed divine pleasures; it is impossible for me to describe it fully.

Thus ends the thirty-seventh chapter of the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "Description of the Greatness of Hearing and Reading the Vighnaraja Khanda."

Chapter 38 : The Description of the Glory of the Legend of Dhumravarna

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: The great sage Varatantu, who was engaged in various types of penance, could not attain peace through his austerities and thus became filled with sorrow. Realizing that this entire world is transient, he became devoted to the Supreme Brahman. At that time, the excellent sage Vishrava, the son of Pulastya, arrived there. Seeing him, Varatantu stood up, bowed to the lord of Brahmins, worshipped him, and asked him with great joy and reverence.

Varatantu said: You are worthy of worship even by the yogis and have arrived here as a purifier. O Lord, it is due to my great merit that I have seen your lotus feet. Please tell me the path to attain the peace of Yoga, so that I may become tranquil. Having renounced everything transient, I am now longing for that alone.

Vishrava said: O dear one, without the knowledge of Brahman, a man cannot attain peace. The Vedas and those who know the Vedas have described Brahman in various ways. Worship their Lord, the leader of the Ganas, known as Brahmanaspati, according to the prescribed rituals, and you will attain peace. The universe is his body, and the elephant head is considered the symbol of Yoga. He is the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the Supreme Being who dwells in his own bliss. O best of sages, because his complexion is smoky, he is called Dhumravarna, the one who bestows the state of Brahman. Worship the Lord of the Ganas, Dhumravarna, with effort. By worshipping him alone, I have attained supreme peace. No one can attain complete peace in any other way; behold that all-pervading Lord through the Vedas and the great sages. I was instructed by Pulastya, and by becoming devoted to Him, I abandoned all other paths and became established in Brahman, without a doubt.

Having said this, the great yogi Vishrava returned to his own abode. Varatantu, reflecting on this, became devoted to the worship of Ganesha. He daily chanted the supreme Dhumravarna mantra from the Mudgala section and worshipped Lord Vighneshwara with joy. He became filled with complete peace, a master of Yoga, and by the grace of Ganesha, he constantly worshipped the leader of the Ganas. Once, while staying at a holy place, he worshipped Dhumravarna and daily recited the Dhumravarna legend before Him. A crow, perched on a tree branch, was suffering from a piercing pain, but it listened to the legend of Dhumravarna. Immediately, it became free from pain and filled with joy; it flew away from that tree, moving according to its own will. In the end, it reached the Lord of the Ganas and attained the state of Brahman. See what merit is attained even by hearing it unknowingly; what more shall I tell you? In this way, many people, having heard the legend of Dhumravarna, attained the state of Brahman after enjoying special pleasures. O great Brahmin, it is not possible to describe everything about them; therefore, the glory of hearing this legend has been told to you in brief.

Thus ends the thirty-eighth chapter, titled The Description of the Glory of the Legend of Dhumravarna, in the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 39 : Description of the Glory of the Ninth Section

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: Prahlada, the renowned yogi who was devoted to yoga, took refuge in Mayuresha and worshipped Him with great devotion. In a dream, he saw the Lord of Gods, seated upon a peacock, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, elephant-faced, and affectionate to His devotees. Bowing to Him, the illustrious Prahlada danced with tears in his eyes. Mayuresha said to him, "Ask for supreme boons." Bowing to the Lord of Obstacles, he said with great joy, "O Lord, grant me that devotion to You which bestows the peace of yoga." Hearing his words, the elephant-faced Lord said, "Look at the ninth section of the Mudgala Purana, which bestows yoga. By it, you shall become worthy of being praised by the best of yogis, and your devotion to Me shall become firm and complete." Having said this, Mayuresha vanished from the sight of the great-minded one. Awakening, he was filled with wonder as he remembered the dream.

Thereafter, he wrote down the ninth section of the Mudgala Purana, which is the essence of yoga, and studied it daily with devotion and reverence. By the grace of the Lord, he attained the yoga described in the hymns and, filled with great joy, worshipped the Lord in nine ways. Remaining in the holy land, renouncing all else, and filled with devotion, he recited the complete ninth section situated in the Mudgala Purana. Once, the demon king named Sindhu destroyed the idol of Ganesha situated in the holy land. Seeing the idol of Sindhu established there by the demons, Prahlada was filled with sorrow, O Shaunaka. All the residents of the holy land, filled with fear and misery, abandoned the Mayura region and fled in all directions. Prahlada, honored by yogis, remained there daily, observing fasts and practicing devotion through renunciation of the land. He performed mental worship of the Lord of Obstacles and, with a heart full of devotion, mentally consumed the remnants of His offerings. Seeing his firm persistence, Mayuresha always placed nectar on the palate of His devotee, satisfying him with it.

After a long time had passed, the son of Shiva, Mayuresha, having been praised by the divine sages, determined to deal with Sindhu. He arrived and destroyed the idol of Sindhu, and the Lord Himself took up residence there. Seeing Him, Prahlada, the best of yogis, worshipped Him and broke his fast with a heart filled with joy. The Lord of the Ganas, pleased and granting boons, appeared before him. Prahlada prayed, "I wish to remain in Your presence and worship You; grant me this." At the end of the Brahma-kalpa, Mayuresha made him one who dwells in His presence, along with another Gana named Brahmapriya.

Listen, O Lord of Brahmins, to another story. The great sage Vishwamitra performed the recitation of the Mudgala Purana with great devotion. A lizard, residing in a tree, heard it unknowingly. Once, while suffering from a fever, it heard the excellent ninth section. Immediately, it became free from fever due to the glory of the merit. Having enjoyed worldly pleasures as desired, it attained its own bliss at the end. Seeing the Lord of the Ganas there, it became one with Brahman. Thus, O Brahmin, many people attained their goals by hearing this section. How many can I describe? It is impossible to recount them all. This ninth section is the bestower of both worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Thus ends the thirty-ninth chapter named "Description of the Glory of the Ninth Section" in the ninth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 40 : Description of the Glory of the Ninth Section

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: O wise one, I have narrated to you this complete and supreme Yoga section, which is the ninth, the bestower of all, and the direct provider of complete Yoga. By listening to just this one section, a human being attains the entire fruit that is contained within the Mudgala Purana. O Brahmana, it is not possible for me to describe the glory of this section, in which the Lord of Yoga, the leader of the Ganas, has been described in his fullness. There is no doubt that by listening to this section, along with its auxiliary parts and methods, one attains the merit derived from the four Vedas. A person who listens to this attains infinite merit, far exceeding that which is obtained by listening to all the eighteen Puranas and the Upapuranas. Various scriptures have emerged from the sound-Brahman (the Vedas); by listening to them, a living being attains merit, but the merit obtained by listening to this section is infinite. The merit obtained by listening to this is infinitely greater than that obtained by visiting all the holy places according to the prescribed rites. By listening to this, one obtains countless fruits, far exceeding those obtained by visiting various sacred lands with devotion. A noble human being who performs various kinds of charity obtains infinite merit from them just by listening to this. It is said that the merit obtained by listening to this is infinite, far exceeding that of performing complete sacrifices according to the scriptural standards. A person who performs various good deeds such as the construction of tanks and temples obtains infinite merit from them by listening to this. What more can be said? There is no other means comparable to this Yoga section, in which Yoga has been described. The complete Yoga section, as described by Vyasa, has been narrated to you. Now, with your permission, I shall depart for my hermitage. O Shaunaka, I have narrated to you the complete Mudgala Purana, which bestows all accomplishments; there is no Purana beyond this. Salutations to you.

Thus ends the fortieth chapter in the Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the essence of the Upanishads, titled the Description of the Glory of the Ninth Section, in the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala, within the Yoga section.

Chapter 41 : The Description of the Completion of the Purana

Shaunaka said: O noble one, I have been residing in great distress, filled with various profound doubts regarding the Puranas. I have been thinking that by listening to the doubts regarding other Puranas, my own uncertainties would be resolved, and thus I have remained in this state of contemplation. O wise one, after listening to the final Mudgala Purana, my doubts have vanished. O Suta, you have surely made me free of doubt; there is no uncertainty in this. Yet, one doubt still remains in my heart; please dispel it. Then, return to your own abode and make me blessed by granting me peace.

The mystery of Ganesha, which was known to the wise Vyasa, is supreme, beyond all, residing within the Mudgala, and is most wondrous and transcendent. Vyasa composed the great scripture, the Mahabharata, which establishes dharma and adharma and grants union with Brahman. In that work, the worship and remembrance of Ganesha were performed at the beginning, but later, the description of the Lord of the Ganas was not fully elaborated. Once the Mahabharata was completed, the great sage felt his task was done; there is no doubt about this. Tell me, what is the mystery behind this?

Hearing the words of Shaunaka, Suta remembered his guru. Instantly, the lord of yogis, Vyasa, appeared there. Seeing him, Suta, Shaunaka, and the others were filled with joy; they stood up, worshipped him, bowed down, and stood with folded hands. Vyasa, the best of sages, having understood the purpose of Suta, placed him nearby and spoke to Shaunaka and the others.

Vyasa said: O Brahmin, there is no doubt that I composed the great scripture, the Mahabharata. In it, the one known as Brahman was indeed described as being within the intellect. He is the bestower of success and the embodiment of Brahman; that is considered the primary truth. The others, such as Vaikuntha, are described as being subject to destruction. I was truly deluded by the power of Siddhi and Buddhi, and I forgot to describe Ganesha in all His aspects. I was ready to compose another work, O sage, when a voice from the sky spoke to me; listen to what it said: "Do not fully describe the glory of Ganesha. This has been created by Ganesha to delude the people. Except for the great sage Mudgala, all other great sages are incapable of describing the true form of Ganapati." Hearing that, I bowed down and remained devoted to the Mudgala. Therefore, you are free of doubt; worship the Mudgala with devotion.

In the Puranas, the supreme form of Ganesha is described, which is the bestower of the knowledge of Brahmanaspati, without a doubt. Nevertheless, all the Brahmins and others are deluded and do not know the Elephant-faced Lord as He truly is, because they are under the influence of Siddhi and Buddhi. By the grace of Ganesha, through devotion, by seeing the complete Lord of the Ganas in the Mudgala, they will know Him, and nowhere else. Therefore, worship the Mudgala daily with reverence; being free from the faults of the Kali age, you will attain everything supreme.

Having said this, the great yogi and wise Vyasa ceased speaking, and at that very moment, Dhumravarna (Ganesha) appeared. Seeing Him, they stood up and bowed to Ganesha with devotion, worshipped Him, and praised Him with hymns dear to Ganapati. Dhumravarna, being pleased, spoke to His devotees with a smile, offering words that bestow happiness.

Dhumravarna said: I have completely shattered the power of the Kali age. O great sages, now walk without fear. Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas took them and established them upon the earth, making them righteous human beings. He told the great sages: "Chant the Mudgala, which gives my mystery, daily with joy; by that, I shall be under your control. There is nothing equal to this, nor any Upanishad more dear, where I reside in five forms, holding the nature of yoga. My fivefold form is revealed through the Mudgala. By listening to this Purana and reading it with understanding, where the Mudgala is read or heard by people, O lords of Brahmins, I reside there, eager to accept their devotion. In the Brahmi (section), my form residing in the intellect is described in part; I reside there, characterized by the formless. In the Brahmanda, I am the form of Omkara, accompanied by various divine plays; I reside there with joy through the influence of my universal form. In the Ganesha (section), I am in the image, the illuminator of knowledge for both; I always reside there, accompanied by the elephant-face and other signs. In other Puranas, I reside as Shiva, Vishnu, and others, wearing their forms through a portion of my essence, engaging in various manifestations. In the Mudgala, I am in my yogic form, devoted to the Upanishads; the yoga of all my forms is declared therein. The Mudgala is dear to me because it is the complete illuminator of them and the illuminator of yoga; therefore, nothing exists equal to it. O best of sages, when I was prayed to with devotion at the beginning of creation, I granted a boon to increase devotion. For that purpose, the complete Mudgala was composed by me in the past and placed in the heart of Mudgala, bestowing all happiness. I inspired that yogi, the expert Mudgala, to narrate the entire Purana, which is my joyful form. Since then, my Purana is served by gods like Shiva, great sages like Marichi, and all beings like Shesha. O Brahmins, without the Mudgala, my true, complete, and all-illuminating form cannot be known by anyone anywhere. Elsewhere, my form is described in a complete manner, but there, delusion surely arises, caused by Siddhi and Buddhi. At the beginning of creation, I was especially prayed to by Siddhi and Buddhi for the delusion of the worlds and for the sake of the divine play of Brahma. At that time, I gave a boon that, except for the Mudgala, they would never know the Brahman residing within the worlds. Therefore, worship me as I reside in the Mudgala with a single mind; by that, you will always walk with devotion to me. There is no other means for attaining me equal to this; O best of twice-born, this must be known for the sake of all success. By merely listening to this, a person becomes fulfilled. I cannot describe the fruits, as they are boundless. O great sages, I am satisfied by the hearing of the Mudgala; I will grant all your desires; ask without hesitation."

Shaunaka and others said: Grant us your devotion, the yoga that bestows peace. Whatever we desire, O Lord, may it always be fulfilled. Make us all Ganapatyas (devotees of Ganesha) and grant us these boons we have requested, O Lord of Qualities.

Saying "So be it," Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, spoke to Vyasa and the others, "Ask for the boons you desire." They, being dear to Ganapati, requested the same from the Lord of the Ganas. Saying "So be it" to them, the Lord of Obstacles spoke to Suta: "O noble one, ask for boons; you have made this known to the Brahmins, and I am pleased with you; I will grant them." Saying "So be it," the Elephant-faced Dhumravarna disappeared and became established in His own bliss.

All of them, with satisfied hearts, went to their respective places and, taking refuge in the Mudgala, worshipped Ganesha. This Samhita, born from Mudgala and augmented by the Vedas, consisting of twenty-three thousand and one hundred and fifty verses, with four hundred and twenty-eight chapters and nine sections, complete and full of all success, was narrated by Mudgala for the benefit of all. It is known as the mystery of the Word-Brahman and directly grants Brahman.

Thus ends the forty-first chapter of the Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the final Purana-Upanishad, in the ninth section, concerning the yoga-charita, the yoga-amrita scripture, in the dialogue between Daksha and Mudgala, describing the completion of the Purana.

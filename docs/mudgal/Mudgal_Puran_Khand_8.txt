Chapter 1 : The Description of the Origin of Ego

Shaunaka said: O wise one, I have heard from you the character of the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha), which grants all desires and is complete and sorrow-destroying. You are blessed, full of knowledge, and surely equal to Vyasa, or perhaps even greater than that great soul. My thirst for that nectar of stories is never quenched by hearing them again and again. Now, please tell me the auspicious greatness of Dhumravarna.

Suta said: Listen, Shaunaka, I shall tell you the greatness of Dhumravarna, which grants all boons, exactly as I heard it in its entirety from Vyasa. Having heard the greatness of the Lord of Obstacles, Daksha was joyful. He bowed to Mudgala and asked with humility.

Daksha said: I have heard the complete greatness of the Lord of Obstacles, which grants all attainments, but I am not satisfied, O great Yogi, for it is greater than nectar. Now, tell me in detail about the greatness of Dhumravarna. What kind of form does this Ganesha, Dhumravarna, possess? What is his nature (Brahman), and by what yoga can he be attained? How many are his incarnations, what are his deeds, and what is his glory? By what means did this deity become embodied? Which demon did the Lord subdue to establish righteousness? Tell me all this greatness, O best of Yogis; there is no one in the universe like you, who is a vessel for bestowing divine knowledge.

Suta said: Being asked thus, the great Yogi Mudgala, knowing Daksha to be full of supreme devotion, spoke to him with a joyful heart.

Mudgala said: Daksha, you are truly worthy of being honored by Yogis in every way; otherwise, one would not have a taste for the story of the Lord of Ganas. I shall tell you the story of the Dhumravarna incarnation, which grants all attainments, in brief, O Prajapati. Even Shiva and the Vedas could not describe it in detail; who am I, possessing such little knowledge? I will tell you an ancient history, which is a dialogue between Shesha and Shankara.

Once, the serpent Shesha bowed to Shankara and asked the omniscient, compassionate Lord of all. Shesha said: There is nothing greater than you according to the Vedas and scriptures. O Lord, you are always absorbed in meditation; which supreme Shiva do you worship? I am full of doubt and ask you with all my heart: for the sake of delusion, O Maheshana, why do you perform new actions?

Mudgala said: Being asked thus, Shiva, seeing his devotee who was fond of yoga and virtuous in his actions, spoke to him with great joy.

Shiva said: When I was asked this in private by Sanaka and others, who are very dear to me, I told them the knowledge related to Ganesha. Once, Sanaka and the others saw me absorbed in my vows and asked me, just as you, Shesha, are doing now, full of doubt.

Sanaka and the others said: You are the great Lord Maheshwara himself; there is no one above you. Whom do you meditate upon, O Mahadeva? This is merely a delusion.

Shiva said: Hearing their words, O Kashyapa, I told them of the Yoga of Ganesha, which grants complete peace. Whether one is a Lord or not is a matter of Maya; how can that exist in the Brahman who has become one with Brahman? I worship that Lord of Ganas, with devotion, by whose grace I was given the status of a Lord and the status of a non-Lord. All those filled with ego do not know the Elephant-faced one. Having conquered that ego, O sages, I serve his two feet.

Hearing Shiva's words, Sanaka and the others, amazed and full of longing for the knowledge of Ganesha, said to him: O Mahadeva, it is impossible to conquer ego, even for the Vedas, the gods, or other Yogis. I am a man, I am a divine form, I am the soul, I am the Brahman—I am established in these. By what yoga did you, Sadashiva, conquer it? Tell us his story in detail, which brings great peace.

Shiva said: O best of Brahmins, I went into the Brahman and became one with it. In the yoga of the Great Statement (Mahavakya), the "I" is not seen. "I am not," and "Brahman is not"—this is the Great Statement remembered as the means for realization.

Then, full of doubt, Sanaka and the others said: We did not understand your words, O Lord; they are very difficult. Therefore, O Maheshwara, tell us a simple yoga out of compassion for your disciples, to lead them to complete peace.

Shiva said: I will tell you an ancient history for the destruction of the "I-ness" and for the happiness of the ego. Once, the Sun, the lord of lights and the soul of all embodied beings, was anointed by Brahma in the kingdom of actions. Having attained that fierce kingdom of actions, the lord of lights became extremely proud. Brahma is the creator of the world through action, Vishnu is the protector, and Hara is the destroyer. Through the power of food and action, the nourishing energy exists. The whole world, including the gods, is dependent on action; there is no doubt about this. I am the deity of action, the supreme mover of actions. The Sun, being very proud, thought that this world appeared dependent on him. Once, the Lord, filled with ego, sneezed. From his sneeze, a man of beautiful form was born. He fell on the surface of the earth, but the Sun did not notice him. He was large-bodied, wide-eyed, and shone with great strength. Suddenly, Shukra, the knower of all meanings, arrived there and asked him with joy and devotion.

Shukra said: Who are you, where is your home, and what do you wish to do? Who are your mother, father, and protector? What do you want from me?

The man said: I am the son of the Sun, born from his sneeze, O best of Brahmins. I am an orphan, O great one, fallen on the earth. You ask me with a compassionate heart; I will be dependent on you. Instruct me, O best of sages.

Shiva said: Hearing his words, Shukra, the best of those who know yoga, looked at his actions through meditation and spoke to him auspiciously.

Shukra said: You have been born from the Sun due to ego; therefore, let your name be "Aham" (Ego). Perform penance, O wise one. Having said this, he gave him the sixteen-syllable Ganesha mantra, according to the rules, and then disappeared. I performed penance there, meditating on Ganapati in my heart, living without food, and devoted to chanting the mantra. With firm determination, enduring cold, heat, wind, and rain, and conquering my senses with effort, I pleased the Lord of Obstacles.

Thus ends the first chapter of the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of the Origin of Ego," in the Dhumravarna character.

Chapter 2 : The Description of the Attainment of the Kingdom by Ahamasura

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Lord Shiva said: Thus, after a thousand divine years, Gajanana (Ganesha) arrived, pleased by my devotion and the respect shown in seeking a boon. He appeared as the one who rides a mouse, the three-eyed one, the elephant-faced one with a large belly, and the four-armed one, adorned with a noose and other weapons, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. He, the single-tusked one with winnowing-basket-like ears, was praised by the gods and sages, served by his own attendants, and was the bestower of all things. Seeing him, I stood up, bowed with folded hands, and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles, who was accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. Then, in order, I worshipped the gods and sages, and again bowed to the Lord, praising Ganesha with folded hands.

Ahamasura said: Salutations to you, O Lord of the Ganas, O Heramba, the Supreme Soul. You are the giver of boons to all; salutations again and again to you, the bestower of boons. You are the Lord of Brahma among the Brahmas, the true form of those who are your own, the essence of Sankhya philosophy, and the one who exists beyond all; salutations to you. You are the form of consciousness in knowledge, the 'I-am' in living beings, the point-form (Bindu) in bodies; salutations to the Lord of Obstacles. You are Brahma among creators, Vishnu among protectors, and Shiva among destroyers; salutations to the single-tusked one. You are the Sun among illuminators, Maya among those who delude, and the King of Gods among the devas; salutations to you, O Vinayaka. You are fire among those who burn, Yama among those who uphold justice, and Nairrita among the demons; salutations to you, O winnowing-basket-eared one. You are the wind among the strong, Kubera among the keepers of wealth, and the form of Time among the Rudras; salutations to you, O Lambodara. You are Daksha among the Prajapatis and Shesha among the serpents; what can I, with my limited intellect, offer in praise of your infinite glory? Salutations to you. I am blessed, my purpose is fulfilled, and my birth, father, mother, lineage, and knowledge have all become fruitful by the sight of your lotus feet.

Having said this, the great demon bowed to Ganesha. Pleased, the elephant-faced Lord spoke to him.

Shri Ganesha said: This hymn composed by you shall grant all desires to those who read or hear it, and it shall instill devotion toward me. A person shall attain happiness, progeny, worldly enjoyments, and liberation, and by merely reading this, they shall gain devotion to me that destroys all delusion. O fortunate one, ask for the boons you desire; I shall grant them, for I am pleased by your penance and this excellent hymn.

Ahamasura said: O Lord, since you are pleased, grant me your devotion. May whatever I desire always be fulfilled. May I never meet death from those bound by the illusions of Maya. Grant me the kingdom of the entire universe along with all its enjoyments. O Dhundhi, grant me health and unparalleled victory in battle, and by your grace, may I always possess infallible weapons.

Saying "So be it," the Lord of the Ganas disappeared and returned to his own abode. Then, filled with great joy, I went to Kavi (Shukracharya), bowed to him, and narrated the entire incident in detail. Hearing this, the great sage praised the great demon and summoned the leading Asuras. At his command, they arrived quickly, and the radiant Kavi spoke words of benefit to the demons. Hearing the account of Ahamasura from the mouth of Shukra, the demons were delighted and, bowing to him, spoke words of benefit.

The Asuras said: Command us, O great Yogi, what is in your mind? We are your servants, ready to act with all our strength.

Hearing their words, Kavi, who was well-versed in all matters, summoned the Vedic Brahmins and worshipped them. Through them, I was anointed for the kingdom of the Asuras, and according to the scriptural rites, I was made the King of the Daityas. There were five powerful and radiant ministers: Shankha, Vedaghnaka, Kala, Paishuna, and Adharmadharaka. He also built a city named Vishayapriya, where I, Ahamasura, ruled with great joy. People of all castes and stages of life came to reside there, and the various Asuras lived happily.

Pramadasura gave his beautiful daughter, Mamata, who was in the bloom of youth, to me. Delighted, I, the King of the Daityas, lived with Mamata, captivated by her Maya and remaining under her influence. The King of the Daityas fathered two mighty sons with her, whom all the Brahmins named Garvashreshtha.

After a long time had passed, Pramadaka said to me, "Why do you remain idle, O mighty one? Conquer the gods and others, O Lord. By the power of the boon, you are fearless, there is no doubt. Make the boons fruitful and engage in great enjoyments."

I, the powerful Ahamasura, said "So be it" to my father-in-law. I summoned Kavi and worshipped him with the help of the Daitya leaders. Taking his command, the King of the Daityas summoned other demons. Accompanied by them and Shukra, the Asura shone with brilliance. I, the Lord, commanded everyone to prepare for the conquest of the directions. The fierce Daityas, fully armed, gathered around me.

Thus ends the second chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which describes the attainment of the kingdom by Ahamasura.

Chapter 3 : The Conquest of the Universe by Ahamasura

Shiva said: I, mounted on my chariot, set out accompanied by the demons. My sons and chief commanders, fully armed, marched along with me. They were of various colors, terrifying, and brought fear to Time itself. All of them, riding on various vehicles, followed me. The leader of the demons shone amidst his four-fold army; equipped with an immeasurable force, he resolved to conquer the earth.

The great warriors, led by Shankha, went to the kings who were very formidable. Having defeated the entire royal lineage, they gathered before the great demon. The kings asked, "O Brahmins, will you fight against such powerful beings?" Some died, some were wounded, and some sought refuge in them. Having made those kings pay tribute and placing his own servants in their kingdoms, the demon lords were filled with joy.

Having conquered the earth with its seven islands, the victorious demon lords went to the netherworld (Patala) to conquer the Nagas. Shesha, the king of the Nagas, sought their refuge. Knowing the power of the boon, the yogi made a treaty with the great demons. Having accepted the annual tribute, he returned to his abode, while Shesha, surrounded by the Naga lords, remained with a heavy heart. Nevertheless, those great demons, having placed the demon and his followers near him, left, being very powerful.

Then, the powerful Ahamasura, accompanied by the demon lords and Shukracharya, went to heaven, filled with the pride of his boon. He sent a messenger to Indra. The messenger went, bowed to the King of Gods, and delivered this message. Then, Indra, filled with anger, consulted with the chief gods and Brihaspati, went to him, and bowed. Brihaspati, honored by him, spoke words that were painful to all but brought victory to the demons.

Indra said: "The powerful one, endowed with a boon, has arrived. The messenger tells me today to seek refuge with the demon lords. Therefore, I have come to you, who know everything, along with the gods. Tell me, O Master, we are under your command."

The Guru said: "Because of the boon of Ganesha, he is invincible; there is no doubt about this. He will conquer everything, so seeking refuge is not appropriate. Therefore, let us go to the Creator (Brahma) with haste. He is the best and will surely do what is beneficial for us in every way."

Then all the gods, along with Indra, went to the Creator. Having bowed, they all told him the story of the demon's origin. Hearing this, Brahma, filled with sorrow, took them to Shiva. Having bowed to him, he told the whole terrible story. He (Shiva) also sighed and went with them to Vishnu with respect. Having bowed, he told the sorrowful story. Hearing this, Vishnu, filled with anger, comforted Maheshwara and spoke words of valor to the gods.

Shri Vishnu said: "I will kill this great demon and his followers. The illusion he possesses does not affect me." Having said this to Mahadeva, he remained there with the gods, awaiting the demon's prowess.

Ahamasura, knowing the news, entered Amaravati (the city of Indra) with the demon lords, filled with joy. Seated on Indra's throne, served by the Apsaras and sung to by the Gandharvas, he shone with supreme splendor. Then he placed the demons in the positions of the gods and enjoyed heaven with the demon lords and his father-in-law.

Then, O Brahmins, he marched with those demon hordes, fully armed. He saw the Brahmaloka, which was very fierce and devoid of the Creator. He entered that city with the demons, filled with joy. Seated on Brahma's throne, he enjoyed various supreme pleasures. Then, having learned everything from the demon lords, he went to Kailasa, the Sun's realm, and the Shakti-loka in order. There, enjoying various pleasures, he went to Vaikuntha to conquer it, surrounded by the demon lords.

He sent a messenger, Kala (Time), who is terrifying to all. He went, bowed to Keshava, and spoke harsh words.

Kala said: "I have come with the demon, who is very powerful. I have been sent by him to you for the sake of a treaty. Leave Vaikuntha, O Lord of Gods, and go wherever you please. O Narayana, I will not kill you; otherwise, be ready for battle."

Hearing the words of Kala, Keshava, filled with anger, spoke to that great demon, scorching him with his gaze.

Shri Vishnu said: "Why do you, a sinful being of little valor, speak to me? I will kill the great demon and his followers this very day. Go, messenger, I will not kill you, you wretch. Should I leave my own abode and become subordinate to the demon lord?"

Then Kala-asura went to Ahamasura and told him everything. Hearing this, all the demon hosts became angry. They became ready for battle, joined by Ahamasura. Stopping them, the great-armed one, filled with anger, spoke words beneficial to all the demon chiefs.

Ahamasura said: "Why will you exert yourselves, O powerful ones? I will now bring happiness, equipped with infallible weapons and boons." Having said this, the demon, having prepared his great bow, released an arrow made of fire, having chanted mantras in anger. Suddenly, the arrow blazed, illuminating the ten directions. It burned Vaikuntha and all the gods all around.

Then the distressed gods released the Varuna-astra (water weapon). Even with that, the fire did not subside; it remained filled with intensity. Then, all of them, filled with sorrow, fled in fear. Tormented by the fire, they left Vaikuntha. Varuna himself, seeing this, went to the battlefield. Accompanied by the Moon, he created water and stood firm. The fierce fire was not calmed by the water. Scorched, Varuna and the Moon fled.

Then Shakti arrived and released the great Varuna-astra. A stream of water was released from it. But just as fire mixed with ghee burns even when water is poured on it, it blazed due to the illusion of Ahamasura. Then, scorched, Shakti fled immediately. The angry Sun himself went there, filled with anger. He also released his own powerful Varuna-astra in the battle. Scorched as before, the Sun-god fled.

Then Shiva himself went there, filled with anger. His efforts defeated, he fled, scorched by his own nature. Then, filled with great sorrow, Vishnu himself arrived. He released the Varuna-astra, endowed with his own power. Even then, the fierce fire did not subside; it blazed everywhere and burned the gods.

Then, scorched, Janardana (Vishnu) fled. All the gods, including Vishnu, fled in fear. They went and stayed in mountain caves, filled with terror. Eating roots and the like, they passed their days.

Knowing this, I (Ahamasura), having gathered my own weapons, went to Vaikuntha with the demons, filled with joy. Staying there, the arrogant one considered himself successful. "There is no one greater than me," he thought, filled with pride. Then, having placed the pride of Shiva in that position, the demon made his own son the lord of Vaikuntha. He made the slanderer the lord of Shakti-loka, the destroyer of the Vedas the lord of all, and the negligent one the lord of Brahma. He made Shankha the lord of Indra's position, and Kala the lord of Yama's realm, and Kamala the lord of Varuna's. Having placed the supporter of unrighteousness in Kubera's position with respect, the powerful one returned to his own city with everyone.

Staying there, he ruled the universe, which was crowded with people. I thought there was nothing equal to my fortune and power.

Thus ends the third chapter of the eighth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Conquest of the Universe by Ahamasura."

Chapter 4 : The Granting of Boons to Gods and Sages

Shiva said: The demon Ahantakar was extremely lustful for other women. He abducted the daughters of Nagas, humans, and especially young maidens, and being a destroyer of all, he enjoyed them by force. O best of Brahmins, he became addicted to meat and liquor, abandoned Ganesha, and engaged in sinful deeds. Once, while I was in the assembly, the supporter of unrighteousness (Adharmadharaka), prompted by all the demon lords, spoke with folded hands.

Adharmadharaka said: You have conquered the entire universe filled with people, O fortunate one. You alone are the Lord of the world, and there is no one else. The gods, led by Vishnu, have retreated to the forests. In the Vedas, the gods and demons are declared as our enemies. O giver of honor, look, the Vedas state that the gods, being immortal, will never die by weapons. All immortals are sustained by rituals; when rituals are destroyed, O demon lord, they will surely die. There is no doubt; destroy the rituals. Many have conquered the three worlds, but in time, they were killed by the gods. Therefore, seeing your opportunity, the gods will kill you. I am striving for their destruction along with the demon leaders.

Hearing the words of Adharmadharaka, the great demons, including Shankha, exclaimed "Well done!" in the assembly. Then, the pleased Ahantakar spoke beneficial words: "O demon, what you have said is good; execute it with everyone's help." Accompanied by the group of demons, he went away joyfully. Adharmadharaka then began the destruction of rituals on earth. Due to the cessation of rituals, people became distressed and devoid of social and spiritual order (Varnashrama) because of that wicked one. He destroyed temples everywhere, as well as the sacred sites and all the idols of Ganesha. He installed his own statues everywhere on earth and appointed demons as their priests. In every house, he made people worship his own image, thus promoting demonic activities. Having completed this task, the supporter of unrighteousness returned, and being highly praised by the demon, he felt fulfilled.

When rituals were destroyed and people became mixed in castes, the gods became filled with sorrow. Along with the sages, the gods fasted and deliberated on how to destroy that demon lord. Then, Brahma, filled with grief, spoke to Mahesh and others: "We, along with all our illusions, have been defeated by that wicked one. O Brahmin, there is no doubt that there are many types of illusions in the Vedas. Apart from Ganesha, the Lord of Ganas, there is no one else who is free from them. The intellect is composed of five thoughts and causes five types of delusions; Ganesha is the master of both Siddhi and Buddhi. Siddhi and Buddhi are the supreme illusions, and there is nothing beyond them. Therefore, Ganesha will undoubtedly kill the demon. Because of the boon granted by Ganesha, that demon lord has become arrogant, forgotten the Lord of Ganas, and abandoned his mantra. The evil-minded one has destroyed Ganesha's sacred sites and all worship of Ganesha. Therefore, O best of gods, he has become filled with obstacles. If we worship Dhundhi (Ganesha), he will kill the great demon. Now, let us chant the single-syllable mantra, meditate on the elephant-faced Lord, worship Him, and through our penance, specifically please Him."

Shiva said: Hearing the words of Brahma, the gods and sages said "Well done!" and acted accordingly. They fasted, followed the single-syllable mantra, and with great devotion, pleased the Lord of Ganas. After a hundred years had passed, the pleased Lord of Ganas appeared before the gods and sages to grant them boons. They saw the elephant-faced Lord seated on a mouse, bowed to Him with joy, and worshipped Him with reverence. Having bowed again, the gods and sages, filled with delight, praised the smoke-colored Lord of Ganas.

The divine sages said: Salutations to the smoke-colored Lord, the giver of all, the compassionate one, the supreme Ganesha, the one beyond the beyond. Salutations to the pot-bellied Lord, the master of obstacles, the creator and destroyer of obstacles, salutations to Heramba. Salutations to the beginningless one, the eldest, the one worshipped by all, the one beyond mind and speech, yet the embodiment of mind and speech. Salutations to the form of Brahman, the Brahman, the giver of Brahman, the Lord of the Vedas, and the master of mantras. Salutations to the great-formed Lord, the God of gods, the giver of boons to the gods, O great-bellied one, salutations to you. Salutations to the one worshipped first, the mother and father of all, the embodiment of all, the soul of all, the embodiment of action. Salutations to the one with winnowing-basket ears, the brave, the heroic, the Supreme Soul, the four-armed, smoke-colored Lord of qualities. Salutations to the creator, destroyer, and sustainer, the embodiment of supreme knowledge, the independent one, the giver and remover of great delusion. O Lord, where even the Vedas and sages have found peace, what can we say in your praise? In you, the unmanifest, the colors have become smoke-colored; hence, you are called Dhumravarna (Smoke-colored) by the knowers of the Vedas. We are blessed to see your lotus feet; you, the unmanifest, have become manifest due to your affection for your devotees.

Having said this, the divine sages bowed to the smoke-colored Lord. Raising them up, the smoke-colored Lord spoke with a voice like thunder: "This hymn composed by you will grant all successes to those who read or hear it. A person will obtain sons, grandsons, grain, and wealth; whatever they desire will be fulfilled. O lords of gods and sages, ask for the boons you desire in your hearts; I will grant them, pleased by your penance and this hymn."

Hearing the words of Ganesha, the divine sages bowed and spoke with reverence: "O Elephant-faced one, if you are pleased and a giver of boons, then kill the arrogant demon. He is the source of sorrow for all and the destroyer of rituals. If he is dead, O Lord of Ganas, there will be happiness. He is devoid of your illusion; he will surely die by your hands. There is no doubt; therefore, kill him, O giver of honor. You granted him fearlessness, and because of that, everyone has been defeated. We have sought your refuge; protect us, O great Lord. Grant us firm and unwavering devotion to your feet; through that, we will be free of obstacles and reach your abode."

Having said this, the sages bowed to Ganesha. Saying "So be it," He disappeared.

Chapter 5 : The Defeat of Ahamkara

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Lord Shiva said: Then, the gods led by Indra, accompanied by the sages, were filled with great joy, believing the demon to be defeated. Waiting for the right time, they remained there, devoted to the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) with joyful hearts.

O best of sages, at night, Ganesha, in his form of Dhumravarna (Smoke-colored), appeared in the dream of the demon king, striking terror into him in every way. Distressed and sorrowful, the demon king awoke and summoned the great demons, narrating his dream in detail.

Ahamkara said: O leaders of the demons, listen to the terrifying dream I have seen. The Lord of Ganesha, Dhumravarna, became filled with rage. The fire born of his anger burned my entire city, and my weapons and missiles became blunt and ineffective. I was rendered powerless by that great soul, and all the gods, along with the sages, were restored to their own abodes. Therefore, surely some misfortune will occur in a short time. O demon leaders, be prepared for that.

Hearing these arrogant words, the demon leaders, filled with sadness, spoke words of comfort to him. The demon lords said: Do not worry in vain; how could any misfortune befall you? By the power of the boon you received, you are fearless, O lord of demons. Dreams are always just dreams for people, there is no doubt about this. Even if they seem real while awake, they are perishable; what is there to be sad about? If a creature gains wealth or faces death in a dream, upon waking, they see nothing at all.

Having spoken thus and comforted the great demon, the demons went to their homes and performed their daily rituals. Now, O best of sages, listen to the deeds of the Lord of Obstacles. Suddenly, the mighty Dhumravarna arrived at a place five yojanas away from the demon king's city. The sage Narada also arrived there suddenly. Seeing the Lord of Ganesha, he bowed down and, with tears of devotion in his eyes, sang his praises with joy.

The Lord of Ganesha said to him: Go to the demon's palace, O sage, and deliver this message of conciliation. Let the gods return to their abodes, let the people follow their own duties, and let the Nagas rule their kingdoms by my grace. Rule your kingdom, O great demon, while remaining in your city. Otherwise, I will defeat you in battle and do what is beneficial for the world.

Saying "So be it," the sage Narada went to the demon Ahamkara and conveyed that message. Hearing this, the demon became enraged and said: Go, O Brahmin, and tell the Lord of Obstacles my firm reply. I will undoubtedly kill Dhumravarna along with the gods. I will never submit to his command.

Having said this to the sage Narada, the demon king dismissed him. Narada went to the Lord of Ganesha and conveyed the demon's words. Hearing Narada's words, the elephant-faced Lord became angry and decided to kill him, taking up his weapon.

Then the sage Narada bowed to him and went to the gods, joyfully narrating the entire event. All the gods bowed to him and approached Dhumravarna. Accompanied by the sages and armed with weapons, they praised him and said: O great one, we bow to you. Command us, O Lord of gods, to fight the demons, for your servants are ready to obey you.

Hearing their words, the valiant Dhumravarna, the Lord of devotees who is fond of his devotees, said to them: Stay here, O lords of gods, and watch this great spectacle. I will kill the great demon who has turned away from my command.

Having said this, the Lord of Ganesha released his formidable noose. That weapon, upon being released, assumed infinite forms. Wherever it went, it seized the demons and fell upon their necks in an instant. The demons residing on the outskirts of the city died, crushed by the power of the noose. A cry of distress arose everywhere in the city. The demons went to the great demon and reported the terrible news: Why are you sitting still, O King? The demons are dying everywhere, crushed by the noose of Dhumravarna.

Hearing their words, the great demon, filled with sorrow and fear, spoke to his ministers. Ahamkara said: Alas, O demons, the dream has become true. What will happen now, O demon lords? Everything appears to be going against us.

Hearing his words, his two sons, the great demons Garva and Sreshtha, spoke to their father, who was agitated by fear. The two said: Why are you grieving, O great one? No harm will come to you. What can Dhumravarna, who is full of illusion, do to harm you? He has come in a physical form and is partial to the gods. We will kill him, have no doubt. Behold our prowess.

Having said this, the two proud demons bowed to the demon king and went to battle. Accompanied by their chief warriors, they rained down weapons, but they were all burned by the brilliance of the noose. Filled with fear, all the chiefs fell in all directions, scorched by the radiance of the noose.

Then, the two great demons, filled with extreme rage, took up their swords and came forward to cut the noose, displaying their great strength and valor. Although their bodies were scorched by the brilliance, they were determined to cut the noose with their swords. Then, bound by the noose, their breath obstructed, the two great demons lost their lives in the battle, O great sages.

Then, crying out in distress, all the demons, filled with fear and having lost their pride, fled in all ten directions, completely bewildered. Then, some came and told Ahamkara the cruel news that his sons had died in the battle. Hearing their words, the demon suddenly fainted. When he was revived by his attendants, he lamented, filled with fear.

Summoning his courage, he went to battle. He released all his weapons to cut the brilliant noose. But they were all burned by the radiance of the noose. Filled with extreme fear, he pondered in his heart: My infallible weapons have become fruitless. What is this noose? What will happen next?

Intent on fleeing, the demon king returned to his own house, summoned his preceptor, and spoke to him.

Thus ends the fifth chapter of the Dhumravarna legend in the eighth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 6 : The Instruction of Knowledge to Ahankarasura

Lord Shiva said: Having bowed to Shukracharya, the knower of policy, the demon Ahankara, filled with fear, spoke these words for his own benefit.

Ahankara said: O Master, the boon I received has become fruitless. Tell me, why has this happened, and dispel my doubts regarding this infallible weapon. Dhumravarna has arrived, promoting the side of the gods. How has he, being full of Maya (illusion), rendered me fruitless?

Shukracharya said: O fool, you consider Dhumravarna to be merely full of Maya; you do not truly know the Elephant-faced Lord. The distinction between being with or without Maya does not apply to Him; the knowers of the Vedas describe Ganesha as the embodiment of Yoga. This Dhumravarna, being enraged, will destroy you. Therefore, O Lord of demons, take refuge in that Lord Ganesha. Just as the sun is covered by mist and remains unseen by people, so too do Shiva and others not fully comprehend the essence of Ganesha. The letters and the sounds of the Vedas appear as if they have emerged from the smoke; because of ignorance, this Dhumravarna appears unmanifest.

The primordial Brahman, which is self-governing and supreme, cannot be bound by the limitations of a boon. A boon may be successful or fruitless according to His will; that is why this Dhumravarna is called the "Self-existent." The imperishable state known as the "Elephant" is the Brahman, which is the head of the great Lord Ganesha. The entire world, which is subject to destruction, is undoubtedly His body. Dhumravarna is the manifestation of the union of these two.

The intellect, consisting of five states of consciousness, resides in His right side, and the Siddhi (attainment) that causes confusion of the mind shines on His left. The Lord Ganesha, the master of both, plays with these two Mayas. Go to Him for refuge, my son, and you shall attain happiness. Through self-realized Yoga, the vision of the Lord of the Ganas is attained; He is the protector of the bliss of the self.

O King, you will never achieve glory. Let Dhundhi, who is the embodiment of both gods and demons, be your refuge. By Him, the gods were established in heaven and humans on earth, while the demons were placed in the netherworlds, all bound by the threefold Dharma. When gods and demons abandon their respective duties and become greedy, He becomes their punisher. When the gods were defeated by the demons, He created you, but you became an obstacle to the gods. You, too, by abandoning Dharma and neglecting the social order, have become intent on the destruction of the gods, O wicked one. Therefore, the Elephant-faced Lord has come to destroy you. He will not destroy you if you remain established in your own Dharma.

When the threefold Dharma is established in the threefold state, this Lord of Intellect resides eternally in the mind. Therefore, take refuge in that Ganesha, who is the form of Brahman and the mover of all gods and demons.

Lord Shiva said: Hearing the words of Shukracharya, I (Ahankara) was filled with joy and respect. Bowing to him again, I spoke to dispel my doubts.

I said: I will take refuge in Dhumravarna as per your command, but please dispel my doubt regarding how He, who is the embodiment of gods and demons, possesses Siddhi and Buddhi, yet is also said to be eternally united with Prakriti (Nature). He is the first to be worshipped by all and is accepted as the Lord of Brahman. Why has the word "Shri" become primary? Leaving aside Siddhi, Buddhi, Shri, and the Omkara, the Lord of the Ganas stands as an alternative. Tell me, O Brahmin, why are all others in the scriptures associated with Prakriti in the form of Shri, while this is a curiosity?

Shukracharya said: The letter 'Ga' represents Siddhi, and the letter 'Na' represents Buddhi. The Lord Ganesha is the master of both and is seen as the embodiment of Yoga. Just as Brahman is not different from Maya, and Maya is indeed Brahman, there is no doubt that Siddhi, Buddhi, and Ganesha are one. This is why He is called the embodiment of Yoga. Siddhi, Buddhi, and Ganesha are the supreme threefold reality for the wise. Consider this, O intelligent one. There is no worship of Siddhi and Buddhi separate from Ganapati; they are not three, but one. By worshipping Ganesha, the threefold is worshipped.

The left side is the form of Prakriti, and the right side is the form of Purusha (the male principle). Know that Siddhi and Buddhi are these, and in their union is the Lord of the Ganas. In all others, Prakriti resides on the left, but in Ganesha, Prakriti is said to be on both sides. The one-formed Maya, consisting of Siddhi and Buddhi, desired to create the world for the sake of diverse experiences. From the body of Siddhi, Shri was born, and from the body of Buddhi, the Prakriti of Omkara emerged. They performed severe penance to realize their own essence. Then, the Brahman residing in their hearts, the mover of Maya, became pleased and revealed His own form. Seeing the one-syllable mantra of the great Lord Ganesha and His radiant, blissful form in their hearts, they pleased Him.

After a thousand divine years, the Lord of the Ganas, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, came to grant them boons. Seeing that Elephant-human form, they were astonished and reflected in their hearts. By the grace of Ganesha and the power of their penance, they understood the mystery. Seeing Him with Siddhi and Buddhi, they rose quickly, bowed, and praised Him.

Shri and Omkara said: Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, the giver of all attainments. Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, who is accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. Salutations to the Lord of Self-Bliss, the creator of creators, the Lord of the orphaned and the sheltered. Salutations to the Supreme Lord, the protector of the supreme, the Supreme Soul, the nectar of the self, the Lord of Obstacles. Salutations to the pot-bellied God, the Lord of Lords, the giver of divinity to the gods, the Elephant-faced one. Salutations to the eldest, the first-worshipped, the Dhundhi, the nameless, the eternal, the Lord of all. Salutations to the giver of Brahman, the Brahman, the form of Brahman, the Lord of speech, the complete Yoga. Salutations to the embodiment of Yoga and peace, the Chintamani, the infinite, the beginningless, the one residing within. How can we praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, the bearer of Yoga and peace, in whom the Vedas and others have found peace? You are not of the mind or speech, yet You are beyond them. Salutations to You, O Lord, may You always be pleased.

Having said this, they bowed. The Lord of the Ganas raised them and said:

Shri Ganesha said: Ask for your desired boons, O great Mayas. I am pleased by your penance and especially by this hymn. This hymn composed by you will grant all attainments to those who read or hear it daily. It grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, leads to the state of Brahman, and increases devotion to Me.

Hearing the words of Ganesha, Shri and Omkara, filled with devotion, said: Grant us steady devotion to You, which provides Yoga and peace. Grant us the power of fulfilled intentions and the service of Your lotus feet. O Lord, just as Siddhi and Buddhi are honored by You, make us Your beloveds. May the entire world of Brahman be under our control by Your grace. May all be enchanted by us through Your favor. May the illusion created by us be destroyed only by Your worship, and not otherwise. May the gods, including Shiva and Vishnu, be filled with illusion and remain engaged in efforts for our sake.

Shri Ganesha said: All these rare boons are granted by Me. You shall enchant this world. The diverse world is created by the two Mayas, Siddhi and Buddhi. I am reflected in that diverse Brahman. My reflection is filled with illusion and diverse states, but through devotion to Me, it becomes free from illusion. You shall be the consorts of My reflection; there is no doubt. Being united with them, O great Mayas, you shall cause both bondage and liberation. I am the eternal Brahman, united with Siddhi and Buddhi; there is no limitation for you, who are the enchantresses.

Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas disappeared. The two Mayas meditated on Him in their hearts. Then, the reflection of Ganesha fell into their hearts, and through that, they divided the four worlds. The Maya that provides external happiness is known as Shri, and the Maya that is the knowledge of the four worlds is known as the Omkara. Ganesha, united with them, plays in various ways. Because He is devoid of illusion, He is the source of all diversity.

Therefore, O great demon, among all living beings who are enchanted, Shri and Omkara are the ones who are free from illusion and are accepted as the primordial. Know that the Lord of Obstacles has done this. Wherever Ganesha is read with the word "Shri," know that He is united with Omkara and is playing with the two Mayas. He holds Shri on His left side and Omkara on His right. The reflection of Ganesha, being the form of Brahman, became the form of all things. Thus, Shiva and others became united with Shri and Omkara. The living beings are described by the knowers of the Vedas as being united with Shri and Omkara. Consider this as the foundation of the primordial Maya. Therefore, abandon the illusion of Shri and Omkara, and worship the Lord of Obstacles. By worshipping Him with Siddhi and Buddhi, you shall attain happiness. Thus, I have told you everything about the actions of Dhumravarna. Go and take refuge in Him, the giver of peace, enjoyment, and liberation.

Shri and Omkara are the creators of diverse states, and Siddhi and Buddhi are the embodiment of the state of Brahman. This is the secret, accepted by all and hidden in the Vedas. Go to Him, O Ahankara, for all attainments.

Thus ends the sixth chapter of the Dhumravarna legend in the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 7 : The Description of the Pacification of Ego

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shiva said: Having heard the words of Shukra, Ahamkara (Ego), being wise and valorous, bowed to him and spoke with folded hands.

Ahamkara said: O Lord, I have been enlightened by you; therefore, I shall now go to Gajanana (Ganesha), who is the embodiment of both gods and demons, the Brahman, and the bestower of all peace.

Having said this, he, accompanied by Shukra and being a knower of the ultimate truth, withdrew his mind from the duality of the world and sought the refuge of the Lord of Yoga. Upon reaching the outskirts of the city, he saw the Great Noose (Pasha). Bowing down with folded hands, he praised the King of Weapons, he who possessed the ego of Ganesha.

Ahamkara said: Salutations to you, O King of Weapons, the embodiment of bondage, and yet the very form of liberation, O ornament of Ganesha’s hand. Delusion is said to be the Maya that binds all living beings, but if one is endowed with the knowledge of Ganesha, one becomes free from all bonds. You are that very weapon of the Great Soul which represents bondage; you are the Great Noose that causes both bondage and liberation. Those who have taken refuge in you have no fear born of bondage. O Weapon, protect me, for I have come to you with reverence. Salutations to you, O Lord of brilliance, the destroyer of the wicked. Salutations to the Noose, the supreme weapon, the Brahman among weapons.

Having praised the Great Noose thus, he bowed down with folded hands. The Noose, being pleased, left him and went to the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha). Whoever reads this hymn will have their bonds destroyed; they will be freed from the noose of Maya and will attain the path of Ganesha.

Seeing the Noose depart, Ahamkara, along with his Guru, went to Dhumravarna. Seeing Him, he bowed down. Rising again, he worshipped the Lord of Obstacles with devotion. Bowing once more, he praised the Lord of All with folded hands.

Ahamkara said: Salutations again and again to You, O Dhumravarna, the Supreme Soul, the Unmanifest, the Primal Seed, and the Supreme Lord. Salutations to You, O God with the large belly, the Lord of the demons, the protector of the lords of the gods. Salutations to You, who are the action, the form of action, and the promoter of various actions. Salutations to You, who are knowledge, the giver of knowledge, and the knowledge of knowledge. Salutations to You, who are the moving, the form of the moving, and the one who dwells in the moving. Salutations to You, who are the stationary, the eternal form of the stationary, and the bearer of that form. Salutations to You, who are beyond both the stationary and the moving. Salutations to You, O Lord of Yoga, the Yogi, and the giver of Yoga. Salutations to You, the giver of Yoga to the yogis, O Chintamani. Salutations to You, the peaceful, the one attainable by the peaceful-minded, the embodiment of peace. Salutations to You, the Lord of the Brahmas, the Brahman, the giver of success and intellect. Salutations to You, the Lord of Success and the Lord of Intellect, and the union of both. Salutations to You, who reside on the mouse, the bearer of the mouse-banner, the four-armed Lord of Svananda. How can I praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, who are in the form of Dhumravarna? You are the Unmanifest, always described in the Vedic discourses. The Vedas became smoke-colored (dim) in their attempt to describe Your form; those who hold the peace of Yoga are certainly unable to describe You. Therefore, the Vedic scholars call You Dhumravarna. I see You, the Dhumravarna, who is difficult for even the yogis to attain. I am blessed and my lineage is sanctified by the sight of Your lotus feet. Having said this, the great demon danced with devotion.

Seeing him immersed in the essence of devotion, the Lord Gajanana spoke to the great soul and embraced him with love.

Dhumravarna said: O great soul, ask for boons; I shall grant them, being pleased by your hymn. O Ahamkara, have no doubt, you shall have no fear. This hymn composed by you will grant all successes and will undoubtedly bestow my devotion. Whoever hears or reads this, O Lord of demons, will have no fear born of ego; it will grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation. I shall grant whatever is desired through the reading of this hymn. He will become dear to me and will always be free from delusion.

Hearing these words of Ganesha, the great demon Ahamkara, having bowed to the Great Soul Dhumravarna, spoke.

Ahamkara said: If You are pleased with me, O Dhumravarna Gajanana, then grant me supreme devotion to Your lotus feet, O Lord. Make me a devotee of Ganesha. Grant me a place and a livelihood that provides for my well-being. Dwelling there, I shall be Your servant, O Gajanana. I do not ask for anything else, O Lord, as I am deluded by the Maya of the Omkara. I shall worship You, who are accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. Enlightened by my Guru and following his words, I shall worship Your form that is full of the peace of Yoga, O Mahodara.

Dhumravarna said: May your devotion to me be firm, and may you be dedicated to my service. You shall live as one dear to Ganesha, there is no doubt. Where my worship does not exist at the beginning of actions, there shall be your place; enjoy the great fruits of action there. Where I am not remembered at the beginning of work, you shall remain firmly established there, and through your demonic nature, cause them to falter constantly. Go to your city, O King of demons, and always protect my devotees. Always inspire them with my ego (the sense of being mine) with reverence. Where I am remembered and worshipped at the beginning of all actions, there you shall act with a virtuous nature, with that very ego. You will be endowed with Ganesha-bhakti and possess my ego; go, go by my command.

Shiva said: Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas fell silent. Ahamkara bowed to Him and went to his city with joy. Realizing this, the other demon kings were filled with sorrow; they left for Patala, trembling with fear. He (Ahamkara), filled with happiness, worshipped the Lord of the Ganas, meditating on the supreme Dhumravarna with a focused mind. He did exactly as the Lord of Obstacles had said, and thus, the demon Ahamkara became peaceful in nature.

Thus ends the seventh chapter of the Dhumravarna-charita in the eighth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 8 : Conclusion of the Dhumravarna Incarnation

Shiva said: O Sages, seeing Him who is the embodiment of peace, the complete one, and who resides in the ego of Ganesha, the gods and Brahmins were filled with astonishment. Then, all of them, filled with devotion, worshipped Dhumravarna. Having bowed to Him again, they all praised Him with folded hands.

The Divine Sages said: Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who are unborn, the one worthy of worship by all from the beginning, the eternal one, the one who is without support, the one whose form is Dhundhi (the seeker), the one who is always unmanifest, and the one who resides in the hearts of all. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who are always free from delusion, the bestower of delusion upon people, the destroyer of delusion for Your own devotees, and the one who possesses the three states of great bliss. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who are the bestower of success and wisdom, the dweller in the city of Ganesha, the one known by self-realization, the protector of the helpless, and the Lord of all lords. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who play by Your own will, who possess attributes and yet are beyond them, O Brahman, the one with the mouse as Your emblem, and the one who is Heramba. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who have an elephant face, a large belly, three eyes, and who disturb the ears (of the wicked) like a winnowing basket, and who are the Lord of the bees (the yogis) who are intoxicated by the nectar of Your devotion. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who have four arms and one tusk, who are adorned with various priceless ornaments, and who are the vessel of all divine qualities. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who created the primal state along with all qualities, who bestow delusion upon the deluded, and who reside in that form as the infinite one. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who, having inspired the creation of the supreme life-force, reside within it as the non-dual essence. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who reside in the deity created by that life-force, which is the essence of Brahman. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who, through the one associated with non-existence, created the bodiless supreme Brahman, the root cause of all, and who reside there as the giver of dwelling. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who reside in the form created by the bodiless one through Your own awakening, O Lord of obstacles. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who reside in the primal existence created by that same essence, which is the "I am" (Soham), and who dwell there as the Supreme Self. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who, through the one inspired by You, created the great point (Bindu), the root of the four, and who reside there through natural yoga. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who created the fourfold body—from the sound-form to the deep sleep, the subtle, and the gross—and play within it. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who are the form of the universe, the source of all, who have infinite faces and signs, and who enjoy the fruits of all moving and non-moving beings. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who are the moving and the non-moving, the creator, the worthy vessel, the destroyer, the light, the sun, and the embodiment of the Goddess. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who are the divine Yaksha, the Sadhya, the human, the Lord of gods, the divine essence, the Lord of directions, and the seed of trees and all things. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all, who are the form of the serpent, the demon, and the various manifestations in the oceans and elsewhere; You play eternally in infinite forms. Yet, O Yogi, You are devoid of these forms, existing always in natural yoga; salutations to You, who are Your own existence. Therefore, the Vedic declarations describe You as "Dhumra" (smoke-colored) because Your form is difficult to describe; hence, the wise call You Dhumravarna. Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all. The Vedas and the great yogis have attained peace in You, the Lord of Brahman, the smoke-colored one. What can we, with our limited understanding, praise You? Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the Lord of all.

Shri Shiva said: Having praised the Lord of Ganesha in this way, the divine sages bowed to Him. The glorious Dhumravarna spoke to those noble ones.

Dhumravarna said: O noble ones, ask for boons; I am pleased with this hymn and will grant them, even those that are very difficult to attain. This hymn composed by you will grant great success and will undoubtedly lead to the state of Brahman. It will grant the happiness of children, grandchildren, and spouses, as well as wealth, grain, longevity, health, and prosperity. Those who read or hear this will have their various desires fulfilled; it grants righteousness, wealth, desire, and liberation to those who worship with it. By reciting it twenty-one times daily for twenty-one days, one can overcome even the most difficult obstacles. There is no doubt that all black magic and external afflictions will be destroyed. I will always grant whatever desire is contemplated by those who praise Me with this supreme hymn. I am pleased by this hymn, O great gods.

Shri Shiva said: Hearing the words of Dhumravarna, the divine sages bowed to Him, and with joyful hearts, they sang the praises of the Lord who grants all success.

The Divine Sages said: O Dhumravarna, O Lord, You have established peace. Because of You, we have all been blessed with boons. Now that ego has been pacified, we shall dwell in our own domains. People, including Brahmins, will now live according to their duties and stages of life. O Lord of Ganas, what more shall we ask? Give us Your devotion, which destroys the fear of ego; we are satisfied with that, O Lord.

Dhumravarna said "So be it" to the Ganas and the sages, and then He vanished. The gods and sages, having bowed to Him, joyfully installed Dhumravarna in that region and worshipped Him with reverence. They then returned to their respective abodes, filled with joy, happy and engaged in their duties, devoted to the Lord of Ganas. I have told you this wonderful story, which grants all success and destroys the fear of ego for those who read or hear it. Thus, the glorious Dhumravarna, having taken various incarnations, made the world free of obstacles through His devotion. The incarnations of Dhumravarna are infinite, and it is impossible for anyone to describe their stories completely. Therefore, the main deeds of His incarnations have been told briefly. O Sanaka and others, worship the Lord of Ganesha with devotion. By doing so, you will undoubtedly become free from ego. He is the Lord of yogis, worshipped by yogis, and the giver of peace to those with peaceful hearts.

Thus ends the eighth chapter of the Mudgala Purana, in the eighth section, concerning the story of Dhumravarna, titled "The Conclusion of the Dhumravarna Incarnation."

Chapter 9 : The Description of the Glory of Dhumravarna

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

The sages led by Sanaka said: O Mahadeva, please describe the glory of the sacred region of Dhumravarna, which grants all desires and brings joy to all.

Lord Shiva said: In the northeastern direction, at the edge of the Himalayas, lies the sacred region of Dhumravarna. Who is capable of fully describing its renowned glory? I shall describe it briefly after bowing to the Lord of the Ganas; listen with focused minds.

Dhumravarna was established with great joy by the divine sages. He is beautiful, possesses a trunk, has four arms, three eyes, one tusk, and holds weapons like the battle-axe. The ego of the Ganesha-devotees is always surrendered there. To his left is Siddhi, and to his right is Buddhi, who is dear to all. In front of the beautiful Dhumravarna stands his mouse.

The region of Ganapa extends for ten yojanas and is square in shape, granting all accomplishments. In front of Dhumravarna, devotees like Mudgala and sages like Shuka worship him and offer praises. Behind him, his attendants, such as Pramoda and Amoda, armed with weapons, serve Dhumravarna.

On his left side, sacred places like Prayaga and holy regions like Kashi reside to serve Dhumravarna. On his right side, the lords of the gods, including Shambhu and Vishnu, along with their attendants and families, always worship the Lord of the Ganas. The serpent kings like Shesha, the great mountains, Gandharvas, Charanas, Siddhas, Vasus, Manus, Adityas, Rudras, Vidyadharas, and Apsaras all reside in that region and worship the elephant-faced Lord.

Whatever is most excellent in the world is present there, filled with devotion to serve Dhumravarna. O Brahmins, it is impossible to describe their dwellings and other details; therefore, I have spoken only a brief summary.

There exists a supreme sacred site that grants great accomplishments, where the remover of obstacles, Dhumravarna, performs his ablutions. By bathing there, a person immediately attains their desires, and at the end of life, they attain liberation without a doubt, regardless of where they may die.

The gods led by Vishnu and sages led by Kashyapa bathe there daily with joyful hearts. Nagas and others from different regions also bathe there, and people reside in that region with great happiness. The sacred sites of Shiva, Vishnu, and others are also present there, granting their respective realms through mere bathing.

By merely seeing Dhumravarna once, a virtuous person immediately attains their desires. By undertaking a pilgrimage there just once, one attains righteousness, prosperity, desire, and liberation, no matter where they may go afterwards.

Dhumravarna was established by the divine sages on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada at midday; that date is considered supreme. People perform a great annual festival there, and on that day, they undertake the pilgrimage. They rejoice with one another, honoring the elephant-faced Lord, and even sages like Shankara feel thrilled.

Those devoted to Ganesha, filled with joy, go there for the pilgrimage, and other devotees like the Vaishnavas also go there with great delight. After completing the pilgrimage, everyone returns to their respective places with joy, remembering Dhumravarna with devotion.

Without a doubt, they all eventually reach the realm of Svananda. The Shaivas and others reach the state of Svananda through their respective paths. O Brahmins, by dying in that region, people attain Svananda, and those who are Ganesha-devotees become one with Brahman.

I have described the glory of Dhumravarna’s region only in part; who could ever describe it in its entirety? O best of sages, all the Ganesha regions in this Brahmanda are manifestations of Brahman. Know without doubt that the various regions granting righteousness, prosperity, desire, and liberation are all manifestations of the four states of existence. The fifth state, described by the sages as the form of Brahman, is the region of Ganapati, wherever it may be situated.

The noble person who listens to, recites, or narrates this glory of the region shall attain all their goals.

Thus ends the ninth chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Description of the Glory of Dhumravarna."

Chapter 10 : Story of the Shiva-Incarnation

The Sanakas and others asked: O Lord, the incarnations of Dhumravarna are known to be of many kinds. Please tell us about the most important ones, along with their stories. Having heard the story of Dhumravarna, we are not satisfied; we wish to know more from you, O Teacher of the World, as you are the one who removes ego and is complete.

Lord Shiva said: O best of sages, it is not possible to describe all the stories of the various incarnations of Dhumravarna. However, I will tell you a little about the most significant ones; listen to this with a steady mind. O great sages, the state that is called natural and described as "not this" (neti) is the Brahman, which is the expression of bliss. From that, truth and untruth were created, and from them, the creators (Brahmas) arose. They are known as the creators of the self and the beyond. From that, the Brahman, which is full of bodies, was created, and from that, the four-fold body was manifested. The gross, the subtle, the causal, and the sound were created, and from them, the universal form was manifested. From that, O sages, the individual forms were created.

These forms of Brahman were born, O great sages, but they were devoid of knowledge and incapable of performing any tasks. Then, they performed penance, constantly chanting the mantra that is the seed of all, saying "Salutations to whoever it may be." After a hundred years, Gajanana (Ganesha) was pleased and showed them his supreme form, which is made of mantras. By his grace, they saw in their hearts the single-syllable mantra, which is full of brilliance and supreme knowledge. Seeing the mantra, the Brahmas were delighted and bowed with respect. Having grasped the mantra and meditated on Gajanana in their hearts, they meditated on Ganesha in the form of the mantra and pleased the remover of obstacles.

After a thousand divine years, Gajanana was pleased and showed them his supreme form, which is worthy of meditation by yogis. Seeing the human-elephant form, they were amazed in their hearts, and Dhundhi (Ganesha) gave them pure knowledge. Through that, they understood the form of the Lord of Ganas, which is both with attributes and without attributes, and is full of yoga. Then, O best of sages, they were filled with great joy and constantly meditated on the Lord of Obstacles and performed japa.

Then, Ganapati, being pleased, went to them out of great devotion, assuming the form of Shiva, the five-faced, three-eyed Lord. He was Shambhu, wearing a garland of skulls, clad in tiger skin, his body smeared with sacred ash, and adorned with the serpent Shesha. He was the one whose flag is the bull, who rides the bull, who wears the moon on his head, who holds the trident, and who is the Lord of Yogis, worshipped by yogis, with Girija (Parvati) on half of his body. Seeing Maheshana (Shiva) arrived, the Brahmas were astonished and began to deliberate with respect. By the grace of Ganesha, they immediately understood the entire glory through the knowledge in Shiva's heart, and they said to each other:

The Brahman that resides in its own glory is naturally in the form of Ganapa. His complexion is smoky (dhumra), hence he is known as Dhumrakshara. His reflection, due to delusion and combined with Maya, fell into the worlds of Brahman, and that is this Lord of the Bull. Because of the influence of the reflection and the original, he is called Dhumravarna. There is no difference in the scriptural arguments; he will be the bestower of boons.

Then, those Brahmas rose, bowed to Ganapa again, and especially bowed to Shiva who resides in the heart. Having worshipped Shambhu according to the rites, they bowed and praised him, the one who is the form of Brahman and the source of bliss.

The Brahmas said: Salutations to you, O Dhumravarna, who bears the form of Ganesha. Salutations to you, O Shambhu, who is the reflection and Shiva himself. Salutations to you, O Lord of Parvati, who possesses immeasurable power. Salutations to you, O Lord with the bull-flag, the natural one. Salutations to you, O Lord of Maya, the mover of Maya, the Supreme Soul. Salutations to you, O Maheshwara, the complete one, Heramba. Salutations to you, O wearer of matted hair, the ever-pure Sadashiva, the Lord of Lords, the wielder of the trident. Salutations to you, the self-governed, the five-faced one. Salutations to you, the Lord of the helpless and the protector, the Lord in the form of the Lord. Salutations to you, the form of Supreme Brahman, the Lord of Brahma and others. Salutations to you, the Lord of the mountain, who sleeps on the mountain, who wears the ash of the funeral pyre on his body. Salutations to you, the wearer of the skull garland and the serpent ornament. Salutations to you, who plays in various forms. Salutations to you, who is untruth on the left side and truth on the right side, and who is the bliss in the union of both. You are the great Maya in equal form; there is no doubt that your body is that. You are the soul of that; salutations to you, the half-woman form. We see such a Maheshana, the form of Brahman, the creator of "not this" for all; we are blessed because of him. You are Dhumravarna himself; no difference is ever seen. Who is capable of praising you, O Lord? Therefore, we bow to you.

Lord Shiva said: Having said this, the Brahmas bowed to Maheshana with respect. The compassionate Shankara spoke to them with joy.

Adi-Shambhu said: O Brahmas, ask for the boons you desire; I will grant them through your penance. I am pleased with your praise and devotion; there is no doubt in my heart about your desires.

The Brahmas said: O Lord, since you are the giver of boons, grant us supreme devotion to you, and grant us the memory of knowledge in our tasks, O Maheshwara. After performing our respective duties, O remover of bonds, make us always free from bonds, O holder of yoga.

Lord Shiva said: Whatever you have requested from me will be blissful. Since your resolve is true, what is there that cannot happen? Listen to the incomparable yoga that leads to freedom from bonds and brings happiness. By attaining that, you will undoubtedly become free from bonds. Know me as the fourth, the creator of "not this" for all. The wise know the self-nature in the union of the four. Therefore, know that the self-governed form and the three-fold dependent form are born from a part of his essence; know that as Gajanana. Dhundhi is declared to be of his own nature, combined with Maya. Because he is the mover of the four, he is the Lord through the non-difference of the union. The non-union, devoid of the five, is always the indicator of liberation. There is no union of the Brahmas there, nor of the worlds in them. In the union of union and non-union, Ganesha is the indicator of yoga. Gajanana is the direct form of Brahman, the bestower of complete peace. The letter 'Ga' is called his own form, and 'Na' is called yoga. The Lord of both is Ganesha, who bears the human-elephant form. Worship him especially, and you will attain peace. You will always be free from bonds, and not otherwise. Just as a smoky region is not seen by humans, yet it is visible due to the smoky obstruction, so too is the Maya of the mind, which is engaged in five-fold variety. Combined with that, they do not see that God through the means of the Vedas. The Vedas are said to be made of letters, and they became smoky due to Maya. Therefore, they spoke of Ganesha as Dhumravarna to their disciples. Know the yoga that grants complete peace, which is of the nature of Dhumravarna; worship that Gajanana through the technique of yoga. Always performing your respective duties while remaining in your own nature, and offering them to Dhumravarna, you will always be free from bonds.

Having said this, Mahadeva disappeared right there. The Brahmas, having bowed to him, created the diverse world. Following the yoga as instructed, those Brahmas, filled with yoga, worship Ganesha daily with respect. This is called the Shiva-incarnation, which is most wonderful, a part of the essence of Dhumravarna, and the bestower of all accomplishments. Whoever hears this story or reads it, after enjoying pleasures, will eventually go to his own blissful abode.

Thus ends the tenth chapter named the Story of the Shiva-Incarnation in the Dhumravarna-Charita of the eighth section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 11 : Parvati-Ganesha Charitam

Lord Shiva said: O Sages, once when I was residing on Mount Kailash along with Parvati, Nandi saw me worshipping Ganesha and spoke to me.

Nandi said: O Shiva, you are the form of everything and are known as the Lord of all. You are the bestower of fruits for all actions and the sustainer of karma. You are the Lord of Yoga for all yogis and the provider of yogic accomplishments. There is no one greater than you, so why do you worship Ganesha? In the Vedas, Ganesha is declared as your son; there is no doubt about this. Why do you, O Lord, worship your own son as if he were your master? Your name, Sadashiva, is famous because you are the Lord of all. There is no Lord above you in the Vedas; you alone are the creator of all beings. O Shankara, why do you worship Ganesha along with Parvati, causing confusion among the people, and for what secret Shaivite reason? Do not worship Ganesha in vain, O Lord. This confuses the devotees of Shiva and leads them toward improper paths.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Nandi, Sadashiva, with eyes blossoming with joy, spoke to him about the supreme Pashupata knowledge.

Lord Shiva said: O Nandi, you are devoted to me, but you do not know the Lord of Ganesha. See, O wise one, that I am also a form of his Ganas (attendants). The Ganas are the collective forms of that great soul, and I, the Lord of all, am considered a Gana of Ganapati. Ganesha alone is the Lord, and all others are considered his Ganas. O respected one, he is the form of Brahman and the form of the universe, the Lord of Brahma. Knowledge is said to be of many types, and his forms are also considered manifold. These forms of knowledge, which illuminate wisdom, have emerged specifically from him. I alone am called the Lord of Knowledge by the Vedic scholars. By taking refuge in this Yoga-knowledge, I have manifested Ganesha. The supreme Brahman born of Samadhi (deep meditation) is called the son. Therefore, Ganesha, who was born from my Samadhi, attained the status of my son. Other gods like Vishnu, who are endowed with knowledge through learning, are not called the Lords of Knowledge; they are my disciples, there is no doubt. Through the power of Yoga, they also manifested Ganesha, and thus he became the son of those yogis as well. In the beginning, I realized Brahman without a guru through the power of knowledge; therefore, I am the chief guru and protector of the world. That is why Ganesha is called the son of Shiva in the Vedas. Vishnu and others are not the primary ones; they are considered the fathers of Ganesha. Therefore, I worship that Lord of Brahman, who is worshipped by Brahma himself, as my master, not out of delusion, but out of reverence.

Hearing the words of Shiva, Nandi was astonished and spoke to the omniscient and universally worshipped Mahadeva.

Nandikeshwara said: There is no doubt that Ganesha is our family deity. O Lord, please tell me the knowledge and the supreme mantra associated with him according to the scriptures.

Lord Shiva said: The mind is said to be of five types, and that intellect is considered supreme. The various illusions and accomplishments are full of duality and pride in the heart. The reflection of Ganesha falls upon these two, mixed with Maya. Abandoning the reflection, Ganesha becomes the human. By discarding the worldly mind, you will become worthy of being worshipped by yogis. Worship him with the single-syllable mantra through yogic practices. 

Having said this, Shiva gave him the supreme single-syllable mantra. Nandi, having bowed to him, went to the forest desiring penance. Nandi, with a focused mind and deep meditation, worshipped the elephant-headed Lord with fasting and penance. After a hundred years had passed, he became a master yogi. Then Ganesha, the bestower of boons, arrived at his hermitage. Seeing the Lord of Ganas arrive, Nandi was filled with joy; he stood up, bowed, and worshipped him with devotion. Again, bowing to Vighnesha, he began to praise him. He was astonished to see Parvati on his left side. By the grace of Ganesha, he immediately attained wonderful knowledge, through which he understood all the actions of Ganesha with reverence. Then Nandi thought in his heart: There is no doubt that this is Mahadeva himself, assuming an external form. He is the husband of Girija, marked by five faces and other signs, three-eyed, and appearing in a human body to show different states of being. That Parvati is the form of bliss, the form of Vishnu, and is said to be the essence of Shiva; the soul within her is considered Shiva. The elephant-form is Shambhu himself, intent on the enjoyment of unity. Because of residing in the half-body of Prakriti, Shiva appeared as both woman and man. In the union of the human and the elephant, he attained the form of Ganesha. Shiva is directly the embodiment of Brahman, accompanied by Girija. The beautiful Ganesha, who is the embodiment of Yoga-Maya, appeared as Parvati and the Lord of Parvati himself. There is no doubt that he came to grant a boon. He resides in the heart of Shiva, and she is the mental creation of Shiva. 

Then, bowing with joy, he prostrated before the elephant-faced Lord again and again, and with hair standing on end, he praised him with folded hands.

Nandikeshwara said: Salutations to Ganesha, the giver of peace, the form of peace, the holder of peace. Salutations to the one who resides in the hearts of humans and the hearts of those with elephant forms, the Lord of Yoga. Salutations to Heramba, who dwells in Siddhi and Buddhi, the giver of self-bliss, the rider of the mouse. Salutations to the husband of Parvati, who resides in the heart of Shiva. Salutations to the Lord of Lakshmi, whose form resides in the heart of Vishnu. Salutations to the Lord of consciousness, who resides in the heart of the Sun. Salutations to the Lord of the Primordial Energy, whose form resides in the heart of Shakti. Salutations to the husband of Savitri, who plays in the heart of Brahma. Salutations to the husband of Gayatri, who dwells in the heart of Omkara. Salutations to the husband of Shachi, whose form resides in the heart of Indra. Salutations to the Lord of Svaha, the supreme Lord who resides in the heart of Agni. Salutations to you in human form, the propagator of various joys. Salutations to you, the human in all diverse states. Salutations to the formless one, the elephant-shaped one, who resides in all non-dual joys and formless states. Salutations to you, the human-elephant form, the form of Yoga. Salutations to the Lord of Yogas, the embodiment of complete Yoga. I am blessed and fulfilled, for I have seen the elephant-faced Lord. How can I praise the Brahman, the Lord of Brahman? Salutations to you, may you be the giver of boons.

Having praised Ganesha in this way, Nandi danced with love. The Lord of Ganas, who is fond of his devotees, spoke to him.

Lord Ganesha said: O fortunate one, ask for the boons you desire in your heart. I am pleased with your penance and this praise, and I will grant them. This hymn composed by you will be the giver of Yoga. It will grant worldly enjoyment to those who desire it and liberation to those who seek Moksha. It will grant all accomplishments to those who read or listen to it always. Whatever they desire, they will surely obtain through this.

Nandikeshwara said: If you are the giver of boons, then grant me devotion to you. O Lord, make me a devotee of Vighnesha. May this place where I performed penance be your abode, O Lord. 

Saying "So be it," the Lord of Ganas disappeared. Then Nandi, along with the Brahmins, installed Ganesha and worshipped him with great devotion. O Sages, this place was named Parvati-Ganapa, and it became a place that grants all accomplishments. Then Nandi bowed and returned, and told the whole story. Hearing it, Shiva was pleased and praised him. Nandi worshipped Vighnesha daily with devotion and became a great master yogi, equal to Shiva. 

I have narrated this story of Ambika-Vighnesha to you. This Ganesha, who is of smoke-colored form, is the giver of all accomplishments. Whoever reads or listens to this story of Ganapati will obtain everything they desire, including worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Thus ends the eleventh chapter named Parvati-Ganesha Charitam in the eighth section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 12 : Description of the Incarnation of Ganesha

Lord Shiva said: There was a demon named Tripura who possessed immense strength and valor. By the boon of Brahma, he conquered all moving and non-moving beings. He caused the cessation of all religious duties and forced humans to engage in acts of intermingling of castes. Distressed by this, the gods sought my refuge. Filled with anger, I went to fight him. A fierce and terrifying battle took place between me and the demon, and between the gods and the demons, which made the hair stand on end. I released all my destructive weapons with great speed, but O great sages, they all proved futile. I became astonished and filled with sorrow, and suddenly, I was defeated by the demon king’s fire-weapon. Leaving Kailasa, I went to a dense forest, sighing and filled with anxiety, having lost all my enthusiasm.

I pondered in my mind, O Brahmins, seeking a beneficial solution. There is no doubt that this demon should die by my hand. Then why has my effort been rendered useless, and by whom? I, who am the Lord, have become powerless, like the sun losing its light. Just then, the divine sage Narada arrived there. I bowed to him and worshipped him with devotion. Then, filled with great sorrow, I asked the sage Narada, and he, being wise, spoke to me for the sake of slaying Tripura.

Narada said: O Shiva, you are deluded and held by ego. By not worshipping the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha), you have become devoid of power, O Lord. The obstacles are said to be the very essence of existence, and their master is Ganesha. Your lordship has been lost now because you did not worship Dhundhi (Ganesha), O Lord. Those who, being deluded, do not know Ganesha, who is the source of the power residing in their own bodies, become powerless. By the mere glance of whose grace, one creates, sustains, and destroys the entire universe, you are devoid of that grace, O Sadashiva. If you are unable to destroy even a blade of grass, how will you defeat the demon? Therefore, take refuge in Him. Endowed with His power, you will slay the great demon Tripura, who is engrossed in the three types of worldly attachments.

Mudgala said: Having said this to Maheshwara, the great sage Narada bowed to him and went away as he pleased, chanting the glory of Ganapa. Then, Shiva, remembering Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, in his heart, performed penance in the Dandaka forest. After a hundred years had passed, the Lord of the Ganas arrived, seated on a mouse, holding ten weapons. Seeing him, Shankara suddenly stood up and bowed. Having worshipped him, he praised Ganesha with great joy and devotion.

Lord Shiva said: Salutations again and again to Ganesha, the Supreme Lord, the smoke-colored one, Dhundhi, the son of Shiva, the mother and father of all. Salutations to Ganesha, the creator of qualities, the vessel of greatness, the destroyer, the Lord of gods, Heramba. Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, who possesses infinite wealth, who is the mover of infinite illusion, and who resides in the infinite. Salutations to the eldest of the eldest, the form of the eldest, the one who is to be worshipped by all, and the one who is to be worshipped before all. Salutations to you, the Lord of Svananda, the bearer of the mouse-banner, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the Lord of the ocean of sugarcane juice. Salutations to you, who are beyond mind and speech, yet who are the embodiment of mind and speech, the Lord of Yoga, the son of Maha-Vishnu. Salutations to the son of Shesha, the one who resides in the field of the three worlds, the deity of the Dandaka forest, the Lord of Brahma. I see that Lord of the Ganas, by whom the Vedas and yogis attain peace; I am blessed in every way. My eyes are blessed by your vision, my head by bowing to you, and my mouth by praising you. My hands are blessed by your worship; everything of mine is blessed by the sight of your lotus feet, O Gajanana. Grant me your devotion, O Lord of the Ganas, so that I may become fulfilled and established in peaceful yoga. Give me eternal strength to slay Tripura, O Maheshwara, make me, the Lord of the Ganas, successful forever.

Hearing the words of Shiva, Ganesha, who was greatly pleased by his penance, spoke to him.

Lord Ganesha said: Everything you have said will be fulfilled, O Sadashiva. Now listen to what else I say for the destruction of Tripura. The supreme illusion is of three types, engrossed in bodily pleasures. That which is called the Tripura of the demons is addicted to various sense objects. For its victory, you must take refuge in my fourth form, which is the form of Ganapa, the masculine principle. Accept my masculine, seed-like form, O Sadashiva. Cast the three-fold illusion upon me, O wise one.

Having said this, Ganesha gave him the masculine mantra. By taking his illusion, he made Shiva free from delusion. Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, immediately disappeared. By the power of the mantra, Shiva slew the demon Tripura. He remained forever endowed with the masculine essence. Having conquered the illusion, he brought it under his control.

Whoever listens to or reads this hymn composed by Shiva in praise of Ganesha becomes free from delusion in all aspects and becomes devoted to the Divine. Endowed with sons and grandsons, wealth and grain, and having enjoyed various pleasures, one finally attains Svananda (the supreme bliss). I have narrated this greatness of Ganesha briefly. Reading or hearing it grants all accomplishments to humans. This incarnation is known as the smoke-colored one. It is the full incarnation of Ganesha, the bestower of worldly success and liberation, and the giver of the fruits of human endeavor.

Chapter 13 : The Granting of Boons to Banasura

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shiva said: Bali had a son named Bana, a demon of great righteousness. He was a devotee of Shiva, possessed of virtuous qualities, and very powerful. From his childhood, he was devoted to Shambhu (Shiva) and recognized no other deity, remaining steadfast in his own duty. Shukracharya gave him the five-syllable mantra and instructed him in the method of worshipping Shankara, inspiring him toward penance. He performed severe penance, meditating on me with devotion, and after a thousand years, I was pleased with him.

Mudgala said: Shankara, the ocean of compassion, went to grant him a boon. Bana worshipped him according to the rites with hymns dedicated to Rudra. Pleased, the Lord said, "O wise one, ask for your boons. Satisfied by your penance and devotion, I shall grant them, O king of demons."

Banasura said: "O Mahadeva, you are the granter of boons. Grant me steady devotion to you. Make me a devotee of Shiva, so I may not be devoted to any other god. Grant me a thousand arms and wonderful strength, such that no one in the moving or unmoving world can equal me in battle." Mahadeva said "So be it" to his excellent devotee and returned to his city with Parvati and his attendants.

Thereafter, the demon Banasura conquered Vishnu and other gods and ruled the kingdom, being naturally righteous. He established the gods in heaven, the great demons in the netherworld, and the Nagas and humans on earth, ruling with justice. He protected all according to their caste and stage of life. Always residing in Kailasa, he worshipped Lord Shankara. Pleased by his righteousness and devotion, Shambhu came to his city to reside with his attendants.

Then, the demon thought in his heart, "Shankara has manifested in the form of the five-fold worship (Panchayatana). I shall worship Shambhu with single-minded devotion; I know no other Lord than Shankara." He believed that by worshipping Shankara, all the moving and unmoving world is worshipped. However, he remembered that in the Vedas, it is said that the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) must be worshipped first. He pondered, "If I worship another deity, how can my single-mindedness remain?"

Thinking thus, the demon king abandoned the worship of Ganesha and worshipped only Shambhu with great reverence. After a full year of worshipping Shiva, he danced before him in his city of Shonitapura. Pleased, the benevolent Shiva said, "Ask for a boon, O great warrior." Hearing this, the son of Bali bowed with great devotion and said, "O Lord, give me a warrior equal to me in strength, who can withstand the power of my thousand arms."

Hearing this, Shambhu became angry and cursed him, saying, "Your kingdom will be lost, and your arms will be severed. You will fight with Vasudeva (Krishna), O wretched demon. Why do you ask this of me? You will be defeated." Hearing this, Bana was filled with grief and bowed to him. He returned home and worshipped Shukracharya, telling him what had happened. Shiva left Kailasa in anger, and the demon king became mentally disturbed.

Shukracharya consoled him and said, "O demon king, you abandoned Ganesha to worship only Shankara; you have acted against the Vedas. The Vedas declare him the Lord of the Elders (Jyeshtharaja); he is the one who must be worshipped first. Just as Sandhya-vandana is mandatory, so is Ganesha to be worshipped first by those seeking single-mindedness. Because you abandoned the Lord of the Ganas, he became angry and clouded your intellect. He is the master of Siddhi and Buddhi. How can you attain the grace of Shiva after abandoning him? Even Shiva resides in his heart. You have been cursed by the great Lord himself. Go and take refuge in Ganesha, the leader of the Brahmanas; then you will be free from obstacles."

After Shukracharya left, Bana was filled with remorse and pondered. He realized the greatness of Ganesha. On the fourth day (Chaturthi), the demon king worshipped Ganesha. He meditated on Ganesha and chanted the best mantra, seeking forgiveness. He worshipped him with the Atharva-upanishad hymns. Pleased, Ganesha, who is fond of his devotees, appeared before him riding his mouse.

Seeing the Lord of the Ganas, Bana was overjoyed. He rose, bowed, and worshipped him with devotion. He prayed: "O Lord of obstacles, O supreme Lord, destroyer of the devotee's obstacles, O creator of obstacles for the non-devotee, salutations to you. O Vinayaka, O Lord of the demon king, salutations to you. O Lord of the Elders, O first-worshipped, salutations. O Lambodara, O mother and father of the universe, salutations to Heramba. O one with elephant ears, destroyer of the darkness of delusion, salutations to the peaceful one. O one-tusked Lord, dweller in self-bliss, salutations. O Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, you are the goal of the yogis. Everything is under your control. I abandoned you out of ignorance, desiring only Shiva. Shiva cursed me and left in anger. Protect me, O Lord of obstacles, and forgive my grave sins."

Ganesha said, "This hymn you have composed will grant all success. I am pleased. Although you abandoned me and were cursed by Shiva, I cannot change the curse of Shiva. However, hear my words: you will fight Vasudeva and your arms will be severed. You will lose your kingdom, but you will regain it soon."

Bana said, "Ensure that I never forget you, O Lord. I will worship Shiva along with you." Ganesha replied, "You will be endowed with the knowledge of Ganesha. In this body, you will become a devotee of Shiva. You will be known as Mahakala in all worlds, and when your time with Shiva ends, you will attain my abode of bliss." Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas disappeared. Bana was filled with joy and worshipped Ganesha thereafter.

Chapter 14 : The Story of Banasura and the Origin of Colors

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Lord Shiva said: Through grace, the granting of boons, and the worship of Ganesha, Shiva’s own intellect became filled with divine wisdom. Accompanied by Parvati, Skanda, and the Ganas, Shiva immediately returned to the city of Banasura. Upon learning of his arrival, Banasura was filled with joy; he meditated upon the elephant-headed deity and went to meet Shiva. He prostrated himself fully before Shiva and the Ganas, wept, worshipped him, and begged for forgiveness. Shiva lifted his devotee, embraced him, and spoke sweet words to delight the king of the demons.

Lord Shiva said: Do not worry in vain. I am satisfied and have come here. Ask for boons, O great one, and I shall grant them, O son of Bali.

Banasura said: Grant me steady devotion to you, and may I never be separated from you. O Mahadeva, O Shiva, please ensure this always.

Shiva agreed and stayed in that city forever. Thereafter, a daughter endowed with all auspicious signs was born to him. She was named Usha, and she married Aniruddha. She had seen him in a dream and, tormented by love, longed for him. Chitralekha brought him to her and gave her the husband she desired. By her illusion, she deluded the Yadavas and rejoiced thereafter. Aniruddha married her, and being deeply infatuated, he lived with her in great joy.

Seeing the son of Kamadeva missing at night, the Yadavas were extremely distressed and could not find his whereabouts. Pradyumna prayed to the Lord of the Ganas, and through the grace of his penance, Narada arrived there. He narrated everything that had happened to the son of Kamadeva. Hearing this, all the Yadavas were overjoyed and became powerful. Krishna, along with Balarama and the radiant Yadavas, resolved to defeat Banasura, who was united with Shiva.

Fearing Shiva, Krishna was hesitant, so Uddhava said: O Krishna, perform the supreme worship of Ganesha. By doing so, you will be free from obstacles, and Shiva will be unable to defeat you in battle. Krishna then performed the Purascharana of the single-syllable mantra, meditating on Ganapati according to the scriptures. Accompanied by the Yadavas, he went to where Banasura resided and blew his conch. Shiva also came forth to fight.

Shiva, filled with sorrow, gave Banasura the armor of Ganapati, which originated from the Sama Veda. Because of the curse Shiva had placed on the Asura regarding the severing of his hands, Krishna fought with that mighty demon king. Seeing Banasura distressed, Shiva came to fight. Seeing him, Krishna remembered the Lord of Obstacles. A great battle ensued between the two, resembling the destruction of the three worlds; it is beyond my ability to describe the battle between Shiva and Vasudeva.

Then, by the power of the Jrimbhasthra (yawning weapon), Krishna deluded Sadashiva and released his discus to sever Banasura’s arms. With the razor-sharp discus, the arms were severed on all sides, leaving only two, and the discus became blunt. Krishna, feeling troubled, remembered the Lord of Obstacles. He heard a voice from the sky, prompted by Ganapati: "Protected by the armor of Ganesha and by Shiva, you will not be able to kill Banasura in battle. His arms were given by Shiva and severed by Shiva's curse; the two remaining arms are protected by the armor."

Then, filled with joy, Krishna withdrew his discus and stood in the battle, freed from the effects of the Jrimbhasthra. Through supreme wisdom, I understood the play of the Lord of Obstacles. Krishna bowed to me, and I embraced Janardana. The demon king performed the marriage according to the rites and, overwhelmed with love, gave his daughter to Aniruddha. Krishna then returned to his own city, filled with joy. Banasura renounced his kingdom and became a devotee of Mahakala. Rising early on Kailasa, he worshipped the elephant-faced deity, especially with Shami, Mandara, Durva grass, and red flowers. Having worshipped Ganapati first and then Shiva, the great Gana remained constantly absorbed in meditation, pleasing both of them.

Shaunaka asked: O Suta, how did the red color become so dear to the Lord of Obstacles, and how does he become pleased by it? Ganesha himself is the Lord of the Vedas, the one who wears red garments, is adorned with red sandalwood, wears red flowers, is smeared with vermilion, and plays in the red ocean. Tell us the supreme reason for this. Since he is the embodiment of all colors, how did he come to be associated with one? O wise one, remove this doubt of ours.

Suta replied: At the beginning of creation, five colors emerged: white, black, red, yellow, and blue. Through mutual distinction and combination, infinite shades were created. These five performed severe penance for the Lord of the Ganas. After a hundred years, the Lord of the Ganas appeared before them. Seeing him, all the colors bowed and praised him, and they asked for boons according to their own beauty. They said to the Lord of the Ganas: "O Lord of the Ganas, be the one in whom we reside; salutations to you. Through you, we become fulfilled and, free from the bonds of illusion, we worship you constantly."

Ganesha agreed and disappeared. Thereafter, all the colors were happy and engaged in their respective tasks. Everything became adorned with various colors, and the entire animate and inanimate world became colorful. Kashyapa, the Lord of the Ganas, took on a blue form; Mayuresha took on a white form. The son of Varenya, the elephant-faced one, was red; he also appeared in smoke-colored and black forms. Haridra Ganapati was yellow. He assumed various forms and various colors.

Thus, through the granting of boons, all the colors were engaged in the worship of Ganesha. Then, the red color performed great penance again, fasting and meditating on the elephant-faced one in its heart. After a thousand years, Ganapati appeared to grant boons. The red color, filled with joy, worshipped and praised him with various hymns dear to Ganapati. Pleased, the Lord of Obstacles said: "Ask for the boons you desire, O red one."

The red color said: "O Lord of the Ganas, may you reside in the red form. May you always be fond of red, O Lord, even while you are the Lord of many colors. By this, I shall be the best among all colors, and I shall always be fulfilled, residing in your presence." Ganesha agreed and disappeared. From that day on, in the Vedas and scriptures, he became fond of red objects.

Whoever listens to or reads this story of Banasura and the origin of colors, the Lord of the Ganas will become the granter of boons to them.

Thus ends the fourteenth chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Story of Banasura and the Origin of Colors."

Chapter 15 : Description of the Buddha Incarnation

Sanaka and the other sages said: O Lord, having heard from your lotus-like mouth the nectar-like story that removes birth and death, is complete, and bestows all happiness, we are not yet satisfied. O wise one, please tell us more about the supreme story of Lord Ganesha’s incarnation.

Lord Shiva said: Long ago, in order to initiate the Kali Yuga, Brahma went to Vishnu. He praised the Lord of Lakshmi and stated his purpose. Brahma said: O Janardana, I am creating Kali, the embodiment of sin. People will not always honor him with joy. The Dvapara Yuga has ended, and the time for Kali has arrived. O Lord of gods, you are the protector of all; please set him in motion.

Hearing Brahma’s words, Keshava replied: I do not welcome Kali, who is of a terrible, sinful nature. I shall incarnate to uphold Dharma; I will never welcome Kali, who destroys righteousness. Then the four-faced Brahma said to Lord Keshava: O Janardana, it has been foretold that you will take the Buddha incarnation. Behold the Puranic tradition regarding your incarnation that initiates Kali, which is established in your own Dharma and dependent upon you.

Hearing Brahma’s words, Vishnu sighed and said, "So be it." With a heavy heart, Brahma returned to his abode. Then, the Lord performed great penance, meditating in his heart upon the elephant-faced Lord using the six-syllabled mantra. After ten years, the Lord of the Ganas, the bestower of boons, became pleased. The Lord of devotees, who is fond of his devotees, appeared before his great devotee. Seeing him arrived, Vishnu worshipped the remover of obstacles and praised him with the essence of the Heramba Upanishad, his neck bowed in devotion.

Then, the elephant-faced Lord, being pleased and compassionate, said: O noble one, ask for the boons that are firmly in your mind. Hearing Ganesha’s words, Vishnu, with folded hands and bowing down to Ganesha, the bestower of all success, said: O Lord, grant me your devotion, which bestows all success. In the Kali Yuga, I shall become the Buddha for the sake of the decline of Dharma. O Lord of the Ganas, I always establish Dharma, but you have declared me to be the Buddha for the sake of the decline of Dharma. This contradiction has come to me, O Dhundhi, O ocean of mercy. For that reason, I have taken refuge at your feet. Therefore, O Lord of obstacles, protect me from this contradictory conduct. You alone, O Vinayaka, make me a protector of Dharma.

Lord Ganesha said: Do not fear, O Vishnu; you will be endowed with Dharma. There is no fault in you for initiating Kali, O great soul. I have created the four Yugas in sequence; by my boon, all have flourished in their respective times. Among them, I have established the highly sinful Kali. Become the Buddha with respect, acting as a follower of my command. Giving up the ego that "I am the doer," offer your actions to me and perform your duties accordingly. Behold the Chintamani in your heart; the world is subject to its will. Consider who you are in the midst of this diverse creation.

The speech consisting of Vedas and Shastras is created by Ganesha. Living beings must act by following his command. The pleased Lord of the Ganas will grant his supreme abode to all living beings who are destined for him; there is no doubt in this. That Ganesha, of smoky complexion, who plays with the Kali Yuga, himself made Vishnu take the form of Buddha for that purpose. Thus, O great Vishnu, remain subject to my command. Perform actions that are outside of Dharma, but offer them to me, and you will attain happiness.

Having said this, the Lord of Brahma, Ganesha, disappeared. O great sages, Vishnu, filled with joy, became the Buddha. He created the great Buddhist scripture, which causes delusion and bestows hell upon those who follow it, for the sake of increasing the faults of Kali.

Sanaka and the others asked: O all-knowing one, tell us what that Buddhist scripture is like, so that by knowing it, people may become free from delusion.

Lord Shiva said: Listen, O sages, to the Buddhist scripture that leads to hell. It provides happiness to those who have abandoned the Varna and Ashrama duties. The Lord of Intellect himself resides in the hearts of living beings. He is the mover of the five minds of even the Brahmas and the Ishvaras. He is called Vinayaka, the Lord of all. Being without a master, he is eternal and acts according to his own will. By his will, Brahman itself appeared in various forms, engaged in various sports, and relied on the power of various illusions. In that state, there is no sin or virtue, no flowers or offerings; it is the fourth state, without doubt.

There is no action that grants heaven, nor is there any hell for Brahman. Abandoning all that causes delusion, a man should become one with Brahman. By knowing him who gives intellect in the heart and is the player of all games, a man will always be free from delusion. One should certainly do whatever brings happiness to the mind; one should never torment the body with Varna and Ashrama duties. The body was created by that Brahman for the sake of enjoyment and bliss; that body must be constantly nourished with various efforts. When the body suffers, the intellect becomes distressed, and the Lord of Intellect residing within also becomes distressed.

Always holding the thought "I am Brahman," one should be free to act as one wishes. Being free from sin and merit, one should focus on the nourishment of the body. The mind should engage in various objects; one should enjoy them, abandoning the Varna and Ashrama system. Actions involving violence to living beings should never be performed. Even blades of grass and the like should not be cut after consideration. Wise men should not perform rituals like the worship of gods. There is no Svaha or Svadha, as one realizes "I am Brahman." One should never give gifts or food to Brahmins; all are equal, so how can there be distinctions like the twice-born? O best of sages, wherever the mind finds satisfaction, that should be known as the best recipient.

O great sages, such is the Buddhist scripture, full of various opinions; it should be known that it is full of the faults of Kali and leads to hell. Abandoning the Dharma of Varna and Ashrama, one should act according to one's own will. Non-violence is the highest principle; this is the secret of Buddhism. Deluding everyone, the soul of the universe, the Buddha, the initiator of Kali, resides in the Kaikata country in a hidden form. Devoted to the meditation of Ganesha and offering his actions to Dhundhi, he became a follower of his command, even while practicing Karma Yoga.

Nevertheless, because he created a path contrary to the Vedas, Vishnu, in his Buddha form, became devoid of worship. At the end of Kali, those deluded by Buddhist illusion, having fallen from Varna and Ashrama, will be found in every house. O sages, Dharma will exist there only in a small measure, and it will be conquered by Adharma, accompanied by constant indulgence. Then, the Buddha himself, meditating on Dhundhi and filled with penance, will pray to the Lord of obstacles for the re-establishment of Dharma. Then, the Lord of the Ganas will appear, pleased, and known as Dhumravarna, he will re-establish Dharma. The Buddha, being happy, will bow to him, worship him, and praise him, and then, having disappeared, he will remain in his own glory.

This greatness of the Buddha has been told in brief. Reading or hearing it immediately destroys delusion and fulfills desires.

Thus ends the fifteenth chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Description of the Buddha Incarnation," in the story of Dhumravarna.

Chapter 16 : The Description of the Victory of Dhumravarna over Kali, the Sixteenth Chapter

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shiva said: When the social order (Varnashrama) is destroyed in the Kali Yuga, the gods, filled with sorrow, will pray to the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) while observing fasts. Seeing the gods performing various penances, Ganesha, the Elephant-faced Lord, will manifest before them with supreme devotion. The gods saw the Lord Ganesha, who had assumed the form of Dhumravarna (the Smoke-colored one), two-armed, riding a horse, holding a sword and a shield. Adorned with garlands of various flowers and smeared with sandalwood paste, the gods bowed to Him, worshipped Him, and praised Him.

Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the performer of various plays, the possessor of immeasurable power, the master of illusion (Maya), and the protector of Dharma. Salutations to You, the refuge of the helpless and the Lord of lords. Salutations to You who are with attributes (Saguna) and salutations to You who are without attributes (Nirguna). Salutations to You who are beyond both Saguna and Nirguna, the embodiment of Yoga, and who reside everywhere in the form of Yoga. Salutations to You, O Heramba, who have the form of a human and an elephant, who are smoke-colored, two-armed, and ride a horse. Salutations to You, the Lord of all, who hold a sword and shield, are smeared with various sandal pastes, and wear various flowers. O Ganesha, the complete one, the complete form, the Supreme Soul, O Elephant-faced Lord, protect Dharma; salutations to You.

O Lord of gods, all the gods have been made devoid of Dharma by You. Now that Dharma is destroyed, we shall die before You. We have been defeated by Kali, who is assisted by the illusion of intellect (Buddhi-maya). O Great-bellied Lord, protect the gods like Vishnu and Shiva. For two thousand five hundred years, we have been defeated; the minor gods in their villages have fled due to Kali. Then, for five thousand years, the deities of the holy places and the holy places themselves were conquered by Kali, and they fled. Then, for ten thousand years, Vishnu, Shiva, and others were defeated, and the best of gods left the earth and fled due to Kali. The power known as Karma, Akarma, and Vikarma resides there, and it is filled with the qualities of Vikarma and is of the nature of Buddhi-maya. You, too, having become like that, remain the giver of fruits yourself. O Lord, You who have assumed the form of a god, are always the instigator of Kali. Kali, filled with anger, does not recognize Your divine form; he is always ready to strike and is constantly thinking of You. Because of Your contemplation, we have all been defeated, and Kali has made us observe fasts. O Lord of obstacles, now Dharma exists only in a small fraction, and the terrible Kali will quickly destroy even that. When Dharma is destroyed, O Lord of Ganas, only great Adharma (unrighteousness) will remain. O Lord of gods, will You create Dharma again? Therefore, O Leader of the Ganas, protect the remaining fraction of Dharma; otherwise, we, whose very body is Dharma, will be destroyed. Protect, protect, O Ocean of Mercy, O Dhumravarna, O Elephant-faced Lord, protect the gods who are the protectors of Dharma and are now near death.

Having said this, the gods bowed to the Lord of Ganas again and wept in their sorrow. The Elephant-faced Lord spoke to them.

Dhumravarna said: Do not fear, O wise ones; I shall protect Dharma. Having killed the fierce Kali, who is invincible to all, I shall especially restore it. This hymn composed by you will destroy the sins of Kali; gods, those who read or hear it will attain all perfections. It will grant wealth and grain, increase health and prosperity, and bestow sons, grandsons, and wives.

Having said this, the glorious Dhumravarna created from His own body a diverse army equipped with various weapons. O Great Sages, they were riding various mounts and were filled with complete radiance. Accompanied by them, the Lord struck down all the wicked people. He terrified the Mlechchhas (barbarians) and those filled with the impurities of Kali, and He Himself terrified Kali, who was intent on destroying Dharma. Touched by the wind of His body, the people who were devoted to Dharma became fearful and took refuge in Ganesha with respect. Kali fought in a hidden form, relying on the people, but he was struck by Ganesha’s forces and became discouraged. Then, Kali, abandoning the hearts of the people, assumed a physical form, approached Dhumravarna, and bowed to Him. Knowing Him to be the Lord of Obstacles, he praised the mighty Dhumravarna with devotion, his hair standing on end with joy.

Kali said: Salutations to You, O Dhumravarna, the giver of happiness to all. Salutations to You, O Lord of Obstacles, who assume various forms. Salutations to You who have infinite feet and hands, and infinite arms, and who move secretly through infinite Maya. Salutations to You, O Heramba, the protector of devotees, and the giver of fear to those who are not devoted. Salutations to You who are without beginning, middle, or end, and who are the fear of the beginning, middle, and end. Salutations to You, the giver of eternal bliss to those who are blissful. Salutations to You, who are without Maya, the foundation of Maya, the Supreme Soul, and who cause delusion to the deluded through Maya. Salutations to You, the giver of the fruits of actions, the giver of knowledge to the wise, and the giver of eternal bliss to the blissful. Salutations to You, the natural one, the self-bliss, the Samadhi, the Yoga, the Lord of Yoga, and the Lord of Brahma. Who is capable of praising You, before whom even the Vedas and others have attained peace? I bow to You; be pleased with me now. Protect me, O Lord of Ganas, I am Your servant who has come for refuge. O Ocean of Compassion, forgive the faults born of this ignorant war.

Having said this, Kali took hold of His feet and fell down. The Lord of Ganas spoke to His devotee who was seeking refuge.

Dhumravarna said: Do not fear, O destroyer of Dharma; I will never kill you. You have come to Me for refuge; listen to My words. The hymn you have composed for Me will destroy the impurities of Kali; those who read or hear it daily will have all their desires fulfilled. In the end, it will grant the supreme bliss of the Self, and it will always instill firm devotion and grant Yoga to the yogis. Stay here, abandoning enmity, according to your own authority and duty. Know that you have attained this by My grace; be subject to My command.

Saying "So be it," Kali bowed to Him and went to his own place. Dhumravarna brought the leading Brahmins, such as Vashistha, and others. The Lord of all re-established them and made them carry out their duties. The Lord, the giver of happiness to all, then became invisible. I have narrated the story of the incarnation of Dhumravarna. By reading or hearing it, one attains all perfections and completeness.

Thus ends the sixteenth chapter of the Dhumravarna-charita in the eighth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, which is the end of the Upanishadic Puranas.

Chapter 17 : The Granting of Boons to the Yugas

Shaunaka said: O Suta, O noble one, you have narrated the sin-destroying, yoga-bestowing, and complete story. Having heard it, we, the twice-born, are satisfied. Having heard of the characteristics of the Yugas in the Puranas, we are astonished and have become filled with great doubts. In the Krita Yuga, women had independence, but in the Treta Yuga, Brahma imposed restrictions. In the Dvapara Yuga, Shvetaketu established the rule of one husband. How was the virtuous Ahalya cursed by Gautama in the Krita Yuga? We have seen many such paths in the Krita Yuga. It is said that in the Krita Yuga, there was no Varna-Ashrama system; people were described as belonging to a single Varna and Ashrama. In the Treta Yuga, the great-grandfather established the Kshatriya dharma, and from then on, people were described as being established in the Varna-Ashrama system. O Brahmana, at the end of the Dvapara Yuga, there were two kings, the Solar and the Lunar. When the Krita Yuga returns, they will rule again. People will live according to the division of Varna and Ashrama. What is this curiosity, which seems contradictory? Are the actions destroyed by the Yuga-dharma in the sinful Kali Yuga? How can there be success in the Varna-Ashrama system there? It is said that in the Kali Yuga, a person can attain success through actions that are four times as intense; how will that happen? Therefore, O noble one, tell us specifically about everything related to the Yugas, accurately, easily, and in a simple manner.

Suta said: The Yugas have been described in various ways by the sages who have seen the truth. One who understands their meaning will not be confused by the differences in opinion. Some say that the Krita Yuga resides in the fourth state of consciousness (Turiya), Treta in deep sleep (Sushupti), Dvapara in the dream state (Svapna), and Kali in the waking state (Jagrat). O great sages, the Self is described as the lord of the qualities, one in all states. Maya is the form of deep sleep, described as having many forms. In that manifold state, there is no deviation; the auspicious soul sees its own Lord everywhere. The subtle and the gross, which are created by Her in parts, appear in that form due to the Lord of qualities. There is no doubt that waking and dreaming arise from different states of mind; in them, the husband, wife, and others become subject to deviation. The restraint was established by Shvetaketu; he is called Shvetaketu because his flag bears a white dream. This measure of the Yugas should be understood by the wise; these Yugas were not described by the sages in the past in this way. Some call meditation the Krita Yuga, sacrifice the Treta, conduct the Dvapara, and the body-related state the Kali. In meditation, O noble ones, there is only one Varna and Ashrama; that knowledge should be known as always having one form. Sacrifice is said to be full of action, and there the Varna-Ashrama system exists; therefore, the conduct of Varna-Ashrama is described in the Treta. Thus, it was said by the sages in the past through various considerations; people should not have any doubt about it, given the logic of its meaning. Now, I will briefly tell you the greatness of the Yugas, through which actions become successful in the Kali Yuga.

In the beginning, Brahma created time for the arrangement of righteousness and unrighteousness. It became fourfold in the form of the ancient Yugas: Krita, Treta, Dvapara, and Kali. O great sages, to grant them success, the Creator gave them mantras. Bowing to Him, they all went to the best forest for penance. Meditating on Ganesha in their hearts, they chanted the mantras of the Yugas. Pleased by the power of their fasting and devotion, the Gajanana (Ganesha) appeared before them after a thousand years as the bestower of boons. Seeing Him arrived, they bowed to the Lord of the Ganas. Having worshipped Him, they all praised Him with voices choked with joy.

The Yuga-purushas said: Salutations to You, O Ganesha, the protector of devotees. Salutations again and again to You, O Heramba, who takes many forms. Salutations to You, the Supreme Lord, who is always for the devotees and the destroyer of the non-devotees. Salutations to You, who dwells in Your own bliss, who promotes various illusions, and who is the protector of those established in their own bliss. Salutations to You, the immeasurable power, the destroyer of various illusions, the giver of delusion to the deluded, and the Master of Maya. Salutations to You, O Lambodara, who holds the worlds in Your belly. Salutations to You, the single-tusked one with ears shaped like winnowing baskets. Salutations to You, the three-eyed one with an elephant face, the Supreme Soul, the four-armed one, and the one worshipped first by all. Salutations to You, the God, the form of God, the God of gods, the Lord of the gods, and the remover of obstacles. Salutations to You, the eldest form of all, the Mother and Father. Salutations to You, who is always worshipped by all and established in Brahman. Salutations to You, O Brahman, the Lord of the Brahmins, the bestower of the state of Brahman, the giver of Brahman to the Brahmins, and the one who resides in peace. How can we praise You, O Lord of Ganas, when even the Vedas and Shiva and others become forms of yoga through You? Thus, such a salutation is auspicious.

Having said this, the Yugas bowed to Ganapati. Pleased and bound by affection for His devotees, He spoke to them.

Shri Ganesha said: The hymn you have composed for Me will be all-granting. Its reading and hearing will be pleasing to Me for humans. Pleased by this hymn, I will grant whatever they desire. The Yugas will indeed be the bestowers of worldly enjoyment and liberation, and will be respected by all. O noble ones, ask for the boons you desire in your hearts; I am pleased by your penance, this excellent hymn, and especially by your devotion.

The Yugas said: O Gajanana, just as we have been created by You, give us unparalleled strength and the highest devotion to You.

Krita said: Make me always full of goodness (Sattva), devoted to action, interested in my own duty, and ready for liberation, O Lord.

Treta said: Make me fond of worldly enjoyment and liberation, and endowed with goodness and passion (Rajas). For that, O Gajanana, let me be devoted to righteousness. Salutations to You.

Dvapara said: Make me endowed with worldly enjoyment and happiness, devoted to righteousness, and for the sake of enjoyment and intellect, endowed with passion and darkness (Tamas), O Lord.

Kali said: O Gajanana, make me always ready for bodily enjoyment, full of darkness, and interested in prohibited actions.

Again, the Yugas, filled with joy, said to Him: O Lord, make us constantly established in our own respective natures.

Hearing their words, Ganesha spoke to them all with a voice as deep as a cloud, bringing happiness to all.

Shri Ganesha said: When the Krita Yuga is attained, the Kali Yuga will exist in it by a hundred-thousandth part. The Treta will exist by a hundredth part, and the Dvapara by a ten-thousandth part. When the Treta Yuga is attained, the Krita will exist by a hundredth part, and the Dvapara will exist by a thousandth part. The sinful Kali will be present by a ten-thousandth part. There is no doubt that they will act by entering into one another. When the Dvapara is attained, the Kali will be present by a hundredth part, the Treta by a thousandth part, and the Krita by a ten-thousandth part. In the Kali Yuga, the Dvapara will exist by a hundredth part, the Treta by a thousandth part, and the Krita by a hundred-thousandth part. The Kali Yuga is known as the giver of delusion due to the lack of discrimination between righteousness and unrighteousness. The Dvapara is described as the one who knows righteousness but performs deluded actions. The Treta is accepted everywhere as being fond of fame and established in its own duty. The Krita Yuga is described as being fond of liberation and established in its own duty. Thus, in order, you will all be dear, O Yugas. Knowing Me, you will be free from bondage and the bestowers of desired objects.

Having said this, Ganesha, the beloved of the Ganas, disappeared. The Yugas, filled with joy, shone, devoted to their own respective duties.

Chapter 18 : Description of the Influence of Yugas and the Attainment of Success

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: O great sages, in the Krita Yuga, the other yugas (Treta, Dvapara, and Kali) also exist in a latent form. All beings in that age are obedient to the laws of righteousness and are moved by the fear of dishonor. In that age, the lifespan of all living beings is equal to that of the Krita Yuga, and even for those inclined toward sinful conduct, the results of their actions are governed by the standards of the Krita Yuga. The curses and blessings of people in that age bear fruit according to the nature of the Krita Yuga, just as the words of the righteous are fulfilled. I shall now explain the distinction: a person committing an offense in the Krita Yuga does not escape the fear of a curse, as the standards of that age prevail.

Similarly, O Brahmin, in the Treta Yuga, the three yugas—Krita, Dvapara, and Kali—exist, but they are dominated by the influence of the Treta Yuga; there is no doubt about this. The lifespan and the results of actions for both the sinful and the righteous are determined by the standards of the Treta Yuga. In the Dvapara Yuga, the three other yugas exist under the influence of the Dvapara, and thus, all results born of karma take that form. The lifespan and other attributes of men are equal to the Dvapara standard; there are so many variations that I cannot describe them all.

In the Kali Yuga, the three other yugas exist under the command of the Kali influence, and therefore, the results of karma are equivalent to the Kali standard. O great sage, there is no doubt that the lifespan and other conditions are equal to the Kali Yuga, whether for the righteous or the great sinners, according to the measure of the age. Therefore, in the Kali Yuga, the success attained by those performing virtuous deeds is minimal, and everything is obstructed by demonic tendencies. However, Yoga is described as the provider of peace, and it remains uniform across the dharma of all four yugas.

In the Krita Yuga, an action performed yields a single measure of fruit, as accepted by the scriptures. In the Treta Yuga, that fruit increases tenfold; in the Dvapara, it increases a hundredfold. By the grace of Ganesha, in the Kali Yuga, everything is multiplied a thousandfold. Conversely, regarding sinful actions, the fruit is immediate in the Kali Yuga; it is ten times more in the Dvapara, a hundred times more in the Treta, and a thousand times more in the Krita Yuga. This was determined by the sages of old due to the greatness of the measure of each yuga. Thus, various standards have been described for the yugas, and this has been summarized in the Puranas, O Brahmins.

Now, I shall explain the path to success in the Kali Yuga, which grants all desires, so that actions performed with proper conduct may yield fruit instantly. Everything in the Kali Yuga is covered by demonic nature; therefore, actions born of karma do not easily bear fruit. If a person desires the success of their karma in the Kali Yuga, they should first perform ten thousand recitations of the Gayatri mantra. Living on a diet of sacrificial food, eating only once a day, practicing celibacy, speaking the truth, and controlling the senses, one should remain pure in conduct and abandon hatred. One should not even mentally think of a woman for the sake of pleasure.

When a person, while maintaining their physical body, is devoted to duty but mentally desires worldly objects, how can success be achieved? By performing such duties while remaining established in one's own dharma, a person of great renown should perform the Gayatri japa, through which they attain the qualities of a Brahmin. Then, one should perform penance and subsequently carry out their duties; thus, freed from all the faults of the Kali Yuga, one shall attain success. O Brahmins, for the noble soul who performs actions according to the prescribed method, success is attained fourfold in the Kali Yuga.

If actions are performed by Brahmins and others with a demonic nature, how can they lead to success in the Kali Yuga? If a human performs virtuous deeds with a purely demonic attitude, the merit is multiplied a thousandfold by joy, but it becomes tainted by the demonic influence. Actions performed with a mixture of divine and demonic nature yield happiness or sorrow accordingly to men. Thus, I have described the mystery of the dharma of the yugas, which, in brief, leads to success in the Kali Yuga. Any human who listens to this account of the yugas, or reads it, or teaches it to others, becomes free from all faults.

Thus ends the eighteenth chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Description of the Influence of Yugas and the Attainment of Success."

Chapter 19 : The Story of the Year and the Intercalary Month

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O Suta, you who are well-versed in all knowledge, please tell me the story of the months. How should Ganesha be worshipped during the intercalary month (Malamasa)?

Suta said: O Sage, in ancient times, the powerful Year (Samvatsara) was born from the limbs of Brahma, and from his limbs, the twelve months were also created. From his shadow, the auspicious intercalary month was born. Together with them, the Year meditated upon Ganesha and performed penance. He chanted the great six-syllable mantra as instructed by Brahma and constantly meditated upon Ganesha with a joyful heart. After a thousand years, Ganesha, who is fond of his devotees, became pleased and appeared before him.

Ganesha, the Lord of the Ganas, said to the Year who was in deep meditation: "O Year, ask of me the boons you desire in your heart." Hearing the words of Ganesha, the Year awakened. He rose and bowed to the Lord of Ganas with devotion. He worshipped Ganesha according to the prescribed rituals, and then, with folded hands, he praised him.

The Year said: Salutations to you, O Lord of smoky complexion, the one with the curved trunk, the seeker (Dhundhi), the protector of the distressed (Heramba), the Supreme Lord, and the Lord of obstacles. Salutations to the one with winnowing-basket ears, the one worshipped by all, the Supreme Soul, the one with the large belly, and the one who is the first to be worshipped. Salutations to the protector of the orphans, the destroyer of sins, the creator, the one who possesses the three qualities (Gunas) yet is beyond them. Salutations to you, O Ganesha, who exists in the beginning, middle, and end, yet is devoid of them, and who guides the Ganas. Salutations to the Lord of all knowledge, the giver of wisdom, the one who is Brahman itself, and the one who is established in Brahman. Salutations to the Lord of success and intellect, the one who is the embodiment of success and intellect, and the Lord of all. Salutations to the one of infinite glory, the one who spreads infinite illusion (Maya), the master of Maya, the one who deludes the deluded, and the one whose form is Maya. Salutations to the one who is the embodiment of peaceful yoga, the bearer of peaceful yoga, the giver of peaceful yoga, and the Lord of Yoga.

How can I praise you, O Lord of Ganas, who are the form of the wish-fulfilling gem (Chintamani)? You are the one in whom yogis find peace and whom the Vedas specifically describe. Therefore, I bow to you, O Lord. Be pleased with me and grant me boons that bring success and happiness to my duties. Grant me constant devotion to your feet, which removes all fear. O Dhundhi, grant me the natural strength to regulate the passage of time. May the actions performed by the wise during the months be auspicious. Grant me the status of being a witness to these actions and the ability to worship the feet of the righteous.

Having said this, the months, along with the Year, bowed to him. Pleased by this hymn, the Lord of Ganas spoke to them.

Shri Ganesha said: O Year and months, let it be as you have requested. You shall undoubtedly be the regulators of time, present everywhere. Accompanied by the Sun, who possesses twelve forms, you shall be the dispensers of the fruits of actions. When humans perform actions as an offering to Brahman during your time, you shall grant them liberation at the end. You shall be the impartial witnesses to all living beings, and you shall be my devotees, free from delusion.

O Intercalary month, you shall be abandoned by the Sun, but I shall be the deity who grants the fruits of actions performed during your time. Whatever impurities humans may accumulate during the months, I shall be the one to grant them the fruits according to their diverse states. Whatever actions humans perform during your time, I shall be the one to grant them the fruits.

This hymn composed by you shall grant all success to those who read or hear it with devotion. I am pleased by this hymn and shall remove the sins arising from the faults of time and the transgression of time, granting both worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Having said this, Ganesha, the beloved of the Ganas, disappeared. The Year, with a pleased heart, became engaged in his duties. The months, empowered to perform their tasks, became joyful, acting as the regulators of time and witnesses to all actions. Whoever reads or hears this story of the Year and the intercalary month shall attain happiness in time.

Thus ends the nineteenth chapter of the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana, which describes the story of the Year and the intercalary month.

Chapter 20 : The Granting of Boons to the Intercalary Month and Others

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: After a long time had passed, the six months remained engaged in penance. Abstaining from food, they all sought to please Lord Ganesha. The months of Kartika, Margashirsha, Magha, Vaishakha, Shravana, and the Adhimasa (intercalary month), O Brahmana, remained intent on meditating upon Ganesha. After ten years, the Lord of Obstacles, the bestower of boons, appeared before them. Being praised and worshipped by them, He said, "Ask for your boons." Hearing the words of Ganesha, the months bowed down to Him, and with joyful hearts and necks bent in devotion, they spoke.

The months said: O Lord of Obstacles, O Lord of all, if You are pleased to grant us a boon, then, O Lord, please make the meritorious deeds performed during our time always bear abundant fruit. By this, may the people who strive during our time become especially devoted to righteous actions. O Leader of the Ganas, make us the abode of devotion and ensure that those who serve us remain free from the faults of their actions.

Lord Ganesha said: By the power of My boon, the deeds performed in all other months will be considered fruitful according to the scriptures. However, the deeds performed in Margashirsha will be ten times more fruitful for humans. The deeds performed in Vaishakha will be ten times more fruitful than those, and those performed in Kartika will be ten times more fruitful still. The deeds performed in Magha will be ten times more fruitful than those, and those performed in Shravana will be ten times more fruitful than those. The deeds performed in the month of Dhundhi will be ten times more fruitful than those. In this order, you will become supreme according to your penance. I am the presiding deity of the Adhimasa, and I am Dhundhiraja; therefore, this month is now named by Me as the great Dhundhi month.

Combined with the sign of the new moon, the month of Shravana is especially dear to Me. It will bestow various accomplishments upon those who perform rituals. Humans who worship Me in Shravana with various efforts, and those who observe fasting for four days in Bhadrapada, starting from the first day until the fourth, and worship Me, should keep a vigil on the fourth day and break their fast on the fifth. By feeding the Brahmanas according to their capacity and giving them generous donations, they will enjoy all happiness and ultimately attain My abode of bliss. Bathing in the five months is considered essential in every way, and rituals performed in the early morning during Shravana are highly regarded. Thus, you months will always be served by people with great meritorious deeds. By My boon, you will always be filled with devotion to Me and dedicated to all the gods.

Having said this, Ganesha, who is fond of His devotees, disappeared. The months, filled with joy, returned to their respective duties. Whoever listens to or reads this account of the months will attain the fruits of the months and become a devotee of Ganesha.

Thus ends the twentieth chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, regarding the granting of boons to the intercalary month and others.

Chapter 21 : Describing the Glory of Dhumra

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka asked: How should humans worship the Lord of Obstacles during the six months? By which primary method does Gajanana become pleased? Who among them attained perfection by worshipping Ganesha? Tell me their stories, which bestow all accomplishments.

Suta said: A person should always bathe at the time of sunrise, observing vows in holy places according to the traditions of their own Vedic branch. Following the path prescribed by one's own branch, one should perform sandhya and other rituals, worshipping the Lord of the Ganas with Vedic and Tantric mantras. Whether following the Puranic methods or others, one should perform the worship with all necessary offerings. O Sage, one should eat only once a day or only at night. One may consume only sanctified food (havishya), fruits, or remain fasting. Maintaining celibacy, one should keep one's nails and hair trimmed. Sleeping on the ground, speaking the truth, and observing silence, one should perform the month-long vow according to one's own dharma. Whether a Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, or Shudra, one should perform this month-long vow according to their own duty. This vow is holy and brings happiness to women, especially widows, for whom this month-long observance is supreme.

Among these, a person should always perform the five main acts with joy: the worship of Ganesha, feeding Brahmanas, donating lamps, serving the Shami and Mandara trees with circumambulations, and caring for cows; these bestow all accomplishments. In this way, one should perform various acts dear to Ganesha, remaining dedicated to Him throughout the month-long vow. O Sage, one may also worship other gods like Shambhu according to one's own inclination while observing the month-long vow. These months must be observed by humans as they are essential; they grant dharma, wealth, desires, liberation, and union with the Divine. Whatever one desires, one attains without a doubt through this primary month-long vow.

I shall now briefly tell you the supreme history of those who attained perfection through this month-long vow with devotion. Long ago, the great demon Shankhasura, the lord of the universe, stole the Vedas along with the Smritis. Consequently, due to the lack of righteous actions, chaos ensued, and mixed-caste activities spread throughout the world. Then, the gods, led by Brahma and accompanied by sages, went to Vishnu and prayed to Him for the sake of the Vedas. Vishnu, having dismissed them, went to Shambhu and spoke to Him with great humility.

Vishnu said: O Shambhu, Shankhasura, who stole the Vedas, is invincible to the gods. Tell me the way by which I may defeat him through your grace.

Shri Shiva said: The month of Kartika is approaching, O Janardana. By observing the month-long vow during that time, you should please Gajanana. By His grace, you will be free of obstacles and will undoubtedly defeat the powerful Shankhasura.

Hearing this, Vishnu, the conqueror of enemy cities, bowed to Shankara and went to the Badari forest with devotion to observe the vow. There, he performed the supreme purashcharana of the single-syllable mantra and pleased the Lord of the Ganas with effort. He served the five-fold Ganesha worship in Kartika, which grants all accomplishments. In Margashirsha, after bowing to Ganesha, he approached the gods. Remembering the Lord of Wisdom, he pondered in his heart: By what means shall I defeat this formidable demon? Through the grace of Ganesha, the inspiration arose in his heart: "I will achieve victory in the form of a fish (Matsya)."

Meanwhile, the Lord Keshav, the slayer of enemies, heard a supreme voice from the sky: "Kill the terrifying Shankha in the form of a fish. Remembering Me, there is no doubt that you will be victorious." Then, remembering Ganapati, Janardana took the form of a fish, fought a fierce battle, and killed the terrible Shankha. He retrieved the Vedas along with their limbs and gave them to Brahma. Praised and worshipped by the gods and sages, He returned to His abode. Thus, in the month of Kartika, through the power of the vow of bathing and other rituals, Shankhasura was killed by Vishnu in his Matsya form.

Listen, O best of Brahmanas, to another story that destroys sins, which is ancient and supreme, related to the Kartika vow. There was a Brahmana of the Pulastya lineage named Somadatta, who was always devoted to his dharma, ascetic, and pure-minded. He went to the sage Gautama to learn about devotion to Ganesha and asked him with humility.

Somadatta asked: By what means can the Lord of Obstacles be seen directly? Since the body is fleeting, tell me the way, O Lord.

Gautama said: The month of Kartika is approaching, O great sage. You must perform the month-long vow to see the Great Lord. Having said this, he taught him the method and gave him a place in his ashram, where he began the observance. With single-minded devotion, he served the five-fold Ganesha worship during the Kartika vow. Fasting and meditating on Gajanana in his heart, he chanted the auspicious six-syllable mantra.

A miracle happened on the final day, which you must hear, O great Brahmana, for it strengthens one's resolve. The illustrious Brahmana Somadatta was engaged in worship on the full moon day of Kartika, observing the vow of sitting in one position. The Durva grass, Shami leaves, and Mandara flowers were placed there. Suddenly, a Brahmana came and took them away. Somadatta, remaining in his seat and observing silence, did not rise; he remained in his discipline, lamenting while remembering the Lord of the Ganas. Then, through his contemplation, he realized something wonderful: the hair on his body was made of all medicinal herbs. Therefore, he thought, "I will give my hair, along with the skin, to the Lord of the Ganas as a substitute for the Durva and Shami."

He took a weapon and prepared to cut his head, offering his own hair along with the tuft to Ganesha in place of the Durva. As he struck his neck with the weapon, the Lord of the Ganas appeared in the form of that Brahmana. He took the Shami, Mandara, and Durva, gave them back to him, and said, "Worship Ganesha, O you who are intent on your vow." Seeing this, the illustrious Somadatta was filled with joy. He completed his vow and, bowing down, spoke to Him.

Somadatta asked: Who are you, O Lord? You took the Durva and then returned it, and my vow was fulfilled. You are not a Brahmana; you are the Lord of Obstacles Himself. Having seen my firm resolve, you have come to test me. Show me your true form, or else I will give up my body. Tell me the truth, O great one, so that I may be satisfied.

Then, through the power of the vow and his disciplined devotion, the Lord of the Ganas was pleased and revealed His form. He had four arms, three eyes, and was adorned with a trunk. He was accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, marked with a battle-axe and other symbols. He was adorned with various ornaments, wore the serpent Shesha as a sacred thread, held the Chintamani gem in His heart, and was pot-bellied with a single tusk. Seeing Him seated on a mouse and adorned with garments, Somadatta bowed down with a heart full of joy. His body thrilled with ecstasy, he danced with tears in his eyes, appearing as if he had transcended his body.

Then, filled with joy, he worshipped the Lord. Bowing again to Heramba, he prayed with folded hands: "Salutations to Ganesha, the Supreme Lord, the giver of eternal peace, the form of yoga for yogis, who resides in the heart. Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, the creator of great obstacles, the Supreme Soul, the destroyer of obstacles for His devotees, the foundation of existence. Salutations to the Beginningless One, the Great Time, the form of Time, the Lord of the Beginning who enjoys at the end. Salutations to Vinayaka, the God, the Lord of all, the giver of positions to the leaders. Salutations to the Pot-bellied One, who contains all within His belly, the Lord of Brahman, the One with boundless compassion. Salutations to You, who are without beginning, middle, or end, who take the form of the universe, the Lord of Self-Bliss, who is known by the Self. Salutations to the giver of perfection to the devoted, the remover of perfection from the wicked, the Lord of Perfection, the form of Great Perfection. Salutations to the Lord of Wisdom, the giver of wisdom to the wise, the beloved of the virtuous, and the giver of ignorance to the wicked. Salutations to You, who are the form of both the known and the unknown, the human-formed and elephant-faced. How can I praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, who are always the form of yoga? By bowing to You, I have fulfilled my purpose. I am blessed among all beings to have seen You, O Lord of the Ganas. My lineage, character, glory, parents, and ashram are all blessed. Blessed is the vow of the Lord of the Ganas in the great month of Kartika; by merely performing it, I have seen You, O Lord. Grant me devotion to Your feet, O Elephant-faced One. I ask for nothing else, as I am devoted to the path of Ganesha."

Hearing his words, Ganesha said to him: "Whatever you desire will be fulfilled. You will become extremely dear to the Ganas, O great sage. You will be a devotee of My feet and will be respected by all. This hymn composed by you will bestow all accomplishments upon those who read or hear it." Having said this, the Lord of Obstacles, the ocean of compassion, disappeared. The illustrious Somadatta bowed to Gautama. From that day on, that sage became known as the beloved of Ganapati, a yogi, the best of those who know yoga, and a knower of the essence of all Vedas.

Thus ends the twenty-first chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, describing the glory of Dhumra.

Chapter 22 : Description of Various Observances in the Glory of the Month of Kartika

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: In the land of Karnataka, there lived a Brahmana of the Atri lineage named Sarvapriya, who observed the Kartika vow. After bathing in the morning, he remained devoted to the worship of Ganesha, and by following the rules, he became dear to the Lord of the Ganas. There, a pure Kshatriya named Bhima, after bathing, watered the Shami tree and performed circumambulations. Another person, a fisherman named Meghapriya, worshipped the Mandara tree after bathing and performed circumambulations. A Vaishya named Pushpavaha, who tended to cows, remained devoted to serving them; he would bathe, meditate on Ganapati, and respectfully worship Him in the form of a cow. A Shudra named Shyamala performed the offering of lamps daily for the pleasure of Ganesha, remaining devoted to the monthly vow after bathing. Another Vaishya, Mahabahu, having bathed and observed the vow, fed Brahmanas daily with great devotion. A craftsman named Padmanama, having bathed daily, bowed down to Ganesha with devotion and remained steadfast in the Kartika vow. A Shudra named Mahipala, who was endowed with virtues, observed the pilgrimage of Ganapati during the month of Kartika.

O Sage, in this way, many people there, for the pleasure of Ganesha, bathed and worshipped the Lord of the Ganas according to their own nature. They were especially freed from ailments, infertility, and other defects. Upon the completion of this supreme vow, they shone with wealth and grain. In the end, they all attained the abode of Svananda, and by bowing to Ganesha, they immediately became one with the Brahman due to the merit of the Kartika vow.

Therefore, O Brahmana, the vow during the month of Kartika should be performed especially for the pleasure of Ganesha, as it grants all accomplishments. Such devotion combined with discipline has never been seen before, nor will it be seen again. By observing some discipline, one can bring Ganesha under one's control. Whatever is done with discipline for the pleasure of Ganesha by men becomes ten million times more fruitful, O Brahmana, and grants all accomplishments. Bathing in Kartika and serving the Lord of Obstacles is supreme; whatever is done according to one's capacity will grant all accomplishments. Whoever performs the five-fold worship dear to Ganapati in Kartika undoubtedly becomes extremely dear to Ganesha. Any charity, fire sacrifice, or other act performed in Kartika grants infinite merit, and the fruits of such actions offered to the Lord of the Ganas become eternal.

What more can be said? Even a single bow made to Ganesha in the month of Kartika daily becomes a source of imperishable merit. I have narrated this glory of the month of Kartika, which grants the realm of Ganesha, bestows merit, and provides supreme happiness in this world. The person who listens to this with devotion or reads it with feeling will attain all their desires and become dear to the Lord of the Ganas. Those who are devoted to other deities but remain steadfast in the Kartika vow will certainly enjoy all worldly happiness and attain their goals in the end.

Thus ends the twenty-second chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "Description of Various Observances in the Glory of the Month of Kartika" within the Dhumravarna Charita.

Chapter 23 : The Narration of the Vajrapanjara in the Glory of the Month of Margashirsha

Shounaka said: O noble one, please tell me briefly about the supreme glory of the month of Margashirsha, which bestows devotion to Ganesha.

Suta said: There was a demon named Andhakasura, who was extremely formidable. After conquering all three worlds, he established an excellent kingdom. All the gods, filled with fear, resided in the forests, and though they were eager to kill him, they could achieve nothing. Then, Lord Shambhu (Shiva), along with the sages, became deeply concerned. Suddenly, Narada, the ocean of compassion, arrived there. Seeing him, the gods, led by Shankara, were filled with joy and asked the noble sage about their intense grief and sorrow.

Lord Shiva said: O best of yogis, we have all been defeated by Andhaka, who even attempted to take Parvati as his wife. O lord of sages, protect these gods and Brahmins who are devoted to their duties and are observing fasts due to the influence of the destruction of their rituals. Tell us the means to kill Andhaka, O knower of all; we shall perform it with devotion, O great-minded one.

Narada said: The month of Margashirsha, which grants all accomplishments, has arrived. You must perform a month-long vow for the pleasure of Ganesha. By the power of this vow, you will surely defeat the demon Andhaka through the grace of the Lord of Obstacles. Having said this, the great yogi Narada, remembering Ganesha and playing his veena, departed as he pleased.

Then, Shankara, along with the host of gods and sages, performed the supreme vow of Ganesha during Margashirsha. He served the five forms of Ganesha daily with effort. Rising at dawn, he bathed and worshipped Lord Ganesha. Observing a fast day and night, Shankara remained in meditation, contemplating only Him with devotion. After completing the month-long vow, Shiva worshipped the Brahmins, fed them, and then broke his fast.

Pleased by the power of the vow, the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) appeared before Shankara to grant him a boon. Seeing the Lord of Obstacles seated on a mouse, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, Shankara bowed to him with joy. Having worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with the gods and sages, Shiva, with hair standing on end and hands folded, praised him.

Lord Shiva said: Salutations to you, O Ganesha, O Lord of Obstacles, O Dhundhi, O Supreme Lord, O Heramba, O Pashi (the one who holds the noose), salutations again and again. Salutations to the one who has a mouse as his vehicle, who holds a battle-axe in his hand, who bears the tusk and grants fearlessness, and who has three eyes. Salutations to you, O hero with ears like winnowing baskets, who wears the crescent moon, who is adorned with vermilion, and who is red in color. O Heramba, O remover of great obstacles, O Elephant-faced one, salutations to you who are the first to be worshipped and worthy of worship by all. Salutations to you who dwell in Svananda, whose form is Svananda itself, who are the form of all, and who are the Mother and Father of all. Salutations to the one who has an infinite belly, the Lord of the Vedas, the one who is Brahman itself, the destroyer of demons and giants. Salutations to the one of infinite glory, who is devoted to protecting his devotees, and who is the playful destroyer of the non-devout. Salutations to the Lord of Yoga, the Yogi, the giver of Yoga, and the one who is always the embodiment of peace. How can I praise you, O Lord of Ganesha, who are the form of Brahman, in whom yogis and the Vedas find peace? I am blessed, O Lord of the Ganas, by the sight of your feet, which are beyond the beyond, along with the gods and sages. Grant me the power, O Lord of Obstacles, which resides in you as knowledge, and grant me unwavering devotion to destroy the darkness of ignorance.

Having said this, the great Lord Shiva danced with the divine sages. The Lord of the Ganas, who is dear to his devotees, spoke to him.

Lord Ganesha said: O Shambhu, take this Vajrapanjara (Diamond Armor) of mine, destroy the demon, and be victorious forever. You shall attain unwavering devotion to my feet, and whatever you desire shall be fulfilled. Whoever reads or listens to this hymn composed by you shall attain all their desires. They shall attain worldly enjoyment, liberation, sons, grandsons, wealth, grain, and devotion to me, becoming one with Brahman—there is no doubt about this.

Suta said: Hearing these words of Ganesha, Shiva was filled with joy, bowed down, and spoke with a longing for devotion.

Lord Shiva said: O Lord, please tell me the Vajrapanjara that is dear to Ganesha, which is full of knowledge, the destroyer of great ignorance, and the giver of happiness.

Lord Ganesha said: First, perform the daily rituals and worship the Elephant-faced one; then, take the Vajrapanjara, which destroys all ignorance. I worship the three-eyed, elephant-faced, four-armed Ganesha, who holds weapons like the battle-axe, who has the moon on his forehead, who has a human body, who is always peaceful in yoga, and who is the Supreme Lord worshipped by all. Having meditated on Ganesha in this way, worship him with mental offerings, bow to him, and then wear the Vajrapanjara.

May the point-formed (Bindu-rupa) Vakratunda, who resides in my heart, protect me. May the eternal foundation of all elements protect me. May the single-tusked one, who holds the form of "I am He" (Soham), protect me, the embodied soul, from the delusion of the body. May the pot-bellied Lord, who is powerful and full of various wisdoms, always protect my intellect day and night. May the Lord of Sankhya, the Elephant-faced one, the giver of success, protect my knowledge. May the pot-bellied one protect me while I am in the midst of untruth. May the Victorious one (Vikata), who is beyond the beyond, protect me out of love for his devotees, as he is the holder of the nectar of devotion. May the Lord, who is the embodiment of bliss and plays with great obstacles, protect me while I am in bliss. May the smoke-colored one, who is the form of happiness and worshipped by all, protect me while I am in the unmanifest state. May Ganesha, who holds his own form, protect me while I am in self-awareness, as he is the Lord of existence established in the state of yoga. May the Lord of the Ganas, who is the direct embodiment of renunciation and delights in the bliss of samadhi, protect me while I am in the state of non-yoga. May the Lord of the Ganas, who possesses the peace of yoga, is of a pleasant mind, and is accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, protect me while I am established in yoga. May the Elephant-eared one, the remover of obstacles, protect me from the front. May Chintamani, the bestower of boons, protect me in the south-east, south, and south-west. May Dhundhi protect me in the west, Heramba in the north-west, Vinayaka in the north, and Pramoda in the north-east. May the Lord of Success protect me from above, and the Lord of Intellect from below. May Mayuresha, who is eager for devotion, protect me in all my limbs. May the Lord of Yoga always protect me wherever I am, holding the battle-axe and the noose, and granting boons and fearlessness.

O Mahadeva, wear this supreme Vajrapanjara of Ganesha, and you shall be victorious. Whoever wears this armor, wherever they may be, shall never fear anything born of various natures. Whoever reads this armor daily shall attain their desires; becoming one with a body of diamond, the human shall move everywhere. Whoever reads this daily in the three junctures of the day becomes like another Ganesha; he becomes free of obstacles in all his works and becomes one with Brahman. Whoever listens to this Vajrapanjara of Ganesha becomes dear to the Lord of the Ganas and is endowed with health and more. By reading this, one attains wealth, grain, cattle, knowledge, longevity, sons, and grandsons; one attains all prosperity and majesty. One shall never fear the thunderbolt, the discus, or the trident, O Mahadeva, by reading this daily. Whatever the mortal contemplates, he attains eternally by the constant reading of this Vajrapanjara of the Lord of Obstacles. By reciting it one hundred thousand times, it becomes a perfected armor; one can even stop the sun and bring the universe under one's control.

Having said this, the Lord of Ganesha disappeared. Shiva, along with the gods, was filled with joy. He performed the recitation one hundred thousand times according to the rules. Having become the master of the perfected armor, he went to the demon Andhaka. Meditating on Ganesha in his mind and engaging in a fierce battle, he pierced the arrogant Andhaka with his trident. Having thus defeated the demon king Andhaka, the powerful Shankara established the gods in their respective places, free from fear. Shankara himself made the world follow its own dharma, through the vow of Margashirsha and the power of the armor. Whoever listens to or reads this story of the destruction of Andhaka shall attain the love of Ganesha for all accomplishments.

Thus ends the twenty-third chapter of the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Narration of the Vajrapanjara" in the glory of the month of Margashirsha.

Chapter 24 : The Description of the Saintly Character.

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: There was a Brahmin named Sadhu. He was constantly filled with love for the worship of Ganesha. Once, filled with devotion, he went to the great sage Bharadwaja. Bowing to him first, he asked with humility.

Sadhu said: O Master, tell me a method that grants great success, by which the Lord of the Ganas may reveal Himself to me, O Great Sage.

Bharadwaja said: O son, perform the Margashirsha Vrata (vow) immediately. By doing so, you will become very dear to the Lord of the Ganas.

Having said this, Bharadwaja explained the complete vow according to the prescribed rites, accompanied by the Ganesha Panchaka. Sadhu bowed to him and performed the excellent vow while staying there. By observing fasting, he pleased the Remover of Obstacles.

On the day of the conclusion of the vow, the Lord of the Ganas, assuming the form of a Brahmin, went to his auspicious hermitage. After the worship was over, He said to that fortunate and great sage, "O Brahmin, give me some excellent food that will satisfy me."

Saying "So be it," he bowed to Him and worshipped Him with devotion. He fed Him with various types of delicious food with great reverence. After eating all the food, He said to him, "I am still very hungry. Give me some satisfying food, or else I will leave."

Hearing this, Sadhu was filled with great astonishment and thought in his heart, "I have fed this Brahmin a hundred prasthas of food, yet He remains unsatisfied. What should I do? Has Ganesha Himself come to deceive me in the form of a Brahmin?"

"It has been said by great Brahmins in the Puranas that the only thing that satisfies Ganesha is a single blade of Durva grass; there is no doubt about this." Thinking thus, Sadhu decided to offer Him food mixed with Durva grass.

Meditating on Ganesha in his heart, he quickly took some milk-pudding (payasam) and, with great devotion, gave Him the food mixed with Durva grass. The Brahmin, now satisfied and pleased, said to him with great joy, "O Sage, you have understood the secret of Ganesha's devotion. Because you were satisfied and did not harbor any doubt, I am pleased. Ask for a boon, O fortunate one, and I will grant it to you out of my grace."

Sadhu said: If You are the one who grants boons, then reveal Your true form to me. There is no doubt that You are Ganesha, who has come here due to my good fortune.

Hearing his words, the Brahmin transformed into the Lord of the Ganas. Seeing Him, Sadhu, who was endowed with saintly qualities, became extremely joyful. He bowed down, worshipped Him, and prayed with folded hands, his eyes filled with tears of devotion, being a man of great fame.

Sadhu said: Salutations to You, O Heramba, the three-eyed Supreme Soul. Salutations again and again to the Lord who is both formless and with form. Salutations to the Lord of the Ganas, the beginningless Supreme Lord, who is pot-bellied, heroic, elephant-eared, and the seeker of truth. Salutations to You, who dwell in Swananda, who are the embodiment of the devotee's feelings, and who are satisfied by the pure devotion of Your devotees. Salutations to You, the Supreme Soul, the form of all, the one worthy of being worshipped first, the Lord of Brahma, and the protector of the Ganas. Salutations to You, the formless one, the most beloved of all, the Lord of Obstacles, and the destroyer of great obstacles. Salutations to You, the mother and father of all, the embodiment of truth, the Brahma, Vishnu, and Shankara. Salutations to You, who are the power, the form of the Sun, Indra, the wind, the Moon, Varuna, and Yama. Salutations to You, who are Agni, Nirriti, Kubera, the supporter of the earth, the human, the animal, and the serpent. Salutations to You, who are the pervader of the moving and non-moving, yet beyond them, who are equal, natural, and self-realized. Salutations to You, the Lord of Yoga and Peace, the giver of peace. How can I praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, O embodiment of Yoga? I am blessed in every way by the sight of Your lotus feet. Grant me the boon of firm devotion to You, O Lord of Ganesha.

Having praised the Lord of the Ganas in this way, Sadhu bowed to Him. The Lord of Ganesha, who is dear to His devotees, said to him:

Shri Ganesha said: Your devotion to Me will become intense, O wise one. Whatever you desire will be fulfilled. This hymn of Mine, which you have composed and which increases devotion, will always grant success to those who read or hear it, O fortunate one. By My grace, this will grant all perfections, fulfill various tasks, and ultimately lead to Swananda.

Having said this, the Lord of Ganesha disappeared. Sadhu, meditating on Him in his heart, remained there. In this way, many people, especially those devoted to Ganesha, attained success through the Margashirsha Vrata.

Hear another story, O best of Brahmins, which grants all success and is wonderful to hear or read during Margashirsha. There was a fisherman named Shambu. He heard the glory of the Margashirsha month, which is centered on Ganesha. When the month of Margashirsha arrived, the wise man bathed and bowed to Ganesha daily while supporting his family. Later, he always served the King. He died in the month of Pausha, and the attendants of Ganesha took him to their abode. Seeing the Lord of Obstacles there, he became one with the Divine. He had performed the Margashirsha Vrata only partially, yet by the power of that vow, he attained the state of the Divine. Thus, many people have attained success through this vow, O Brahmin.

Having enjoyed all pleasures here, they ultimately go to Swananda. If this is the result for those who follow simple rules, what more can be said for those who follow the full prescribed rites? The noble person who hears or reads this daily during Margashirsha will constantly be endowed with success. I have told you a portion of the glory of the Margashirsha Vrata. What more do you wish to hear, O best of sages?

Thus ends the twenty-fourth chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which contains the glory of the Margashirsha month and the description of the saintly character.

Chapter 25 : The Killing of Virochana

Shaunaka said: Please tell me about the vow of the month of Magha, which increases the pleasure of Lord Ganesha. I am never satisfied listening to the stories of Ganesha, which grant all accomplishments.

Suta said: Prahlada had a son of great brilliance who was a protector of the demons, known as Virochana, the crest-jewel among the powerful. Approaching Shukracharya, the best of sages, the leader of the demons bowed down and spoke with a sigh, filled with sorrow.

Virochana said: O Master, the demons are oppressed by the gods and remain filled with fear in the netherworlds. Please instruct me for their welfare.

Shukra said: Worship the Lord Sun with the mantra "Akrsnena." By doing so, you will become the protector of the demons.

Having bowed to him, the mighty and valiant Virochana went to the Kama forest and performed supreme penance. Meditating intensely on the Sun, he offered water (arghya) and, remaining dedicated to his worship, served the Sun constantly. After a thousand divine years, the brilliant Sun, pleased, went to Virochana's hermitage to grant him a boon. Seeing him arrive, Virochana bowed down and, having worshipped the Lord of the gods, praised him with the Atharvashirsha.

Pleased, the Sun, the bestower of boons to his devotees, said: O great demon, ask for the supreme boon you desire in your heart.

Virochana said: O Lord Sun, let there be no death for me from any being, whether moving or stationary, and grant me the kingdom of the three worlds.

Astonished, the Sun gave him his own supreme crown and said with deep intent: Wear this on your head; by it, you will be free from death as long as no other hand touches your head. If another's hand touches your head, O demon, you will die. Be careful when you are without the crown. The kingdom of the three worlds is now granted to you by me; whatever you desire will be fulfilled. Having said this, the Sun, who knows all purposes, disappeared. Virochana, with a joyful heart, then went to his home.

There, the demon king was anointed for the kingdom by Shukra. Surrounded by demons, he conquered the earth with his great strength. After defeating the rest, he went to Amaravati. Having brought the King of the gods under his control, he then went to Vaikuntha. A fierce battle took place between Vishnu and Virochana. Having defeated Vishnu, the great demon was filled with joy. The gods, along with Vishnu, fled and took refuge in the forest. All the heavens were enjoyed by that great-souled demon.

Having placed the demon lords in the positions of Vaikuntha and others, he returned to his own city accompanied by the host of demons. Then, joined by the demon lords, he enjoyed various supreme pleasures, wallowing in sin and lusting after women and meat. After a long time had passed, the wicked one, incited by the demon lords, destroyed the duties of the Varnas and Ashramas on earth. He made humans perform demonic acts everywhere. O sage, the inhabitants of the three worlds cried out in distress.

After a long time, the gods, afflicted by fasting, along with the sages, praised Keshava (Vishnu) for the demon's destruction. The Lord of the gods sighed and pondered in his heart, but could not find a way to destroy the demon and became distressed himself. Then, Vishnu remembered the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) for the sake of relief from sorrow. By the grace of Ganesha, the sage Devala arrived there.

The gods bowed to him and asked for the success of their mission. For the sake of killing Virochana, he spoke to them joyfully. The highly auspicious month of Magha has now arrived. Perform the bathing rituals and serve the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha). Having said this, the great yogi Devala explained the entire procedure of the Magha vow, which consists of five parts related to Ganesha.

Having instructed them in the vow, the yogi went his own way. Devala, Keshava, and the others, engaged in the worship of Ganesha, performed the Magha vow, which grants all accomplishments, according to the scriptural injunctions, and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles. When the vow was completed, a voice from the sky spoke to them: O Vishnu, take the form of a woman and kill the mighty demon. Remembering Ganesha in your heart, go to that demon leader; the great demon Virochana will be under your control.

Hearing this, the gods were overjoyed and praised the Lord of the Ganas. Then, the noble ones respectfully urged Vishnu: Go, go, O great Vishnu, kill the mighty demon. Make the moving and stationary worlds joyful again, O Janardana.

Saying "So be it," Vishnu meditated on the Elephant-faced Lord and went in the form of a woman to where the demon king was. He stood in a garden filled with flowering trees. There, the demons saw Narayana in the form of a woman. Infatuated, they approached her and asked with humility: Who are you, O beautiful one, and whose are you? Tell us why you have come. Obey us, O noble one; we are your servants, without a doubt. O fair-eyed one, look at us all.

Hearing their words, the beautiful Mohini, full of gestures and allure, spoke to them.

Mohini said: I am a wanderer of my own will, beautiful and unattached. I am known as Mohini, and I roam without fear. The one who will be the mighty lord of the world, him I shall choose as my husband, O demons. Do not delay, go!

Hearing her words, the demons were agitated. Some of them went to Virochana, who was with his ministers. They bowed to the great soul and respectfully told him the news. Hearing this, he was instantly filled with great infatuation. Deluded by Ganesha's Maya, he went to that flawless woman. The great demon Virochana saw her and was captivated. Seeing the great demon arrive, Vishnu was delighted. He remembered Ganesha in his heart and stood right there.

Then, the Lord of the Ganas himself resided in their hearts and caused a confusion of intellect, making them both behave in that manner. Virochana, seeing the goddess, became filled with desire. He approached her alone, filled with joy. The great demon, deluded by the great illusion, spoke to her with humility, folding his hands.

Virochana said: Brahma, Vishnu, Maheshwara, Indra, and all the moving and stationary beings have been conquered by me, the lord of the universe, and now I am your servant, O beautiful lady. I will be under your command forever. Be my wife and come to my home, my beloved.

Hearing his words, the beautiful Mohini said to him: If you ever act against my word, I will leave you.

Saying "So be it," he took her and went to his supreme home. He became devoted to her and remained by her side. Through various clever means, Hari, in the form of Mohini, deluded the wicked one who was ready to be destroyed. The noble Mohini, who enchants everyone, said to him: O lord of great intellect, perform an oil massage with my hands. Afterward, take a bath and eat, O observer of good vows. Then, touch me, O demon king, and I shall be yours forever.

Deluded by Ganesha's Maya, he said "So be it" and prepared to have the massage with scented oil. Taking the oil, Mohini removed the supreme crown from his head and, with her hands, joyfully rubbed his head. At the mere touch of her hands, the demon's head shattered into a hundred pieces. Mohini was delighted.

Saying "Victory to Lambodara (Ganesha)," Mohini disappeared. Seeing the demon dead, the demons fled to the netherworld. Then, with the divine sages, Vishnu went to his own realm. Madhava (Vishnu) made the worlds follow their own respective duties.

Thus, through the Magha vow and Ganesha, Janardana killed that demon king who had conquered the three worlds. Whoever listens to this or reads it while remembering the Lord of the Ganas will be filled with all happiness forever.

Thus ends the twenty-fifth chapter of the Magha Masa Mahatmya in the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Killing of Virochana."

Chapter 26 : The Salvation of Various People in the Glory of the Month of Magha

Suta said: There was a Brahmana of the Mudgala lineage named Vishvasaha, who was extremely devoted to Lord Ganesha and always yearned for the company of the righteous. Once, this fortunate soul observed the vows of the month of Magha and worshipped the Ganesha Panchaka (five forms of Ganesha) daily with great devotion. One morning, while he was going to bathe, a terrifying demoness saw him and rushed to devour him. Seeing her hideous face, he became frightened, but he chanted the name of Ganesha and sprinkled water on her using sacred Durva grass.

O great sage, as soon as the water touched her, she became purified and addressed Vishvasaha. The demoness said: I have sought your refuge; please save me from the ocean of worldly existence. By your mere sight, my mind has become pure. Hearing her sorrowful words, the noble Vishvasaha asked with compassion: Who are you, O sinful one? What evil deeds have you committed, and why are you weeping so bitterly?

The demoness replied: In my previous life, I was a beautiful Brahmana woman, but I abandoned my duties and indulged in sinful sensual pleasures. After death, I suffered in hell and was reborn as a demoness, constantly tormented by insatiable hunger. I have wandered everywhere, eating whatever I could find, yet the fire of hunger within me never subsided. Ever since my birth as a demoness, I have never felt satisfied. Seeing you, I came to eat you, but by the touch of your water, I have regained the memory of my past lives. O noble one, please save me, for you are capable; otherwise, I shall end my life before you.

Hearing this, the wise Vishvasaha reflected and said with joy: The glory of Ganesha’s name is beyond description; I have heard this in the Puranas and it is true. By the water mixed with Durva, you have regained your memory. I now grant you the supreme merit of my Magha bath for one day; go now to your own abode. Having said this, he transferred the merit of one day of his Magha bath to her through water. Immediately, the messengers of Lord Ganesha arrived, placed her in a celestial chariot, and took her to the divine realm of Svananda.

Witnessing this miracle, Vishvasaha completed his bath and continued his worship of Ganesha. When his vow was concluded, Lord Vinayaka himself appeared to grant a boon to the Brahmana. Seeing the Lord of Ganas, Vishvasaha stood up, bowed, and began to dance in ecstasy. The elephant-headed Lord said: Ask for any boons you desire, O fortunate one.

Vishvasaha replied: My life and birth are blessed, for I have seen the elephant-faced Lord. Even the Vedas and sages like Shankara do not fully know you, so how is it that you have come yourself? I am fulfilled by the sight of your lotus feet. What else should I ask? Just grant me firm devotion unto you. O Lord of Ganas, I bow to you, the supreme of the supreme, the Lord of all. You are the creator, the sustainer, and the destroyer, manifesting in various forms. I do not know how to praise you, for it is only through the wisdom born of your vision that I am able to speak.

After Vishvasaha bowed, the Lord of Ganas raised him and said: This hymn you have composed will grant all desires to those who read or hear it. It will increase devotion and grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation. Your devotion to me will remain firm and bring you happiness. May all your desires be fulfilled. Having said this, the Lord vanished. Vishvasaha continued his worship and eventually attained the same state as the sages.

Hear another wonderful story of the month of Magha. A Chandala named Nila heard the stories of Ganesha and began observing the Magha bath and singing Ganesha’s songs every night. Once, while returning home after singing, five ghosts confronted him. Seeing their terrifying forms, he remembered Ganesha, and at the mere remembrance, the ghosts became frozen. They were purified by the sight of his devotion and begged him to save them from their ghostly existence. Nila, knowing the truth, said: I will not give you the merit of the entire song, but I will give you the merit of one verse. By that, you shall be liberated. As soon as he gave them the merit of one verse, they attained divine bodies and ascended to the realm of Ganesha. Nila also continued his devotion and eventually attained the Lord with his entire family.

Thus, I have narrated the glory of the Magha vow, which grants worldly enjoyment and liberation to those who hear or read it. It bestows wealth, progeny, and health. What more do you wish to hear?

Chapter 27 : the Enlightenment of Shivadatta

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O Suta, you who are well-versed in all meanings, please describe the glory of the month of Vaishakha, the month of Shravana, and the wonders of the intercalary month (Malamasa).

Suta said: The great demon Vritrasura, having conquered the three worlds with his might, prepared to destroy the gods by obstructing their religious duties. The gods, filled with fear, sought refuge with their preceptor, Brihaspati, while observing fasts. The highly radiant and noble-minded Brihaspati said to them: O gods, along with Indra, perform the excellent vow of Vaishakha. By the power of this vow, the Lord of the Gods will become capable of slaying the great demon with a thunderbolt made from the bones of Dadhichi.

The divine sages then bowed to him and went to the forest, where they secretly and joyfully performed the Vaishakha vow. The divine sages daily worshipped the Ganesha-panchaka (the five-fold Ganesha) and meditated upon Him with great devotion. On the full moon day of Vaishakha, while engaged in worship, they saw in a dream the Lord of the Ganas, who bestows all happiness, standing in the form of an idol. The idol, skilled in speech, said to them: I will slay the great demon by taking refuge in the body of Indra.

The gods and sages, upon waking, were astonished and, after discussing their shared dream, realized its significance. Then, the Lord of the Gods, filled with resolve, took the gods and went to the sage Dadhichi. Having requested and received the thunderbolt made from his bones, he, surrounded by the gods, waged war. O Brahmana, the fierce battle between Indra and Vritrasura, and between the gods and the demons, was so terrible that I cannot describe it. Indra, remembering Ganapati, struck the great demon with the thunderbolt. Seeing him dead, the demons fled to the netherworld.

The gods, filled with joy, along with Brahma, went to the forest of Badari to see Ganapati and praised Him.

The divine sages said: Salutations to You, the Supreme Soul, the Lord of the Ganas, and the embodiment of the Ganas. Salutations to You, the player of infinite illusions, the Supreme Lord, and the Lord of Brahma. Salutations to You, the destroyer of great obstacles, the great soul, the Lord of obstacles, and the protector of the devotees. Salutations to You, Heramba, the protector of the lowly, Dhundhi, Lambodara, and the Lord of all gods. Salutations to You, the protector of the helpless, the Lord of those who have no lord, the Lord of all lords, and the one with the curved trunk. Salutations to You, the highest of the high, the bestower of yoga and peace, the Lord of yoga. Salutations to You, who are with attributes (Saguna) and without attributes (Nirguna), and who are beyond both. Salutations to You, who move through infinite illusions, who are beyond illusion, who are illusion itself, and who bewilder the deluded. Salutations to You, who sit upon a mouse, who carry the mouse-banner, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, and who dwell in Your own bliss. How can we praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, who are the embodiment of peace and beyond the beyond? Even the Vedas and the yogis, despite their knowledge of scriptures, are unable to fully comprehend You.

Having said this, the divine sages bowed to Him and returned to their respective abodes with joyful hearts. The people, following their respective castes and stages of life, became free from distress, and all the divine sages were filled with divine grace. Thus, O Brahmana, by the vow of the month of Vaishakha, the Lord of the Gods slew Vritrasura and attained happiness along with the gods.

Listen further to the glory born of the Vaishakha vow, which destroys all sins and sorrows for those who hear it. There was a Brahmana named Shivadatta, who was a devotee of Ganesha and worshipped Him daily. He lived according to his dharma, along with his wife and children, and performed the Agnihotra sacrifice with great radiance. The great yogi Garga, who was well-versed in all meanings, arrived there, and Shivadatta worshipped him with great devotion. Seeing his son-in-law (or disciple) always engaged in service and desiring liberation, Garga was very pleased.

Garga said to Shivadatta: O son, tell me what you desire. I am pleased with your character and will grant it without doubt. Hearing this, Shivadatta, with folded hands, bowed to the great soul and spoke words that bring happiness.

Shivadatta said: O best of yogis, please tell me the secret of the Vedas, by which I may become fulfilled through service.

Garga said: Among the eighty-four lakh species, the human form is the best, as it allows for the acquisition of knowledge. It is the vessel for Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, and there is nothing better than this. Among humans, the Brahmana is considered the best by the scriptures, as he is entitled to all rites and is a knower of the scriptures. Among them, one who follows his own dharma and performs penance is superior, and among them, the wise man is the greatest, as he sees the Self everywhere. Among them, the yogi is especially superior, and among yogis, the devotee of Ganesha (Ganapatya) is the highest; there is none greater than him. Regardless of one's caste, the Ganapatya is considered the highest. Know this, O seeker, as the destroyer of all doubts. I have revealed to you the essence of the Vedanta. Therefore, worship Ganesha, and you will be fulfilled.

Thus ends the twenty-seventh chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which deals with the glory of the month of Vaishakha and the enlightenment of Shivadatta.

Chapter 28 : The Discourse of Shuka

Shivadatta said: O great Yogi, please speak and dispel all my doubts. The yogis here express many different opinions. Some call Shiva, Vishnu, the Sun, Shakti, or Ganesha the Supreme. Others speak of Karma, Jnana, the disembodied soul, or the Self as supreme. Many hold various views, such as Yoga, Sankhya, Bliss, the Unmanifest, Knowledge, Food, Prana, or the Mind. You, however, speak of Ganesha as supreme in every way. Please tell us, O Lord, who is worthy of worship by all, for you are our ultimate refuge.

Garga said: I will describe to you an ancient history. Listen with a focused mind, my son, to this great and universally accepted discourse. Once, in the Naimisha Forest, Vashistha joyfully performed a sacrifice. Great sages, kings of gods, and beings from the three worlds, all learned in various disciplines, gathered there for Vashistha’s grand festival. Yogis led by Shuka, Nagas like Shesha, and others assembled and conversed with one another. Experts in Vedanta debated, each firmly holding to their own doctrine. Since they could not reach a conclusion, they bowed to Shuka, the bearer of peace, and asked:

The Sages said: O Bhagavan, knower of the essence, wisest among yogis, you are supreme in scriptural debate. Please tell us the essence for our benefit. You are the greatest of yogis, established in the state of divine glory. We shall follow whatever conclusion you provide.

Hearing their words, Shuka, remembering Ganapati, spoke to the assembly:

Shuka said: The food-sheath (Annamaya) is known as Brahman in the waking state, as understood through Vedic discourse. Prana is Brahman, the illuminator of the prana-sheaths. The mind-sheath is called Brahman. The intellect-sheath is the indicator of Brahman. These three sheaths represent the subtle, dream-state, and inner-self. The bliss-sheath is known by the wise as Brahman, corresponding to the deep sleep state. Consciousness, residing in awareness, is the Fourth (Turiya), known as Asmita (I-am-ness), the illuminator of the sound-body. The Bindu (point) is called Brahman, the sustainer of the body and soul. The union of these four bodies is the 'Thou' (Tvam). Brahman is considered fourfold, characterized by creation, sustenance, and dissolution. The indweller (Dehi) in these four bodies is the Self, devoid of creation, sustenance, and destruction, and is the basis of the 'I' (Aham). Due to the illusion of the Bindu, the One appears as many, but the Vedas declare Him as the basis of 'That' (Tat). When identified with the body, He is called the Jiva; when free from body-identification, He is the Supreme Self.

The union of body and soul is called Yoga. Being devoid of mind and speech, He is called the form of pure knowledge. He is neither external nor internal, neither equal nor the ego. He is not the union of the four, but is remembered as the essence of the Bindu. He is not in the form of ego and is always devoid of duality. Therefore, what can be said of that which is the form of pure knowledge? Everything is known through knowledge, but knowledge itself has no other knower. Thus, Brahman is called the 'Is' (Asi). Knowledge associated with nature sustains the person. The Lord, having created the bliss arising from the union of nature and the person, plays in duality as the 'Is'. From knowledge, the sense of rising occurs. Beyond that is the Brahman called 'Supreme Knowledge', devoid of play, attained by yogis through the destruction of knowledge.

When knowledge is destroyed, who can describe the state? Thus, Brahman is called the ultimate Sankhya. Duality exists in the Brahman that is always body and soul. Beyond that is the Supreme Knowledge. Through the destruction of knowledge, one attains the state devoid of rising. In Brahman, there is no rising for one who has become Brahman. When Brahman is full of rising, it is called Sankhya; when it is devoid of rising, it is the state of the yogis. The union of all these Brahmans is the self-evident Brahman. He who is the Lord of Ganas is the Lord of Brahman; He is attained through peace and yoga. In union, the Lord of Ganas is the letter 'Ga'; in separation, He is the letter 'Na'; He is the Lord of both.

Garga said: Having said this, the Yogi Shuka became silent. Everyone was amazed and praised him. Freed from doubts and having abandoned their narrow views, they became devoted to the worship of Ganesha. They attained peace and became followers of Ganesha. I have told you the Shuka Gita, which grants the knowledge of Brahman. Whoever hears or reads this will become one with Brahman. There is no knowledge equal to this. By hearing it, one attains all perfections and eventually resides in Supreme Bliss. It is the essence of the Vedas and Upanishads. It should not be taught to the wicked, but only to those with pure hearts.

Chapter 29 : Granting of Devotion to Shiva

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shiva Datta said: Having heard the Gita of Shuka from your lotus-like mouth, I am greatly satisfied. Now, O Father, tell me how I may attain Ganesha.

Garga said: There is a supreme vow that is near and grants great success. By observing it during the month of Vaisakha, you shall become a devotee of Ganesha. Having said this, the great yogi Garga gave him the supreme five-syllable mantra of Ganesha, accompanied by the proper rituals. Initiated by him, Garga went his own way, and Shiva Datta performed the supreme vow for the pleasure of Ganesha.

O Shaunaka, during the month of Vaisakha, absorbed in meditation, he pleased Ganesha, the granter of all success, using the five-syllable mantra. On the fourth day of the bright fortnight, the Lord of the Ganas approached the sage in the form of a hungry Brahmana, seeking alms. Seeing him arrive, the illustrious Brahmana Shiva Datta bowed with folded hands and spoke to the guest.

Shiva Datta said: Please be seated. Tell me, O Sage, why have you come? If it is possible, I shall do it for the pleasure of the Lord of Obstacles.

The Brahmana said: Know me, O fortunate one, as the son of Parashara. I have come to your house hungry for a meal. Hearing these words, he spoke with joy: O Sage, are you speaking of the vow of the bright fourth day? The gods and sages observe this fast to serve the Elephant-faced Lord; have you forgotten, O fortunate one? Tell me.

Hearing this, the son of Parashara said to him: I do not know of any vows; I am always hungry and craving food. Hearing this, the son of Garga bowed and offered him the food prepared for Ganesha. Having eaten and become satisfied, he spoke with joy.

The son of Parashara said: I am satisfied with your devotion, O best of Brahmanas. Ask for a boon, and I shall grant whatever is in your heart, O Sage.

Garga said: What shall I ask for, O great Brahmana? The entire world is filled with illusion. I am devoted to Ganesha; go now to your own abode.

Hearing these words, the Brahmana said to him again: I have come as a boon-giver; I will take you to my own home. Seeing his persistence, Garga said: Show me Ganesha, my Lord; I do not seek any other boon.

Being told this, the Brahmana spoke words of delusion: Why do you seek the Elephant-faced one, abandoning all kinds of prosperity? I am the chief of the great yogis and will give you all happiness. Saying this, he showed Garga the realm of Indra. Indra, accompanied by various gods, bowed to the Brahmana and said: I am your servant, O fortunate one; enjoy the divine pleasures here.

Having said this, he created supreme splendor through illusion, but Garga, considering it perishable, did not waver in his devotion to Ganesha. Then Indra disappeared and returned to his city. Then Brahma arrived there, accompanied by the Satyaloka. Brahma, filled with joy, said to Garga: Rule over the Satyaloka, O fortunate one, and worship the Lord of the Ganas afterwards.

Seeing the incomparable pleasure, Garga remained devoted to the Lord of Obstacles. He was not deluded and said: I do not desire such enjoyment, O Creator. Then Brahma, the grandfather of the worlds, disappeared. Then Keshava arrived there, accompanied by Vaikuntha. Seeing the splendor of Vaikuntha, Garga remained devoted to Ganesha, and having rejected the great Vishnu who was praying to him, he remained absorbed in yoga.

Then he also left, and Shankara arrived there, accompanied by Kailasa, which is full of various enchanting splendors. Though requested by Shiva to rule over Kailasa, he did not do so, for his mind was fixed on Ganesha. Shiva then disappeared, and the great yogi, the son of Parashara, spoke again to Garga, who was intent on criticizing.

The son of Parashara said: You are a fool and unfortunate. What will you do with Ganesha? Enjoy the various supreme pleasures that are manifestations of Brahma. After enjoying the pleasures born of true resolve, you will eventually worship the Lord of the Ganas; there is no doubt. Become the lord of the universe.

Garga said: The pleasure born of the needy is false and dependent on others, O Sage. The pleasure born of true resolve is the only supreme truth. The distinctions of truth and falsehood are undoubtedly filled with illusion. I do not desire them, as I reside in a body that is momentary, O best of yogis. Even if you make me immortal through divine power, I do not desire such divine pleasure, O master of yoga. Why have you come here to deceive me, O great villain? Go to your own abode, O Brahmana, you who are a breaker of devotion to Ganesha.

Thus insulted by him, the great sage, the son of Parashara, laughed and immediately took the form of Ganesha. He said to the fortunate Shiva Datta: Behold me, the Lord of the Ganas, O son of Parashara. Seeing the Lord of the Ganas, he bowed with folded hands. His eyes filled with tears, and he danced, overwhelmed by love.

Having regained his composure, he worshipped the Elephant-faced Lord. Filled with joy, Shiva Datta prayed with folded hands.

Shiva Datta said: Salutations to you, O Ganesha, son of Parashara. Salutations to the son of Shiva, the son of Shesha. Salutations to the son of all, the mother and father of all. Salutations to the brother, the friend, and the one who takes the form of a daughter-in-law. Salutations to you, who have infinite forms, who enjoy various pleasures, and who are the Lord devoid of desire. Salutations to you, the storehouse of infinite virtues, the Lord of virtues. Salutations to Ganesha, the supreme Lord, the embodiment of complete bliss. Salutations to you, the creator, the sustainer, and the destroyer, who are the witness, devoid of beginning, middle, and end. Salutations to the Lord of self-bliss, the one who has a mouse as his vehicle. Salutations to the Lord of success and intelligence, the granter of success and intelligence. Salutations to the Lord of yoga, the embodiment of yoga and peace. Salutations to the Elephant-faced one, the granter of peace. Salutations to the supreme Lord, the protector of the poor. Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, the granter of happiness to all. How can I praise you, O Lord of the Ganas? The Vedas have attained peace in you. Therefore, salutations to you, O Lord; be satisfied, O Great-bellied one. No one is capable of describing your virtues, O Lord, just as no one can count the dust particles, the stars, or the drops of rain. I am blessed in every way, for I have seen the Elephant-faced one, who is beyond the reach of Vedanta and accessible to yogis like Shiva.

Having praised Ganesha in this way, the best of sages bowed. The Lord of the Ganas, who is fond of his devotees, said to his devotee:

Shri Ganesha said: Ask for boons, O fortunate one; I shall grant whatever is in your heart. I am pleased with your devotion and this hymn, O best of sages.

Shiva Datta said: Grant me firm and unwavering devotion to you. I do not ask for anything else, O Lord of the Ganas; I am devoted to your feet.

Shri Ganesha said: Your devotion to me shall be intense, O wise one. By merely remembering me, your work shall be accomplished and will grant great success. This hymn composed by you, when read or heard by men, will constantly grant all success. Whatever one desires will be fulfilled. By reading this hymn, O sage, devotion to me will be granted.

Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahma, disappeared. Shiva Datta, contemplating Ganesha, remained in his hermitage. He became the foremost among the devotees of Ganesha, always worshipped by the best of yogis. In the end, the yogi merged into the Lord of Obstacles, O Shaunaka.

Thus, the supreme vow of Vaisakha, which grants all desires, has been told to you, O fortunate one; hearing it grants all success. Whoever reads or hears this story of Shiva Datta, which is connected to the Vaisakha vow, shall attain all its fruits.

Thus ends the twenty-ninth chapter of the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled the Granting of Devotion to Shiva, in the Vaisakha Masa Mahatmya.

Chapter 30 : The Description of the Deliverance of Various People in the Glory of the Month of Vaishakha

Suta said: There was a Vaishya (merchant) by the name of Vishwavasu, born in the Dravida region. Upon hearing the glory of the month of Vaishakha related to Lord Ganesha, he became filled with joy. He was always devoted to his family and his business. During the month of Vaishakha, he would bathe and engage in the act of offering lamps (Deep-dana). On the night of the full moon, while returning to his home, he abandoned his business and stayed on the path. At that moment, a cruel-looking demon came to that street in the city to devour him. Seeing the demon approaching, Vishwavasu became terrified; he remembered Lord Ganesha in his heart and fell to the ground in fear.

Seeing him fallen on the ground, the demon suddenly remembered his past life and began to weep while touching him. After a moment, the demon bowed to the fortunate Vaishya and spoke. Dhumra said: Know me as Dhumra, born in a Vaishya family. By merely seeing you, I have remembered my past life, without a doubt. In my previous birth, I was a Brahmana named Suruchi, living in the lineage of Vishwamitra, but I was addicted to sinful deeds. I gave initiation into the Puranic Matsya Purana to a Shudra. He would read this Purana with joy every day. This act was condemned by the Brahmanas as it violated one's own duty. When I died, the messengers of Yama took me to their abode.

Chitragupta then said to Yama, who is skilled in justice: "This Brahmana was always devoted to his own duties and was of good conduct, but he foolishly taught the Matsya Purana to a Shudra. Even when warned by the Brahmanas, the sinner persisted in his sinful act." Hearing this, the King of Dharma, filled with anger, said to the messenger: "Look at me and punish him." The messenger did as he was told. O best of Vaishyas, I have suffered greatly as a demon, wandering day and night, consumed by hunger. Seeing you, due to my past merits, I have become one who remembers his past life. Now, O wise one, deliver me from this cycle of worldly existence. If you do not, I will give up my life before you. Having said this, the great demon bowed and wept.

The Vaishya, filled with compassion and surprise, asked: "O wise Dhumra, tell me what I should do to deliver you?" Dhumra replied: "You perform the offering of lamps before Dhundhi (Ganesha) in the month of Vaishakha. Give me the merit of that act, and by that, I shall be liberated." Vishwavasu replied: "If a noble person offers a lamp before Ganesha, the merit obtained is infinite; I will never give that away. However, listen to my words: take the merit of the lamp lit with a single wick, and by that, you will be liberated." Having said this, he gave him the merit of the lamp lit with a single wick offered before Ganesha. Then, celestial beings arrived in a grand chariot, took the demon Dhumra, and ascended to the abode of Ganesha.

O great sage, how can I describe the glory of the vows observed in the month of Vaishakha for the devotees of Ganesha? Listen further, O sage, to another great account of the month of Vaishakha, which destroys all sins for those who hear or read it. A great sinner, a Shudra, once came to the temple of Ganesha at night, intending to steal clothes. During the month of Vaishakha, he approached the Lord and saw a lamp flickering near Dhundhi. He adjusted the wick, making the lamp shine brightly, all while still intent on stealing. Suddenly, the king's guards arrived to protect the temple and, seeing him, killed him.

After suffering various torments in the realm of Yama, the Shudra was brought before Yama, who said in anger: "You adjusted the wick of the lamp before Ganesha, which provided light and brought joy. You have earned the merit of that act; you shall enjoy the fruits of that first, and then tell me your sins, O sinner, and I shall do as you wish." The Shudra said to Yama: "O Lord, first give me the merit, and afterwards, I shall suffer for my great sins." Yama agreed. Consequently, he was born as a Vaishya named Dharmapriya, who was wealthy and lived a life of pleasure in his youth. He became a devoted worshipper of Ganesha and performed the lamp offering with great joy. In the end, free from sin, he attained the Lord of the Ganas and became one with the Brahman due to his past impressions.

Thus, even a small virtuous act in the month of Vaishakha delivers people; there is no doubt about it, O sage. This account of the month of Vaishakha, which grants both worldly enjoyment and liberation, has been told to you briefly. Whoever reads or hears this will be endowed with all prosperity and, in the end, will be absorbed into Lord Ganesha, becoming devoted to Him.

Chapter 31 : The Killing of Jalandhara

Salutations to Shri Ganesha. Suta said: O Brahmin, now listen to the greatness of the month of Shravana, which bestows happiness and is dear to the Lord of the Ganas; I shall describe it to you briefly.

Rama, the son of Dasharatha, who was valiant and the incarnation of Lord Vishnu himself, was seated in his assembly when some Brahmins arrived, and he bowed to them. They were honored by him, and they spoke of the misdeeds of Lavana. They said, "O Rama, we have come to you for his destruction; please slay him, O Raghava." Rama agreed and asked the Brahmins, "O learned ones, by what means will I be able to conquer this invincible demon?"

The Brahmins replied, "You will be victorious by performing the consecration (abhisheka) of Ganesha using the Ganesha Sukta. The month of Shravana, which has now arrived, is the most auspicious, O Lord. It is extremely pleasing to Vighnesha; observe the vow during this month." Having said this, they respectfully explained the procedure to him. Shatrughna, the slayer of enemy heroes, worshipped Ganesha there. Observing the fast and consuming only milk, he performed the abhisheka and pleased the Lord of Obstacles.

After completing the month-long vow and bowing to Ganesha, Shatrughna, along with the Brahmins, went to face the demon Lavana. Having trapped him after he was deprived of his trident, the younger brother of Lakshmana remembered the Lord of the Ganas and killed him with a volley of arrows. A fierce battle ensued, which was terrifying to all. Then, Shatrughna severed his head with a sword. The gods showered flowers upon Shatrughna and shouted, "Victory to Heramba!" Honored by the Brahmins and having bowed to the great sages, he established his residence in the beautiful city of Mathura. After some time, having installed his son there, the wise and devout Shatrughna went to Ramachandra in Mathura.

Thus, the fruit of the observance of the great Ganesha during the month of Shravana is said to grant all accomplishments. Now, listen to another great glory of the Shravana vow, which was performed by Shiva in the past for the destruction of Jalandhara.

The demon Jalandhara had conquered all moving and non-moving beings, and by the power of a boon, he became the lord of the three worlds. He forced the gods to perform demonic deeds everywhere, and neither Svaha nor Svadha (offerings) were performed anywhere on earth. After a long time, he went to seize Parvati, which led to a war between the gods and the demons. The gods were defeated and fled in all directions. Jalandhara, enraged, fought with his weapons. Even Shambhu (Shiva) was defeated by the Lord of the demons and fled in fear. The great power (Shakti) vanished, and Jalandhara was astonished.

The demon king returned to his home, deeply distressed and overwhelmed by the arrows of Kama, and he remembered the Mother of the Universe. He found no peace anywhere and burned with the fire of desire, suffering greatly. Meanwhile, all the gods took refuge in a mountain valley and deliberated on the destruction of Jalandhara. Brihaspati then said to Shiva and the other gods, "O beloved ones, perform the observance of the Lord of the Ganas in the month of Shravana."

Agreeing, the gods, led by Shambhu and the sages, pleased the Lord of the Ganas with Durva grass. Day and night, they worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with one-pointed focus. When the month of Shravana ended, they set out on a journey in the month of Bhadrapada. After completing the fast on the fifth day and bowing to Gajanana, they were guided by a voice from the sky and went to confront the great demon.

After a fierce battle with Jalandhara, Shiva remembered Gajanana in his heart and struck him with his trident. Upon being struck, the great demon merged into the body of Shiva. The gods and sages then worshipped Shambhu with devotion. Having thus defeated the great demon Jalandhara, Sadashiva restored the gods to their respective positions as before. Lord Shankara established the world with its social order (Varnashrama) and remained devoted to the observances of the month of Shravana. With single-mindedness, Shambhu served the Lord of the Ganas, constantly meditating upon him.

I have thus narrated to you the vow of the month of Shravana, by the power of which Shambhu conquered Jalandhara. Whoever hears this daily, causes others to hear it, or reads it himself, shall enjoy all pleasures here and attain supreme bliss in the end.

Chapter 32 : Destruction of Galava's Doubts

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: In the city of Avanti, there lived a supreme Brahmana named Galava, who was well-versed in all dharmas and an expert in all scriptures. Possessing the power of both curse and blessing, and endowed with the radiance of penance, the sage once traveled across the entire earth. After completing his journey, he went to his guru, the great sage Vishvamitra, and stayed there, being of good conduct. Seeing the treasure of penance, Vishvamitra, absorbed in the worship of Ganesha with single-minded devotion, Galava approached him with folded hands and spoke.

Galava said: It is universally accepted that the body, composed of the five elements, is perishable. O Lord, why do you worship Ganesha, who resides within such a body? You are the guru of the greatest yogis and the bearer of the status of Brahman. Why, having abandoned the nature of Brahman, do you worship the Lord of Obstacles? He is described in the Vedas as the Lord of Brahman (Brahmanaspati). How did He, who is the Lord, take on a body and become the elephant-faced one? Is this worship for the sake of pleasure or for attaining Brahman? Tell me, O Master, what is the opinion of the yogis regarding this as stated in the Vedas? By worshipping Ganesha, a man becomes pure-hearted, and without doubt, he becomes a knower of yoga and established in yoga in a short time. When one is focused on the nature of the Self everywhere, why should one abandon the elephant-faced one and wander like an inert or mad person? O great yogi, are you worshipping the elephant-faced one while being established in the peace of yoga, or is it for the sake of gathering the people?

Suta said: Having heard him speak thus, the great sage Vishvamitra rebuked Galava and spoke to his disciple, the giver of knowledge.

Vishvamitra said: You are a fool and do not know Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman. Do not think of Him as being like everyone else, O Galava. The intellect, consisting of five states of consciousness, takes on a body according to its own will. It creates various illusions and siddhis at all times. Ganesha, the master of these, took on a body for the sake of play, just as one who possesses maya plays with it. His body is the embodiment of the universe, and his head is that of an elephant; in the union of these, the elephant-faced one is the bearer of a body that is full of Brahman. I have described that Dhundhi (Ganesha) resides in the hearts of yogis, while for the ignorant, He is formless and composed of the five elements. For Ganesha, there is no difference between the Saguna (with attributes) and Nirguna (without attributes). There is no birth or destruction for Him, O best of fools. He is said to be embodied, yet He is devoid of a body by His own will. The Lord of Yoga remains by concealing His own Self. When the entire universe, along with Brahman, dissolves, He remains in the yoga known as Brahmanaspati, being one with it. The form of the man and the elephant are established as non-different. The longing for devotion arises for the sake of attaining Him. A yogi becomes one with Brahman through the power of yoga, yet the power of Brahman does not exist in his body. The meaning of the word Brahmanaspati—the essence—always exists in the body of Ganesha; therefore, yogis worship Him.

Having said this, the great and powerful yogi Vishvamitra became silent and worshipped the Lord of the Ganas. The disciple Galava bowed down and spoke again, asking him to remove his doubts by criticizing his own ignorance.

Galava said: I am covered in ignorance and ask you, O best of sages. Save your disciple through yoga and make me established in yoga. O best of yogis, Ganesha is always established in the peace of yoga; how is He known as a Deva (god) by those who speak the Vedas? The Devas are endowed with Sattva, humans are governed by Rajas, and the Asuras are said to be Tamasic, dwelling in the three worlds. O all-knowing Master, please remove this doubt of mine so that, being free of doubt, I may worship the elephant-faced one.

Vishvamitra said: The Devas, such as Indra, are endowed with the quality of Sattva and dwell in heaven; there is no doubt that Indra is the supreme among them. At the end of the day of Brahma, they always dissolve. Other Devas are merely workers, and they become humans again. One attains the position of the creator Brahma through karma; he is described as the form of karma and does not reach the equality of a Deva. He is called Parameshthi (the supreme abode). Nothing higher than that is attainable by humans. Shiva, Vishnu, the Sun, and Shakti are the four Ishvaras (Lords) who are true in their resolve, both with and without attributes. Ganesha is the embodiment of Brahman and acts according to His own will, O wise one. This should be understood by those seeking certainty. The root 'divu' is read by sages as meaning 'play'. Because they play everywhere, the Devas are called 'all-worshipped'. Being devoted to their own dharma and engaged in their own work and play, Indra and others are called Devas by those who know the scriptures. Having created the entire moving and non-moving universe, Brahma plays in it always; therefore, he is called a Deva by the sages. Having created infinite millions of universes by His maya, the four, including Shiva, play in them as Devas. Because their resolve is true, they are called Ishvaras. They are called Devas because of their playful nature in both the Saguna and Nirguna states. Having created the fourfold universe—the non-existent and the existent—the Lord of Obstacles plays within it, being united with the soul of the universe. Because He plays in the worlds and among the Brahman-knowers and the Ganas, He is called the Lord of the Ganas (Ganeshvara); see this described in the Vedas. Therefore, Brahma and the others, who are called Devas, are never equal to Him.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Vishvamitra, Galava was greatly astonished and spoke to the great sage, filled with doubt.

Galava said: All the Devas are consumers of sacrificial offerings (havirbhujas). In the same way, Maheshvara and others are also partakers of these shares. If the gods are troubled by the destruction of their rituals by demons, then Shambhu and others also observe fasting. Therefore, they are seen as equal to the Devas; there is no doubt in this. Tell me, O best of yogis, to remove the doubt of your disciple.

Vishvamitra said: The Devas, including Brahma, are indeed made to fast by the demons due to their ignorance and demonic nature. Now, hear another truth that destroys all doubts. Indra and other Devas are described as having limited power. When a Manvantara passes, the Devas, including Indra, are deprived of their shares and abandon their bodies through penance and meditation. Other Devas become the ones who enjoy the shares in the next Manvantara; therefore, Indra and others are called beings of limited power, O sage. They are consumers of offerings only in their own Manvantaras. When the Manvantara is destroyed, the Devas are deprived of their shares. Similarly, Brahma himself is not a permanent consumer of offerings; he enjoys them only for the duration of two Parardhas. Therefore, his power is limited to a Kalpa; there is no doubt. How can there be equality between him and Indra? The wise call him Parameshthi, the eater of offerings. The offering to the one with unlimited power is accepted everywhere. Shiva and others are known as consumers of offerings, and they are superior to them. Therefore, they are endowed with power and are the eternal consumers of offerings.

Galava said: O giver of honor, the Devas are known in the scriptures as consumers of offerings. Since Ganesha is devoid of a share in the sacrifice, how is He a Deva?

Vishvamitra said: In various sacrifices, Ganesha is invoked first. He is to be worshipped, is a consumer of offerings, and is the giver of all success. Because He is the refuge of the 'one part' (the collective), He is called Ganesha. What doubt is there, O sage, regarding the union of the groups? In the Vedas, the sacrifice for the Lord of the Ganas is described separately. The Lord of Obstacles is the consumer of offerings there, known as Brahmanaspati. Vishnu, Shankara, and others are Devas who rely on individual shares; therefore, they should be worshipped in their own positions with effort. In all actions, the Lord of Obstacles is declared to be the first to be worshipped. He is worshipped by all because He protects all the groups of obstacles.

Galava said: The five Devas are considered equal, both Saguna and Nirguna. How did Ganesha become the one worshipped even by those who are worshipped by all?

Vishvamitra said: The Ishvaras, starting with Shambhu, are described as Saguna and Nirguna. They were not of the form of yoga through the nature of true resolve. All those who rely on a single quality are called Saguna. They dissolve at the time of the great dissolution because they possess qualities. The Saguna are perishable, while the Nirguna are not. Shiva and others are considered by the wise to be in the union of both, being the form of yoga. Because of the union of the man and the elephant, Ganesha is the very embodiment of yoga. He is both Saguna and Nirguna, and He became one with that. The Lord is always the form of yoga, whether Saguna or Nirguna. Having created both by His own will, He plays, being devoid of attachment. Residing in the city of Svananda, the elephant-faced one is embodied. There is no destruction of Svananda; reflect on this in the Vedas and other scriptures. Therefore, He is imperishable and shines as the bearer of a body. As the Lord of Yoga, He remains in the Saguna state while concealing Himself in the Nirguna. Ganesha, the giver of the peace of yoga, conceals the Nirguna and is immediately engaged in the form of yoga by His own will; there is no doubt. Therefore, He is called the King of the Elders by those who speak the Vedas. Because of the nature of being the eldest of the elders, He has attained the status of being worshipped by all. The intellect, consisting of five states of consciousness, is considered to be the giver of illusion. Ganesha, the master of these, is worshipped and resides in my heart.

Having said this, the great yogi became silent. Galava, having abandoned his doubts, bowed down with folded hands.

Thus ends the thirty-second chapter of the Shravana Masa Mahatmya in the eighth section of the Shri Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Destruction of Galava's Doubts."

Chapter 33 : Glory of the Month of Shravana

Suta said: Galava, the best of sages, who was endowed with good intellect, again asked the excellent sage Vishwamitra with humility for the sake of attaining Ganesha.

Galava said: O Lord, I wish to see the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha); please tell me the means for that. I will act according to your command.

Vishwamitra said: Know, O Galava, that the month of Shravana has arrived, which bestows happiness. By performing rituals during this month, you shall see Ganesha, O seeker of perfection.

Having said this, the sage gave him a one-syllable mantra. Galava, having bowed to the great Brahmin, became absorbed in that practice. Meditating on Ganesha during Shravana and intent on the preliminary rites, he chanted the king of mantras according to the prescribed rules. Consuming only milk, Galava pleased Ganesha; always absorbed in meditation, the wise sage contemplated Him.

On the day of the new moon, the Lord of Ganas, controlled by devotion, arrived riding a lion, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, knowing the hearts of all. Seeing Him arrived, Galava, filled with love, stood up, bowed, and first worshipped the Lord of Obstacles. Bowing again to the Lord of all, he praised the leader of the Ganas, the bestower of all perfections, with folded hands and tearful eyes.

Galava said: Salutations again and again to the Lord of Obstacles, who wears a form of immeasurable illusion, who is devoid of illusion, who is the Supreme Soul, who is the Lord of Yoga, and who grants auspicious union. Salutations again and again to the Lord of Obstacles, who wears an inconceivable form, who rides a lion, who has four arms, and who is beautiful. Salutations to Heramba, who resides in the city of Svananda, who is always in the form of self-bliss, and who possesses infinite glory. Salutations to the Lord, the pot-bellied one, who is the form of the Lord of Gods, who is the destroyer of demons, and who is the Lord Himself. Salutations to the One-Tusked Lord, who is the form of Brahman, who causes the play of Siddhi and Buddhi, and whose body is the form of Brahman. Salutations to You, who are with attributes and without attributes, who are the essence of attributes, who always grant peace, and who are of smoke-colored complexion. Salutations to You, who hold the noose and the goad, who hold the one tusk and the gesture of fearlessness, and who are to be worshipped first, being the source of all. How can I praise You, O leader of the Ganas, when even the Vedas are bewildered? Therefore, O Lord of Obstacles, be pleased by my mere salutation.

Suta said: Having praised Ganesha thus, he danced, overwhelmed with love, repeatedly saying, "I am blessed, I have accomplished my purpose." The Lord of Ganas said to him, "Ask for boons, Galava. Being pleased by your observance of the Shravana vow, I shall grant them. This hymn composed by you shall be the bestower of all desires. O Brahmin, those who read or hear it will have their devotion to Me increased. It shall grant wealth, grain, fulfillment, worldly enjoyment, and liberation. May it also grant health and increase faith in Brahman."

Having said this, the Lord of Ganesha became silent. Galava, having bowed to Him, spoke with folded hands. Galava said: Grant me Your devotion, O Lord, and make me a follower of Ganesha. Grant me the yoga that gives peace and fulfillment; I ask for nothing else, O Gajanana. Saying "So be it," Ganesha disappeared. Galava was filled with sorrow due to the separation from Ganesha.

Then he went to the excellent Brahmin Vishwamitra and bowed to him. He narrated the whole incident, and he (Vishwamitra) also spoke, filled with joy. Vishwamitra said: You are blessed, you have accomplished your purpose, and you are worthy of respect, O great sage. Through you, my good disciple, I have been delivered, there is no doubt.

Praised thus by Vishwamitra, Galava bowed to him and went wherever he wished. He always worshipped Ganesha with devotion and a single-minded heart. Galava became worthy of being worshipped by yogis and became a knower of the Supreme Truth. He went to the regions of Ganesha, absorbed in Him. Finally, he happily took up residence in Mayurakshetra. There, he worshipped the Lord of Ganas with devotion. He told stories to the Brahmins who had renounced the world in that region.

This glory of Galava, which destroys sins, has been narrated. By reading or hearing it, a creature attains all perfections. Listen to another story, O sage, which brings all happiness. In Maharashtra, an outcast once heard the glory of the Lord of Ganas, which is based on devotion, from a Brahmin long ago. He fixed his mind on it and became devoted to the Lord of Ganas. When the month of Shravana arrived, he would bathe, go to the temple, and bow down daily with discipline and devotion. Then, engaged in his own work and supporting his family, he performed various tasks for the sake of wealth.

When the month of Shravana ended, he died by divine will. The attendants of Ganesha, filled with joy, took him to their abode. On the way, as he was being taken, a breeze suddenly emanated from his body and touched the bodies of five thieves by divine will. Their intellects were transformed, and the thieves, who were addicted to sinful deeds, said to each other: "We are always of sinful conduct and addicted to theft. We are full of misery to the world; what will be our fate? The fate of sinners in hell is full of misery. There is no doubt that the torture of Yama is unbearable there. Since we will always be dwellers in hell, from now on, no theft should ever be committed by us, O thieves."

Having decided this, they stayed in the forest on the way. Suddenly, ten Brahmin ascetics arrived there. Seeing them joyful with cows, the thieves, who had decided to be sinful, approached with swords to kill them. The Brahmins said: "Why have you come with weapons, O thieves? Take the cows easily, and we shall go." The thieves said: "Do not commit violence, O wise ones, which leads to Brahmin-slaughter. The sin born of Brahmin-slaughter is like a permanent stain." Hearing their words, the thieves, holding swords, said, trembling with fear of sin: "What kind of suffering does the sin of Brahmin-slaughter cause? By what means do sins perish, O Brahmins? We have been of sinful conduct since birth and have committed various sins until now. What should we thieves do? Tell us the means to destroy sin out of compassion, O best of Brahmins."

Hearing the words of the thieves, the Brahmins, who were astonished and filled with compassion, intent on the salvation of the world, said: "Sins are destroyed by repentance, O leaders of thieves. From now on, do not commit sins. Worship the Lord of Ganas, the leader of Brahman, during Shravana. By that, you will be free from sin and attain the supreme goal." Having said this, the noble ones, having taken leave of them, went to their ashram with the cows, filled with joy.

Seeing that the month of Shravana had arrived, the thieves went to their homes and worshipped Ganesha. Bathing in the morning, bowing to Ganesha with devotion, and circumambulating Him, they spent the time, O sage. They all ate only once a day and were devoted to celibacy. They performed the vow for a month and became saints. Having enjoyed all pleasures here, they attained Svananda at the end. Thus, various people attained their goal in Shravana, O Brahmin. Their complete story cannot be told. If this is the case for those with some discipline, what to say of those who perform the vow!

Thus, the glory of the month of Shravana, which grants the status of a follower of Ganesha, has been narrated briefly, O tiger among sages, which brings happiness. Whoever hears, makes others hear, or reads it, attains the status of Ganesha and enjoys various pleasures at the end, O best of sages.

Thus ends the thirty-third chapter in the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Glory of the Month of Shravana," in the story of Dhumravarna.

Chapter 34 :The Dialogue between Dadhichi and Dhaumya

Suta said: O best of sages, I shall now narrate to you the wonderful story of the Malamaasa (the intercalary month), which grants all desires. Listen to it briefly.

Troubled by the impurity (mala) in the form of the sins of the Kali Yuga, the gods, led by the foremost sages, sought refuge in Lord Keshava. Having praised Vishnu, the protector of Dharma, with various hymns, they spoke of the terrible sins born of Kali.

The divine sages said: O Janardana, Dharma exists only in a small measure, overwhelmed by Adharma. Protect those of us who have come to you for refuge, as we are oppressed by the sinful Kali. O Great Lord, why have you abandoned the Earth?

Hearing their words, the Lord, the protector of all, spoke with a voice as deep as a thundercloud.

Hari said: Do not fear, O gods and sages. I shall surely accomplish your task and conquer the invincible Kali.

Having said this, the great Vishnu dismissed the sages. Seeing the terrible strength of Kali, he pondered in his heart. Seeking a way to destroy the impurities of Kali, the Lord went to Mayurakshetra and, devoted to Ganesha, engaged in penance. He observed the month-long vow dedicated to Ganesha, the remover of all impurities, during the Malamaasa.

He worshipped Ganesha with great effort, eating only ghee, bathing at dawn, and meditating on Dhundhi (Ganesha) according to the scriptures. Having completed the month-long vow, Janardana performed the breaking of the fast with Brahmins and fell asleep at night.

In his dream, he saw the Lord of Obstacles, Ganesha, with a smoke-colored body and an elephant face. He was mounted on a horse, holding a sword and a shield in his two hands. The Lord, who nurtures his devotees, spoke to the great Vishnu with a voice as deep as a thundercloud, bringing him great joy.

Shri Ganesha said: O Vishnu, you have performed this Malamaasa vow, which destroys all impurities, out of devotion to me. I am the presiding deity of the Malamaasa, especially in the form of Dhundhi, and I destroy all the deep sorrows and sins born of impurity. Therefore, by becoming like this and remembering me in your heart with devotion, you will conquer Kali and establish Dharma.

Having said this, Ganesha disappeared. Upon waking, Keshava was filled with great joy as he remembered the vision. Bowing to Mayuresha, he assumed a human-like form, mounted a horse, and, holding a sword, became known as Kalki. With the Lord of Obstacles in his heart, he created a vast army from his own body to destroy the wicked.

Kali, who resides in the hearts of many, tried hard to fight him but became powerless. Janardana then established a righteous world, destroying the wicked and gathering the best of Brahmins. Having done this, the Lord, praised by the sages, disappeared and returned to Vaikuntha with a joyful heart.

Shaunaka asked: O Suta, you mentioned that Ganesha, in his Dhumravarna avatar, fought Kali in the past and established the Krita Yuga. Now you say Kalki Vishnu conquered the wicked Kali. Why are there two different accounts that create doubt?

Suta said: The terrible Kali was created by the Self-born (Brahma). Accompanied by an army of sins, he conquers Dharma at every moment. To destroy him, the gods and sages have repeatedly defeated him through various incarnations. Yet, by the boon of Ganesha, Kali is never completely eradicated; he rises again.

In that age, a woman becomes pregnant at the age of ten, and the lifespan of humans is only thirty years. Everyone on Earth is of mixed caste, and Dharma remains only in a small fraction. At that time, Vishnu, prayed to by the gods, takes the form of Kalki to conquer Kali and re-establish Dharma.

When a woman becomes pregnant at the age of five and the lifespan is only sixteen years, seeing the terrible Kali, the gods led by Shiva are unable to defeat him. Dharma exists only in name, and people are filled with misery and cries of distress. At that time, they all seek refuge in Ganesha. The smoke-colored Lord of the Ganas then conquers the invincible Kali.

Thus, O great sage, having re-established Dharma, he acts according to the times. Seeing the strength of Kali, he takes these incarnations. In this way, Janardana, by observing the vow in the Malamaasa, conquered the impurities of Kali, which are the source of all great sins.

Now listen to another greatness of the Malamaasa. Dhaumya, who was full of gentle penance and great radiance, went to Dadhichi, who was well-versed in Yoga, to attain the knowledge of Yoga. Bowing to him, he stood humbly, having been welcomed by the sage. Dhaumya, the best of those who perform penance, spoke to the great Dadhichi with folded hands.

Dhaumya said: Through penance, one can attain heavenly enjoyments, but it is said that penance alone does not grant the realization of Brahman. Therefore, O Yogi, teach me the Yoga that grants peace, so that I may become fulfilled.

Hearing Dhaumya’s words, Dadhichi, the great sage, spoke with joy.

Dadhichi said: Brahman is described in many ways in the Vedas, but the Ganesha-Brahma is the foremost, as it grants the knowledge of Brahman. In the past, I was devoted to penance and, out of rivalry with King Kshupa, became a constant devotee of Shiva. I defeated Vishnu, who had come for Kshupa, and remained in the natural state of Brahman. However, seeing the sense of independence and dependence there, I became weary, for neither is seen in the true nature of Brahman.

Seeking peace, I went to Shankara and praised him with various hymns. Shiva smiled and asked, "O Dadhichi, why have you come?" I bowed and said, "O Lord, I am confused. How can there be independence or dependence in the natural state of Brahman? I am a devotee of Shiva and know you to be the Supreme. Please teach me the Yoga of peace."

Shri Shiva said: You have asked well. I shall tell you the great Yoga that leads to oneness with Brahman. Independence and dependence do not exist in the fourth state, the natural Brahman. The joy of the Self is attained through this Yoga. Yoga without these five (the senses/mind) is called 'Ayoga' (non-union). When the five-fold mental states are abandoned, you will attain peace. I am not different from Ganesha. When you realize this, you will become the wish-fulfilling gem yourself.

Having said this, Shiva gave me the great one-syllable mantra of Ganesha. I asked, "I am a devotee of Shiva; how should I worship Ganesha?" Shiva replied, "The Ganas are his form. In the union of all, I am the Gana of Ganapati. Consider all as the form of Ganesha; where is the contradiction in that?"

Having said this, Shiva became silent. I became free of doubt, bowed, and went to the forest. Having practiced the Yoga taught by Shiva, I attained peace. Now, I worship the Elephant-faced Lord with devotion, seeing Shiva in the form of the Guru. I have told you everything that leads to the realization of Brahman. You too, worship the Lord of the Ganas with devotion.

Having said this, Dadhichi gave him the sixteen-syllable Ganesha mantra, which grants all success. Dadhichi, the knower of Yoga, continued to worship the Lord of the Ganas with great devotion.

Chapter 35 : The Description of the Glory of the Intercalary Month (Malamasa)

Suta said: Dhaumya meditated upon the Elephant-faced Lord (Gajanana) within his heart and chanted the mantra. Gradually, established in the state of yoga, he became devoted to tranquility and self-control. Seeing the arrival of the special intercalary month (Malamasa), he undertook a vow dedicated to that month to destroy the impurities of ignorance. Bathing at dawn according to the prescribed rites and remaining devoted to the five-fold Ganesha worship, Dhaumya always remembered Ganapati with a pure mind. Seeing the arrival of the Lord of the Ganas, the great sage, filled with devotion, became overjoyed. Rising, he first bowed to Him and worshipped Him with devotion. Then, bowing again, he praised the Lord of Obstacles with folded hands.

Dhaumya said: I bow to Ganesha, the bestower of all accomplishments, who acts differently toward those who are bound by worldly existence compared to those who are His devotees. I am blessed in every way to have seen the Elephant-faced Lord today. How can I praise You, O Great One, the Lord of Yoga and the embodiment of Yoga? By Your mere vision, I speak with wisdom. O Best of the All-Knowing, listen to this complete hymn that brings You joy. Salutations to You, O Ganesha, the bestower of yogic peace. Salutations again and again to Heramba, who is the peace of all peaceful things. Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, who is the eldest, the bestower of the highest position, and the embodiment of all that is supreme. Salutations to You, who are the embodiment of power in all names and forms, who are the Sun to the soul, and who are Vishnu to those who are equal. Salutations to You, who are Maheshwara in the unmanifest, the true Self in all unions, and who, by a portion of Your essence, are the cause of liberation in those who renounce. Salutations to the Lord of Speech (Brahmanaspati), who is the embodiment of peace in all yogas, and salutations to the One who plays through the distinctions of beginning, middle, and end. Salutations to the Lord of Success and Intellect, who is the mover of various illusions. Salutations to You, who dwell in Your own bliss and are intent on protecting Your devotees. How can I praise You, O Lord of the Ganas? Even the Vedas and the yogis are not capable of doing so; therefore, O Lord, I bow to the Elephant-faced One.

Having said this, the great sage fell to the ground like a rod, holding the feet of the Lord of the Ganas. Ganesha, the Lord of the Vedas, lifted him up and said, "O Dhaumya, choose your boons; I am pleased by this hymn and will grant them. Whoever reads or hears this hymn that you have composed for Me, may it grant all accomplishments and fulfill all desires. It shall grant yogic peace, increase devotion toward Me, and I will grant whatever they desire, being pleased by this hymn."

Suta said: Hearing the words of Ganesha, Dhaumya was filled with joy and spoke to the Lord of the Ganas, the Lord of devotees who is fond of His followers. Dhaumya said: "O Lord, since You are pleased, grant me the highest devotion and the yogic peace in which You always rejoice." Saying "So be it," the Lord of the Ganas disappeared. Dhaumya, feeling deeply saddened, remained right there. He established Ganesha there with the help of learned Brahmins, and by constantly meditating upon and worshipping Him, he practiced devotion.

Shaunaka asked: It is said in the scriptures that those who perform no actions are Sattvic, while those who perform actions are called Rajasic. Those who perform forbidden actions are certainly Tamasic. Are humans on earth divided into these three types? Suta replied: Sattvic people attain liberation through the dedication of their actions to Brahman. Rajasic people, bound by desires, wander in the cycle of birth and death. Tamasic people, devoted only to their physical appetites, go to the hells. Thus, people attain three types of destinations based on the three qualities. Among them, Shankara is described as having a Tamasic nature. Great souls who seek the four goals of life worship Him. If the devotees of the Tamasic are considered to be destined for hell, how can all Shiva-devotees become one with Brahman?

Suta said: You have asked well, O Brahmin, for the benefit of all. I will tell you what I heard from the all-knowing Vyasa. Tamas is the form of ignorance, which the scriptures equate to deep sleep. Consider that everything arises from ignorance in two ways: the internal Sattvic state is like a dream, and the external Rajasic state is like the waking state. Because He is the root of both, Shankara is called Tamasic. I will tell you the authority of those who bear the three qualities. Moment by moment, Rajas creates various states. The Sattvic creates, the protector maintains, and the Tamasic gathers them and always makes them dissolve. Therefore, O Brahmin, all this is pervaded by the three qualities from conception until death. Actions, non-actions, and forbidden actions are all connected to the three qualities. These qualities are described as the yoga of action. Wise men should never think that the gods, led by Shambhu, are to be judged by these qualities. Therefore, the devotees of Shankara attain everything easily.

Now, listen to the supreme glory of the intercalary month. There was a merchant named Shama in Malava. Hearing of the glory of the intercalary month, which destroys impurities, he performed the best vow with devotion to Ganesha. Upon the arrival of the intercalary month, he would bathe at dawn and remember the Lord of the Ganas. One night, while the merchant was on the road and asleep, ten powerful ghosts came to devour him. By divine grace, Shama happened to wake up. Seeing the great ghosts, he remembered the Lord of the Ganas. He said within himself, "O Ganesha, Ocean of Compassion, protect me from these ghosts, for I am devoted to Your feet. Only seven days of the intercalary month remain; if You do not protect me, my vow will be broken. After the month ends, You may take my life, but do not let me be killed while I am observing this vow, O Ocean of Mercy."

Seeing him engaged in the vow of the intercalary month, the ghosts became terrified. They regained the memory of their past lives and, bowing to him, they wept. Surprised, the merchant asked the powerful beings, "Why are you suffering? Tell me." The ghosts said: "O merchant, in our previous lives, we were Brahmins who lived without discipline, and after death, we went to the abode of Yama. There, we suffered various intense tortures. Now, we wander here and there, suffering from cold, heat, and other dualities. By divine fate, we came to devour you, but by the merit of seeing you, we have regained the memory of our past lives. O Ocean of Mercy, you are observing the vow of the intercalary month; please save us. You bathe daily while remembering Ganesha. Give us the merit of just one of your baths, and by that, we will be freed from sin and attain the state of no-return."

Hearing the words of the ghosts, Shama, naturally compassionate, said: "What have I done? I have only performed the daily bath and remembered Ganesha during the intercalary month. Who can measure the glory of remembering Ganesha? Listen to what I have heard in the Puranas. I give you the merit of one bath performed with the remembrance of Ganesha. By that, you will be freed from your bonds, without a doubt." Having said this, he gave them the fruit of one bath performed with the remembrance of Ganesha during the intercalary month. Then, the attendants of Ganesha arrived in a celestial vehicle, took the ghosts, and departed for the abode of bliss, devoted to the Lord of the Ganas. Seeing the Lord there, the ghosts became one with Brahman. See this great miracle of the intercalary month, O Shaunaka. In this way, many people have attained success. How many can I count? I have told you briefly the auspicious story of the intercalary month, which grants everything upon hearing. Whoever reads, causes to be read, or hears this will attain the supreme state, and after enjoying all pleasures here, surrounded by their loved ones, they will attain the highest goal.

Chapter 36 : The Description of the Glory of the Annual Vow

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Suta said: O Sages, I shall now narrate the wonderful character of the months, which bestows devotion to Ganesha; listen to this supreme account. One who performs morning ablutions and remains devoted to the Ganesha Panchaka (the five-day worship of Ganesha) daily for twelve months shall attain all his desires. Even that which is considered impossible or unattainable by mortals can be achieved by one who remains devoted to the Ganesha Panchaka for twelve months.

I shall tell you an ancient story regarding this, which, by hearing or reading, purifies humans and grants all accomplishments. There was a great king named Nala in the land of Nishadha, who was righteous, generous, and always devoted to his duty. Together with Damayanti, he pondered in his heart, "How can I, a human, become capable of traveling through the three worlds?" If a human being, while in a mortal body, could move at will, his fame would be blessed and transcend everything in this world. Thinking thus, the king went to the best of sages, Gautama. After bowing to that highly fortunate one, he asked what would be beneficial for him.

Nala asked: O Master, how can a human being, while possessing a mortal body, travel through the three worlds? Tell me that, O knower of truth, so that I may perform it exactly as required.

Gautama said: O Great King, in the month of Shravana, on the first day of the bright fortnight, bathe in the morning and perform your daily duties as prescribed by your Vedic branch. Then, O King, with devotion and discipline, worship the Ganesha Panchaka. Upon reaching the new moon of Ashadha, remain devoted to the Lord of Ganas and worship a golden idol of Ganapati in a pavilion. Select twelve Brahmins who are well-versed in the Vedas. O wise one, perform a Homa with one-tenth of the number of your Japa (mantra recitations). Offer food to the Brahmins and perform the sacrifice. Then, after the final oblation, feed them with milk-based sweets (payasam).

On the first day, worship Ganesha again, the granter of all success. Feed the Brahmins along with their wives and auspicious women. Invite twenty-one or even more guests and satisfy them particularly with generous donations. Give the women blouses and various ornaments, and after bowing to them all, dismiss them with their blessings. Offer the best of the Ganesha idol along with all accessories to your Guru. Give donations to others according to your capacity with joy. O King, act in this way, and you will become filled with knowledge; you will attain the three worlds and everything you desire in your heart.

Having said this, the great yogi Gautama became silent. Nala bowed to him and returned to his home. Nala, who knew all duties, performed the supreme annual vow devoted to Ganapati according to the prescribed rites. Consequently, he became revered by yogis and filled with knowledge, capable of traveling through the three worlds while still in his human body.

O Brahmin, many other people also performed this annual vow; they became revered by yogis and filled with knowledge. Having attained their desired and rare supreme success, they all went to Svananda (Ganesha's abode) and became one with Brahman at the end. It is not possible for me to narrate all their stories, O fortunate one, as they are infinite; therefore, I have described them briefly.

Those who were not capable of performing the full Ganesha Panchaka service performed the vow with some specific rules. They too attained success and their desires with joy, and at the end, went to the realm of Ganapati and attained Brahman.

There was a Shudra named Bandhya who performed great deeds. Desiring a son, he engaged in various rituals. He did not obtain offspring and, filled with great sorrow, went to a dense forest where he saw some Brahmins. He bowed to those highly fortunate ones and asked for a way to obtain a son. They told him about the annual vow. Hearing the full procedure of this supreme Ganesha vow, he bowed to them and returned to his home with joy.

He was not capable of performing the full Ganesha Panchaka, so, O Brahmin, he performed it according to his limited capacity. After bathing, he performed the supreme worship of an earthen Ganesha idol daily with name-mantras and remained celibate. He followed the rule of eating only once a day and performed the excellent vow. After completing the vow, he worshipped Ganesha with joy. He served the Brahmins with righteousness and supported his family with the food he received.

After a short time, O great sage, he obtained a son. Seeing him full of knowledge and beautiful, he became overjoyed. Gradually, five sons and three daughters were born to him. The Shudra considered Ganesha to be the one who enabled all this. At the end, he went to the Svananda realm with his wife. Having enjoyed all pleasures here, he became one with Brahman.

O Brahmin, many people, by performing even a little bit of this vow with discipline, attained success; how many can I name? I have described the concluding ceremony (Udyapana), which is accepted by all. It should be performed in all months, even in the monthly vow. This Udyapana is also prescribed for the Chaturmasya vow and similarly for the vow performed during the intercalary month (Malamasa) with devotion.

The annual vow has been described to you in brief. Reading or hearing this will grant all results.

Thus ends the thirty-sixth chapter of the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of the Glory of the Annual Vow."

Chapter 37 : The Description of the Instruction of Vibhanda's Vow

Shounaka said: O Suta, please tell me about the Ganesha Chaturmasya vow, which grants all desires. I am never satisfied hearing these stories that increase various wonders.

Suta said: I will tell you an ancient and supreme history, which grants all desires upon hearing. There was a brahmin known as Brihadasva, who was a knower of the supreme truth, endowed with penance, possessed of great brilliance, and capable of both cursing and blessing. Through the power of his various penances, inner knowledge arose in his heart, and he became devoted to tranquility and self-control. He became established in the Brahman, and in the end, he became a sage absorbed in the void, peaceful by nature, and equal to a knower of Brahman.

The great yogi Vibhanda suddenly arrived at his hermitage. Brihadasva bowed to the great soul and worshipped him with devotion. After the chief sage had rested, Brihadasva, filled with joy and humility, spoke excellent words to Vibhanda.

Brihadasva said: The Brahman is devoid of support, always of one form, and beyond the meditator and the meditation; there is no doubt about this truth. By great fortune, having seen you, the supreme lord of yogis, I am now fulfilled by the sight of your feet.

Hearing his words, Vibhanda, knowing him to be skilled in yoga and a bestower of peace, spoke to him.

Vibhanda said: If I am of the form of Ganesha, then there is no duality. O sage, where does the happiness of the supportless state arise?

Hearing this, the astonished Brihadasva asked him, always longing for that meaning which bestows perfect peace.

Brihadasva said: O Lord, ocean of compassion, tell me of the state of perfect peace. I do not know the state beyond the ego.

Vibhanda said: Long ago, I was devoted to penance. At that time, women sent by Indra arrived to disturb my penance. They tried to enchant me with dance and song, but I remained firm. Then, those women, filled with desire, tormented me with gestures and glances. Due to my agitation, my seed fell to the ground. Distressed, I left my hermitage. Then, all the apsaras went to heaven. I went to the brahmin Jajali and bowed to him. Honored by him, I lived with penance and asked him about the devotion to Ganesha.

I asked the yogi Jajali, who knew the supreme truth: "O Lord, why do you worship Ganesha, abandoning all the gods of Indra?"

Jajali said: O great-minded one, see in the Vedas that there is no Brahman higher than Ganesha. The knowers of the Vedas call him the Lord of Brahman. Long ago, I was devoted to penance, and by my intense penance, the entire moving and unmoving world was pervaded. Then, the great sage Lomasha arrived at my hermitage. I bowed to him and worshipped him. The all-knowing sage Lomasha, who was compassionate and worshipped by the great yogis, spoke to me.

Lomasha said: What do you wish to achieve by your penance? Practice yoga, and you yourself will become Brahman. Through penance, all enjoyments are obtained by humans, but because of the nature of falling again, it is full of duality.

Hearing this, I asked him for the path of yoga with humility. Lomasha told me that he had previously asked Brahma about this and had heard the Ganesha Purana. By the greatness of hearing it, he became eloquent and attained the state of being worshipped by yogis. He then installed Ganesha in his hermitage and worshipped him. After a hundred years, Ganesha was pleased and appeared to him. Lomasha asked for the boon of Ganesha's devotion and a long life to worship Him. Ganesha granted him that he would be devoted to Him in every age.

Later, when Indra wanted a palace built, Vishwakarma became distressed and remembered Ganesha. I (Lomasha) arrived there, and Indra asked me why I carried a leaf on my head. I explained that this body is perishable, and the leaf serves as a house to protect me from rain and heat. Indra was amazed, and I gave him the knowledge of the past. Indra then asked for the yoga that removes birth and death. I told him to worship Ganesha.

Indra then asked how to see Ganesha. I told him that the night is the time for the gods, and the night (Ratri) had performed penance from the full moon of Ashadha to the full moon of Kartika to please Ganesha. Ganesha was pleased and granted her the Chaturmasya vow, which is the bestower of success.

Vibhanda continued: O Indra, perform this Chaturmasya vow. I then returned to my hermitage. Indra performed the vow, became free from attachment, and ruled his kingdom. In the end, he attained the supreme state.

Jajali asked how gods, who are bound by the fruits of their actions, can attain the supreme state. Lomasha explained that actions performed in the land of Bharat (the earth) bear fruit. Jajali then performed the vow, and Ganesha appeared to him. Jajali praised Ganesha with a beautiful hymn. Ganesha was pleased and asked him to choose a boon. Jajali asked for steady devotion to Ganesha's feet.

Vibhanda concluded: I have told you the greatness of this vow. Since then, I have been performing this annual Chaturmasya vow to please Ganesha. You too should perform this wonderful vow, and you will become worshipped by great yogis. Having said this, the sage gave him the eight-syllable mantra of Ganesha.

Chapter 38 : Describing the Misfortune of Kalyan

Vibhanda said: O best of the yogis, tell me the story of Ballalesh. How did Ganesha come to be known by the name Ballalesh, the bestower of auspiciousness? By whom was this deity installed near the village, and what is the story that grants all accomplishments?

Jajali said: Long ago, in the Sindh region, there was a city named Palli. There lived a merchant named Kalyan who was righteous. His wife, Indumati, was a virtuous and beautiful woman. From her, a son named Ballal was born, who was filled with devotion. On the eleventh day, his father named him Ballal, and the Brahmins declared that he was endowed with the power of devotion. Ballal was always absorbed in the meditation of the Lord of the Ganas; due to his devotion, he was always oblivious to hunger and thirst. His mother, filled with joy and wonder, would feed her son while he was deeply absorbed in remembrance. O best of sages, the child never cried for milk. Soon, the merchant's son began to crawl on his knees. The wise boy would make an image of Ganesha out of dust on the ground and worship it with devotion using dust as an offering.

Then, Ballal began to walk, always engrossed in Ganesha. With faltering speech, he would respectfully say, "Victory to Vighnesha!" After some time, accompanied by his friends, he would worship Ganesha wherever he went on the earth. He would instruct the children, "Do not play, dear friends. Such play is destructive; instead, engage in activities marked by Ganesha. Worship the Lord of the Ganas daily, and everything will be auspicious for you." Following his advice, the other children also became devoted to Ganesha.

After some time, a great obstacle to their devotion arose. Seeing this, the merchant's son was deeply saddened. Thinking it over in his heart, he went with the children to a mountain near the village for the sake of devotion. There, he and the children placed a beautiful stone and worshipped the Elephant-faced Lord with forest leaves, fruits, and flowers. After performing the three-time worship, he would return home at night, eat, and sleep, being the foremost among the devotees of Ganesha.

In the morning, he would rise and go with the children to worship Ganesha again. Then, the people of the city, having beaten the children, said, "Where do you go, children, without eating? How do you live daily on one meal at night? Do not ever go with Ballal." Though the angry townspeople instructed their children, they still went to the forest. All of them, led by Ballal, remained engrossed in worshipping Ganesha with various hymns.

Then, the angry townspeople went to Kalyan, the great merchant, and said without fear: "Ballal takes the children and goes into the forest daily. He returns at night, and they are all fasting. Our children have become emaciated because of that wicked boy. They will die today or tomorrow. Discipline your son now. Even though we try in many ways and beat our children, they remain attached to your son."

Hearing their words, Kalyan became furious. He left them and quickly went to the forest to kill his son. Carrying a large stick, he approached the children and saw them. They had built a temple out of grass, wood, and colored minerals, and were busy worshipping the deity. Some were circumambulating, some were chanting, some were in meditation, some were practicing penance, and some were reciting Puranas. The merchant saw his son, Ballal, absorbed in meditation before Ganesha.

Kalyan broke the temple with his stick. Seeing this, the children were terrified and fled in all directions. Only Ballal did not move from his firm posture. The father grabbed his son, who was a great devotee following his vows, and beat him with the stick like Yama. Blood flowed from his body, yet he did not let go of Ganesha, who resided in his heart. Even while facing death, the merchant's son remembered the Lord. Then, the father grabbed the Ganesha idol and threw it far away into a mountain crevice. He tied his son to a tree with vines by his hands and feet. Then, the wicked merchant said to him:

Kalyan said: "Disregarding me, you are always intent on worshipping this deity. Now, son, suffer the wonderful fruit of that. People, devoid of shame, speak of your actions. Die, you son who brings disgrace! Because of you, I am now censured everywhere. I was once happy and had a good reputation in the cities. Therefore, leave your body and go to your Vighneshvara, son. That deity will surely do everything auspicious for you."

Having said this, the great sinner returned to the city. Upon arriving home, he was struck by misfortune. O sage, he suddenly became afflicted with leprosy, blindness, deafness, hunchbackedness, and was covered in worms. The merchant who had insulted his son was filled with great sorrow. His wife, seeing him in such a state, lamented with grief. She asked her husband, but he could not hear her words. Due to his muteness, he could not say anything and was filled with misery. Then, the terrified townspeople told her the story of what happened in the forest and how Ballal was beaten. Filled with great sorrow, the virtuous Indumati went to the forest with the people, weeping bitterly to see her son.

Thus ends the thirty-eighth chapter of the eighth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Description of the Misfortune of Kalyan."

Chapter 39 : The Description of Ballaleshwar

Vibhandaka asked: O Lord, tell me what path Ballala attained and what supreme state he reached, for he was a great devotee of Ganesha and suffered much, O best of sages.

Jajali replied: Ballala, while residing in the forest, remembered the Lord of the Ganas, even though he was bound by his wicked father and suffering from bleeding wounds. He prayed: O Ganesha, Lord of the gods, why have you become an obstacle to your devotee? Are you truly against me? When you deceive me to test my devotion, I remember that I have already surrendered this body to you. My body may fall, but I will never abandon you, O ocean of mercy. If my devotion to your feet is firm, then let my father become a leper, blind, hunchbacked, mute, and deaf by your grace, while I remain free from these bonds. O Lord of obstacles, I bow to you again and again.

As soon as these words left the mouth of Ballala, the sole devotee of Ganesha, he became exactly as he had prayed. Then, Ganesha, pleased and bound by the words of his devotee, appeared in the form of a Brahmana. The best of Brahmanas saw the devotee Ballala bound, and instantly, the bonds fell away. His wounds healed, O best of sages, and Ballala, filled with joy, recognized Ganesha in the form of the Brahmana. He rose, bowed, worshipped him, and with folded hands, praised him.

Ballala said: Salutations to you, O Ganesha, the abode of all, the Supreme Lord, the ever-joyful, the one who transcends all, the infinite, the Lord of gods, and the protector of devotees. Salutations to you, O Heramba, the Lord of obstacles, the Supreme Soul, the giver of great yoga, and the embodiment of peace. Salutations to you, O dweller of Svananda, the one who plays in Svananda, the Lord of Yoga, and the one who assumes a most generous form. Salutations to you, whose body is existence itself, the one without a beginning, and the one who dwells everywhere in the form of yoga. Salutations to you, the giver of Siddhi and Buddhi, the embodiment of Siddhi and Buddhi, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, and the one with the elephant face. Salutations to you, O god with a large belly, the four-armed one, the one who has a mouse as his emblem, and the one who carries the noose and the goad. Salutations to you, who assumed the form of a Brahmana out of love for your devotee and came to fulfill his words. You are my mother, my father, my friend, and my family deity; you are my wealth and my knowledge. I bow to you in every way. O Heramba, ocean of mercy, please forgive all the sins I have committed through my body, speech, and mind, whether in waking, dreaming, or deep sleep.

Having said this, Ballala, overcome with devotion, danced before the Brahmana with tears in his eyes and hair standing on end. Then, the Lord of the Ganas abandoned the Brahmana form and appeared in his true Gajanana form to his great devotee.

Shri Ganesha said: Ask for boons, O great yogi, that are in your heart. I am pleased with your devotion and will grant them. This hymn you have composed will grant all success to anyone who reads or hears it. I will grant whatever is desired by those who praise me with this hymn, and at the end, it will grant union with me.

Ballala said: Grant me firm devotion to you and the company of those who are devoted to you. May I have no regard for those who are averse to you, O Gajanana. I ask for no other boon, O Vinayaka; I am your servant. Make me always absorbed in you.

Ganesha, who loves his devotees, said: "So be it. My name here will be known by your name, Ballaleshwar. I shall always remain here as the Lord of the Ganas, protecting all yogis who are filled with devotion." Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, disappeared, and a magnificent temple appeared there instantly.

Seeing the beautiful temple with Ballaleshwar, the merchant Kalyan was astonished. He bowed and turned his mind toward worship. The materials for worship appeared everywhere, and the merchant worshipped Ganesha with them. Having completed his daily rituals, he became absorbed in meditation, then rose with joy and bowed to the Lord of the Ganas. He attained divine knowledge and became free from the illusion of "self" and "other," becoming dear to Ganesha.

Then his mother arrived and saw him. She was amazed to see him free from wounds and standing in the temple. Hearing the story from him, he told her: "I will never see my father who hates Ganesha. I have had many mothers and fathers in many births; to which of them should I hold an attachment that leads to liberation? Go to your home; I am certainly not the son of a wicked man who destroyed the temple and hindered devotion to the Lord." Hearing this, she was filled with sorrow and returned to her place, while Ballala remained there, worshipping Ganesha with joy. Suddenly, a celestial chariot of light arrived, and by it, the merchant Ballala went to the Lord of the Ganas.

Vibhandaka asked: People are bound by their actions, whether good or bad. How did the yogi Ballala, free from the illusion of self and other, view his father, the merchant Kalyan, who was a hater of Ganesha?

Jajali replied: Among yogis, the devotees of Ganesha are considered the best. Among them, this merchant Ballala became the king of devotees. Those devoted to Ganesha are always to be worshipped and respected, while those who are the opposite are to be avoided like enemies. Therefore, the merchant Ballala, for the sake of Ganesha, rebuked his father, who was a critic of Ganesha.

Vibhandaka asked: In whose family a devotee of Ganesha is born, he is the savior of millions of his ancestors. This one was the son of the merchant Kalyan and became absorbed in Svananda. Yet, how did his parents fare on earth? What was their ultimate fate? Hearing this wonderful story, I am filled with amazement.

Jajali replied: If a devotee of Ganesha born in a family strives for the liberation of his lineage, then that is his path. Or, if he goes to the Lord of the Ganas, the liberation of his kin happens instantly, without doubt. To protect the curse of Ballala, Ganesha, who is moved by his devotee's heart, granted them liberation in another birth. This greatness of Ballala has been told to you briefly; it grants all success to those who hear or read it. Those who are not equal to him in strength are called Ballalas, and their master is the Lord of the Ganas, known as Ballaleshwar. You too should perform this great vow with devotion, the wonderful vow of the four months, with dedication to Ballaleshwar. By his grace, you will become perfect in yoga, O best of Brahmanas; have no doubt about this. The person who hears or reads the greatness of Ballaleshwar will enjoy great pleasures and eventually become one with Brahman.

Chapter 40 : The Description of the Greatness of the Chaturmasya Vow

Suta said: Jajali then gave the great mantra, the five-syllabled mantra of Ganesha, to Vibhanda according to the prescribed rules, as he was well-versed in the scriptures. Having received the mantra, the highly fortunate Vibhanda stayed in Jajali's hermitage, worshipped Ballalesha, and performed the vow with devotion. He performed the Ganesha-panchaka (five-fold worship) and became absorbed in meditation. On the full moon day of Kartika, Lord Ballalesha appeared. Seeing Him, Vibhanda stood up suddenly and bowed down. He worshipped and praised the Lord of Obstacles with various hymns, feeling great joy. Then, the Lord Ballalesha, having become pleased, bestowed upon the sage the peace of yoga and disappeared. From that day on, Vibhanda became a devotee of Ganesha and wandered, being guided by his mind, always focused on Ballalesha.

Vibhandaka said: Having performed this, I became established in the peace of yoga and was revered by all. O Brihadashva, you too should perform this supreme vow of the Lord of Ganas. Having said this, the great yogi gave him the ten-syllabled mantra of Ganesha according to the rules. After bowing to him, Brihadashva went to his own place and performed the Chaturmasya vow, remaining absorbed in the Ganesha-panchaka. The Lord of Obstacles granted Brihadashva the peace-giving yoga, and he also worshipped the Lord of Ganas daily with devotion.

O best of Brahmins, hear another story related to the Chaturmasya vow. The energy of Vibhanda once fell upon the earth. A doe in the forest consumed it along with some grass. O great sage, she immediately became pregnant. A son was born to her who was the embodiment of Brahmanic radiance. Because he had a horn on his forehead, he was known as Rishyashringa. Guided by Narada, Vibhanda brought him to his hermitage and had all the sacraments performed by Brahmins. Once, the excellent king Lomapada brought the sage to his city by sending celestial nymphs. Seeing the suffering of the people due to drought, the king gave his own daughter, the virtuous Shanta, to him in marriage. Immediately, rain fell, destroying the people's suffering. The sage lived happily with Shanta.

Once, the Brahmin went with his wife to visit his father. He bowed to Vibhanda and stayed there, eager to serve him. Seeing his son, who was full of humility and austerity, the great yogi Vibhanda spoke to him with affection. Vibhanda said: He who is established in Brahman is called a Brahmin. Therefore, my son, you should become an excellent Brahmin. Having said this, he gave him the auspicious mantra of Ganapati. He bowed to him and attained the status of a son. Together with his wife Shanta, he remained engaged in the Chaturmasya vow, serving the Ganesha-panchaka and remaining absorbed in meditation. Being daily devoted to yoga, he prayed to the Lord of Ganas: "O Lord of Ganas, make me and my wife worthy of being worshipped by the best of yogis." Pleased by his vow, meditation, and austerity, the Lord granted the wise Rishyashringa the yoga. Thereafter, he became a devotee of Ganesha along with his wife and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles with joy.

Meanwhile, the excellent king Lomapada arrived there and prayed to Vibhanda with affection. He requested and took Rishyashringa along with his wife to his city, where he remained under the king's command. The king also worshipped the Lord of Ganas daily, instructed by his son-in-law, and became devoted to the Chaturmasya vow. In the end, the king, who was the best among the righteous, attained the Lord of Obstacles. O Brahmin, many people have attained perfection through this vow. How many can I tell you? It is impossible to describe everything. There is no doubt that there is no vow as supreme as the Chaturmasya.

There was once a fisherman named Nadakara. Once, O great sage, he saw some Brahmins and bowed to them. Seeing them engaged in the Chaturmasya vow, he asked with devotion: "O best of Brahmins, please tell me about this supreme vow." Seeing him humble, they respectfully told him about the supreme Ganesha vow and its rules. Having heard them, he bowed and went to his home with respect. He bathed and performed the Ganesha vow during Chaturmasya. He worshipped Him for a short time and then lived, lamenting his body which was devoid of devotion to Ganesha.

Once, while he was on the road for some work in the forest, ten forest demons came to eat him. Terrified, he remembered Ganesha, the destroyer of all, in his heart. The demons approached him, wanting to eat him. Seeing them, he took water, uttered the mantra "Namo Ganesha," and sprinkled it on the wicked ones, trembling with fear. Touched by the water consecrated with the Ganesha mantra, they regained the memory of their past lives and wept, bowing to him. They said with folded hands to Nadakara, "O great sage, we are full of sorrow."

The demons said: We were born as Brahmins in our previous life, but were born as Shudras later. We were constantly devoid of good conduct. Attached to worldly desires, we died and went to the abode of Yama, who threw us into various hells. Having suffered the pain of our sins, O giver of respect, we became demons, consumed by hunger, and wandered here and there. By divine grace and the glory of our fortune, we have seen you, O saint. Through the water you sprinkled, we have regained the memory of our past lives. Now, save us, as we have come to your refuge. Give us the merit born of singing the praises of Ganesha.

Hearing their words, Nadakara was filled with compassion. Knowing the greatness of the vow, he spoke to them about the power of the Name. Nadakara said: The merit of singing the praises of Ganesha cannot be described. It was told by the gurus and Brahmins well-versed in the truth. Therefore, O demons, listen to my advice. I perform the singing of His praises daily during Chaturmasya. I will compassionately give you the merit of one Name. There is no doubt that this merit, even if performed during Chaturmasya, will free you from your bonds. Having said this, he gave them the merit born of one Name.

As soon as the merit was given, the messengers of Ganesha arrived there. They took them to their own realm, O great sage. Seeing the Lord of Ganas there, they became one with Brahman. Thus, the greatness of this vow cannot be fully described. I have told you in brief the wonderful story of the Chaturmasya vow, which grants the fruits of worldly enjoyment and liberation. Whoever hears, makes others hear, or reads this with a focused heart will attain the fruits of the vow and achieve all their heart's desires.

Chapter 41 : Meeting of Kaundinya and Brahma

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O ocean of compassion, tell me the greatness of Ganesha's name. It is said that by the merit of just one name, ten generations are liberated.

Suta said: O Lord, who is capable of describing the greatness of Ganesha's name? It is infinite, as described by the knowers of the Vedas in the Vedic discourses. I shall tell you an ancient history here that grants all accomplishments; there is no doubt that those who hear or read it will attain the highest state.

There was a famous Brahmin named Jaratkaru, who was the best among the knowers of Brahman. Endowed with immense penance, he was capable of both cursing and blessing. His son, the high-souled Astika, who was virtuous, came to him and bowed down with folded hands. Eager to serve, he stayed there filled with joy. Once, he spoke to his father, the king of sages.

Astika said: O high-souled one, having saved the best of the serpents at the sacrifice of Janamejaya, I have come to pay homage at your feet. Seeing those groups of serpents dead in the fire and suffering greatly, I became filled with fear, O most holy one. Seeing this world as full of suffering, I do not desire it, O great sage. Therefore, tell me a path that grants peace and yoga.

Hearing these words of his son, the great sage Jaratkaru, filled with joy, spoke these words inspired by yoga.

Jaratkaru said: O son, without the knowledge of Brahman, peace is never attained, regardless of one's status in various Brahmanical states or involvement in food and life-forces. Worship Him who is known as Brahmanaspati, who is the complete giver of peace and the revealer of the Vedas, according to the prescribed rites.

Hearing this, the Brahmin Astika, endowed with penance, said: O father, why do the Vedas declare Ganesha to be the supreme? How did the Lord of gods, who bears a name and form, become known as Brahmanaspati? Tell me his true nature.

Jaratkaru said: The word 'Gana' refers to a group, and the groups are of the nature of Brahman. He who is the master of those who are identical in their inner and outer states is Ganesha. His body is of the nature of Savikalpa (manifested knowledge), and his head is of the nature of Nirvikalpa (transcendental knowledge). The union of these two results in the elephant-headed form. In the beginning, there were various beings bound by ignorance; for the sake of granting them knowledge, He assumed a body. Through self-realized yoga, one attains His vision. By that yoga, the Lord of obstacles makes the yogis reside in His own abode.

Success (Siddhi) is of the nature of power and is always considered to be a source of illusion. Intellect (Buddhi) is said to be the holder of illusion and is of the nature of the five-fold mind. The Lord of the Ganas plays as the master of both, residing in the attainment of the mind. He is the supreme beyond the supreme, assuming this form through the power of Maya and Yoga.

I shall tell you an ancient and wonderful history regarding the greatness of Ganesha's name. When I was in my mother's womb, I left it to pursue yoga and met Kaundinya, who was skilled in yoga. He was a great leader among the Ganapatyas in the city of Sthavara. He always worshipped Ganesha in the form of Chintamani with Durva grass. Seeing me, he was filled with joy and said, "Welcome, O great sage Jaratkaru, sit down." After welcoming me and offering hospitality according to the rites, he asked, "O great Brahmin, what do you wish to ask?"

Hearing the words of Kaundinya, I, filled with joy, said to him: "O father, I have been wandering the earth. Despite performing various penances, I have never found peace. Therefore, I have come to your hermitage. Tell me the path to supreme peace."

Kaundinya said: Worship Ganesha with devotion, then you will attain peace. Otherwise, O Jaratkaru, you will remain full of toil.

Hearing his words, I asked him again: "Why do you call the deity who bears a name and form the giver of peace?"

Then, out of natural compassion, he said to me: "Listen, I will tell you an ancient and wonderful history. I was once striving intensely for yoga through penance, but I could not find that Lord. Then I went to Brahma. After bowing to that high-souled one who grants yoga and peace, I asked him the same."

Brahma said: "Worship Ganesha with devotion, then you will attain peace. Go to the city called Sthavara and worship Chintamani. The mind is said to be restless, but where it becomes still, I have named that place Sthavara. My Lord Chintamani, who manifests the five-fold mind, was established by me long ago as the direct giver of peace. At the beginning of creation, I worshipped Him there through penance. By the power of His boon, I became revered by yogis."

Hearing his words, I asked Brahma again: "O Lord, how can the one who bears a name and form be the giver of peace?"

Then Brahma said to me with a joyful heart: "He remains as the Lord of the Ganas, bearing this name. His body is the entire universe, and the elephant head is Brahman. For the sake of His devotees, He assumed this form. His city is Svananda, His beloved wives are Siddhi and Buddhi, His sons are Labha and Kshema, and His vehicle is the mouse. Thus, He has various forms that are all manifestations of Brahman. Therefore, He is called Brahmanaspati in the Vedas. In the beginning, the worlds were bound by illusion and delusion; for the sake of granting knowledge, He assumed a body. He is a body-bearer by His own will, He is endowed with Maya by His own will, and He is of the nature of Yoga by His own will; hence He is called Vinayaka. His body is not made of bones, flesh, or skin; O best of Brahmins, He is devoid of those and possesses a body of pure consciousness."

I shall tell you the great history of the dialogue between Bhushundi and the immortal sages.

Thus ends the forty-first chapter named the meeting of Kaundinya and Brahma in the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 42 : The Discourse on the Greatness of the Name of Ganesha

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Kaundinya said: Oh, I have heard the wonderful greatness of Ganesha from you. O Bhrishundi, tell me the conversation that took place between the divine sages.

Brahma said: Once, all the sages, after deliberating among themselves, went to the Creator (Brahma) to resolve their doubts. Having bowed to the Creator of the worlds, the sages, led by Kashyapa and accompanied by the kings of gods and chief sages, spoke with great joy.

The divine sages said: This Ganesha is worthy of worship by all and is the one to be worshipped before all others. He is eternal, possesses name and form, and is the true meaning of the term Brahmanaspati. In the Vedas, O Brahman, it is said that Brahman is Brahman alone, and within that, there is never any distinction of attributes or lack thereof. Ganesha, who bears a body, is called Brahmanaspati in the Vedas. Therefore, we have come to you, filled with doubts, to ask about this. You are the one worshipped by yogis and the great-grandfather of all. O Lord, dispel our doubts through your own realization.

Brahma said: Hearing their words, I became anxious and, being filled with confusion, could not say anything immediately. Accompanied by all of them, I went to Lord Vishnu. Having bowed to Him, the great sages asked their profound question. Vishnu, also filled with perplexity, could not find an answer. Along with them, He went to Shankara (Shiva) for the sake of attaining knowledge. Having bowed to the Lord of Girija, they presented their profound doubt. Hearing that, Shiva was also unable to provide a definitive answer. Shiva then spoke these comforting words to them, remembering his own guru, the Lord of Ganas, Bhrishundi, in his heart.

Sri Shiva said: The great sage Bhrishundi, who knows all states and is our benefactor, exists at the beginning, middle, and end. He will narrate the complete greatness of the name of Ganesha. He knows it; there is no doubt, for by that name he became the Lord of Siddhis.

Brahma said: O sage, accompanied by him, the divine sages went to the great Bhrishundi, who resided in a pure hermitage. Having bowed to that great soul, all the divine sages stood there. He, having worshipped them, spoke these beneficial words.

Bhrishundi said: My birth and actions are blessed, for I have received your great vision. Tell me, why have you come here? You are all bearers of the divine power of Ganesha himself; you have come to purify yourselves through Ganesha.

Hearing the words of Bhrishundi, Shankara, having bowed to that great soul—the guru of the masters of yoga—spoke.

Sri Shankara said: O great-minded one, you know the complete and profound truth of Ganesha; there is no doubt, you are Ganesha himself in form. Therefore, we have all come here filled with doubts. Tell us, I will listen, that which is excellent and dispels illusion. How did He, who is eternal and possesses name and form, become Vighneshwara? And why is He called Brahmanaspati in the Vedic discourses, O master of yoga?

Hearing their words, the great sage Bhrishundi, who knows the essence of all and is the foremost among the devotees of Ganesha, spoke to them.

Bhrishundi said: 'Ga' is said to be the power of the Sun, 'Na' is Vishnu, and 'Sha' is remembered as Shiva. Ganesha is the master of them, and therefore He is Brahmanaspati. 'Gana' refers to a group, both external and internal; He is the master of them, and therefore He is Brahmanaspati. 'Go' is the knowledge in ignorance, and 'Na' is the bliss of the self that brings cessation. He is the master of them, and therefore He is Brahmanaspati. All universes are born from 'Ga' and all Brahmas are contained in 'Na'. He is the master of them, and therefore He is Brahmanaspati. Thus, His name is endowed with many meanings. O divine sages, how many of these meanings can I tell you? It is not possible.

The great Maya, which is of the nature of consciousness, is described as fivefold. That which is intellect is the giver of illusion, and Siddhi is considered the supreme Maya. Through these two, He created various things in the beginning for the sake of play. He is Brahmanaspati, the Lord of the universes and Brahmas that are filled with illusion. For the sake of destroying their illusion and for the sake of contributing to their liberation, Ganesha, the beloved of the Ganas, took on a name and form. O divine sages, His name is endowed with the reality of meaning; how can there be distinctions like destruction and non-destruction in Him?

Now I will tell you about the form of Ganesha, which grants happiness, by which you will worship Him constantly, free from doubt. The forehead of the great soul represents the totality and the individual; the three eyes represent the three Vedas, and the trunk represents the Turiya (the fourth state). Dharma and Adharma are the lips of Ganesha, and the dot (bindu) is the shape of the throat. The tusk is the embodied soul, and the belly represents knowledge. The ears are the Sankhya philosophy, and the hands represent the meaning of the scriptures. The feet of Ganesha are remembered as the forms of Maya and the illusory. O divine sages, the body formed by the union of these is described by yogis and seers of truth as being of the nature of self-realization. Because He is devoid of ego, this body of Ganesha, even though He is a bearer of a body, is said to be beyond the union of these elements.

In the union of union and non-union, the elephant-faced Lord became the bearer of a body for the sake of contributing to the worlds and the Brahmas. The universe below the neck is His body, and the head is the Brahman in the form of an elephant. By the union of the two, Ganesha became the bearer of a body. Thus, the pure yogis, free from the illusion of Maya, meditate on the body of Ganesha with various meanings. His body shines, endowed with the reality of word and meaning, and is devoid of states like destruction and non-destruction.

O great souls, all this mystery of the name and form of Ganesha has been told just as it was spoken by Mudgala. A person becomes Brahman-like by chanting 'Ganesha' even once. If one chants it twice, Ganesha becomes indebted to him; there is no doubt. The excellent person who remains chanting 'Ganesha' is considered by the Lord of Ganas to be one who has remembered the entire universe and Brahman. Therefore, he who does not remember the name of Ganesha is a wicked soul, to be abandoned, the crest-jewel of the outcasts.

The person who remains on earth having bowed to Ganesha, by him, the entire universe and Brahman are bowed to. The great soul who has held the form of Ganesha in meditation has, without doubt, meditated on the yoga that includes the universe and Brahman. O divine sages, he who has seen the form of the Ganesha image has seen the various Brahmas and the universe, and he has seen the elephant-faced Lord himself. O great souls, the universes and Brahmas are infinite; how can their worship be performed due to their infinity? Therefore, for the sake of ease, the Lord, who is of the nature of yoga, became Ganesha, possessing name and form, out of a desire for devotion. By His worship, everything—the universe and Brahman—is worshipped immediately; there is no doubt, it is of the nature of peace.

O divine sages, all this mystery of Ganesha, full of name and form, has been told to you in brief. Worship Him especially, free from all doubts. By that alone, O divine sages, you will become fulfilled. Having said this, the great yogi, Bhrishundi, who knew the essence of all and was like another elephant-faced Lord, became silent.

Chapter 43 : The Description of the Mystery of Chaturthi

The divine sages asked: O best of sages, the greatness of Ganesha, which grants all accomplishments through mere sight and remembrance, is truly extraordinary. O lord of Brahmins, when people perform the darshan and sing the praises of the Lord of Ganas even once, or more, there is no doubt that they enjoy all desired pleasures and, having attained the state of Brahman, are never reborn. Please tell us the reason for this.

Bhushundi replied: If there is a lack of faith, half the fruit is lost. If there is a lack of devotion, one-fourth is lost, and if there is hypocrisy, three-fourths are lost. Therefore, people who lack complete sincerity do not attain the fruit described in all the scriptures. Furthermore, O wise ones, know that in these scriptures, merit and sin are considered equal in strength, brilliance, and nature. Just as there is supreme merit in singing the praises of Ganesha, there is incomparable sin in slandering Him. O dear ones, observe how people who are focused on external appearances worship Him, yet simultaneously engage in slandering Him.

I will tell you something else to remove your doubts: the sin arising from transgressing against Him is a hundred times greater than that arising from pride. Knowing the Lord of Ganas, if people still transgress against Him, they become devoid of happiness and surely go to hell. There is no doubt that by transgressing against Ganesha, those people are afflicted with various obstacles in birth after birth. Can the small merit gained from honoring Ganesha provide happiness if it is destroyed by the power of transgression? Just as the Lord of Ganas was forgotten due to a lapse in memory, all the gods once became like water during the sacrifice of Brahma. Even the gods, who were endowed with great penance, were recreated by women; observe, O divine sages, the sin caused by even a slight transgression. Therefore, people who are full of sorrow go to hell due to their remembrance of Ganesha being overshadowed by their transgressions against Him.

I will tell you another thing mentioned in the scriptures: all actions performed in the past were fraught with obstacles. If humans perform actions while ignoring these obstacles, they certainly do not attain the full merit. In this way, many distinctions were made by the Lord of Obstacles for the sake of His eternal play, which cannot be fully described. The greatness of Ganesha is endowed with infinite glory; it was for the sake of revealing His nature that He allowed slander and other such things to exist. Therefore, O gods and sages, worship Ganesha with great effort, and by doing so, you will attain the complete Lord Ganesha.

The divine sages asked: Ganesha is of the form of Yoga; how did He assume a masculine form? O Yogi, tell us the story of this aspect.

Bhushundi replied: Just as the supreme Brahman is described as neuter, being devoid of both feminine and masculine forms, and is considered the path of the One, so too is the truth. The feminine, masculine, and neuter are all three-fold indicators, but the Atman, known as the Turiya (the fourth state), is the masculine indicator devoid of the three. The Vedas speak of Him as Gajanan (the elephant-faced one) for the sake of people's understanding, as they were unable to describe the One path in its entirety. Therefore, He is called the Lord of Ganas in a masculine form, just as words like 'Yoga' and 'God' are used in their respective contexts. His left side is His natural form, which is Siddhi (Success), and His right side, O noble ones, is Buddhi (Intellect), which is the masculine indicator. In their union, Ganesha is undoubtedly the embodiment of Yoga. He is endowed with the masculine and other forms, yet He is not devoid of them, nor is He separate from them.

The divine sages asked: The Chaturthi (fourth day) is a date very dear to the Lord of Ganas. Why is it called 'Rikta' (empty) and forbidden for auspicious works like marriage? The date of the auspicious-formed Lord is itself a giver of auspiciousness; how did it become associated with the state of being 'empty'?

Bhushundi replied: The Chaturthi, which is dear to Ganesha, exists in both fortnights and is the primary of all vows, leading to the attainment of Ganesha. The vow is four-fold, complete, and grants all accomplishments; it should be performed by all humans as a fundamental duty. By doing so, the four goals of life are attained without a doubt; otherwise, all actions performed by men become fruitless. Therefore, it is accepted by the virtuous in all works. Even those busy with various tasks should not break this vow. In works like marriage, people feast and engage in various worldly matters; how can there be a vow in such a state? Therefore, those who are free from doubt worship the Chaturthi, which is the abode of auspiciousness, complete, and the giver of all accomplishments, through the path of the vow.

The whole world is filled with people subject to birth and death. Those who perform the Chaturthi vow are freed from birth and death; having renounced the fruits of action, they attain their own abode. Free from all troubles and having enjoyed their desired pleasures, those who perform the Chaturthi vow become one with Brahman. By the mere performance of the vow, the devotee makes the world 'empty' (of worldly attachments), and therefore, the Chaturthi is called 'Rikta' (empty), there is no doubt about this. Because those who perform the vow renounce the world and go to Ganesha, the world becomes 'empty' for them, O sages and gods. No other day is considered as divine as the Chaturthi. Because it grants the state of Brahman and gives happiness to all, it is the best. I have told you all this mystery regarding the supreme vow of the great Ganesha; what more do you wish to hear?

Chapter 44 : The Description of the Instruction of the Jaratkaru Mantra

The divine sages asked: O great sage, Brahman is described in various ways for the purpose of order and attainment. Regarding Ganesha, there is no doubt, due to the influence of the differences in the vessels (the recipients). All beings like Agni and Indra are seekers of the word Brahman; O fortunate one, in the Puranas, it is said that Brahma and others originated from them. How did the grandfather (Brahma) become known as Brahma from them, and how did Shambhu and others become the Vedas, O wise one? Hearing their words, the best of sages, Bhushundi, was pleased and spoke these words to dispel their doubts.

Bhushundi said: The essence is described as the form of Brahma, there is no doubt. Brahma and the other gods originated from that, and from them, the divine sages. The Ashvini twins, who reside everywhere, originated from the nose. Through the form of Brahma, the Earth element emerged from them. Nature is called the Earth, and the Purusha is called the scent. By the union of these two, the Ashvini twins are considered by the wise to be in the form of Brahma. The body made of the Earth element is of those who possess the three gunas; there is no doubt that Brahma and others emerged from them. The cosmic form (Virat) of the Earth was born from them, and the Pranava (Om) is the body of the Earth; thus, O divine sages, it was born. By this method, Brahma is described by those who know the scriptures. From Him, the universe emerged; there is no wonder in that.

A man, by worshipping the Ashvini twins and overcoming the illusion born of the Earth, then worships Varuna, the supreme, for the sake of the Water element. At that time, the Ashvini twins disappear, and that great soul sees the primary Varuna-Brahman, from which this world was born. Thus, the traditional paths are considered to be in the form of Brahman. Indra and others, established in the form of elements, manifest through those elements. Those who hold the elements are established in the forms of bodies like Shiva; therefore, the gods led by Indra have them as their foundation. Through the secondary path, gods like Shambhu came into being; there is no doubt that the primary is not described as coming from Indra and others. In the primary sense, the lords of the gods, Indra and others, originated from Shiva and others with complete aspects; there is no doubt about this.

Having said this, the great yogi Bhushundi stopped. The divine sages bowed to him and spoke with joy. The divine sages said: O great sage, we are now free from all doubts. This is no wonder for you, who are in the form of Ganesha. Then the divine sages went to their respective abodes, filled with joy. Free from doubt, they worshipped the Lord of the Ganas daily.

Brahma said: O son, this secret that grants peace has been told to you. Therefore, worship that Lord of the Ganas, and you will attain peace. Having said this, Brahma gave him the six-syllabled mantra of Ganesha, according to the prescribed method, which grants all accomplishments.

Kaundinya said: Then, bowing to Brahma and the Chintamani (Ganesha), I worshipped the Lord of Obstacles with the mantra through yoga and penance. After a hundred years passed, the Lord was pleased and became the bestower of boons. He came to me to grant a boon, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi. Seeing Him, I suddenly stood up, worshipped and bowed to the Elephant-faced Lord, and praised Him with joy, O great sage Kauthuma. Then, having granted me my desire and the yoga itself, the Lord of Yoga went to His own abode, and I remained as if dejected. From that day on, I became a devotee of Ganesha in every way. I always worshipped Him, the Chintamani, with my wife.

O great sage, knowing that Durva grass is extremely dear to Ganesha, I worshipped Him daily with ten thousand Durva blades with devotion. My wife, being with me, spoke a slightly pure word: "Why do you worship me, intent on the practice of grass?" Hearing the words of my wife, filled with doubt, I spoke to her to give her devotion to the Lord of Obstacles. By offering Durva, the Lord of Obstacles becomes pleased, more so than by wealth, clothes, or offerings of food. Without Durva, the Lord of the Ganas remains hungry; various other types of worship become fruitless. Who can count the fruit of offering even one blade of Durva with devotion? Therefore, one should worship Him with Durva.

Then the doubt in her heart did not go away. I took one blade of Durva and gave it to her for her benefit. I said: "Go to the Lord of the gods, the King of gods, along with this Durva. Give this gold to the Lord of Obstacles who is present in the worship, O Lord." She, thinking that you should accept what is less or more from your beloved, said "So be it," bowed to me, and went to the Lord of the gods. The Goddess, having worshipped Him, told me the beneficial word. That Indra, filled with joy, did the same, O Brahmin. He placed the three worlds in a pot in order, yet even then, it was not equal to the Durva, O great sage. Seeing this, filled with great distress, he remembered the five gods. They also came from their cities for the success of the task. All the gods, including Shiva and Vishnu, with their wives, stood by the pot, but they too were not equal to the Durva.

Then, by their illusion, they threw the entire universe, crowded with worlds, into the pot, yet it was never equal to the Durva. Seeing this, filled with fear and due to my curse, O Brahmin, they came to my hermitage, filled with great sorrow. Brahma, bowing to me there, spoke with grief: "O best of sages, we have been made your servants by the merit of the Durva. The universe is not equal to even one blade of Durva, O Lord, which was thrown by you in the worship of Ganesha, O best of Kaundinyas. The fruit born from one blade of Durva, the Chintamani Himself will give to you, O fortunate one, for we are not equal to it. Command us, we will do your best work. O Lord, protect the gods who are your daily servants."

Hearing their words, I worshipped them and spoke: "O Brahma, go to your own abode with everyone, O Lord. I am not attached to the fruit of the Durva; it was done by me to enlighten my wife. O great gods, I am a servant, please forgive me." Then all those lords of the gods went to their respective abodes with joy. My wife, filled with faith, became a devotee of Ganesha forever. O great sage, I worship the Lord of the Ganas daily with my wife, and I worship the Chintamani who resides in the heart-stable field with joy. Therefore, O best of Brahmins, if you desire peace, then worship the Lord of Obstacles; by that, you will become worthy of being worshipped by yogis. Having said this, he gave me the supreme six-syllabled mantra of Ganapati, according to the method, O son, which grants all desires. Bowing to that great sage, I asked with humility, for the sake of attaining yoga, the question that was full of doubt.

Chapter 45 : Describing the Attainment of the Yoga of Jaratkaru

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Jaratkaru said: The Vedas and those who speak on the Vedas have described Brahman in various ways, associating it with food, vital breath, and other categories for the sake of systematic realization. Among these, the mind-made Brahman is the illuminator of the mental sheath; however, O noble one, that is not the ultimate Brahman, as it is subtle and born of the transformations of dreams. The mind, intellect, ego, and consciousness—these four are known by the best of sages in the scriptures as the internal instrument. Consciousness is described here as the seat of the Chintamani (the wish-fulfilling gem). Because it illuminates consciousness, the mind is considered a part of Brahman. Vital breath, mind, and knowledge are said to be the three types of dreams, and within them, in a partial way, the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) is the form of Brahman. Why do you speak of the Lord of Brahman as the provider of peace? How is it that what is stated in the Vedas is not understood, O Brahmin?

Kaundinya said: You have asked well, O Brahmin, for the benefit of all. I shall speak with great affection after bowing to the Leader of the Ganas. Various paths, such as Sankhya and Yoga, have been remembered for the purification of the categories, and they should be understood according to Vedic discourse. Words like food and vital breath are considered pointers to Brahman. Within that, consciousness is described as the ultimate internal instrument. Know, O giver of respect, that in the process of purifying the categories, the consciousness resting in one part and the mind established in Brahman are to be understood. Where in the Vedic debates is the mind considered supreme? The intellect, which is the giver of peace and accepted by all, is beyond it. The intellect residing in the internal instrument is influential only in a limited way; it is not considered primary, and the wise hold the same view regarding consciousness. I shall now tell you of another path that leads to knowledge and is based on the Vedas. Listen with a focused mind, O Brahmin, for it destroys all doubts.

The body is said to be the seat of action, and the mind is considered the seat of knowledge. In their union, the intellect itself becomes the source of delusion and the holder of duality. Through the worship of Ganesha, the suffering of duality is removed. By union with the Brahman-formed self, the intellect becomes the supreme among the wise. Thereafter, it dissolves into the supreme Lord of the Ganas. At that moment, the being becomes peaceful and becomes Brahman itself. If I am the form of Ganesha, then where does the illusion born of my body exist? Where is the mind-born delusion, and where is the supreme giver of discernment? In their union, which is full of Yoga, how can illusion arise for me? Even that which is associated with duality is remembered as being devoid of duality. Knowing this, the great Yogi attains supreme peace. In Ganesha, this Lord of the Ganas becomes manifest, O best of sages. By this method, know consciousness to be supreme and full of intellect. Knowing the Chintamani within it, a person becomes Brahman.

Hear another thing, O lord of Brahmins: the mind is the holder of discernment, and from discernment, pride arises in various ways. Therefore, the wise have called the ego mind-made. It is said in Vedic discourse that the intellect is considered superior to the mind. The intellect is described as the nature of knowledge, and it became twofold with joy: inert in the body, rooted in action, and consciousness-giving, which is mind-made. Knowledge of growth and decay is always established in the body. Know that to be born of the intellect, O Jaratkaru, through good Yoga. The knowledge called discernment regarding the body and the self is established in the mind. It is born of the intellect, devoid of decay and growth, and is the holder of consciousness. In their union, the intellect itself becomes the form of knowledge, the supreme of the two. The expression "I am Brahman" is devoid of mind and speech. Know the great consciousness to be the form of the intellect. I shall now tell you with affection what destroys all doubts.

Consciousness is said to be the illuminator, and the light is considered to reside in the intellect. Therefore, the Lord of the Ganas is called the Chintamani and the Lord of the Intellect. Consciousness is said to be of five types: scattered, deluded, distracted, and as described by the best of sages, one-pointed and restrained. The consciousness that is the body and the embodied soul is one-pointed, as accepted by the scriptures. Consider the mind-made Brahman to be the form of the body. Therefore, do not think the mind-made consciousness is the supreme. Consciousness that is the cause of illumination is accepted in all scriptures. Consciousness that is in union or separation is considered the indicator of restraint. Various Brahmans are indeed in the states of one-pointedness and restraint. Because it illuminates the body and the embodied soul, consciousness is called one-pointed. Because it illuminates union and separation, it is called restraint. Thus, everything has been explained; worship the Leader of the Ganas. By doing so, you will become worthy of being praised by the kings of Yogis and will attain great peace.

Jaratkaru said: Having said this, the great Yogi Kaundinya became silent. Jaratkaru, having bowed to him, returned with joy. Having accomplished the great Yoga, I became a follower of the Ganas, O son. You too, worship the Lord of the Ganas with respect for the sake of peace.

Suta said: Having said this, the great Yogi Jaratkaru became silent, O great sage. Then Astika, who knew the secrets, spoke.

Thus ends the forty-fifth chapter in the eighth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of the Attainment of the Yoga of Jaratkaru."

Chapter 46 : The Glory of the Brahmanaspati Sacrifice

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Astika asked: O father, I have been a devotee of Shiva since childhood. How can I worship Ganesha by abandoning Him? Please tell me how I can remain a devotee of Shiva.

Jaratkaru replied: No one can attain peace by abandoning Ganesha. Even Shiva and other deities meditate upon Ganesha to attain peace. The term 'Brahman' is applied to Shiva, Vishnu, and others only as a partial manifestation of Ganesha, as declared by the knowers of the Vedas. Great yogis attain peace by worshipping Him through yoga, but that peace is minor; it is not the supreme peace according to the yogis. Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman (Brahmanaspati), is the giver of peace even to those who are peaceful. Worship Him according to the prescribed rites, and you shall attain true peace.

Ganesha, though associated with Maya, is described in five forms, yet He is also beyond these five divisions and is called 'Nirmayika' (without Maya). The distinction between Him being with or without Maya is merely an illusion. In their union, Ganesha is undoubtedly the embodiment of peace. Therefore, Shiva is not different from the form of Ganesha. Abandon the sorrow born of ignorance and worship the Lord of Ganas, realizing that there is no difference between them.

I shall tell you the dialogue between Shankara and Upamanyu, which grants supreme peace. The great sage Upamanyu, a follower of Pashupata, became distressed upon realizing that the natural Brahman is Shiva. He went to Shiva, worshipped Him, and with folded hands, asked: "O Sadashiva, the natural Brahman is within one's control and yet possesses attributes. In the absolute Brahman, there is neither 'within one's control' nor 'under another's control.' Tell me the yoga that leads to the realization of Brahman, so that I may attain the state of Shiva and remain in peace."

Hearing this, Sadashiva was pleased and spoke of the Ganesha-yoga. Shiva said: "Once, when I was in Kashi, the great sage Jaigishavya, a leader of the Pashupatas, asked me: 'How can the natural Brahman exist within the natural state of Shiva? Tell me the supreme yoga that grants peace.' I replied: 'Worship Ganesha, and you will attain peace. I have attained the Dhundhi (Ganesha) who resides in the heart through yoga.' Jaigishavya, being distressed, said: 'I will not worship any other deity except Vishweshwara (Shiva). Is Shiva not the giver of peace?' I replied: 'The unmanifest power, the sun of truth, Hari, and I (Shiva)—the union of these four is called supreme bliss. That very Lord of Ganas manifests in five forms through Maya. When devoid of Maya, He is considered beyond the five. In their union, the Lord of Ganas is the giver of peace and the embodiment of the Absolute. He is the Lord of Brahman, and we are the expounders of Brahman. He assumed our form for the sake of play; why do you consider Him different, O sage?'"

"Abandoning the sense of duality, worship Ganesha with devotion. Shiva is not different from Him; contemplate Him as that very form." Having said this, Shiva gave him the one-syllable mantra and the procedure for the worship of Ganesha. After practicing the Ganesha-yoga, Jaigishavya performed the Brahmanaspati sacrifice. Having completed the sacrifice, he worshipped the Brahmins and sent them off. Ganesha, the bestower of boons, appeared there. Seeing Him, the sage stood up, worshipped Him, and praised Him with the Yajur-hymn. After granting the yoga of peace to the wise Jaigishavya, the Lord disappeared. From then on, the sage worshipped Shiva and Dhundhiraja in Kashi with the spirit of Ganesha-devotion.

Jaratkaru continued: "Therefore, you too should worship Ganesha for the sake of peace. He is the giver of peace to all. Having said this, I gave the one-syllable mantra to my disciple Upamanyu. He worshipped Ganesha and became the embodiment of peace. Ganesha, pleased with his devotion, appeared before him, granted him peace, and disappeared. Therefore, my son, abandon your doubts and worship the Lord of Ganas; you will not deviate and will attain peace."

Suta said: Having said this, the great sage gave the six-syllable mantra of Ganesha to Astika. Astika practiced it, meditating on the elephant-faced Lord in his heart. By performing the sacrifice to Ganesha, he became filled with peace. He then went to his mother and initiated her into the worship of Ganesha, bringing her peace as well. Thus, he worshipped Ganesha daily with love, and in the end, he attained the state of Brahman in the abode of Ganesha.

I have briefly narrated to you the glory of the Brahmanaspati sacrifice and the greatness of Ganesha-devotion. Whoever listens to or reads this with devotion will undoubtedly attain success, worldly enjoyment, and liberation. They will attain the merit of the Brahmanaspati sacrifice and, in the end, become one with Brahman, established in supreme bliss.

Chapter 47 : Description of the Threefold Basis of the World

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Shaunaka asked: How did the festival of Dashara and the tenth day (Dashami) of the Ashvina month come to be? Why do the gods and others perform the Shami tree worship on that day?

Suta replied: After Lord Vishnu, the conqueror of enemy cities, killed the great demon Shankha, he returned the Vedas, along with all their auxiliary branches, to the Creator, Brahma. Seeing them, a delighted Brahma remembered the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha). The Vedas, which had been created by Him in the form of Kapila for the sake of the success of rituals, had been stolen by the sinful demon Shankha. Seeing this great wonder, Brahma praised the Remover of Obstacles and prayed for the Vedas to be free from all impediments.

Then, a wondrous voice from the sky replied: O Brahma, by worshipping the Shami tree, you shall become free from all obstacles. That Shami tree and Ganesha are not different; therefore, worship it today according to the prescribed rituals. Then, touch the Vedas and make them auspicious. Having said this, the voice from the sky, which was the voice of the Elephant-faced Lord, ceased.

Hearing this, a joyful Brahma, along with the gods and sages, worshipped the Shami tree and made the Vedas auspicious. The time when the Shami was worshipped according to the rites was the evening of the tenth day of the bright fortnight of Ashvina. Thus, the Dashara festival, associated with the Shami tree, was established. Listen now to how it grants success in all endeavors.

The beings residing in the ten directions are fearful and cruel; they destroy one's work. The tenth day (Dashami), through the power of Shami worship, destroys these obstacles; hence, it is called Dashara, as it leads to the success of all tasks. After performing the Shami worship in the evening, Brahma looked upon the Vedas, which were now restored with all their parts, and instructed everyone accordingly.

The worship of the Shami on the tenth day of the bright fortnight of Ashvina should be performed by all living beings in the evening for the success of various tasks. By doing so, they wander in the ten directions free from obstacles and attain all successes; there is no doubt about this. Dashara is considered the destroyer of obstacles located in the ten directions. From this day forward, O gods and sages, you shall worship the Shami, which promotes all work, and by casting off yearly sorrows, you shall live in happiness.

O Brahmins, those who worship the Shami and read the Vedas, Puranas, and other scriptures will attain true knowledge. One who never worships the Shami on Dashara will be beset by various obstacles and will eventually go to hell. Having said this, Brahma, along with the Vedas, returned to his own realm, and the gods and sages also departed. Since that time, the bright tenth day of Ashvina has become the Dashara, associated with the Shami, and is the granter of success in all matters. Therefore, men, gods, and Nagas naturally worship the Shami and study the Vedas. Whoever hears or reads this account of the origin of the Shami worship will attain their desired goals.

Shaunaka asked: O Suta, tell me, what is the basis of this entire world? Some sages say it is dependent on Karma (action). Others say it is dependent on Ishvara (God), and some say it happens by chance (Yadruchha). Which of these three is the truth?

Suta replied: O best of sages, know that all three views held by the knowers of Brahman are true. Everything is created by Ganesha out of his own free will for the sake of play. The Maya (illusion) that grants power and prosperity is described as the source of this. That which is clouded by the pride of power is called the intellectual illusion (Mayika). Because of the desire for the fruits of Maya, one becomes burdened with labor.

Through the union of Maya and the Mayika (the one under illusion), Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, plays according to his own nature, devoted to non-duality. There is no one who impels the Lord of Obstacles; therefore, everything exists in the hearts of yogis by His own will.

Listen further, O Brahmin: Maya is of the nature of various delusions. It is inert and incapable of acting on its own. When the Mayika (the individual soul) is deluded by it, he moves. Then, Maya, endowed with existence, plays in various ways. The goddess, held by the Mayika, moves in various forms; there is no doubt that she exists only by the will of the Mayika. When the great Maya is abandoned by the Mayika, she becomes incapable of doing anything due to her inert nature.

Some sages, devoted to the path of meditation, say that everything exists simply by being dependent on God. I shall tell you further: Maya is the form of Karma. It is the supreme threefold power that plays eternally. It moves everything—Karma, Akarma, and Vikarma—and grants heaven, liberation, or great suffering. Whatever action one performs, one enjoys the fruit thereof; thus, this entire world is dependent on Karma in these three ways. One must inevitably enjoy the fruits of good and bad deeds; even after hundreds of millions of kalpas, the deeds performed do not leave the soul. This is what the Brahmins who are attached to the body-theory say, and this is the truth established in the scriptures.

Now, O Shaunaka, listen to the single underlying truth of these three. Those who are deluded by Ganesha's Maya do not understand. Ganesha exists in the form of Maya and the Mayika, playing according to his own nature. By His will, the Ishvara (the individual ruler) also exists; deluded by the Lord of Ganas, he does not recognize the Elephant-faced Lord. He thinks, "I am the best, I am the Supreme Brahman, this world is based on me, and by my will, all creatures act." Whatever the will of the Lord of Ganas is, the Ishvara himself acts accordingly out of joy; therefore, no difference is seen.

The great Maya, which is the form of Karma, is also endowed with Ganesha's will. It moves, but because it is deluded, it does not know Him. It thinks, "I am the best, this world is based on me, and I sustain all moving and non-moving things through the power of Karma." Whatever the will of the Lord of Ganas is, the living beings of the world perform their actions accordingly.

Therefore, O Brahmin, all three views are considered to be dependent on the One. Thus, without contradiction, it always manifests in three ways. The followers of Maya-vada say it is dependent on Karma; the followers of Advaita-vada say it is dependent on Ishvara; and the yogis say the world is dependent on chance (Yadruchha). Thus, O best of sages, all three are true according to their respective proofs.

Thus ends the forty-seventh chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which describes the threefold basis of the world.

Chapter 48 : Description of the Greatness of the Months

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Suta said: Listen, O great sage, to the account of the twelve months, which is dear to Ganesha and grants all accomplishments, described here in brief. In the month of Shravana, by serving the five forms of Ganesha, Varuna himself attained the status of a guardian of the directions and gained knowledge born of Ganesha. In Bhadrapada, Vayu, by serving the peacock-mounted Ganesha, attained yoga, the status of a guardian of the directions, and became a lord of the Ganas. In Ashvina, the gods and Gandharvas, by serving Vighneshvara in ancient times, became endowed with their own divine prosperity, O great sage. In Kartika, the Vidyadharas, by serving Vakratunda, became proficient in their respective duties and possessors of knowledge. In Margashirsha, the Adityas, by serving Vikata, became radiant, all-powerful, and filled with knowledge, O great sage. In Pausha, the Vasus, by serving Lambodara, attained their own positions and became endowed with knowledge and devoted to various states of being. In Magha, the Rudras, by serving Ganesha, became omnipresent, highly fortunate, and moved about fearlessly as followers of Ganesha. In Phalguna, Nirriti, by serving Ganesha, attained the position of a guardian of the directions and gained the wisdom of rituals and the enjoyment of deeds. In Chaitra, all the Apsaras, by serving Dhumravarna, attained various forms of beauty, music, and divine grace. In Vaishakha, the Yakshas, by serving Heramba, became skilled in their respective tasks and devoted to various enjoyments. In Jyeshtha, the Nagas, by serving Vinayaka, became powerful, capable of performing tasks, and devoted to various enjoyments. In Ashadha, the birds, by daily serving Gajanana, became endowed with enjoyments and skilled in their own tasks. In the same way, the mountains, by serving Dhundhiraja with their minerals, became holders of various gems and providers of enjoyment.

By taking refuge in Gajanana during their respective months and engaging in the five-fold worship of Ganesha, all these beings attained success. How many such vows and their results can I describe? Even ten thousand years would not be enough. I have told you this entire greatness born of the months; reading or hearing it grants all accomplishments immediately. Whatever one desires, one attains without a doubt, and in the end, O sage, one merges into the divine abode of Ganesha. I have narrated everything you asked, O sage. What else do you wish to hear? Tell me, and I shall speak of it.

Thus ends the forty-eighth chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the greatness of the months in the story of Dhumravarna.

Chapter 49 : The Recitation of the Ganesha Hridayam

Shounaka said: O Suta, please narrate the dialogue between Shankara and Sanaka and the others, which is supreme and bestows all accomplishments. Having heard about the various incarnations and the character of Dhumravarna, what did the sages led by Sanaka ask for the sake of attaining Yoga?

Suta said: O Brahmanas, having heard the account of the Dhumravarna incarnation and its destruction of evil, the sages, with their hair standing on end in devotion, asked Lord Shankara.

Sanaka and the others said: O Sadashiva, we are satisfied after hearing the supreme greatness of the Dhumravarna incarnation. Now, O Lord of all, O leader of Yogis, please instruct us in the best, easiest, and most auspicious method for attaining Yoga.

Lord Shiva said: I shall tell you the Ganesha Hridayam, which grants all accomplishments and bestows supreme peace upon the seeker quickly. Long ago, O Brahmanas, I was absorbed in the meditation of Ganesha. There, the best of rivers, Ganga, asked me a profound question.

Ganga said: Tell me, O Shankara, whom do you meditate upon daily? I wish to know from you who is considered the supreme.

Lord Shiva said: O knower of all states, I meditate upon Ganesha, the Lord of gods, the Brahman of Brahman, the eternal family deity. O Devi, I shall tell you his secret heart, which grants everything, by which you shall know that Lord. Long ago, being covered by ignorance, I performed penance. By the power of that penance, I perceived the elephant-faced Lord in my heart. By his mere vision, I attained enlightenment, and knowing him as the Lord of Ganas, I became worthy of being worshipped by Yogis. I shall tell you that supreme Ganesha Hridayam, by which you will become proficient in Ganesha Yoga.

(The ritualistic procedure follows: The Rishi is Shambhu, the meter is various, the deity is Ganesha, the seed is 'Gam', the power is knowledge, and the pin is 'Nada'. It is used for the attainment of desires.)

Meditation: I meditate upon Ganesha, who is like vermilion, three-eyed, with a large belly, dressed in red, holding a noose, a goad, a tusk, and a gesture of fearlessness in his hands. He is accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, is elephant-faced, one-tusked, adorned with various ornaments, bestows happiness upon his people, and is the Lord of all.

After meditating thus, one should worship him with mental offerings. After chanting the root mantra, one should recite the Hridayam:

I bow to Ganesha, the one-tusked, the Chintamani, Vinayaka, Dhundhiraja, Mayuresha, Lambodara, Gajanana, Heramba, Vakratunda, Jyeshtharaja, Ashapura, Varada, Vikata, Dharanidhara, the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the Brahmanaspati, the Lord of auspiciousness, the one worshipped by all, and the supreme leader of obstacles. These twenty-one names of the great Ganesha, combined with their meanings, are called the Hridayam.

Ganga said: O Sadashiva, O ocean of compassion, please tell me the meaning of these twenty-one names so that I may know the Ganesha Hridayam.

Lord Shiva said: The letter 'Ga' represents the diverse moving and non-moving world, and 'Na' represents Brahman, the supreme of the supreme. Those who reside in them are the Ganas; I bow to that one supreme Ganesha. The 'Danta' (tusk) represents the illusory form, and the one who holds it is the supreme. I bow to the one who dwells in the intellect and yearns for the devotion of his people. He who is the light of the consciousness, dwelling in all yet untouched by impurities, who is devoid of worldly enjoyments yet the source of all, him I bow to as Chintamani. Vinayaka means the one who is the leader of all, yet has no leader himself. I bow to him with a heart full of devotion. The Vedas, Puranas, Maheshwara, sages, gods, humans, Nagas, Asuras, and all beings seek him; I bow to that Dhundhiraja. The 'Mayura' (peacock) aspect represents the illusion that creates confusion; I bow to the Lord of Maya. He from whose belly this universe is born and in whom all Brahmas reside, I bow to that Lambodara. The elephant-head is the supreme Brahman; I bow to that Gajanana. 'He' represents the world and 'Ramba' represents Brahman; I bow to Heramba, the protector of both. He whose body is the universe and whose face is the supreme soul, I bow to that Vakratunda. He who is the father and mother of the worlds, yet has no parents himself, I bow to that Jyeshtharaja. He who fulfills the desires of all, I bow to that Ashapuraka. He who is the bestower of boons, I bow to that Varada. He who is the truth beyond the illusory world, I bow to that Vikata. He who supports the earth and the intellect of the Yogis, I bow to that Dharanidhara. He who plays with Siddhi and Buddhi, I bow to that Siddhi-Buddhi-pati. He who is the Lord of the Ganas and the essence of all, I bow to that Brahmanaspati. He who is the destroyer of all inauspiciousness and the giver of peace, I bow to that Mangalyapati. He who is worshipped by the wise and the Vedas, I bow to that Sarvapujya. He who grants enjoyment and liberation, and removes obstacles for his devotees, I bow to that Vighnaraja.

O beloved, I have told you the secret of the twenty-one names. Whoever knows this heart of Ganesha becomes one with the supreme Brahman.

Ganga said: O Shankara, I do not yet have the experience of this. Please tell me more.

Lord Shiva said: Take the mantra of Ganesha and perform the Purascharana; you will attain knowledge without doubt. The mantra is not different from Ganesha. 'Ga' is Brahma, 'A' is Vishnu, and the Bindu is Shiva. The combination of these is the great power. The universe is born from this sound; hence, the single-syllable mantra is the form of Ganesha. (Lord Shiva then explains the ritualistic process of Japa, Homa, Tarpana, and feeding the Brahmanas).

Having said this, he gave her the mantra. Ganga went to perform penance, reached Mayuresha, and practiced the mantra. By the grace of Ganesha, she attained the realization of the meaning of the twenty-one names.

Sanaka and the others asked: O Lord, how did the river attain the state of Brahman?

Shiva said: A Yogi who worships Ganesha with nine-fold devotion, offering his body, speech, and mind to him, attains the supreme. By this method, the sages worship the Lord. This secret of Ganesha has been told to you. Just as the heart is the most important part of the body, this Hridayam is the heart of Ganesha. Whoever worships him with this will enjoy all pleasures and eventually become one with Yoga.

Having heard this, the sages led by Sanaka bowed to me and went to their forest. They served the single-syllable mantra and became Gana-devotees.

Mudgala said: Thus, Shiva gave the mantra to Shesha. Whoever hears or recites this holy Ganesha Hridayam attains his desires and becomes one with Brahman. Reciting it daily grants sons, wealth, health, and eternal prosperity. By reciting it twenty-one times for twenty-one days, one attains the desired fruit. It can accomplish the impossible, release one from bondage, and destroy all enemies. In battle, one attains victory. Nothing is difficult for one who recites this. Whatever a person contemplates, they attain. There is nothing equal to this for quick accomplishment. It should never be given to one who is devoid of devotion or is wicked, but to a sincere devotee, it should be given, and Ganesha will be pleased.

Chapter 50 : Description of the Fruits of Listening

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: The form of Ganesha, which is meditated upon by yogis, is accepted everywhere. Is it possible for non-yogis to meditate upon this form directly? There is no doubt about this. Why is it said in the Vedas and other scriptures that this is the supreme object of meditation for yogis? O learned one, explain the reason for this to dispel my doubts.

Mudgala said: Non-yogis perceive Ganesha, the Lord who is composed of the five elements, as a being similar to all others, possessing a physical body. In their hearts, the supreme yogic form of the Lord of the Ganas does not shine clearly. However, yogis specifically know Him as the embodiment of Yoga. Listen with a focused mind as I explain the distinction.

The Vedas describe Brahman as both the collective and the individual. Yogis perceive this Brahman in the forehead of Ganesha. They see the three-fold and multi-fold divine light of the great-souled Ganesha within the three-eyed Lord. His trunk is described as the supreme causal form; the point (bindu) is His throat, and the tusk is remembered as the essence of the "So'ham" (I am He) mantra. His belly is the embodiment of knowledge, and His ears represent the four-fold Vedas, while His four arms represent the four-fold Brahman.

Yogis perceive the two feet of the Lord of the Ganas as the Brahman that is both associated with Maya and delusion, and also free from delusion, acting as the supreme witness. His beautiful axe represents the Brahman of dissolution, and the goad represents the Brahman as the controller. The lotus represents creation, and the noose represents supreme delusion; it is the instrument of bondage and liberation, destroying the wicked with joy.

He is the dispeller of fear for His devotees, the protector, and the granter of desires. Those who seek worldly fruits call Him the fulfiller of desires. He is the Chintamani, the giver of worldly enjoyment and liberation, the inexhaustible light, the nectar, and the eternal Brahman. His city is the supreme Brahman known as Svananda, and the ocean of sugarcane juice is described as the essence of Brahman.

The five-fold intellect is His intelligence, and the illusion of divine power and success (Siddhi) are what yogis call Maya, based on their yogic realization. They see the mouse as His all-pervading vehicle. Righteousness and unrighteousness are on His chest and back, respectively. His hairs are the medicinal herbs, and the blinking of His eyes represents the creation and dissolution of the universe. His two sons, Labha (Gain) and Shubha (Auspiciousness), are the initiators of all actions; whatever goal one sets, one reaps the fruit accordingly.

Thus, there is no doubt that His various forms are manifestations of Brahman. Because of this, the Lord of the Ganas is called Brahmanaspati. Yogis, such as Shuka, meditate upon Him as the embodied one—the form of Brahman, both with form and formless, the all-pervading Lord. They know the Lord of Obstacles as one who is with attributes up to the throat and without attributes in His elephant-headed form, and they understand the yoga of the union of these two. Therefore, O Prajapati, the form of Ganapati described in the Vedas is naturally of a yogic nature.

Daksha said: O best of the learned, how did the mouse become His vehicle? Tell me this specifically, for you are all-knowing and an ocean of compassion.

Mudgala said: Through Maya, the all-pervading Brahman deludes various people and enjoys the fruits of their actions, remaining untouched by their merits and demerits. The word "musha" means to steal; thus, the mouse (mushaka) represents the all-pervading Lord who, like a thief, dwells in the heart and consumes all by deluding them. When the elephant-headed Lord was propitiated through penance by this all-pervading power, He became pleased and made it His vehicle to increase its joy.

All this has been told to you regarding the story of Dhumravarna, which is filled with various narratives and grants all successes. O Daksha, whoever listens to this, reads it, or causes others to hear it, will be endowed with all happiness and, in the end, will attain Brahman. There is nothing equal to this that grants all success, where the Dhumravarna Ganesha is directly glorified. Whatever one desires, one will certainly attain through this; there is no doubt that by listening to this, a human being attains auspiciousness.

Whoever gets this book written and worships it in their home will never have any fear from demons or other such beings. After enjoying various pleasures and being surrounded by sons, grandsons, and friends, they will eventually reach Svananda and become one with Ganesha. By listening to or reading this story of Dhumravarna, there is no doubt that one attains righteousness, wealth, desire, and liberation. A person of excellence might perform various pilgrimages and penances, but the fruits of all those are immediately attained by listening to this. Whatever merit one might gain from performing sacrifices, charity, or pilgrimages, a person attains all that by listening to this.

What more is there to say? Among all the various means, O Prajapati, there is nothing equal to this. Consider, O wise one, can any other practice be compared to this, where the Lord of Yoga, Dhumravarna, is directly described? I have told you the complete story of Dhumravarna, which grants all success, briefly and to the best of my ability. Even Shiva, the Vedas, and the yogis are not capable of describing Him in detail; who am I, O Prajapati? Now, worship the Lord of Ganesha with effort, or if you wish to hear more, tell me, and I shall speak.

Suta said: Having said this, the great yogi Mudgala became silent. Daksha, with a joyful heart, bowed to him with folded hands. O great sage, the story of Dhumravarna has been told to you just as it was told to Daksha. You have heard this wonderful story, which grants all success and is filled with various narratives. What more do you wish to hear?

Thus ends the fiftieth chapter of the eighth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the fruits of listening to the story of Dhumravarna.

Chapter 1 : Description of the Threefold Austerities

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Shaunaka said: O venerable one, I have heard the great narrative centered on the stories of the elephant-faced Lord, which bestows all happiness and grants the state of Brahman. Yet, even after drinking this supreme nectar of Brahman, I am not satisfied. Who, being a connoisseur of the virtues of the Lord of the Ganas, could ever abandon them? We are blessed, O great soul, to have taken refuge in your presence, where such yoga-bestowing tales flow from your lips. Now, please tell me everything about the greatness of Lambodara (Ganesha). What is the nature of this Lord of Ganas, and what is the nature of His Brahman? How many are His incarnations, what are the deeds of that remover of obstacles, and for what purpose did He come to the earth? Tell me all of this in detail.

Suta said: Having heard the character of the elephant-faced Lord requested by Daksha in this manner, the great sage Mudgala, the foremost among yogis and a devotee of Ganesha, spoke.

Daksha said: Having heard the greatness of the elephant-faced Lord briefly, I am not satisfied, O great soul, just as one is never satisfied after drinking nectar. Therefore, please tell me the story of Lambodara in detail, which grants all accomplishments and is complete for those who hear and read it. Those who hear, read, narrate, or inquire about the stories of Ganesha are the four types of fortunate beings. Blessed are those people in this world who, with devotion, O best of sages, are fulfilled by these stories; they are the yogis who are the ornaments of yoga.

Suta said: Seeing his devotion, the sage Mudgala was pleased and spoke with respect to that great soul, the descendant of Prajapati.

Mudgala said: You are the direct son of Brahma and are equal to Brahma himself; there is no doubt about this. What is there to be surprised about regarding the Lord of Ganas? I desire to be the servant of those in whose lotus-like mouths the remembrance of Ganesha resides. Now, listen, I will tell you the story briefly, for who is capable of describing the greatness of Lambodara in full? I will narrate the dialogue between the great soul Asita and Naidhruva, which bestows all accomplishments.

Having bowed down, the great sage Naidhruva went to the forest for a year to perform penance to attain his goal. He performed severe austerities for a thousand divine years. By his penance, the lords of the gods became fearful. They wondered what this Brahmin wished to achieve through such penance, for there was nothing in the universe equal to his austerity. Then, Asita arrived at his hermitage unexpectedly. The sage rose, bowed with folded hands, and worshipped his uncle with devotion. Following his instructions, he sat down. The great yogi Asita, pleased with his nephew, spoke words for his benefit.

Asita said: Why are you emaciating your body with such penance, my son? The penance that involves the destruction of the body is not considered supreme according to the scriptures. Therefore, abandon this physical strain and perform the supreme penance, by which you, having become a true Brahmin, will eventually attain Brahman.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Asita, Naidhruva, filled with devotion, asked the great yogi again, filled with doubt.

Naidhruva said: O sage, the learned ones speak of penances like Krichchhra and Chandrayana performed according to the rules. Is there something supreme among them?

Asita said: Penance is said to be of three types, which cause heat (tapa). Listen to them according to the rules, and you will become worthy of being honored by yogis. The penances like Krichchhra, Chandrayana, the five-fire sacrifice, and living on air are all forms of external penance. Know for certain, my son, that this is the external state. Such penance is said to yield fruits in this world and the next, but after enjoying the fruit, one becomes destitute again.

Now listen to the second type of penance, which is extremely difficult to perform. It arises in the body through the control of breath (Pranayama). In this, there is undoubtedly a heating of the prana and the body, and that is why it is called penance. By piercing the six chakras and being absorbed in meditation, one restrains the breath in the thousand-petaled lotus (Sahasrara). By the power of this penance, a person attains minor siddhis, becomes a knower of the path of knowledge, and gains the knowledge of the three times. He attains distant hearing, the ability to travel at will, the power to see land in water and water on land, and cities in the sky. He becomes all-knowing and brilliant, and can even shake the realms of Brahma and others. He knows the history of everyone in a Kalpa. He who is absorbed in meditation knows this through the power of yoga. He goes to the realm of the Creator and enjoys various pleasures, but when his merit is exhausted, he certainly becomes destitute again.

Now hear from me the third form of penance, which bestows supreme yoga. It is the twofold penance practiced through the path of the Guru. Through this, the mind becomes one-pointed, there is no doubt. One sees the nature of the Self everywhere with the eyes of knowledge. By the mere experience of this, one becomes devoted to tranquility and self-control, and through this, a heat is generated in the body and mind. Despising worldly objects, he wanders like one who is dull, mad, or a ghost, knowing the essence of all things and being established in the Self. Then, through the yoga of restraint, he becomes devoted to renunciation and, by restraining the mind everywhere, attains liberation himself.

When one realizes "I am Brahman," where can there be fear? The world is not the world; the Self is devoid of mind and speech. There is no rising of the Self from itself, nor from another. Thus, through the yoga of restraint, being devoted to tranquility and self-control, the yogi always remains in contentment. When the body and mind are established in this state of contentment, a constant heat is generated, and that is called penance. Then, abandoning the stage of restraint and being devoted to peace, having abandoned the five-fold mind, he becomes Brahman itself. Then, being free from injunctions and prohibitions, the yogi wanders, experiencing the fruits of his past actions (Prarabdha). Just as rivers flow into the ocean, he enjoys his experiences as they come. Within his body, a heat is always generated due to the peace and the enjoyment of experiences, and that is called penance.

This yoga-based penance has been described to you by me. The third one is the one that grants Brahman; strive for that. The great sages and yogis who are devoted to this, the Brahmins endowed with such penance, become Brahman itself.

Thus ends the first chapter of the fifth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "Description of the Threefold Austerities."

Chapter 2 : The Slaying of Bhasmasura

Mudgala said: Upon hearing the words of Asita, Naidhruva was astonished. He bowed to that great soul and spoke with humility.

Naidhruva said: O Master, tell me of the supreme Yoga, the path that grants liberation. To whom should I offer my devotion to attain this, O best of Yogis?

Asita said: Listen, my son, I shall tell you the story of Lambodara (Ganesha). By this, you shall attain the supreme Yoga without a doubt. Once, long ago, your father and I lived with pure hearts, practicing austerity and remaining free from impurities. We strove for the attainment of Yoga, remaining focused, possessing inner knowledge, and finding joy in the simple life. By mastering concentration and restraint, we attained a natural state of bliss. Seeing our freedom from delusion, we were amazed, yet feeling a lack of peace, we went to Brahma.

There, the sages Vishvamitra and Vashistha, filled with pride and rivalry, had also arrived. They bowed to the Creator and then, filled with arrogance, asked him, "O Creator, tell us, who among us is superior in austerity and the practice of Yoga?" Hearing this, Brahma, the grandfather of all, replied, "I consider that sage superior who has truly conquered anger. You are both full of anger; what austerity is there in that? He who conquers the mind and attains the peace-granting Yoga is the greatest."

The two sages, humbled by their pursuit of superiority, asked, "O Lord of the people, what is this anger that is so terrifying? Where does it dwell, and what is its form? By what Yoga do Yogis conquer this wicked force? Tell us, O Lord, so that we may overcome it."

Brahma said: I shall tell you a story that grants all success, by which one can conquer anger, lust, and pride. In the past, a demon named Bhasmasura, the son of Shakuni, performed intense penance. Having received the five-syllable mantra and initiation, he worshipped Lord Shiva with great devotion, his body smeared with ashes. After a thousand years, Shiva, surrounded by all the gods, came to grant him a boon. Seeing Shiva, the demon rose, bowed, and praised him with hymns. Pleased, Shiva said, "Ask for a boon, O demon, I shall grant it to you."

Bhasmasura said: O Shambhu, O Maheshwara, grant me this: whosoever's head I place my hand upon, let them be reduced to ashes immediately. I fear no one else; grant me this power that surpasses all.

Hearing this, Shiva was astonished but replied, "So be it, O great demon. By my command, whoever's head you touch with your right hand shall instantly turn to ashes."

Shiva then returned to Kailasa. The demon, however, began to think, "The Goddess, the Mother of the Universe, resides on Shiva's left side. She is beautiful and radiant. I have never seen such beauty. Shiva is naked and unworthy of her. I shall use this boon to turn Shiva into ashes and take Parvati for myself."

Thinking this, the demon rushed toward Kailasa. Seeing him approach without the usual signs of reverence, Shiva realized the danger and fled in fear. The demon pursued him relentlessly. Lord Vishnu, seeing Shiva fleeing, remembered Ganesha in his heart, wondering what would happen. Vishnu, filled with sorrow for Shiva, praised Ganesha, the remover of obstacles. Shiva, too, terrified, prayed to Ganesha for protection.

Shiva said: O Vinayaka, do you not see how I am being terrorized by this demon? He intends to turn me to ashes by placing his hand on my head. O remover of fear, O dweller of the heart, protect me! I have no other refuge but you, O Lord of the Ganas. Protect me, for I am at the point of death.

As Shiva prayed, Ganesha entered the heart of Vishnu, becoming the source of wisdom and compassion. Vishnu assumed the form of a beautiful woman and approached the demon. The demon was instantly infatuated by the sight of Mohini. He asked her, "Who are you, O beautiful one? Tell me, and I shall do whatever you wish. I am the lord of all here; there is no one equal to me. Serve me, and I shall be your slave."

Hearing this, she smiled and said, "I am Mohini, the enchantress. If you wish to be with me, you must follow my commands." The demon agreed. She said, "Dance as I dance." As she danced with grace, the demon, blinded by lust, followed her movements. In the process, she placed her hand upon her own head, and the deluded demon did the same. Instantly, Bhasmasura was reduced to ashes. Seeing this, she was amazed and praised Ganesha.

Thus ends the second chapter of the Lambodara Charita in the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 3 : The Description of the Attainment of the Kingdom by Krodhasura

Brahma said: Lord Shiva, whose ears are adorned with ornaments, praised Ganesha, the bestower of success and the beloved of his devotees, with the most excellent hymns. At that moment, Lord Shiva, having been addressed by the gods, prepared to abandon the form of Mohini. Upon realizing that Bhasmasura was dead, Shiva was filled with astonishment and, brimming with devotion, danced while chanting the praises of Ganesha. Accompanied by all the gods, he went to the city of Mukunda. There, Shiva beheld Vishnu in the form of a woman. Seeing that form, which caused great delusion, the great Lord Shiva was struck with wonder and, overcome by desire, approached her, unaware of the truth. Seeing Shiva thus deluded, Vishnu, still in the form of a woman, began to walk away, and the Lord of gods, Shiva, followed her, deeply agitated. Then, Vishnu abandoned that form and returned to his male state. Shiva, feeling ashamed and distressed, stumbled, his mind completely bewildered.

Thereafter, all the gods returned to their respective abodes with joy. Shiva, having praised the glorious Vishnu, also returned to his own place. From the seed of Shiva that fell, an Asura was born; he was dark-complexioned, had copper-colored eyes, and was very powerful. He approached Shukracharya, bowed to him with humility, and said, "O Sage, I am endowed with brilliance; please protect me as your disciple." The great sage, having realized everything through meditation, spoke to him, bringing joy to the man.

Shukra said: When Shankara saw Mohini, he was pierced by the arrows of desire. Later, seeing Vishnu, he felt anger in his heart. His mind became completely restless, and he thought, "I shall capture her with effort." From that desire to capture her, anger arose. At that moment, his seed suddenly fell. Because you were born from that, you shall be named Krodha (Anger).

Then, O best of Brahmins, the Asura king, well-versed in the scriptures, had all the sacraments performed for him according to the prescribed rites. Krodha studied all the Vedas and other scriptures in the presence of his guru. The great Asura married the daughter of Shambara, named Priti, who was beautiful, possessed of all graceful limbs, and endowed with beauty and charm, which further increased his worldly attachments.

Then, approaching Shukra and bowing to him with folded hands, the brilliant Asura spoke words that would bring happiness to the Daityas. Krodhasura said: By your command, O Master, I shall conquer the universe. Please grant me a mantra that will increase my glory in all respects.

Brahma said: Hearing his words, the excellent and all-knowing Shukra gave him a great mantra to fulfill the purpose of the Daityas. Having bowed and received the mantra according to the rites of the Sun, he went to a forest filled with tigers and other beasts, determined to perform penance. Standing on one leg, the great Asura chanted the excellent mantra, meditating on Aryama (the Sun) in his heart with his gaze fixed upward. He pleased the Lord of gods through effort, remaining without food and standing like a wooden post, indifferent to cold, heat, and other fears.

After a thousand divine years, the Sun, being pleased, appeared before him and spoke in a deep voice to grant a boon to the great Daitya. The Sun said: O highly fortunate one, ask for the boons you desire, and I shall grant them, as I am pleased with your severe penance and the desires of your heart.

Hearing the words of the Sun and seeing him standing before him, the Daitya bowed with great devotion and worshipped the repository of brilliance. Again bowing and folding his hands, the Asura praised the Lord of gods with the Saura Sukta. The Sun said: Ask for a boon, O wise Daitya; whatever you desire, I shall grant you without doubt.

Krodhasura said: O Sun, may I never meet my death at the hands of those who are the leaders of the gods, who are endowed with the powers of creation, sustenance, and destruction. Make me capable of conquering the universe, and grant me the kingdom of all moving and non-moving beings, O son of Aditi. May I be free from disease, may whatever I contemplate in my mind be successful, and ensure that no one is ever my equal, O Lord of the sky.

Hearing his beautiful words, which were a source of fear for all, the Lord of brilliance, being well-restrained, spoke to him. The Sun said: O Krodha, whatever you have asked for shall be fulfilled. Because you are restrained by penance, O Daitya, I have granted you this supreme boon.

Having said this, Aryama departed to his realm, filled with sorrow. Krodha, filled with joy, returned to his home. He brought happiness to all his friends and Daitya allies. After bowing to Shukra, he remained in his place, filled with pride. The Daitya king, having produced two sons named Harsha (Joy) and Shoka (Grief) from his wife Priti, began to enjoy various pleasures and became very arrogant.

Having invited the all-knowing Shukra and worshipped him, and having obtained his permission, the Asura built a city. It was named Aveshana, beautiful and adorned with all splendor. O sage, the Danavas, Daityas, and Rakshasas came there. People of all four varnas settled in the city, and the people lived there happily with their friends and relatives. Then, Shukra arrived and, with the help of the best Brahmins, anointed Krodha as the lord over all the Danavas.

In that city of Krodha, there were five fierce chiefs who were all-knowing in policy, possessed of great illusion, and endowed with great strength. They were Bali, Jrimbha, Rahu, Ravana, and Malyavan. Accompanied by them, the fierce Krodha shone brilliantly. Other Rakshasas and Danavas, such as Prahasta, also served the great Krodha with devotion and great strength.

Chapter 4 : Conquest of the Universe by Krodhasura

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Brahma said: After some time had passed, Krodhasura, filled with joy and pride, spoke to Bali and the other leading demons.

Krodhasura said: Listen, all you powerful demons led by Bali. I shall conquer the entire universe with your assistance.

Hearing his words, the demons, filled with joy, bowed to their king and spoke with folded hands.

Bali and the others said: You have spoken well, O Lord. We shall conquer the lords of the gods. By your grace, O King of demons, command the great warriors. Your prowess is unparalleled in the entire universe. You are fearless, O Great King; go and conquer all the gods.

Hearing their words, Krodhasura was delighted. He bowed to Shukracharya, summoned him, and felt honored by his approval. He then called all the demon hosts, who arrived joyfully before their king.

Accompanied by his army, the great demon set out. He appeared everywhere with an infinite four-fold military force. Krodhasura himself, seated on a chariot and surrounded by demon lords, first went to conquer the kings of the earth.

Who on earth could fight against the great heroes led by Bali? The kings, terrified by the demon lords, sought refuge. Some, filled with arrogance, fought and were killed. Others abandoned their kingdoms and fled to dense forests. Having thus conquered the earth and the seven oceans, the demons rejoiced.

They made the kings pay tribute and accepted those who sought refuge. They installed their own tax collectors in place of those who had died. In the forests where the kings had fled, the demons established their own rule. Thus, the demons were established in every city.

Then, the leading demons, accompanied by Krodhasura, went to the netherworlds (Patala) to conquer the powerful Nagas. Shesha, the wise, having learned of the situation through a messenger and knowing that Krodha was filled with the pride of his boon, went to meet him himself. Seeing Shesha surrounded by great Nagas, Krodhasura was pleased and treated him with respect. After accepting the annual tribute from the Nagas and giving them jewels and other gifts, Shesha returned to his own abode.

Then, protected by Shukracharya, the demon king went to all the divine realms and destroyed the celestial gardens. The great demon then sent a joyful messenger to Indra to convey his mighty deeds. Indra, along with Brihaspati and the other gods, fled and hid in a cave.

Learning of this, the joyful Krodhasura arrived in Amaravati and sat upon Indra’s throne. He divided all the divine realms and distributed them among the demons, who then took up residence there. They enjoyed the pleasures meant for the gods, O Sage, and lived in mutual affection, loving their adventurous life. The Gandharvas, Charanas, and Apsaras served them, while the gods, deprived of their homes, suffered greatly.

Then, one day, the demon king, surrounded by his followers, went to the famous Satyaloka. Knowing this, Brahma fled. The cruel Krodhasura stayed there and enjoyed the pleasures of Satyaloka with his powerful demons.

From there, surrounded by demons, he went to Vaikuntha. Knowing this, Vishnu fled out of fear of Krodha. Remaining there, the cruel Krodhasura enjoyed the pleasures created by Vishnu along with his kin.

Then the demon went to Kailasa to conquer Shankara. Mahadeva also fled, scorched by the heat of Krodha’s presence. The demon Krodha, staying there, rejoiced in his heart. Surrounded by demons, the cruel and powerful one spoke to them.

Krodhasura said: O Bali, Ravana, and others, listen to my beneficial words. There is no place left unconquered; tell me where I should conquer now.

The demon lords said: You have conquered everything, O King. Only the Sun, the lord of the gods, remains unconquered. Strive for that, O Lord of demons.

The demon Krodha, filled with joy, then said to them: O demon lords, abandon the Sun, who is my chosen deity.

Hearing his words, the great demons were agitated. Filled with guile and intent on sowing discord, they said to him: O Lord, there is no doubt that the Sun is our chosen deity. We shall worship him with special devotion, as he is fond of those who are devoted to him. If you wish for our welfare, abandon the side of the gods. Otherwise, O Lord, worship Aryama (the Sun) or conquer him by force. We shall conquer the god who relies on the side of our enemies and is intent on our destruction. Therefore, O King, do what is beneficial.

Hearing the words of the demon lords, Krodha was delighted. He sent the proud Ravana as a messenger. Learning the entire situation from Ravana, the radiant Sun, though fearful, spoke to him in anger.

The Sun said: I will not abandon the side of the gods and become a follower of Krodhasura. I am a god, there is no doubt. Tell the arrogant Krodha, who is blinded by his boon, that I have abandoned my kingdom and gone to the forest; you may rule the kingdom.

Having said this to Ravana, the Sun left his realm. Ravana returned. Then, the overjoyed demon Krodha, surrounded by the wicked, arrived in the solar realm and rejoiced upon capturing it. The evil-minded one considered himself accomplished. Having conquered the universe, he enjoyed supreme pleasures.

After establishing the demon lords in the heavens, the demon king returned to the city of Priti on earth. Krodhasura, the powerful, ruled there, enjoying the three worlds and bringing joy to his friends. Addicted to women, meat, and wine, and fond of sensual pleasures, he did not realize that time was passing, as he was deluded by the passage of time.

Thus ends the fourth chapter named the Conquest of the Universe by Krodhasura, in the fifth section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 5 : The Granting of Boons to the Divine Sages

Brahma said: Then, the powerful Asura named Krodha (Anger), filled with excessive arrogance, commanded his cruel demons to disrupt all righteous actions. Following his orders, the demons went everywhere on earth and began to torment the Brahmins who were the upholders of the Varna and Ashrama systems. They captured people of other castes as well and rendered them devoid of all religious duties. The demons destroyed sacred places of the gods and cut down the trees used for sacrificial rituals. Everywhere, people were terrified and ceased performing their duties; neither Svaha, nor Svadha, nor Vashatkara (sacrificial offerings) were heard anywhere.

Then, the powerful Krodhasura said to the Brahmins, "O Brahmins, perform all actions dedicated to my name. I am the Lord of the world, O noble ones, there is no doubt. What will be unattainable for those who worship at my feet?" Consequently, the Brahmins, who were devoted to penance, left their worldly comforts and went into forests filled with lions and tigers. Some, who were weak, remained under his command in misery, and all people became devoid of the Varna and Ashrama duties. Everywhere, idols of Krodha were installed, and the demons initiated his worship in every home. Thus, that enemy caused chaos throughout the three worlds, and all the gods, distressed by the cessation of religious duties, became fearful.

The gods, suffering from the lack of sacrificial offerings and constant fasting, began to strive for his destruction. However, the lords of the gods could not find any way to attain peace, and they became filled with great sorrow and trembling with fear. In the meantime, Vashistha, the best of the yogis, sighed and spoke to the gods for their benefit.

Vashistha said: "We are undoubtedly subject to creation, sustenance, and destruction; therefore, his death will certainly not come from us. O lords of the gods, worship Ganesha, the Lord of Brahma, who dwells in Svananda and whom the Vedas describe as being beyond the three Gunas. He will kill Krodhasura and grant us our positions; of this, there is no doubt, so perform your efforts, O gods."

Hearing the words of Vashistha, the divine sages were filled with joy and praised the great treasure of knowledge. Then, they chanted the king of mantras dedicated to Lambodara (Ganesha) and, filled with devotion and established in penance, they entered into meditation. Through various rituals and yogic practices, they pleased the elephant-faced Lord, all remaining devoted to fasting and proper conduct.

Thus, after a thousand years had passed, the pleased and benevolent Lambodara appeared before them. Seeing the Lord of Ganesha arrive, the divine sages stood up with joy and prostrated before him like sticks on the ground. They worshipped the Lord with various offerings, and after bowing again, they praised him with folded hands.

The divine sages said: Salutations to the Lord of Ganas, the bearer of the large belly, the elephant-faced one; salutations to you, who are worthy of worship by all. Salutations to the Lord of Svananda, the giver of all bliss, the Lord of the helpless and of all. Salutations to the remover of obstacles, the destroyer of the obstacles of devotees, and the creator of great obstacles for the non-devotees. Salutations to you, the formless one, the supreme Lord, the one beyond creation, sustenance, and destruction, O Mahodara (the one with the great belly). Salutations to the one who deludes the deluded, the supreme soul, the giver of all accomplishments, and the embodiment of all arts and knowledge. Salutations to the one with infinite faces, hands, and feet, the Lord of Gunas, and the controller of all qualities. Salutations to you, who are the beginning, middle, and end, the formless and the one with form. Salutations to the king of the elders, the Lord of the eldest, the giver of all, the one worshipped first, and the one who resides at the end.

O Lord, the entire diverse world and the diverse Brahma have emerged from your belly; therefore, you are Lambodara. All are born from your belly, and you are not born from anyone's belly; therefore, you are Lambodara, salutations to you. O Lord of obstacles, you know the end of all bellies, but no one knows the end of your belly; salutations to you. You are the giver of bliss to those who seek bliss, and having seen you directly, we are truly blessed. You are beyond mind and speech, yet you are the embodiment of mind and speech; it is a wonder that you, Lambodara, have come here, salutations to you. How can we praise the Lord of Ganesha, from whom the Vedas and others have originated? We have attained peace here; please be gracious to us, salutations to you.

Having said this, all the gods and sages danced, and the Lord of Ganesha spoke to them with a voice as deep as the clouds.

Lambodara said: Speak your boon, O noble ones, O lords of the gods, and O pure sages. I am pleased by your penance, devotion, and intent, and I shall grant your desires. This hymn composed by you shall be the granter of all things; whoever reads or hears it shall attain supreme happiness here and hereafter. I shall grant whatever is desired through the recitation of this hymn; may it be the giver of various accomplishments and the enhancer of devotion to me.

Brahma said: Hearing the words of Lambodara, the divine sages were delighted and, bowing down, they praised the Lord of all, the protector of devotees.

The divine sages said: If you are pleased and are a granter of boons, O elephant-faced Lord, then kill Krodhasura now, O Lord. Because of him, we have been displaced from our positions and rendered devoid of religious duties; the sages and the entire world are deprived of Svadha and Svaha. Therefore, O Lambodara, O Lord, since all have emerged from your belly, kill this one and protect all your devotees. Grant us your devotion, which destroys all anger; through that, we shall worship you with peace. O Lambodara, when your devotion is attained, one enjoys auspiciousness forever and is remembered as having attained the state of Brahman.

Hearing the words of all the sages and gods, the great Lord Lambodara, bound by their devotion, said, "So be it." Then, the Lord disappeared, and the gods and sages remained there, filled with fear, awaiting the time of his destruction.

Thus ends the fifth chapter of the Lambodara Charita in the fifth section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 6 : Description of the War Between Gods and Demons

Brahma said: O Vishwamitra and Vashistha, listen to the most wonderful story of the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha), which bestows great peace. Once, the demon Krodha was sitting in his assembly when he heard a terrifying voice from the sky. It said that Lambodara (Ganesha), having been prayed to by the sages and gods to destroy you, will kill you and bring happiness to the world. Hearing this, Krodha fainted, suddenly overwhelmed by fear. The demon lords and sages present there immediately attended to him. The great demons, including Bali, were filled with sorrow and fear, and asked him, "O giver of honor, what has happened? Tell us."

Hearing the words of the demon lords, the fearful demon told them about the voice from the sky. Hearing this, the arrogant demons became angry and said, "O Lord, we will surely kill the gods and the sages today. The gods are our enemies and have done us great harm. O great one, command us all to destroy the gods." Then, the highly elated Krodha prepared for battle himself and, surrounded by the demon lords, immediately went to the great mountain. Seeing the furious Krodha surrounded by demon lords, all the gods became anxious and remembered the Lord of the Ganas.

Upon being remembered, the powerful and mighty Lambodara arrived riding on a mouse. The sages and gods saw Him adorned with weapons like the noose, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, with three eyes and a radiant navel. They bowed to Him, shedding their fear, and with great joy, they praised Him and respectfully narrated the dire situation. Hearing the grave news, the pleased Lord Lambodara, along with the sages, spoke to the leaders of the gods.

Lambodara said: Do not fear, O wise ones. I will kill the great demon. I will surely ensure that all people remain established in their own dharma. Then, the demon Krodhasura arrived nearby. Seeing the divine Lambodara, he became terrified. He said to the demon lords, "That voice was undoubtedly true. This Lambodara is surely being prayed to by the gods, O demons." Hearing his words, the demons, with eyes reddened by anger, spoke to their demon lord with pride and strength.

The demon lords said: We will undoubtedly kill the gods along with Lambodara. Do not worry in vain; protect your back, O great-minded one. There is no doubt that the Lord of the Ganas, who has taken a body, has arrived. What harm can a body that is subject to birth and death do to you? The demons then remembered Shukracharya with devotion. Upon being remembered, he arrived immediately. The demons bowed to their guru and narrated the entire situation. Hearing it, Shukra replied to them.

Shukra said: A foolish person desires success while abandoning the Lord of Success (Siddhi). Similarly, like a fool, one desires brilliance while abandoning the Lord of Intellect (Buddhi). O demon lords, tell me, how can you be free from obstacles while abandoning the one who is always the remover of obstacles? Therefore, abandon the war and take refuge in Him; that would be auspicious. There is no doubt, otherwise Krodha will die.

Hearing Shukra's words, Bali said to him, "He is the giver of success through action and the giver of brilliance. Through action, He makes one free from obstacles or creates obstacles. Therefore, O great yogi, we will conquer Him through our actions." Hearing his words, all the demon lords and others said, "Well said, well said," and stood ready for battle. Seeing the inevitable outcome, the yogi Shukra remained silent.

All the gods, filled with anger and equipped with great weapons, arrived at the battlefield, and the demon lords did the same. Then, a great and terrible war ensued, with mutual destruction between the demons and the gods. The gods and demons fought, determined to die, driven by their weapons and the pride of their strength. The sounds of the heroes, musical instruments, horses, elephants, and conch shells filled the directions. The dust raised by the army covered the sun. No one could distinguish friend from foe, and they struck each other.

Then, a river of blood flowed from the wounds of weapons, and all the dust particles were submerged in it on the battlefield. Seeing each other, they struck with anger, both gods and demons intent on victory. The war lasted for three terrible days, causing fear to all. Then, all the demon groups fled in terror. The gods, filled with joy, shouted, "Victory to Lord Lambodara!" Seeing this, the demon lords, filled with anger, came to kill them. Bali, Ravana, Jrimbha, Malyavan, Kumbhakarna, Rahu, and other great heroes—they were too many to count.

Struck by their barrage of weapons, the gods fled, leaving the battlefield, broken and scattered on all sides. Then, Indra, Shankara, Vishnu, Surya, and Chandra, all the leaders of the gods, arrived filled with anger. They unleashed a great and terrible rain of weapons, which caused all the demon groups to flee in the ten directions. Then, the furious Ravana and others arrived. A terrible duel ensued, causing fear to everyone. Jrimbha fought Mahendra, Rahu fought Surya, Bali fought the angry Vishnu, and Ravana fought Shankara. Kumbhakarna fought Chandra, and various other demon lords fought the leaders of the gods and the wind deities, all desiring victory.

Thus ends the sixth chapter of the Lambodara Charita in the Fifth Section of the great Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 7 : The Arrival of Krodhasura

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Brahma said: A terrible and fierce battle raged for one night, during which rivers of blood flowed like torrents of water. Neither the gods nor the demons, who were as formidable as Yama, could achieve victory. Indra crushed the demons with strikes from his thunderbolt. Then, the highly enraged Jrimbha, possessing great strength and valor, took up a formidable mace and fought the King of the Gods. With his mace, he rendered Indra unconscious, causing the entire army of gods to cry out in distress. Indra, regaining his senses, fought fiercely again and struck Jrimbha down to the earth with his thunderbolt. Ravana, struck by Shiva’s trident, fell to the ground, and consequently, all the demons fled the battlefield. Vishnu rendered Bali unconscious with his mace, and Chandra caused Kumbhakarna to faint in agony. Rahu, severely afflicted by the radiance of the Sun in battle, fell to the ground unconscious, crying out in despair. All those who engaged in personal combat with the gods were defeated by the leaders of the gods and fled.

Out of compassion for his devotees, Shiva and Vishnu did not kill Ravana and Bali on the battlefield. Then, the enraged leaders of the gods, filled with fear, killed the demons with a rain of weapons. The demons, overwhelmed by terror, thought the dissolution of the world had arrived and, seeking to save their lives, took refuge in the gods, joyfully chanting, "Victory to Lord Lambodara, victory to the Lord of Obstacles, victory to the Lord of Gods, Heramba, the One with the Curved Trunk."

Seeing the slaughter of the demons, Krodha (Anger) was filled with great astonishment, and sighing, he pondered what would happen next. Learning of their father’s grief, Harsha (Joy) and Shoka (Grief) entered the fray and rained down weapons like clouds. Struck by their infallible weapons, the gods fell with severed limbs, tormented by the terror of the demons. Suddenly approaching Indra, Shoka struck him on the head with his mace, rendering him instantly unconscious. He then gathered his forces and protected the Lord of the Gods. Shukracharya then restored the demon leaders to health with his magical knowledge.

Bali and the others returned to the battlefield, and Harsha suddenly struck Chandra unconscious with his discus. Then, the enraged Shiva fought fiercely and killed all the great demons with his trident. Seeing Shiva’s strength, Harsha arrived and fought him; he was the best among the radiant sons of the demon king. A fierce and terrible battle ensued between Harsha and Shiva, each desiring victory over the other. Shiva struck him down with his trident, and seeing Harsha fallen and unconscious, the demons were filled with fear. Seeing the demon leaders fallen, Shukracharya arrived and restored Harsha to consciousness with his own power.

Then, the jubilant demons fought fiercely. Seeing their strength, Vishnu released his formidable discus. Many demons were killed by the sharp-edged discus, falling to the ground with severed limbs. Then Shoka arrived and released a radiant trident, which suddenly struck Vishnu in the chest. Janardana fell unconscious, and the demons, filled with joy, raised a great cry on the battlefield. Then, Harsha, in a great rage, struck Shiva with a death-like mace, causing him to fall unconscious. The gods, all defeated, cried out in distress and fled, terrified by the two demons.

Seeing this great wonder, the valiant Lambodara, filled with rage, released his goad upon the circle of demons. Seeing that radiant weapon, the demons were astonished, and in an instant, the two demon leaders, Harsha and Shoka, were struck down. By his maya, the Lord of the Ganas seized Shukracharya and threw him into a cave, after which all the others, led by Rahu, fell. Then, Krodha, filled with great rage, arrived himself. Overcome by grief for his sons, he unleashed a rain of arrows. Pierced by those arrows, the gods fell with severed limbs, and the directions were filled with cries of distress. The valiant Lambodara, having reassured the gods, arrived on the battlefield, holding his noose and sword.

Thus ends the seventh chapter of the Lambodara Charita in the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Arrival of Krodhasura."

Chapter 8 : The Description of the Pacification of Krodhasura

Brahma said: Seeing the great demon approaching the battlefield, Lambodara, the knower of all thoughts, spoke to the enraged Krodhasura.

Krodhasura said: O Lambodara, you great fool, why have you come to the battlefield? Desiring the welfare of the gods, you shall die before me. I have conquered the entire universe and subdued the gods; do you, who live like an animal in the forest, wish to conquer me in vain? My anger can burn the entire three worlds, both moving and stationary; why have you come before me, fully prepared for battle? You have killed my army and my two sons; now, filled with grief, I will destroy you, who are blinded by pride. Take refuge in me, O Gajanana, or else you shall die; I will burst your great belly with a single arrow.

The great demon insulted Lambodara with various such words, and the Lord, filled with divine pride, replied.

Lambodara said: Why do you speak to me, O king of demons, saying you will destroy me, the great villain? I am the establisher of true religion, while you are the destroyer of righteousness. O wicked one, you are engaged in evil deeds due to the boon granted by the Sun, but because of your great sins, all of that has become fruitless. You cannot die, as you are subject to birth and destruction; I, however, was never born, nor shall I ever die. Know me to be the Brahman, always dwelling in self-bliss; how will you conquer me, who am beyond mind and speech?

Hearing these words of the wise Lambodara, the leader of the demons was astonished and spoke to him again.

Krodhasura said: O Brahman, if you were not born, how do you possess a body? And if you are beyond mind and speech, tell me, how do I see you?

Lambodara said: This Siddhi (perfection) on my left side is the form of illusion; all beings wander in this world, deluded by this illusion. On my right side resides the intellect itself, which holds this illusion; through the intellect, man becomes deluded again and again. The intellect, which is the form of consciousness, is said to be fivefold; I am the master of this Siddhi, which is composed of five illusions. Know that the entire diverse universe and the diverse Brahman are always within my belly; that is why I am called Lambodara (the one with the large belly). Everything is born from my belly, sustained by me, and at the end, I playfully keep it all within my belly. Therefore, take refuge in me if you wish to live. Shukra knows me, but though he has taught you, you do not understand. I do not desire the slaughter of demons, nor am I fond of killing gods; I protect all who follow their own dharma, without doubt. Whether the demons are in the netherworld, humans on earth, or gods in heaven, if they are established in their own nature, I remain in my self-bliss. When the gods, filled with anger, killed the demons, I became the provider of success to the demons in the netherworld. Later, the demons, empowered by penance, defeated the mighty gods and sought to destroy them by abandoning their duties. At that time, I became the provider of success to the hosts of gods, and those gods, knowing the vulnerabilities of the demons, killed them all. You have conquered everything, O demon, by cutting the roots of the gods; you have become the lord of all, therefore I will certainly destroy you.

Hearing these beautiful words, Krodhasura was astonished and, with a heart filled with joy, spoke to him.

Krodhasura said: I bow to you, O Lord of the Ganas, who are the supreme form of both gods and demons. Now, O giver of refuge, protect me, who am filled with devotion. Show me your true form, which destroys all doubts—the Gajanana who is the embodiment of Brahman and the meaning of Lambodara.

Hearing his words, Lambodara was delighted and, knowing his heart was filled with devotion, revealed his form. He granted the demon divine vision, and the enraged demon saw Lambodara and was filled with supreme wonder. The great demon bowed to the Lord of Brahman with his hair standing on end, and Lambodara returned to his previous form. Krodhasura, filled with devotion, worshipped him, bowed down, and praised him with tearful eyes.

Krodhasura said: Salutations to you, O Lambodara, the embodiment of the yoga of peace. Salutations again and again to you, O Vighnesha, the giver of all peace. Your trunk is undoubtedly the form of the unmanifest; salutations to you, the embodied one, the manifest form. You are always seen by yogis in self-bliss as the Lord of Brahman; therefore, you dwell in self-bliss. Salutations to you, the holder of union. Everything in the universe is born from your belly, O Lord; there is no doubt that you are Brahman. Salutations to you, O Lambodara. By your grace, O Lord of the great belly, I have realized that there is nothing beyond you. Salutations again and again to the Supreme Lord. Salutations to you, O Heramba, the remover of obstacles, the compassionate one. Salutations to you, who are without beginning, middle, or end, and who are the embodiment of truth. Salutations to the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, who knows the play of Siddhi and Buddhi. Salutations to you, the giver of Siddhi and Buddhi, O Vakratunda. Salutations again and again to you, who are the soul of all and worthy of worship by all. Salutations to you, who are worthy of all worship and the protector of your devotees. Therefore, be pleased, O Vighnesha; I am your servant, O Gajanana. Salutations again and again to the great-souled Lambodara.

Having praised him thus, the great demon bowed to Gajanana. The Lord of the Ganas, who is dear to his devotees, spoke to him.

Lambodara said: Ask for a boon, O fortunate one, whatever your heart desires. I shall grant it out of devotion, as I am pleased by this hymn. This hymn composed by you shall be the giver of all success. Whoever reads it shall have no fear born of anger. Whoever listens to it shall also attain the same, without doubt. I shall grant whatever they desire through the recitation of this hymn.

Krodhasura said: If you, O Lambodara, are pleased and are a giver of boons, then grant me firm devotion to your lotus feet, O Lord. Command me, O fortunate one, so that I may serve you. Give me a place and a means of livelihood to sustain my body.

Brahma said: Hearing these words of Krodhasura, Lambodara was filled with joy and spoke to the leader of the demons.

Lambodara said: My firm devotion shall be yours, O sinless one. Dwell here without fear; there is no doubt about this. Wherever there is an endeavor, O great demon, where I am not remembered, there you shall rule as a demon, O wise one. Consume all the fruits of their merits and good deeds; all that is yours, there is no doubt. Protect my devotees who dwell there as you please. You shall become dear to the followers of Ganesha, without doubt.

Thus addressed, the great demon Krodha was filled with supreme joy, bowed to Lambodara, and went to his own excellent abode. All the demons, including Rahu, left him in fear and entered the netherworld, and Shukra was filled with joy. Thus, the Lord of the Ganas pacified Krodhasura, and Krodha remained, practicing devotion to Ganesha.

Chapter 9 : The Description of the Hymn Offered by the Divine Sages to Lambodara

Brahma said: Seeing the demon Krodhasura thus pacified, the pure divine sages were filled with great joy and worshipped the Lord of the Ganas. After worshipping Him according to the prescribed rituals, they bowed to Lambodara again. Then, with necks bowed in devotion, they all began to praise Him.

The divine sages said: We bow to that Lambodara, who is the supreme, the primordial, the source of all, the end of all, the complete, the infinite, the eternal, the Self-existent, and the one who resides in the inconceivable. We bow to Lambodara, who is devoid of knowledge (of duality), immersed in His own bliss, the seed of all, the essence of Sankhya, the infinite knower, and attainable only through the yoga of the disembodied. We bow to Lambodara, who is the Jiva, full of supreme bliss, the Lord of all, the embodiment of consciousness, the one who rests in the dissolution of Prakriti, and who is devoid of bodies and devoid of the sense of 'I'. We bow to Lambodara, who is the non-dual, the union of body and soul, the illuminator of all, the eternal, the ancient, the Purusha, and the Lord of the Ganas. We bow to Lambodara, who is the form of the Bindu, attainable by the mind, devoid of feet yet having four quarters, the one who assumes many forms, and the single-tusked Lord. We bow to Lambodara, who is the form of Nada, the Lord of the Gunas, the one who is everywhere, whose eyes, ears, and mouth are everywhere, and whose hands, belly, and lotus-feet are everywhere. We bow to Lambodara, who resides in deep sleep, the giver of equanimity, the knower of duality, the primordial, the one who manifests in two ways, the root of bliss, and the one who shines even in darkness. We bow to Lambodara, who is subtle, who resides within, the omniscient, the primordial, the leader of the Ganas, and the one who sports in the sheath of intellect. We bow to Lambodara, who is gross, the pride of all, the supreme, endowed with Rajas, the infinite, the primordial, and the one who sports in the enjoyment of food. We bow to Lambodara, who is the spiritual form, moving in darkness, the light of substance, the ultimate truth, and the knower of the body and senses. We bow to Lambodara, who is eternal, the cause of the transformation of the Rajasic body, the form of action, residing in the various senses, and the immeasurable soul of all beings. We bow to Lambodara, who is the form of knowledge, residing in all gods, the Lord who grants knowledge to all senses, and the great one who is the presiding deity of all. We bow to Lambodara, who is the form of space, the illuminator of all, full of Nada, the primordial source of all, and the primordial Lord who always sports with the elements. We bow to Lambodara, who is the form of air, the mover of the world, residing in the Pranas, the giver of His own divisions, and the one who moves through the body in ten ways. We bow to Lambodara, who is the form of fire, the illuminator of all, the mover of duality, residing in the belly, and the one who pervades and divides all at the junction. We bow to Lambodara, who is the form of water, full of essence, the giver of nourishment, the Lord of the six tastes, and the one who manifests the eternal moist nature. We bow to Lambodara, who is the form of earth, the supporter of the world, the root of all food, residing in various herbs, the form of shape, and the elephant-faced Lord. We bow to Lambodara, who is the Virat form, infinite, having eyes everywhere, having a thousand heads, and having hands, faces, and feet everywhere. We bow to Lambodara, who is endowed with Rajas, the creator, the Lord of the twice-born, the primordial form, the grandfather, and the one who resides equally in gods and demons. We bow to Lambodara, who is the manifestation of Sattva, the bearer of the form of Hari, the one who is Tamasic and resides in the form of Shankara, the one who moves as the sun, and the one who is the beloved of the Mother of the world. How can we praise Him, who is resplendent with such diverse forms and yet devoid of all? Even the gods, led by Shiva, are unable to describe Him; we bow to that Lambodara.

Brahma said: Having praised Him thus, the divine sages bowed to the elephant-faced Lord. Lambodara, the bestower of success, spoke to those noble sages: "This hymn composed by you will increase my love. Those who read or hear it will immediately attain all success. It is a means to achieve Dharma, Artha, Kama, and Moksha, it grants the knowledge of Brahman, and it will always be revered in all tasks. Through this hymn, O divine sages, one can perform acts of destruction and driving away of enemies; there is no doubt that it destroys the evil deeds of others. By reading this hymn, a man attains sons, grandsons, wealth, grain, a wife, and happiness. One who is imprisoned is immediately released by reading this hymn. By reciting it twenty-one times, one attains all the desires of the heart. He who recites this hymn twenty-one times will enjoy the fruits within twenty-one days. O sages, O immortals, I am pleased; ask for a boon, even if it be very difficult to obtain."

Hearing the words of Lambodara, the divine sages bowed to Him. With joyful hearts, tearful eyes, and controlled senses, they said: "O Lambodara, You are pleased and You are the bestower of boons, O great Lord. Grant us pure devotion to You, devoid of anger. O Lord, You have pacified the demon Krodhasura. By this, O Lord of the Ganas, You have brought peace to the world. Everyone will now be engaged in their own duties and become righteous. By Your grace, we shall remain in our respective positions. O Lord of the Ganas, since the sages have become fearless and are performing their duties, what boon shall we ask?" Having said this, they bowed to Lambodara, the giver of happiness, and danced before Him with hearts full of joy. Lambodara, the powerful one, said "So be it," and disappeared before the eyes of the divine sages.

Then, the gods and the twice-born created a beautiful four-armed image of Ganesha and installed it with reverence. They worshipped it especially on the western seashore. From that time on, that place became the sacred site of Lambodara. The pure divine sages remained in their respective positions by His partial manifestation, while they always performed devotion to Him in His complete form. This is the story of Lambodara, which destroys anger, as heard by Vishvamitra and Vashistha. This Ganesha, Lambodara, is the embodiment of Brahman. He performs the rise from the self to the supreme through His own play. His supreme form cannot be described by anyone; it is attained through the union of all and through the service of yoga. This Lambodara is the teacher of complete yoga. He is attained through the service of yoga in the union of the united and the disunited. Because the forms of the united and the disunited originated from His belly, He is called Lambodara by the knowers of the Vedas.

Asita said: Having said this, Brahma himself became silent, O wise one. Vashistha bowed to him and went to his own hermitage. The wise Vishvamitra also sighed and went to his own place. Thus, when I went there, I asked the grandfather (Brahma) about this.

Chapter 10 : The Description of the Peace of Vatsara and Asita

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Naidhruva said: O Lord, having heard this story which brings peace to the agitated mind, I am not satisfied. Please tell me more about the deeds of the Lord of Obstacles. How did Brahma respond when you asked him? O ocean of compassion, if it leads to Yoga, please tell me.

Mudgala said: Being asked thus by Naidhruva, his uncle Asita began to narrate another great story that bestows supreme Yoga.

Asita said: Listen, my son, I shall tell you about the greatness of Ganesha, the Lord of Brahma, which grants peace and Yoga. I first bowed to Brahma and asked him with humility: "O Father, tell me the great Yoga that grants peace and saves everyone. You are the self-existent Lord of Yoga, yet you appear to be under the control of Maya. How is it that you, who are the embodiment of Brahman, act according to your own will? We have come to you devoid of peace; tell us what we must serve to attain it."

Asita continued: When I asked this of the Grandfather, who is the best of all knowers, he spoke with affection.

Brahma said: Karma, Jnana, and the natural state are the three; the fourth is the union of these. When these are balanced, the Self-bliss of silence is attained. Why should the Brahman, which is self-governed, be dependent? It is attained by the son through the Yoga of self-realization. Union is associated with Maya, and through the absence of union, all connections perish. By detachment and one's own intellect, Brahman is attained; there is no other way. When the union and non-union are brought into Yoga, that Yoga grants peace. Only through such peace can the son be attained. The Vedas declare Ganesha to be the embodiment of peace. If you sages worship Him with devotion, you will become peaceful and esteemed by other Yogis. My words are not false. The body is the manifest (Samprajnata), and the head is the unmanifest (Asamprajnata); the union of these two in the form of an elephant is Ganesha. The Ganas are the groups, and the groups represent the Brahman; He is the Lord of these, hence He is called Ganesha. The intellect is the form of consciousness, and it is also that which deludes the mind; Ganesha is the Lord of both Siddhi and Buddhi. Abandoning the delusion of the mind, if you worship the Chintamani with the peace of Yoga, you will attain that form of Yoga.

Asita said: Having said this, the Creator became silent. I, along with Vatsara, bowed to him and went to the forest. We practiced what the Creator instructed, and through self-control and tranquility, we attained steady peace. Yet, we remained devoted to the worship of the Lord of Ganas. After a year, the Lord of Ganas, the bestower of boons, appeared. Seeing Him, we bowed, worshipped Him according to the rites, and praised Him with necks bent in devotion.

Asita and Vatsara said: Salutations to you, O Lambodara, who are always the embodiment of Yoga, whose body is the form of Yoga, and who grants the peace of Yoga. Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, who removes the obstacles of the devout and creates great obstacles for the non-devout. Salutations to you who dwell in self-bliss and grant self-bliss, whose vehicle is the mouse, and whose flag bears the mouse. Salutations to the Lord of the helpless, who is the Lord of all, the Lord of the Lords, O Vinayaka. Salutations to Heramba, the Mother and Father of all. Salutations to the eldest of the elders. Salutations to the immeasurable, the infinite player, the one with infinite forms. Salutations to the Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, the protector of devotees, the guardian of various powers, the player of infinite Maya. Salutations to the elephant-faced one, the first to be worshipped by all, the giver of success. Salutations to the one with the curved trunk, the brave one, the single-tusked one, the one with the large belly, the Lord of Lords. Salutations to the helper of the Asuras, the giver of boons, the destroyer of the Asuras. Salutations to the Lord of the Brahmins, the speaker of Brahman, the embodiment of Brahman, the knower of the Vedas. Salutations to you who are beyond mind and speech, yet are the essence of mind and speech, the Lord of Yoga, the supreme Lord, free from all afflictions. How can we praise you, O Lord of Ganas, when even the Vedas and the greatest Yogis bow to you?

Asita continued: Being praised thus, the Lord of Ganas said to us: "O sages, ask for a boon; I will grant it, bound by your devotion. The hymn you have composed is dear to me; it grants all success and bestows my devotion." When asked, we said to the Elephant-faced Lord: "O Lord of Ganas, grant us supreme and firm devotion to your feet. Grant us the status of being dear to you. We seek no other boon, O Lord." Saying "So be it," Ganesha disappeared. We then worshipped the Lord of Ganas with devotion.

Thus, O son, I have told you the knowledge of Ganapati. Become self-controlled and worship Him. Having said this, Asita gave the single-syllable mantra to Naidhruva. Then, the Yogi Asita, who knew the supreme truth, disappeared. Naidhruva worshipped Ganesha with great effort and performed penance.

Chapter 11 : Description of the Brahman of Lambodara

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Naidhruva, following the path of yoga and devoted to tranquility and self-control, gradually attained peace and became worthy of reverence even by the yogis. Thereafter, he did not let go of the Lord of the Ganas; he performed intense penance, becoming devoted to service, solely to please the Lord of the Ganas. Knowing of his penance, the Elephant-faced Lord went to him. Seeing Him, Naidhruva stood up immediately and bowed with folded hands. With tears in his eyes and hair standing on end due to devotion, the illustrious sage worshipped Him and praised the Lord with Ganesha hymns. Pleased, the Lord of the Ganas granted him firm devotion, made him dear to the Gana community, and then disappeared. From that day on, the yogi Naidhruva became a devotee of the Ganas and is now honored everywhere for worshipping the Lord of the Ganas.

O Daksha, I have thus narrated to you this supreme story that grants everything; the greatness of Lambodara bestows all peace. I have also told you the story of Krodhasura; there is no doubt that listening to it brings peace from anger. In all their respective sections and across the western horizons, the sages have established Lambodara, the Elephant-faced Lord. By merely seeing Him, one obtains the desired fruits, especially by visiting Him on the seashore where He is mounted on a mouse.

Thus, the aspects of Lambodara are remembered as infinite and diverse, always present to protect the dharma of the people. O noble one, even a croreth part of their stories cannot be narrated by beings like Brahma; who am I, a human-formed being, to do so? Long ago, He was requested by them for the sake of the world's creation, and assuming an incarnation of power, He became the bestower of all purposes. Long ago, He was requested by Maya to destroy Mahishasura, and the King of Obstacles himself became the protector of dharma. To destroy the creators of illusion, He became the son of Shesha; Lambodara himself, mounted on a mouse, is remembered as such.

The Lord of the Ganas was worshipped by Savarni Manu, and there He became Lambodara, the Shakti Vinayaka. The Vedas describe various forms of Brahman to the Creator, and the world became diverse for the sake of His play. Remaining in His own blissful state within them, He is the holder of Brahman; through the yoga of non-dual union, He is remembered as Lambodara. O Creator, through the samadhi of non-dual union, the diverse world-Brahman is born and destroyed within Him. Their distinctions were created, maintained, and withdrawn by Ganesha; thus, He is called the speaker of Brahman in the Vedas.

The distinctions are of an unreal nature, and their Brahman is what is spoken of; know that power to be Lambodara, the Elephant-faced Lord himself. Since all things arise from His belly and return there at the end, He is called Lambodara, the essence of the non-existent. O Creator, I have narrated this story of Lambodara to you; listening to or reading it grants all desires and is accepted by all. O Creator, what else do you wish to hear? Tell me, and knowing the nature of the Lord of the Ganas, I shall tell you more.

Thus ends the eleventh chapter of the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which is the Upanishad of the end, titled The Description of the Brahman of Lambodara.

Chapter 12 : Description of the Incarnation of the Form of Shakti

Shounaka said: O Sage, you know the glory of Lambodara, which bestows happiness and from whose lotus-like face the nectar of peace flows. I have heard the supreme glory of Lambodara which destroys anger, and my desire to hear more is growing with devotion. Therefore, O fortunate one, tell me again the story of Vighnesha, which was heard by Daksha from Mudgala, the ocean of Yoga.

Suta said: O Sage Shounaka, you are blessed. Listen to the nectar-like story, which was heard by Daksha and narrated by Mudgala. Upon hearing the story of Lambodara, Prajapati Daksha was thrilled, and bowing to the sage Mudgala, he spoke beneficial words.

Daksha said: You are blessed, O Mudgala, you are worthy of worship by yogis, there is no doubt. You tell the story that bestows welfare and increases joy. I am never satisfied even after hearing this nectar-like story again and again. Now, O King of Yogis, tell me the story of Ganapati again. How did this Lambodara become the form of Shakti? Tell me his main character, which grants all desires. How did the Lord, worshipped by Shakti for the sake of slaying the demon Mahisha, manifest in that form? Tell me that. How did the son of Shesha become the Lord of Ganas in person? Why is he remembered as riding a mouse for the destruction of Maya? How was he worshipped by Savarni Manu? How did the incarnation of Shakti-Ganesha come to be? O ocean of compassion, tell me all this in detail. O Lord, the incarnations of Lambodara are blissful to hear. Tell me more, O King of Yogis, about the nature of Ganapatya, especially regarding initiation and what kind of Ganesha is remembered. How is the worship of Ganapati performed by people? In what manner is the mantra received from the mouth of the Guru? Tell me the path of Ganapatya with its rules in detail, so that I may become a Ganapatya with proper initiation. O Sage, you are omniscient, there is no doubt. By great fortune, I have had your vision; the wonder of your feet is the cause of all satisfaction.

Suta said: Being asked thus by Daksha, the great yogi who is dear to Ganapati spoke to the Lord of the people, pleased by his devotion.

Mudgala said: You are blessed and accomplished, O Daksha, there is no doubt, because you have such wonderful love for Ganapati. Seeing your devotion, I will tell you about Ganesha, which is purifying and grants everything to the speaker, the listener, and the inquirer. In the beginning, what was called Brahman remained in Brahman itself. His own will arose there, which became His Maya. There is no doubt that everything became twofold by His will. Listen to how it arose from Himself for the sake of Yoga. I have described that which is always established in its own bliss and devoid of various illusions as the "rising from the other." O venerable one, see in the Vedas that the Brahman which is full of will and plays with Maya is called "rising from itself." The Brahman that rose from itself created duality. From that duality, it became two-fold due to the nature of being one and many. One was the form of the deity, and the other was the body with many forms. From these two, this entire moving and non-moving world was created. All these Brahmani-Ganas, due to the lack of power of action, were unable to perform their own tasks, and thus they became engaged in penance. The supreme Lords chanted the mantra, "Salutations to You, who are the form of the self of all." After a thousand divine years, the Lord of Ganas, the Lord who is in the form of a man and an elephant, became pleased and manifested in their hearts. Seeing Him, the Brahmani-Ganas became full of knowledge. Then, being meditative, they worshipped Him who was established in their hearts. O Prajapati, He manifested before them in person, having assumed the form of Shakti, pleased and the bestower of boons. The Goddess, possessing four arms and all limbs, burdened by the weight of her breasts, shone as the Supreme Soul. Seeing Her, all were amazed and asked Her, who is devoted to their welfare: "Who are You? Why have You come here? Tell us everything, O Supreme One."

The Goddess said: I am Ganesha, O fortunate ones, whom you meditate upon with penance. Pleased by your penance, I have come as the bestower of boons. Having said this, that supreme and wonderful Shakti assumed the form with an elephant face before their very eyes. Seeing Lambodara, they were amazed and began to praise Him, and then He assumed the female form again. Seeing Her, the Brahmani-Ganas were amazed and devoted to Ganesha. By the grace of Ganesha, all became endowed with knowledge. Then, O Prajapati, they understood His actions through devotion. This Lambodara is Himself the form of Shakti, there is no doubt. The Shakti is Ganesha, and Ganesha is called Shakti. She is the direct mother of all, in the form of Lambodara. Then, bowing to Her, the Lord of the people and the Brahmani-Ganas praised Her with folded hands, especially with hair standing on end.

The Brahmani-Ganas said: Salutations to You, who are the form of Shakti and the essence of all actions. Salutations to You, O Mother of the world, who grants existence to all. Salutations again and again to You, who are infinite, who gives everything to everyone, who is the form of devotion, and who is the Great Maya. Salutations to You, O Vighnesha, who is always established in Your own bliss, who moves the world and Brahman, and who assumes various forms. Salutations to You, O Ganesha, who is immeasurable, the protector of devotees, the creator, the sustainer, and the destroyer. There is no doubt that the world and Brahman arose from Your womb. Salutations to Your womb, which is the form of creation. O Goddess, You always nourish by breastfeeding; salutations to Your breasts, which are the form of sustenance. At the end, at the time of dissolution, You devour everything with Your mouth; salutations to Your mouth, which is the form of the destroyer. You are beyond mind and speech, but out of love for Your devotees, You have assumed a body; salutations to You, the Lord of devotees. How can we praise You, O Great Maya, who bears the name of Shakti? Salutations to You, who are established everywhere in all forms of power. Your power is the essence of the Vedas and the yogis. Who is capable of praising You? Therefore, we bow to You.

Suta said: Praised thus, the Mother of the world, the mother of the Brahmani-Ganas, was pleased and spoke to them, satisfied by their devotion.

Shakti said: O Brahmani-Ganas, choose the boons you desire in your hearts. Pleased by your penance, devotion, and praise, I will grant them to you, O all-powerful ones. This praise of Me, composed by you, will grant all desires. It will grant devotion and all power, O fortunate ones. Whatever one desires, I will grant by the recitation of this praise. It will undoubtedly be the bestower of worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Shakti, the Brahmani-Ganas bowed to Her and praised the Supreme Mother of all with great joy.

The Brahmani-Ganas said: O Supreme Goddess, if You are granting boons, then grant us supreme devotion. O Shakti, grant us that by which delusion is destroyed, there is no doubt. O Great Goddess, what should be done? Grant us supreme power for that, O Mother, and we will do what is dear to You.

Suta said: Hearing their words, the bestower of all was satisfied and said, "So be it," and then She disappeared. Then, by the power of the boon, they created the moving and non-moving world, and they always play among them, granting their respective enjoyments. They sustain the worlds and destroy them, O Prajapati; there is no doubt that they remain in their own glory. O Daksha, this is the ancient incarnation of Lambodara, the form of Shakti, which grants happiness, that I have told you. The person who listens to this with devotion will have all their desires fulfilled. It makes one attain Brahman and is the supreme bestower of worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Mudgala, Daksha was happy and spoke to the sage again, full of doubt.

Daksha said: O Mudgala, you have told me the glory of the incarnation of Shakti. I have heard it, O fortunate one, yet please remove my doubt. O Yogi, the Brahmani-Ganas are without bodies, so how does penance and other things happen among them? O Lord, how do tears, praise, and worship occur among them? I am confused now.

Suta said: Hearing his words, the great sage, the crest jewel of all the wise, spoke to the Lord of the people.

Mudgala said: The Brahmani-Ganas are the essence of Brahman; there is no such thing as body and the embodied among them, this is the truth from the mouth of the Vedas. Yet, they are spoken of as having all forms, being devoid of bodily changes and yet having bodily forms. O Daksha, they are both devoid of and endowed with these two. Consider this in all the scriptures, O wise one. Therefore, everything is always possible among them, there is no doubt. Hear another word of mine that destroys all doubts. Who can say there is no Brahman-nature in Brahman? All their enjoyment, penance, etc., O wise one, how can it be described by the scriptures as if they had bodies? Know this, O Prajapati, for the sake of teaching the disciple. Do not create useless doubt or confusion there.

Suta said: Hearing the words of Mudgala, Daksha was happy and said to him, "My doubt is gone, O Lord, by your words." Now tell me the glory of Lambodara, O giver of respect. You are omniscient, so you do everything without doubt.

Thus ends the twelfth chapter of the Lambodara Charita in the fifth section of the Shrimad Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "Description of the Incarnation of the Form of Shakti."

Chapter 13 : The Bestowal of Power upon Shakti

Mudgala said: In ancient times, a cruel demon king named Mahishasura became the ruler of the world due to a boon granted by Brahma. Having conquered all the gods and the lords of the demons in the heavens, the foolish demon established himself as invincible in every way. He rendered the earth devoid of righteous deeds, and all the gods, being distressed, resorted to fasting and penance.

Thereupon, the gods and sages worshipped the Supreme Shakti, who is the embodiment of all, the dweller of her own bliss, and the manifest power. After a thousand years, the Goddess appeared, empowered by boons to slay Mahishasura. The gods prayed, "O Great Goddess, we have no fear from any man, but we fear Mahishasura. Protect us from this great terror. Destroy him for the happiness of all; we are your children, protect us from this great danger."

Saying "So be it," the Goddess, mounted on a lion and filled with divine wrath, set out to slay the demon. She went to the forest, assumed the form of a Mohini (enchantress), and sang a song filled with divine essence. A demon heard this and, upon seeing the Great Shakti, became infatuated. He went to the demon king and described her in detail, saying, "There is nothing in the universe equal to her in beauty and radiance. Recognize her as a jewel among women and take her to enjoy your kingdom."

Hearing this, the infatuated Mahishasura sent his chief minister to bring her with respect. The minister, seeing the enchanting Great Maya, became spellbound and said to her, "Mahisha is the supreme lord of the entire universe, deathless, ever-youthful, and glorious. I have been sent by him for your sake, O beautiful one. Become the wife of the great demon king."

Hearing this, the Goddess became filled with anger and spoke to the arrogant minister: "O great demon, how dare you speak such words to me? I shall slay you, a fool, for speaking thus." Hearing her words, the enraged demon king ordered his forces to bring her by force. When the great demons arrived from all sides, the glorious Goddess became filled with wrath. She burned them all with the fire of her anger. Seeing this, the minister fled in terror.

Upon hearing this dire news, the infatuated Mahishasura himself arrived with his army. Seeing her, he was struck by the arrows of desire and sent his ministers to capture her. The Goddess slew them all with her weapons. Then, filled with extreme rage, the demon himself entered the battlefield. The Goddess, full of anger, drew her bow and shot terrible arrows. Some demons died, some were severed, and others fainted.

Then, the enraged Mahishasura fought the Great Goddess with various weapons. A fierce and terrifying battle ensued, as both desired victory. For one year, the demon fought her. Finally, in a fit of rage, he hurled a mountain at her. Struck by the mountain, the Goddess fainted. As the demon moved to seize her, she became alert, saw the mighty demon, and, making herself invisible, went to the Himalayas.

There, filled with sorrow, she pondered, "Alas, I could have turned everything to ash in half a moment. There is no doubt that a demon would die at the hands of a woman. Yet, how did he defeat me and gain glory? Surely, this obstacle was created by Vighneshvara (Ganesha). I am deluded; I went to the battlefield without worshipping him first. I am the form of Shakti, and by my power, the whole world exists. Yet, I was defeated because I was devoid of his power. This is a great wonder. The power of the Lord of Obstacles is the only truth; everything else is an illusion. When the Lord of Ganas is pleased, the living beings become supreme. Brahma and all Shaktis are his forms; nothing is powerful without him."

Thinking thus, the sorrowful Goddess abandoned everything and performed severe penance, fasting completely. Reaching the Vindhya mountains, she meditated on Vighneshvara in her heart and chanted the supreme mantra. After a thousand years of severe penance, Ganesha was pleased and appeared before her. Seeing her reduced to mere skin and bones, the compassionate Ganesha awakened her.

Vighneshvara said: "Behold, O Shakti, I have come to you whom you meditate upon constantly. Ask for a boon; I will grant it, bound by your devotion. I am especially pleased by your devotion and penance. There is no doubt that I am under your control; behold me as the grantor of boons."

Hearing this, the Goddess realized the truth. She stood up, bowed to the Lord of Ganas, worshipped him with devotion, and praised him with folded hands.

Shakti said: "Salutations to you, O Vighneshvara, the destroyer of the obstacles of your devotees, and salutations to you who specifically create obstacles for the non-devotees. Salutations to Ganesha, the protector of the Ganas, the Supreme Soul, who exists everywhere in the form of qualities. Salutations to you, the wielder of immeasurable power and the mover of immeasurable Maya, who are yourself devoid of Maya and the giver of delusion to the deluded. Salutations to you, the giver of Brahma-knowledge, the Lord of Speech, the form of Brahma, and the beloved Heramba. Salutations to you, the giver of the fruits of actions, the sustainer of actions, the form of Karma-yoga, and the Supreme Lord. Salutations to you, the form of knowledge, the holder of knowledge for the wise, and the yoga of knowledge for those devoid of it. Salutations to you, who are equal, the giver of equality, the source of bliss, and the one beyond all. Salutations to you, who are natural, devoid of artificiality, the soul of the natural, and the yoga of the natural, the one free from delusion. Salutations to you, the Lord of all, who are the bliss of the self and the embodiment of self-bliss, yet associated with Maya. Salutations to you, the Lord of the universe, who are beyond yoga and the holder of renunciation. Salutations to you, O Lambodara, the form of peace and yoga, the giver of yoga, and the wish-fulfilling gem (Chintamani) who is beyond the realm of the mind. O Lord, forgive my offense; deluded by forgetfulness, I went to fight the supreme without worshipping you first. Now, grant me the boon of your grace and supreme devotion, by which I have pleased you. Grant me victory for the slaying of Mahishasura. Salutations to you, O Supreme Lord."

As she praised him with devotion, she began to dance with tears in her eyes. Lambodara, seeing her state, said, "O Goddess, whatever you have asked for shall be fulfilled. This hymn you have composed will grant the essence of devotion and will be the reason for my forgiveness of your offense. I shall grant both worldly enjoyment and liberation to those who praise me with this hymn. Whatever you desire, I shall surely grant."

Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahma, disappeared. The Goddess, though still feeling a sense of regret, remained there in meditation. She installed an idol, worshipped it with the help of Vedic Brahmins, and then went to fight. From that day on, that place of the Lord of Ganas became famous on earth as a place that grants all perfections and fulfills the journeys of people. It grants the state of Brahma and is always dear to Vighneshvara.

The Goddess, by the power of the boon, slew Mahishasura after a fierce battle, desiring the protection of the world. Later, she returned to the domain of Vighneshvara and worshipped the Lord of Ganas daily with great devotion. Whoever hears or reads this supreme character of Lambodara with devotion shall have all their desires fulfilled.

Chapter 14 : The Humbling of Shiva and Vishnu

Mudgala said: Listen further, O fortunate one, to the character of Lambodara, which is known as the nature dependent on Shakti and is the bestower of all accomplishments. Once, all the gods gathered in the great region of the Vindhya mountains to visit Ganapati. There, a debate arose in the presence of all the sages: "The five gods are considered equal, whether with attributes or without. How did Vighneshwara become the god worthy of worship by all? Why is he to be worshipped first by all four of us? Even the devotees of other gods must worship Gajanana first; why is this reversal? Shiva, Vishnu, and other gods are consumers of Ganesha’s remnants; why is it said in the Vedas that he is the first to be worshipped? If the four gods are equal, then Ganesha should be worshipped according to the rites of one’s own deity. If that were the case, everything would be consistent with one's own path. How is Ganesha accepted as the first to be worshipped in all auspicious and inauspicious acts?"

As this dispute arose, Shakti, filled with anger, spoke to all those sages and gods for their own benefit. Shakti said: "This Lambodara is the only one spoken of in the Vedas. We are not like him, O sages. The entire universe is born from his belly, there is no doubt. Brahma and Shiva also reside within him at the end. In the middle, the Lord of the Ganas plays in the form of a partial manifestation. Therefore, he is the first to be worshipped and the god worthy of worship by all. He is the father and mother of all, and the bestower of everything. Why do you doubt him, the Lord of Speech?"

Hearing her words, Shiva and Vishnu, filled with anger, spoke to the Mother of the World. Shiva and Vishnu said: "O Shakti, why do you speak with such affection and devotion to Ganesha? We five are equal; there is no superiority among us. We regard each other as equals, O Goddess, there is no doubt. Why do you boast of Ganesha’s superiority? If Lambodara is the only god, and we are all born from his belly, and we dissolve into him at the end, then tell us, how do we create, sustain, and destroy the world? Even so, we are not Lambodara according to the Vedas. Therefore, we shall see what great power Lambodara possesses in your presence, and only then will we worship him. If we see his superiority, we will make him the first to be worshipped by all; otherwise, O Goddess, it is certain we will not."

Then Shiva and Vishnu, having resolved this, prepared an immense offering and sat down to worship with true intent. Having invoked the Lord of the Ganas at the same time, they worshipped him with great devotion, though it was a form of deception. Ganapati appeared there in person. Seeing the immense offering, he was astonished in his heart. Through meditation, Vighnesha understood what was in their hearts and, having drawn out the power of their true intent, he remained there. Deprived of their power, Vishnu and Shiva continued the worship with joy and offered the supreme food to the Lord of the Ganas. Ganapati consumed the immense offering in a single mouthful and said, "I am especially hungry."

Hearing his words, Shiva and Vishnu, the lords, tried to manifest more food through their true intent, but even then, the offering did not become even the size of an atom. Filled with sorrow, they said to each other: "Where has the power of our true intent gone? We have become like ordinary mortals; what will happen to the gods? The great divine power of Brahma and Ishvara, known as the power of true intent—we are deprived of it and are considered mere mortals. What shall we do now? We are like living beings, no doubt. This Lambodara will cast us into hell in his anger. Or perhaps, death has surely come to the mortal world. To whom shall we go for refuge, being masters of deception?"

"Previously, we were addressed by Shakti, but we were filled with ignorance and created a great obstacle by arguing. Now, the best refuge will be the one who is auspicious. Ganesha is indeed the Lord of Brahma." Then, Gajanana, filled with anger, appeared in a wonderful form to consume them. Seeing him, they ran in terror, reached the feet of Shakti, and fell down, trembling with fear. Then she comforted them, took a blade of Durva grass, and went to him. Bowing to the Lord of the Ganas, the Goddess, with hair standing on end and neck bowed in devotion, spoke with folded hands.

Shakti said: "Forgive this terrible offense, O God of Gods, I bow to you. Shiva and Vishnu, who were filled with the pride of your Maya, were born from your belly. Do not punish Shiva and Vishnu, O Ganesha. You are capable of sustaining everyone, O Lord; you always perform the sustenance of all." Having said this, the Great Maya gave him a blade of Durva grass and mentally imagined the four types of excellent food. Then, the Lord of the Ganas was satisfied with just the blade of Durva grass and bestowed great devotion upon Shakti, for he is fond of his devotees.

Seeing the Lord of the Ganas pleased, Hari and Shiva came there, bowed, and spoke: "Forgive our terrible offense, O Lord of the Ganas, especially since we were deluded by your Maya, O bestower of eternal bliss. Now we have known your true form, O Great Lord. You alone are the one to be worshipped first and the one worthy of worship by all." Having said this, the two great lords praised Ganesha. He was satisfied by their devotion. Requested by Shakti with a heart full of emotion, he restored Shiva and Vishnu to their former state. They became endowed with the power of their intent again and always worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with devotion. The gods and sages became free from doubt and worshipped Dhundhi with pure hearts. There is nothing beyond Ganesha; therefore, he is the Lord of the Ganas first, and there is no doubt that he is the same at the end, while in the middle he is remembered in various forms. He is the seed of all, and therefore he is the first to be worshipped. Ganesha is declared in the Vedas as the one worthy of worship by all. The sages became pure in their nature of devotion to Ganesha and worshipped him wherever they were. Whoever hears or reads this character of Lambodara, Vighnesha will become the bestower of everything to him.

Chapter 15 : The Glory of Shakti Vinayaka

Mudgala said: A royal sage named Suratha, oppressed by his powerful and unrighteous ministers, left his kingdom and went to the forest. There, instructed by a great sage, he worshipped Shakti. By her grace, the king became powerful. Having regained his kingdom, the royal sage ruled righteously and worshipped Shakti with a heart full of devotion. Later, he died and was reborn as Savarni, the son of the Sun, a Manu, wise and respected by all. Due to his previous impressions, he became a devotee of Shakti. Going to the forest, he worshipped the Great Shakti daily. Fasting, he performed excellent penance, meditating on the Great Maya in his heart and chanting the nine-syllable mantra. He read the greatness of Shakti daily with devotion. After a hundred years had passed, the Great Goddess was pleased.

Knowing that he was endowed with penance and devoted to the path of knowledge, Lambodara came there as the bestower of boons. The Lord of the Ganas appeared in the meditation that was in his heart, glowing with a red hue. Seeing him, the Manu was astonished. Then he saw the form of Shakti, and again the Lord Lambodara, and again the Lord endowed with Shakti. He saw Shakti standing on his left side, full of grace and devotion to Ganesha, and he was greatly amazed. By the grace of Shakti and the birth of Lambodara, that great knowledge became manifest in the heart of Manu.

Then Manu, filled with devotion, thought deeply: "Lambodara is Shakti himself, and Shakti is the soul of Lambodara. There is never any difference between Shakti and Lambodara. Shakti is born from the left side, and the Lord of the Ganas from the right. The Brahman known as eternal bliss is the non-dual essence of both. For the sake of play and the protection of devotees, they have taken these forms; there is no doubt that this is how all this appeared." As the powerful Manu was thinking this, both of them appeared before him to grant him boons.

Seeing them, the king was astonished and wondered where the Goddess and the Lord of Obstacles had gone, but through the power of meditation, he realized it. Then they said to him, "Ask for a boon, O virtuous one. We are pleased with your penance and devotion." Hearing their words, Manu opened his eyes and saw them. Having worshipped them according to the rites, he praised them with devotion. Bowing to both gods, he spoke with folded hands.

Savarni said: "Salutations to you, O Lord of the Ganas, salutations to you, O Protector of the Ganas. Salutations to you, the four-armed one. Salutations to the beginningless one, the Supreme Lord, the Supreme Soul. Salutations to you, the remover of the obstacles of devotees. Salutations to Heramba, salutations to you again and again. Salutations to you, the form of Lambodara. Salutations to you, the one who takes many forms. Salutations to the Great Goddess, salutations to the Lord of the Gods. Salutations to you, the elephant-faced one. Salutations to you, the one with winnowing-basket ears. Salutations to you, the single-tusked one. Salutations to the remover of the delusion of Maya. Salutations to you, the dweller of the city of eternal bliss. Salutations to you, the bearer of the axe and other weapons. Salutations to you, the awakener of all. Salutations to you, the giver of delusion to the deluded. Salutations to you, the King of Dhundhi. Salutations to you, the curved-trunk one. Salutations to you, the one worthy of worship by all. Salutations to the one who performs infinite plays. Salutations to the Great Shakti, salutations to you, the bearer of Shakti. Salutations to you, the one who creates many differences. Salutations to the Supreme Lord. The Shakti that exists is the same as Vighnesha; there is no difference anywhere. Due to ignorance, the distinction of male and female appears. Who am I, with my limited knowledge, to praise you, whom even the Vedas, yogis, and lords of gods wonder at? By your vision, I have been enlightened. Please grant me great yoga now, and grant me firm devotion to you."

Having said this, he fell to the ground like a rod at their feet. Both of them, filled with joy, spoke to the king. Shakti and Vinayaka said: "O fortunate one, you shall attain the yoga of peace through the practice of yoga, and you shall have firm devotion to our feet, O son of the Sun. After enjoying the full Manvantara and establishing your fame in the three worlds, you will become one with the eternal bliss and attain Brahman at the end. Whoever reads or hears this hymn composed by you will attain all accomplishments, there is no doubt."

Having said this, the Lords of the Ganas disappeared. Manu returned home and worshipped them with emotion. After enjoying the Manvantara, he became one with Brahman. He became a famous devotee of Ganesha. I have told you of the incarnation named Shakti Vinayaka, who is the bestower of all accomplishments and is complete through devotion to Lambodara. To those deluded by Maya, Shakti appears different, but O Daksha, that Shakti is Lambodara himself, there is no doubt. The Maya of male and female forms should be abandoned through the practice of yoga. Then Lambodara, the Lord of Brahma, is attained by yogis. Some call him Shakti, others call him the Supreme Lambodara. Know that the difference is only for the sake of the devotee's own preference, there is no doubt. The Shakti, who is the form of eternal bliss, is described in the Vedas, and he is called Lambodara, the bearer of Ganesha's incarnation. Whoever hears or reads this character of Shakti Vinayaka will undoubtedly attain worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Chapter 16 : The Description of Shesha’s Great Suffering

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: I shall narrate the greatness of the son of Shesha briefly. Listen to it, O Lord of subjects, for it bestows yogic peace. Once, Brahma, residing in Satyaloka, was absorbed in meditation, having become bewildered himself for the welfare of all beings. He wondered how his creation could always remain unbroken. From his own breath, a man emerged. He bowed to the Creator and praised him with various hymns. Brahma then said to him, "Tell me quickly what you desire." Hearing the words of Brahma, the man became joyful. With his neck bowed in devotion, he spoke to the Lord of subjects.

The man said: O Lord, protect me, your son born from your breath. Grant me a name, a place, and various kinds of food. Grant me everything else that brings happiness; I bow to you. You are the refuge of all beings, and especially mine now. Hearing his words, Brahma became filled with affection. He embraced him and kissed him, being fond of his son.

Brahma said: O wise one, you have grown just by the sight of my illusion (Maya). Therefore, your name shall be famous as Mayakara (the maker of illusion). Whatever you desire will be fulfilled for you. You shall dwell in the three worlds, and your movement shall be unobstructed. Everything born of the four categories of life shall always be under your control. You shall never meet death from any of the four types of beings. You shall undoubtedly conquer everything in battle. You shall always be endowed with health and other blessings, O my son.

Hearing the words of Brahma, Mayakara became joyful. Having bowed to him, he went away, wandering as he pleased. Then, the chief of the demons, the powerful Viprachitti, seeing him endowed with strength, approached and bowed to him. Knowing the story of Mayakara, the wicked-minded and powerful Viprachitti spoke to him again.

Viprachitti said: O fortunate Mayakara, listen to my supreme advice. You have obtained boons that are difficult to attain; there is no doubt about this. O wise one, you must enjoy these boons daily. Only then do these boons become beautiful, otherwise not, O Lord. Just as a woman endowed with beauty and youth, when united with her husband, shines greatly when enjoyed daily, O giver of honor, so too, when various enjoyments are met and experienced, it becomes meaningful. If left unused, they always curse the person. Therefore, O fortunate one, utilize these boons specifically. Otherwise, the boons will curse you for lack of enjoyment. We, the chiefs of the demons, shall be under your command. Enjoy pleasures with us, who are endowed with strength, O mighty one. The demons, endowed with strength, are unconquerable in every way. They will act according to your command, O Lord; there is no doubt.

Thus, Mayakara, being instructed by him, became wicked-minded. He became subject to him and went to the netherworld (Rasatala). There, the son of Brahma was honored by the chiefs of the demons, who were like Yama, and Mayakara was pleased. Then, the demons brought Shukra, and with respect, they had him perform the coronation of the son of Brahma, who was anointed by Brahmins. The son of Brahma became the great king of the demons. Mayakara shone brightly, becoming a source of fear to all. Enjoying various pleasures, he became proud of his strength. The lord of the demons considered himself to have achieved his purpose. Then, having conquered the earth along with the oceans, he defeated the gods and sat on the throne of Indra. Then, all the demons served him specifically. Having become the enjoyers of heaven, they were joyful with their kin, O Lord.

Then, he approached Shiva and became ready for battle. Leaving Kailasa, Shankar himself fled. Then, the demon lord shone, having gone to Vaikuntha. Leaving Vaikuntha, Vishnu also fled in fear. Then, seeing him as a peace-breaker, the demons said to him again: "Conquer Brahma, who resides in Satyaloka, the leader of the gods. He is the giver of everything to the gods, a partisan, and the grandfather. He is the destroyer of the demons; there is no one equal to him. Taking refuge in him, Mahadeva and others have remained. Given time, they will surely kill us with great speed. You have conquered everything, O King, yet look at this behavior. You have never been honored by Brahma, the best of messengers."

Mayakara, the demon, having heard their words, considered the truth and became ready to conquer the Creator. Seeing him arrived at Brahmaloka, Brahma, surrounded by the host of gods, fled, filled with grief. Then, he (Mayakara) shone in Satyaloka immediately. Honoring the demons, the demon enjoyed happiness with them. Then, he placed his own people in Satyaloka, Kailasa, the abode of Vishnu, the solar realm, the Shakta realm, and the realms of other gods. Returning to the earth, the great demon, surrounded by his kin, shone in his own city named Prapancha. Then, the demon sent the chiefs of the demons to conquer the netherworld, and they all went together. By them, the serpent kings were defeated, and Shesha went to them for refuge. Accepting the burden of taxes, he remained surrounded by the serpent lords. Even then, the demons, who were sources of fear, remained there. They oppressed the serpents, being strong like the time of death.

Then, Shesha, extremely distressed, remembered the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) for the sake of removing obstacles and for the sake of his son, with respect. By meditation, the serpent lord pleased the Lord of obstacles. Then, after a full year had passed, the elephant-faced Lord appeared. Emerging from his heart, he said to him: "Look, O fortunate Shesha, at me, your son born of meditation."

Daksha said: How did Heramba become his son with such little effort? Tell me, O great yogi, the great character of Shesha.

Thus ends the sixteenth chapter of the fifth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled The Description of Shesha’s Great Suffering.

Chapter 17 : The Description of the Instruction of Shesha’s Yoga

Mudgala said: In ancient times, the sage Kashyapa and his wife, desiring a son, worshipped Shiva and Vishnu with intense penance, mantra meditation, and samadhi. After ten years, both deities appeared before him, granted the boon of a son, and departed. Through their combined essence, Shesha was born as his son. Being the supporter of the earth, he was Vishnu himself in the form of a protector. At the end of Brahma’s day, this powerful great serpent, who also possessed the essence of Shankara, burned the vast universe with his flames.

He is known as the thousand-headed Purusha, Shesha. He is deathless, infinite, and the destroyer of all. Because he remains after the dissolution of the universe, he is called Shesha. He supported the cosmic egg and became the foundation of all. Shesha was born as the direct embodiment of Hari and Hara. Brahma came and anointed him to the task of ruling. Having become the lord of the Nagas, he was accepted everywhere. Then, bowing to Kashyapa, he spoke these beneficial words.

Shesha said: O Lord, how shall I support this vast and complex universe? O great yogi, my father and master, instruct me as your disciple for this purpose.

Then, the best of sages, having purified the six-syllable mantra of Ganesha according to the scriptures, gave it to him. Shesha, having bowed to him, performed excellent penance. He went to the forest, meditated on Ganesha, and became absorbed in chanting. He pleased the Lord of Obstacles daily by fasting and worshipped him with devotion, praising him with various hymns. Thus, after a thousand years, Ganesha was pleased and appeared before him, finding him reduced to mere bones and skin. Seeing him, the king of Nagas, Shesha, rose with respect, bowed, and worshipped him with folded hands, praising him with joy.

Then, the Lord of the Ganas said: Choose what you desire. Pleased by your penance, I shall grant whatever is in your heart.

Shesha, bowing to the Lord of Obstacles, said with joy: O Lord, grant me the strength to support the universe, I bow to you. Grant me devotion that bestows both worldly enjoyment and liberation, O Lord of the Ganas. Make me deathless, a lord of the directions, and beyond all fame.

Saying "So be it," the Lord of the Ganas disappeared. Shesha, filled with joy, returned to his own abode. From that day on, the serpent himself became the supporter of the earth. He attained the status of a guardian of the directions and became respected and the granter of all desires. Then, through the power of yoga, Shesha, skilled in various illusions, assumed many forms and shone throughout the universe.

After a long time, fearing Hiranyakashipu and considering worldly wealth to be perishable, he became intent on seeking knowledge. Having performed vast penance, he became self-controlled and established in the state of yoga, turning his focus inward. Gradually, the lord of the Nagas became filled with supreme bliss. Seeing peace through yoga, he remained established in it. Recognizing Vishnu as the supreme reality, he became self-restrained and worshipped Keshava through the Vaishnava path.

Then, the boon-giving Vishnu, praised by Shesha, appeared. Shesha requested: "O Keshava, grant me your vision daily. May you reside in my body forever, O Janardana." Saying "So be it," Narayana became one with him. He remained forever in the body of the serpent, and the protector of the Nagas became extremely delighted. Through the power of yoga, he perceived the illusion of the world, and despite being beyond duality, he experienced the bliss of the dual state.

After a long time, he became devoted to the path of yoga. Through samadhi, he became naturally established in Brahman. Recognizing the natural state of Shankara, he became devoted to the Shaiva path. Seeing Brahman as the embodiment of free will, he attained peace and remained established. Then, the boon-giving Shambhu appeared, and Shesha, having bowed, praised Sadashiva with Shaiva hymns. Seeing the pleased Shankara, he prayed for supreme devotion and the constant companionship of Shankara for the sake of divine vision.

Saying "So be it," Lord Shankara bound him to his head, fulfilling his desire for constant vision. Then, the infinitely joyful Shesha, devoted to the path of Shambhu, served the bull-bannered Shankara with deep emotion. Then, through the power of yoga, Shesha realized that supreme natural state, and knowing it to be free from delusion, he became devoid of all restlessness. In his mind, he considered the Brahman of Shankara to be the supreme Lord. Bowing to Shiva, the great serpent asked about the peace of yoga.

Knowing Shesha to be a worthy vessel for the yoga of Ganesha, Sadashiva spoke the knowledge of the Lord of the Ganas, which is beneficial to all.

Shiva said: Know Ganesha, O respected one, to be the one who grants the peace of yoga. He is the embodiment of truth and non-truth, combined with the natural state. O wise one, that in which the universes dissolve through union is called the supreme peace-giving Brahman, which is self-evident. Through the union with the Lord of the Ganas, one attains that supreme self-evident vision, by which one becomes a dweller in self-bliss.

The state of total union is considered a great flaw; therefore, it is never considered the complete peace-giving state by yogis. In the Brahman that is yoga-oriented but devoid of self-realization, one cannot attain that vision through separation. When people attain cessation through non-duality, that is called peace, but the wise do not consider it the primary state. The flaw of being non-yogic always persists. The Lord of the Ganas remains through the influence of being devoid of illusion.

The yoga that exists in the union of union and non-union is the one that grants peace. That peace is accepted by all according to the Vedas. The 'Ga' represents union, and the 'Na' represents the one who goes into yoga. O wise one, see in the Vedas that Ganesha is the Lord of both. The mind is composed of five layers, and it is the same that dwells in the intellect. Delusion is the creator of confusion; the wise consider the realization of this to be true success. The master of both is Ganesha, who is called Chintamani. He is the direct illuminator of the mind and the giver of peace through the service of yoga.

Worship him, O great serpent, and you shall attain peace. Otherwise, no man can attain peace even in ten million years.

Having said this, the great Lord Mahadeva became silent. Shesha Naga bowed to him and went to the supreme forest of penance.

Chapter 18 : The Description of the Story of Shesha

Mudgala said: Then Shesha himself performed excellent penance there. Becoming devoted to tranquility and self-control, he purified the planes of Yoga. Gradually, having reached the end of the five planes of the mind and having attained peace, the great serpent worshipped the Lord of the Ganas. With the nature of a devotee of Ganesha, he worshipped Him with devotion. Pleased by the power of such penance, Ganesha, the elephant-faced Lord, appeared before him. He approached his great devotee, whose eyes were stilled in meditation, and said, "Ask of me boons that are very difficult to obtain." Hearing His words, the awakened serpent saw the elephant-faced Lord, Lambodara, and bowed down with folded hands. Having bowed to the Lord of Obstacles, he worshipped Him again with devotion and, remaining steady with joined palms, prayed.

Shesha said: Salutations to You, O Ganesha, salutations to the Lord of Success and Wisdom. Salutations again and again to Chintamani, the destroyer of great obstacles. Salutations to You, who are the form of the one worshipped at the beginning of all, and who are worthy of worship by all. Salutations to the King of the Elders, the Mother and Father of all elders. Salutations to Vinayaka, the leader of all, and to You who are served by all leaders. Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, who causes the downfall of the positions of those who are not devoted, and who destroys the obstacles of those who are truly devoted. Salutations to Dhundhiraja, who is sought by all, the giver of great success, the Supreme Lord of those who seek. Salutations to You, the Lord of the Vedas, the giver of knowledge to the wise, the giver of happiness to the beings, and the form of peace. Salutations to You who dwell in Svananda, who are always the embodiment of self-bliss, and who are the performer of various pastimes. Salutations to You, the form of the thief, who has a mouse as a vehicle, whose form is dear to the mouse, and who has a mouse on Your banner. Salutations to You, who are beyond Yoga, O Brahman, the embodiment of Brahman, and to You, O Heramba, who are free from Maya and established in non-meditation. Salutations to Ganesha, who is Yoga, the Lord of Yoga, the giver of Yoga to the yogis, and who is devoid of the fluctuations of the mind.

How can I praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, who are beyond mind and speech? O Lord, You exist as the one devoid of both mind and speech. Therefore, the Vedas and others are undoubtedly baffled by You. I see You now by good fortune, O Lord, whom even yogis like Shuka strive to see. As he praised Him thus, Shesha, born of the essence of devotion, danced with hair standing on end and tears in his eyes. The Lord of the Ganas then said to that best of devotees, "Ask for boons, O Shesha, I will grant them, moved by your devotion. This hymn composed by you shall grant the peace of Yoga. It shall be the giver of Dharma, wealth, desire, and liberation, and the granter of all successes. I will grant whatever is desired through the recitation of this hymn, and there is no doubt that hearing it will increase devotion to Me."

Then the King of Serpents, longing for devotion, bowed to the Lord of Obstacles and spoke these beneficial words. Shesha said: Grant me supreme devotion to Your lotus feet, O Lord of the Ganas. O Lord of the Ganas, make me always established in extreme peace. The mind is said to be of five types; dwell therein always, O Lord, in the form of radiance, destroying my delusions. Since the mind is fickle and attached to worldly matters, there is no doubt it will be so; therefore, become my son. I will worship You constantly as a son in this world, and in my heart, I will worship You as my family deity with peace. By Your grace, let this place be a Ganesha-kshetra, a granter of all success and peace to the devotees, O Lord. Seeing me as the supporter of the mind, O Elephant-faced Lord, people will call You 'Dharanidhara' (the supporter of the earth), O Lord. Since I am indeed Your form, O Leader of the Ganas, there is no doubt about it; therefore, by my name, You become Dharanidhara. I am called Dharadhara, the bearer of the gross word, and You, O Lord, are Dharanidhara through Yoga, dwelling in the plane of the mind.

Having said this to the Lord of Ganesha, the son of Kashyapa became silent. The Lord of the Ganas, pleased and fond of His devotees, said to him: Everything you have said, O Shesha, will be fruitful. You will certainly worship Me, always established in the form of peace. You will be the creator of the science of Yoga, O respectful one. No one will ever be equal to you in Yoga, O wise one. Always be a devotee of Mine; dwell in My navel, O Dharadhara. Worship Me especially, free from delusion by nature. The fatigue and suffering of bearing the earth will never be yours, O Shesha; it will be as small as a mustard seed. Vishnu, the Lord of the Universe, will always dwell in your body with reverence, and even He, the Lord of the Universe, will be like a flower to you. You will undoubtedly have a share in all sacrifices. You will be a guardian of the directions, O fortunate one, and undoubtedly respected by all.

Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas showed His various forms to Shesha, the supreme possessor of various Yogas. The King of Serpents saw the collective and individual forms, the form of sound, the point, the 'I am', the knowledge, and the bodiless form. Shesha was amazed to see His own form, which is beyond Yoga and full of the peace of Yoga, and he became free from doubt. The Lord of Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, immediately disappeared. Shesha installed the Lord of Ganesha there with the help of Brahmins. The Lord of Obstacles, Dharanidhara, remained there, granting success to devotees merely by their pilgrimage. Shesha went to his abode, Daksha, filled with joy. The King of Serpents himself dwells in the navel of the Lord of Obstacles. He worshipped the Lord of the Ganas with exclusive devotion. Then, the incarnation of Vyasa, the protector of Dharma, occurred. The sage went to the refuge of Brahma and heard about Ganesha. He worshipped the Lord with penance and the Puranas. Pleased, the Lord of the Ganas granted him a boon and departed. He respectfully divided the branches of the Vedas.

Then, after a long time, due to the delusion of Maya, the yogi became a 'yoga-bhrashta' (one who has fallen from Yoga) due to the fickleness of the mind. Once, while Vyasa was performing his daily libations in the river water, Shesha, in a subtle form, fell into his cupped hands. Seeing him, Vyasa was amazed and, out of fear, dropped him into the water. The serpent, the size of a thumb, spoke to the sage. The serpent said: O Lord, there are fierce creatures in the water who will devour me. Protect me, for I have come to your refuge, O fortunate one. Hearing this, the famous Vyasa was amazed, bowed to him, and spoke words beneficial to himself with joy. Vyasa said: Who are you, O fortunate one, who have come into my hands? You speak like a human; I do not know your nature. Hearing his words, Patanjali spoke to him, delighting him with meaningful words. If contemplation is abandoned, where does it go? O tiger among sages, you ask out of Maya, O Lord. By Maya, one in the form of a serpent speaks like a human; there is no doubt that nothing is difficult for Her. Hearing his words, the sage bowed to him again and asked, "Show me your true form, salutations to you."

Then, the Lord himself appeared in human form. Shesha, in the form of a Brahmin, spoke to the great sage. Patanjali said: O fortunate one, I have fallen into your hands for your benefit. Know me as Patanjali, O sage, Shesha, the supporter of the earth. Know that I have come suddenly, inspired by Ganesha, to destroy your delusion, O best of Brahmins. Knowing this, Vyasa was overjoyed, bowed to him, and said: O Lord, how should contemplation be abandoned by one whose mind is involved? Seeing this diverse world, it becomes like that. O you who are worshipped by the King of Serpents, tell me this great wonder. Patanjali himself taught him the science of Yoga, just as it was shown by the Lord of Obstacles. The best of Brahmins, the yogi Shesha, who grants all desires, disappeared. Vyasa became filled with sorrow there. He practiced what he had heard from the mouth of Shesha according to the scriptures in the past. Having controlled his mind, he became worthy of being worshipped by yogis. Having abandoned that contemplation of the five-fold mind, the best of sages became of that form in Chintamani. Vyasa also composed a commentary for the sake of hidden meaning, for the benefit of humans, O Lord of the people. This greatness of Shesha, which grants the peace of Yoga, has been told. Now, O Daksha, listen to the story that is at hand.

Chapter 19 : The Meeting with the Mouse Deity

Mudgala said: Emerging from the heart of Shesha, the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) stayed there in the form of a son, having mounted a mouse out of respect. Then, rising up, Shesha bowed to him with folded hands. He worshipped the Lord of all and bowed to him again. With Atharva Upanishads and folded palms, the King of Serpents praised him, his neck bent in devotion.

Shesha said: You are the Lord of Yoga, the leader of the Ganas, and known as the Lord of the Vedas. Why have you taken the form of my son in this display? Then, Gajanana deluded him with his Maya. Deluded, the King of Serpents considered Gajanana to be his own son. Shesha, along with his wife, took the child and pampered him. Gradually, the Lord became five years old in his house.

Now listen, O Lord of the people, to the story of the demon. The wicked demon Mayakarasura caused the destruction of rituals. That evil-minded one performed demonic acts everywhere, due to which the leaders of the gods were forced to observe fasts. Then, all the people, including those of all castes and stages of life, cried out in distress. No one was able to perform sacrifices or offerings. Then, all the great sages and gods, led by Shambhu, became fearful and deliberated on how to destroy the demon.

There, the great yogi Brihaspati of noble intellect spoke: Worship Ganesha with devotion to attain your goal. Ganesha is the knower of the four goals of life (Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha). He, who is beyond the four, will destroy this great villain. Hearing this, the gods, guided by their guru, performed great penance, remaining without food and dedicated to various rituals.

When a hundred years had passed, Gajanana became pleased. Being satisfied by their devotion, the glorious one came to grant them boons. Seeing him, the gods and sages, filled with joy, bowed to him, worshipped him, and praised him with various hymns. They respectfully requested Ganesha, the Lord of all: O Lord of the Ganas, kill the demon Mayakarasura. Also, grant us your devotion, which is free from Maya. We are all servants of your lotus feet, there is no doubt.

Hearing them, the Lord of the Ganas said with joy: I will surely kill Mayakarasura as the son of Shesha. But, O gods, if my vehicle becomes the primary one, consisting of the four divine aspects, then I will mount it. My intense devotion will surely be yours, and you will always be my firm servants. Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of the gods, vanished, having emerged from Shesha’s meditation for the sake of his devotees.

Then, the gods and sages deliberated on what this vehicle consisting of the four divine aspects should be. Shiva himself spoke excellent words to them: This God is known in the Vedas as the one who rides a mouse. The mouse is known as the thief of the essence, the holder of the secret of theft. Everything that has name and form is the unmanifest Brahman. The enjoyer of the pleasures within names and forms is the one who acts through his own Maya. Those who are deluded by ego do not know that the one who enjoys the pleasures is the Lord himself, existing in the form of a thief.

That very mouse is called the mover of humans; hidden by Maya, he enjoys pleasures like a thief. Similarly, the God who dwells in life, the Sun, the Soul, enjoys pleasures in various living beings like a thief. Vishnu is the enjoyer in all, without doubt, the Lord who is both manifest and unmanifest, acting like a thief through Maya. Likewise, Shiva, the Lord who is beyond the fourth state, is the enjoyer, acting like a thief, free from the illusion of Maya. Make this thief-like enjoyer your vehicle, O dear ones. Give this mouse-form to Ganapa.

Hearing this, all the gods and sages were delighted and said to Shiva: Well said, well said, O Sadashiva, you are all-knowing. Then, meditating on the Lord of the Ganas, they created a body from the four aspects and gave their own essence to it, the form of the enjoyer. By that essence, the mouse became alive and powerful. Brahma, along with the sages, performed its consecration. Theft, both internal and external, exists everywhere; among them, this one is called the King.

Consecrated, the mouse became joyful. He was made the enjoyer of the sacrificial share by the gods and Indra. Gathering together, all the gods and sages came to Shesha’s abode. Seeing them, Shesha was astonished. The glorious King of Serpents worshipped them all, seated them on various seats, and spoke to them.

Shesha said: My birth, charity, penance, knowledge, and nature are blessed by your presence, O ancestors, sages, and gods. Why have you all come? Tell me, O sages of the gods. I will do whatever you ask with devotion, by your grace. Hearing his words, Shankara, the Lord of all, spoke to the King of Serpents, bringing him joy.

Shiva said: O Shesha, you are blessed in all the worlds. The Lord of the Vedas has taken birth as your son. This incarnation on the mouse is five years old. Do not think of him as just a son; he is the Lord born of penance and meditation. This is his primary vehicle, born of the essence of the four gods, holding the dharma of the thief, residing in the hearts of all. Mounting this, the powerful Lord of the Ganas, riding the mouse, will immediately kill Mayakarasura.

Hearing their words, the King of Serpents was filled with sadness and said nothing to the gods, out of his affection for his son. Then, Lord Shankara spoke to him again: Do not grieve, O auspicious one, the remover of obstacles will not die. Your son Gajanana has been requested by the gods and sages through various penances to destroy Mayakarasura. You worshipped Gajanana with penance in the beginning; by the power of that boon, he became your son.

In the meantime, Gajanana arrived there, riding the mouse, playing with the young serpents. Seeing him, the sages and gods stood up and bowed. They praised the Lord of the Ganas with hymns from the Sama Veda. Then, the Lord of the Ganas spoke beneficial words to them: O wise ones, ask for your desired boon.

Then, the sages of the gods, filled with great joy, said to him: O Lord, kill the demon Mayakarasura, who is terrifying to all. Grant us, O Lord of the Ganas, unwavering devotion to your feet. We are your servants, O Ganesha, please protect us. He said to them: So be it, but I have no vehicle. Therefore, O great gods, give me a suitable vehicle. There is no death for the one who is in the form of the four goals of life. How can I deal with the wicked Mayakarasura, O gods? If my vehicle is in the form of the four goals of life, then I will kill him; there is no need for further deliberation.

Chapter 20 : The Meeting of Mushakaga and Mayakarasura

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of the Mushaka (the mouse), the king of gods and others were filled with joy. With folded hands, they spoke to the Lord of Obstacles, the remover of all impediments.

The gods and sages said: O Lord, your vehicle is composed of the four Vedas and is the source of all enjoyments in Svananda. Now, this vehicle has been created by the four primary aspects of Shiva, each contributing their own essence, O Elephant-faced One. You, as the Supreme Lord, are the enjoyer of all beings, much like a thief who takes what belongs to others; therefore, accept this thief-like vehicle.

Having said this, the gods and sages praised the powerful Mushaka, who possessed the form of a mouse.

The gods and sages said: Salutations to you, O wise one, who takes the form of a thief and resides in the hearts of all. Salutations to you, O Mushaka, who brings about internal enjoyment. By your illusion, you bewilder the moving and non-moving worlds; you are the enjoyer like a thief, and we bow to your thief-like form. We bow to you, who are the essence of the entire universe, the supporter of all, the Lord of all, and the destroyer of time. We bow to you, the beginningless Great Lord, the bestower of the four goals of life, the vehicle of Ganesha, and the one who dwells on Ganesha’s banner.

You are the Lord of the Ganas, present everywhere and dwelling in every heart, there is no doubt. You are the very essence of Ganesha’s power. Salutations to you, O Supreme Lord, whose body is the form of Brahman, who are the essence of all thieves, and who possess the fourfold form. You are the protector of the four, and by assuming that form, you have become the primary vehicle of Ganesha today, O Lord.

Praised in this way, the pleased Mushaka said: O fortunate ones, I shall indeed become the vehicle of Ganesha. Having said this, the wise Mushaka, filled with joy and radiance, praised the Lord Ganesha upon seeing him.

The Mushaka said: Salutations to you, O Ganesha, the Lord of Yoga, the Supreme Lord, and the one beyond the beyond. Salutations to you, O Lord of Obstacles, the bearer of great obstacles, the infinite Great Lord, praised by the greatest of lords. Salutations to you, O Heramba, son of the Infinite, destroyer of demons, and protector of the gods. Salutations to you, O Lord of Brahman, who dwells in Svananda and grants bliss to your devotees. Salutations to you, who are the guide of the four, the embodiment of the fourfold union, and the bestower of the four paths. Salutations to you, who are the form of Yoga, the giver of Yoga to the yogis, and the embodiment of peace. Salutations to you, O Lord of Siddhi and Buddhi, whose vehicle is the mouse and whose banner bears the mouse. O Elephant-faced One, by the authority of the Vedas, make me your vehicle, joined in devotion to serve you. I have been held by you as your Mushaka; therefore, O Lord, grant me non-dual devotion. My name has become associated with yours, O Lord; therefore, make me, your devotee, truly wonderful.

Having praised the Lord of the Ganas in this way, he bowed down like a staff. Ganesha lifted him up, filled with devotion, and spoke to him.

Ganesha said: O virtuous and wise one, you shall always be my vehicle. My devotion will become firmly established in you. This hymn composed by you will grant devotion to me and will be the bestower of righteousness, wealth, desire, and liberation. Whoever reads or hears this will be blessed with children and grandchildren, and whatever they desire will be fulfilled. Hear another thing from me: this hymn, composed by the gods and sages, will undoubtedly be honored by all. Any human who praises me with this "Thief's Hymn" will attain all their desires without doubt. At the end, they will reach Svananda and become one with Brahman. By my grace, they will become as dear to me as you are.

Having said this to the great Mushaka, Ganesha mounted him and went to where the demon king was. The gods, sages, and the Nagas, led by Shesha, followed him. The powerful Mushaka-rider sent Brahma as a messenger to Mayakarasura to enlighten him. Brahma went to the demon and spoke words of welfare, saying: "Take refuge in the one who rides the mouse, otherwise you will perish."

Brahma continued: "He is the Ganesha who dwells in Svananda, the one described in the yoga of the four Brahmas. He is the embodiment of both gods and demons; surrender to him with devotion. He has accepted me as his son, and I am telling you this for your own good."

Hearing the words of Brahma, the sinful Mayakarasura, with a corrupted mind, spoke in anger.

Mayakarasura said: "You have come here to deceive me by taking the side of the gods. The fifth Brahman exists everywhere in the form of yoga. That which has neither come nor gone, O Prajapati—that Ganesha who has arrived, I will destroy him along with all the gods. You have come in the form of a messenger, O wise one, so I will forgive your offense; otherwise, I would have killed you, the favorite of the gods."

Then Brahma went to Ganesha, bowed to him, and narrated the incident. Hearing this, Ganesha became angry and ordered the gods to defeat the demon. Then, the gods struck the demon army with a barrage of weapons in the battle. The demons, shattered and broken, fled to their hideouts and reported the situation to Mayakarasura, who was sitting in his assembly.

The demons said: "Why are you sitting still, O great warrior? The gods are filled with anger. They have arrived in the city, and we are being tormented by their weapons."

Hearing their words, the angry Mayakarasura, surrounded by his chief demons, went to the battlefield. Seeing Mayakarasura, the gods fled, but they approached the Mushaka-rider and praised his great deeds.

Thus ends the twentieth chapter of the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Meeting of Mushakaga and Mayakarasura."

Chapter 21 : The Story of the Incarnation of the Mouse-Rider

Mudgala said: Seeing the demon Mayakara, the Lord of the Ganas was pleased. Mounting his mouse, he proceeded to the battlefield. Seeing the Lord of Obstacles equipped with his four weapons, the wicked demon ordered his army of demons to slay the gods. Then, all the powerful and wrathful demon hosts showered torrents of weapons like arrogant clouds. Seeing this, the angry gods fought back, and a great and terrible battle ensued between the two armies. Desiring victory, they rushed at each other and perished; rivers of blood, which struck terror into the hearts of onlookers, were formed there. Sometimes the demons were victorious in the fray, and sometimes the gods; filled with rage, they did not rest even at night. For seven days, the masters of weaponry fought thus, until the gods, broken and crying out in distress, began to flee. Seeing this, an enraged Shankara arrived on the battlefield, and then the gods, led by Vishnu, fought with great fury. By their strikes, many demons were killed, and the demons fled in all ten directions, terrified, O Prajapati.

The demon kings, all unconscious, fell to the earth. Seeing this, Mayakara arrived, roaring like a lion. The great demon, filled with wrath, unleashed a rain of weapons, by which the gods were wounded and fell to the ground, O Prajapati. With a fire-weapon, the great demon struck down Shankara, and all others, scorched by the heat, fled in the ten directions. Seeing the strength of the demon, the Mouse-Rider was pleased and, filled with joy, the mighty one himself entered the battlefield. Seeing him mounted on the mouse, the demon leader, filled with anger, laughed and spoke like a drunkard.

Mayakara said: Why have you come to fight with me, O Lord of Obstacles? With a single arrow, I shall kill you and destroy the gods and sages. The entire world functions according to the four categories of existence; therefore, I have no death. What will you do, O Lord of Gods? You wish to fight with one who has conquered the entire universe; you are but a child, and this is surely your childish nature. Bow to me and go home; I will certainly not kill you. I shall only destroy those ignorant ones who incited you. You are known as the Mouse-Rider, and I consider you equal to a mouse; like a fool, you do not know me, the one who strikes terror into all.

Seeing Mayakara speaking of his own prowess, the Mouse-Rider spoke these beneficial words.

The Mouse-Rider said: Why do you speak to me so, O lowest of demons? I am no child. I am the one who dwells in Svananda, and I have assumed this form to slay you. I shall kill you without doubt, for you are now devoid of the four goals of life. See my prowess, O fool, O leader of demons.

Having said this, filled with rage, the Lord of the Mouse released a lotus from his hand, which was a light composed of the essence of Brahman. Seeing the great lotus approaching in the form of light, Mayakara, proud of his strength, grabbed it. The lotus held by the demon king was not held by his hand; it fell upon his throat, and the demon leader died. The lotus returned to the hand of the Lord of Ganas instantly, and the demon lords were struck with wonder. Alas, how did the proud King Mayakara die? His body appears now just as it was before. His splendor has been taken away by the power that arrived here; the demon leader died without being struck by any weapon.

Then, all the demon hosts, terrified on all sides, fled to the netherworld, and the gods shone with joy. Having bowed to the Mouse-Rider, the gods and sages, skilled in truth, worshipped him and praised him with folded hands.

The divine sages said: Salutations to the Mouse-Rider, the enjoyer of all offerings. Salutations to Ganesha, the God of all gods. Salutations to the pot-bellied Lord, the Master of obstacles, the Supreme Soul. You are the remover of obstacles for your devotees and the creator of obstacles for the wicked. Salutations to you, Heramba, who are dear to your devotees. Salutations to the Supreme Lord who dwells in Svananda. Salutations to the pot-bellied, worshipful One, who is the form of all. Salutations to the One who is worshipped first, the One with the curved trunk. Salutations to the three-eyed One, the bearer of the lotus in your four hands. Salutations to the eldest Lord, who sits upon a mouse. Salutations to the immeasurable Lord of Ganas, the bearer of winnowing-basket ears. Salutations to you, the Lord of all, the Brahman in the form of Brahman. Salutations to the One who is without beginning, who exists in the middle in many forms, and who is the same at the end; salutations to the One with the three forms. Salutations to the son of Shesha, the follower of Shiva, the descendant of Parashara. Salutations to you, the father of all, and the mother, O Lord of Brahman. Salutations to Ganesha, the creator, the sustainer, and the destroyer. Salutations to the form of peace, the giver of peace. Salutations to the One who possesses infinite qualities, who dwells in the hearts of yogis. How can we praise you? Be pleased with us, O Lord of Ganas, salutations to you.

Having praised Ganesha thus, the divine sages bowed. The Mouse-Rider, with a pleased heart, spoke to them joyfully.

The Mouse-Rider said: The praise you have offered me shall be fruitful for all. Whatever you desire, I shall grant, bound by your devotion. I am under the control of those who read or hear this daily. It grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, is complete, and increases sons and grandsons. Choose a boon, O Lords of gods, along with the sages. I shall grant your desires, for I am pleased by this hymn; there is no doubt in this.

The divine sages said: You have accomplished the great destruction of Mayakara. Pleased by that, O Lord of Ganas, what other boon can we ask for? Grant us firm devotion to you, O Lord, which bestows peace. By that, O Lord of Ganas, we are fulfilled; there is no doubt.

Saying "So be it," Ganesha disappeared. The gods, relieved of their distress, went to their respective abodes. The sages went to their hermitages filled with great joy, and all beings returned to their duties as before. Shesha, who had fainted, fell to the earth, and Ganesha appeared in his heart that very instant. He said to the great serpent: "Establish my image. Worship me there and spend your time, O serpent." In the crevice of the netherworld, there shall be a region that grants great success. Those who worship me there, I shall grant them everything. Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, disappeared. Shesha returned to his home and did exactly as instructed with devotion.

A region extending for a hundred yojanas is known as the field of Ganapati. It is said that everything in the netherworld grants success, and it is also called Svananda. By mere worship there, a man obtains his desires, and through the prescribed rituals, he attains the state of Brahman. This is what I have told you, O Daksha, regarding the deeds of the Mouse-Rider. It grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, and is complete for those who hear or read it. By dwelling in the worlds of Brahman like a thief, the Lord of Ganas enjoys all pleasures; therefore, he is called the Mouse-Flag. By the remembrance of his devotees, the Mouse-Flag removes the impurities born of merit and sin and makes them one with Brahman. The great festival takes place on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Jyeshtha. It is the birth date of Ganesha when the sun is in the middle of the sign of Mars (Angaraka). He is called the King of Thieves because he steals the various sins; therefore, O Prajapati, the pot-bellied Mouse-Rider is proclaimed.

Thus ends the twenty-first chapter named "The Story of the Incarnation of the Mouse-Rider" in the Lambodara Charita of the fifth section of the Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 22 : Story of the Son of Shakti

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Mudgala said: O Prajapati, listen to the account of how the Lambodara incarnation, who is known to ride the mouse, became the son of Shakti. The Goddess, residing in the realm of Shakti, created the entire universe. For the sake of protecting and sporting within it, she manifested in three forms through her Maya. She is known as Mahakali, Mahalakshmi, and Mahasaraswati, who destroys the divisions of her devotees. From them, various other goddesses like Durga and others were born. Accompanied by these goddesses, she performed excellent penance for a thousand divine years by the method of the single-syllable mantra. Pleased by this, Lord Vigneshwara became the bestower of boons. Being praised and worshipped by them, he granted their desired boons, which are too numerous to recount, O Prajapati. By the power of those boons, they became engaged in their respective duties and worshipped Lambodara daily as their family deity.

The primordial Shakti, Mahamaya, again began an extremely difficult penance for the sake of peace and eventually attained it. Even then, filled with devotion, she worshipped the Lord of the Ganas. After a hundred years had passed, he appeared in her heart. Seeing him residing within her heart, she praised him with various hymns. Then, emerging from within, he spoke to the Mother of the Universe, saying, "O noble lady, I am your son born of meditation, there is no doubt in this. Behold me, O Mahamaya, standing outside as Lambodara." Awakened, she was delighted to see Lambodara outside. With tears in her eyes, the Goddess worshipped him using the Atharvashirsha. Pleased, the King of the Ganas told her to choose a boon, saying, "I shall grant it to you who are filled with devotion; I am your son, protect me."

Shakti said: O Lord Gajanana, remain stable in this form. O child, grant me firm and supreme devotion to your lotus feet. The Vedas declare that the son is the Brahman born of the meditation of yogis; what is surprising about this, O Vinayaka, that you have done? Hearing this, the King of the Ganas spoke these beneficial words: "Establish my image and worship it constantly. With respect, treat me as your son and family deity, and always maintain the feeling of Brahman towards me. Worship me there with offerings and rituals; by doing so, you will become fulfilled and filled with devotion." Having said this, Lambodara disappeared, O Prajapati. Shakti did exactly as he said and worships that Lord of Brahman.

Thus, O giver of honor, the various incarnations of Lambodara are most wonderful for the sake of all accomplishments for those who worship him. The Lord, the leader of the Ganas, was worshipped by the goddesses, including Raktadanta, through penance for the destruction of the demons. Praised by the goddesses, the Lord became pleased and granted boons. By the power of those boons, many demons were slain. The great goddesses, established in yoga, worshipped him, the destroyer of obstacles, as their family deity for the sake of peace, O Prajapati. All those Kings of the Ganas should be known as being of the nature of Lambodara, and they should be understood in all ways as the bestowers of the state of Brahman. Whoever hears or reads this nectar-like story of the incarnation of Lambodara will attain the fruits of all desires.

Thus ends the twenty-second chapter named the Story of the Son of Shakti, in the Lambodara Charita of the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 23 : Describing Ganapatya Initiation

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Mudgala said: O highly fortunate one, now listen to the nature of the Ganapatya path, by which you will become well-versed in the status of a Ganapatya. A person who is prepared to engage in devotion to Ganesha should, for the sake of pleasing Him, become initiated. Having bowed to a Ganapatya guru who is established in great peace and is well-versed in the meaning of the Vedas, one should first address him, saying, "O great Lord, please grant me initiation."

"You are indeed the Lord of the Ganas, devoted to the Ganapatya path. O noble one, make me a virtuous Ganapatya." When asked in this way, the guru, having observed the disciple to be virtuous, endowed with Vedic and Smarta practices, and yearning for the essence of Ganesha, should speak. He should first teach him the supreme duties of a Ganesha devotee, which I shall now tell you; listen, for they bestow all happiness.

One should never eat food belonging to others, nor look at a menstruating woman. One should never commit adultery, even in thought. One should not become angry for selfish reasons, nor be filled with greed. One should never consume forbidden food, nor ever commit violence. One should not betray the guru, nor engage in backbiting, nor insult elders, nor act in ways contrary to the paths of the Vedas and Shastras. One should be detached from this world and the next, and be devoted to the Supreme Brahman. Having instructed him in such various duties, the guru should then impart the mantra.

First, he should make the disciple perform penances (Krichchhra) and the like. Thereafter, having informed the Ganapatyas, he should look for an auspicious day. Or, he should respectfully choose a day that brings festivities to the Lord of the Ganas, such as the fourth day of the bright or dark fortnight. Having observed a time dear to Ganesha that grants great success, the guru should respectfully give the mantra to the disciple for the sake of devotion.

Listen, O Daksha, to the procedure there, which I shall describe as it bestows happiness. Having constructed a pavilion in a clean place, one should smear it with cow dung. A wise person should himself make an earthen altar there, square in shape, and place pots in the directions within it. First, one should have the Ganesha Yantra drawn with saffron, and worship the eight Ganeshas according to the prescribed rites.

Thereafter, he should worship the Ganapatyas who are already initiated, and without stinginess, provide them with ornaments, clothes, and other gifts. He should have them perform Japa with the Ganesha mantra, either for the number of Purascharana or, with devotion, one hundred thousand times. Or, he should have them do ten thousand or one thousand, and the highly intelligent one should perform a Homa with one-tenth of that amount.

Then, he should offer the sacrifice (Bali) and perform the Purnahuti. Having worshipped the Lord of Obstacles again and bowed to the guru, the disciple should pray with all his heart, "O Lord, save me, your disciple. O noble one, grant me the mantra, especially the Ganapatya one."

Having worshipped the guru again, he should once more set up a pot filled with the desired deity, using the Ganesha mantra. Meditating on the Lord of Obstacles there, he should worship Him according to the simple path. Then, the guru, along with the disciple, should cover the disciple's head with a cloth. Bowing to his guru, the disciple should pray, "O best of the twice-born, grant me the mantra for the sake of crossing the ocean of worldly existence."

Thus requested, the great sage, having well-instructed the disciple and meditating on Ganesha in his heart, should grant the mantra to him. Then, taking those pots and starting from the eastern direction, the guru should bathe the disciple while chanting the mantra one hundred and eight times. It is said that each pot grants a specific success upon bathing; the middle one is said to bestow the state of Brahman and all-around success.

Having taken the bathed disciple and bowed to Ganesha, the disciple should worship the deity and then dismiss Him. He should bow to the Ganapatyas and then give them ample Dakshina without stinginess. The highly illustrious one should feed them with Modakas, cakes, laddus, and milk-rice, along with fine rice and various cooked dishes. Having fed them to their heart's content, he should bow again and again, praying to them specifically, "I have been saved from the ocean of existence."

He should give gifts to the guru and always bow to the lineage of the guru. Upon seeing Ganapatyas, a man should rise and bow to them. O Lord of the people, he who does not bow upon seeing Ganapatyas, there is no doubt that he has directly offended the Lord of Obstacles.

Having thus received initiation, one should always worship Ganesha and perform the Purascharana of the mantra in His presence. Now I shall tell you the rules; listen to them with a focused mind. One should not go to the abode of Ganesha without having cleaned one's teeth. If one goes to the temple after having engaged in sexual intercourse, one immediately falls from grace; therefore, one should bathe before going.

One should not touch the Lord of Obstacles while wearing impure clothes. One should not worship the Lord of the Ganas while wearing someone else's clothes. One should not wear torn clothes while worshipping the deity. One should worship the Lord of Ganas in silence and according to the rules. If a man speaks while engaged in the worship of Ganesha, he becomes afflicted with obstacles due to the sin of interrupting the worship.

If one who has applied oil to the body and has not bathed comes to the temple, he will suffer a fall. One who goes to the abode of Ganesha wearing shoes and without washing his feet will be afflicted with obstacles. If a man goes to the temple without having worshipped the Lord of the Ganas after being anointed with sandalwood paste and the like, he will suffer a fall.

If one stays in the presence of the deity while suffering from indigestion, or if one passes wind, he will be afflicted with obstacles. If one goes to the Ganesha temple without playing the drum, O Daksha, he will suffer a fall due to the violation of the divine secret. If a man comes to the temple and speaks untruths, or fails to perform the praise of the deity, he will suffer a fall.

O Lord of the people, one who remains disciplined in the presence of the deity every day, maintaining decorum, will obtain his desired fruits. The Ganapatya who worships Ganesha, the bestower of all success, according to the scriptures and with reflection, becomes Ganesha himself.

Thus ends the twenty-third chapter of the fifth section of the Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Description of Ganapatya Initiation."

Chapter 24 : Describing the Nature of the Ganapatyas

Mudgala said: O Prajapati, if a person born in the Kshatriya lineage desires eternal initiation into the path of Ganesha, listen to the procedure I shall describe. He should have this performed with reverence by the hand of a Brahmana. He himself should only chant the mantra, and not perform homa or similar rituals with his own hands. Abandoning the science of weaponry and the staff entirely, that Kshatriya shall become a Ganapatya; there is no doubt in this. A Vaishya should abandon his professional work, especially buying and selling, and then he becomes eligible for the initiation of Ganesha. For Shudras, the name-mantra should always be spoken by Brahmanas. By abandoning violence and such, a Shudra can attain the supreme initiation. A Shudra should perform all rituals himself using the name-mantra; he should not recite verses or similar texts for the worship of the Lord of Ganas. He should not perform rituals like homa, nor should he perform penance. A Shudra who is initiated and possesses great glory becomes a Ganapatya. If one who is initiated into Ganesha’s path deviates, he will become afflicted by obstacles due to the Lord’s displeasure.

Now I will tell you the signs of those who are initiated. By these signs, a person can recognize the true Ganapatyas. After performing morning ablutions and the sandhya according to the path prescribed in the scriptures, one should complete the worship as ordained by the Agamas and the Vedas. One who performs worship on the twelve parts of the body with mantras, and applies the paste to all parts—listen to those mantras, O Lord. One should take the sandal paste offered to Ganesha and apply it with discipline. Chanting "Namo Murdhanam" (Salutations to the head) while meditating on the dweller of Svananda, one should worship the head. One should worship the forehead by uttering "Namo Ganathaya," and the base of the right ear by saying "Namo Gajakarnaya." Similarly, worship the left ear by saying "Namo Shurpakarnaya." Then, worship the throat by reciting "Namo Vighneshaya." In the same way, worship the right arm by saying "Namo Herambaya," and the wise one should worship the left arm by saying "Namo Siddhinathaya." Worship the heart by saying "Namo Buddhishaya," and the stomach at the navel by saying "Namo Lambodaraya." Worship the right flank by saying "Namo Vakratundaya," and the left flank by saying "Namo Chintamanaye"—there is no doubt in this. Worship the back at the level of the navel by saying "Namo Dhundhaye." These twelve mantras have been told to you, O Prajapati. The wise one should worship the forehead according to the rules of Sarvabhadra, and similarly worship the limbs above the navel.

Applying paste to the entire body is highly recommended, especially for the devotees of Ganesha. Their paste is always considered to be mixed with red sandalwood. One should wear a garland made of Shami or Mandara wood around the neck or on the arm, as it is auspicious and grants all success. There is no doubt that one should wear it according to the size of the divine wood; one should never wear a garland made of other wood. Just as the Tulsi wood is for the Vaishnavas, so the Shami and Mandara wood is for the Ganapatyas. In their absence, O Lord of the people, one should wear one made of coral, or alternatively, a garland of Rudraksha beads, following the prescribed path.

Always remaining pure in nature, devoid of feelings of friend or foe, and liberated from the duality of opposites, the Ganapatya should walk the earth. One should regularly observe the fasts of the Shukla and Krishna Chaturthi, and all other Ganapatya vows with faith. If one performs a vow or visits a pilgrimage site dear to another deity, one should do so with strength, and then everything is established. One should observe the Ganapatya holy places with discipline. If one is empowered, it is certain, as stated by the sages of old, that just as Lord Ganesha is the first to be worshipped among all, so too are his vows and rituals for the devotees of other deities. Just as for the devotees of Ganesha, O Daksha, there is only one Lord of Ganas, one should perform the vows and rituals as necessary.

The chanting of Ganesha’s name is always present there, and they narrate various beautiful stories to one another. They sing the deeds of Ganesha with joy. Seeing people who have become worthy vessels, they instruct them for the sake of supreme realization. Here I shall tell you the dialogue between Narada and Indra. Once, the yogi Narada, while wandering, arrived at Indra’s abode. Worshipped by Indra, he sang a song dedicated to Ganesha. Bowing to him, Mahendra spoke excellent words.

Indra said: Tell me, O Brahmana of great fortune, the signs of the Ganapatyas. By what signs do the Ganapatyas always walk?

Narada said: The body is smeared with red sandalwood paste, and a garland of Shami or Mandara is on their hands and necks. They are known to be devoted to hearing the name of Ganesha. These wise Ganapatyas are filled with emotion while chanting His name. Those who have no duality in their hearts, who are desireless, yogic, and pure, who are always inclined to bow—yogis call them Ganapatyas. Those who are always devoted to their own duty and never value liberation, who are immersed in devotion on the path of the Lord of Ganas—yogis call them Ganapatyas. Those who do not rely on places or houses, who depend only on their past karma, and who always fix their minds on Chintamani—yogis call them Ganapatyas. Those whose bodies are engaged in righteous acts according to the Shruti and Smriti for the sake of realizing the Lord of Ganas, and who surrender all actions to Him—yogis call them Ganapatyas. Those who are unaffected by blame or praise, who are devoid of attachment to women or gold, and who wander with a longing for devotion to Vighneshvara—yogis call them Ganapatyas. Those who are not covered or influenced by anything, who are disregarded by others but consume the best of divine offerings, and whose hearts are free from faults—yogis call them Ganapatyas. They do not desire Kailasa, Vaikuntha, or the state of Brahman, nor the earth or the netherworld; they always desire devotion to Ganesha—yogis call them Ganapatyas.

Mudgala said: Having said this to Mahendra, the sage Narada went wherever he pleased, singing of Vighneshvara, O Prajapati. I have told you the nature of the Ganapatyas. By listening to this, the ignorance born of sin is destroyed.

Chapter 25 : Granting of the Boon of Shami and Mandara

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: O Ocean of Compassion, tell me the greatness of the Shami and Mandara trees. How did they become so dear to the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha)?

Mudgala said: There was a Brahmin named Aurva, who was devoted to Vedic and Smriti rituals. His wife was also devoted to her own duties, and he was a man of great fame and established in Brahman. In time, a radiant daughter was born to them, and the couple named her Shami. That girl was beautiful and virtuous, and by the age of seven, she became very dear to her father. Wishing to find a suitable husband for her, the Brahmin went to the ashram of Shaunaka, the best of sages.

There, he was honored by Shaunaka and saw his excellent disciple, Mandara, the son of Dhaumya, who was rich in knowledge. He was well-behaved, devoted to serving his guru, and eager for righteousness. The Brahmin was filled with joy and considered him a suitable son-in-law in his heart. After consulting with Shaunaka, he returned home. On an auspicious day, he brought Mandara and gave his daughter to the noble soul. Shami, endowed with beauty, shone brightly with him, just as a jewel shines when set in gold.

After some time, seeing that she had reached youth, Mandara went himself to bring Shami home. Aurva, filled with joy, honored him and gave him his daughter. Mandara, after bowing to his father-in-law, set out on the path. Once, the great and famous Ganesha-devotee named Bhrushundi came to their ashram to rest. Bhrushundi had arrived to wander through their ashram. Seeing him with his trunk, they laughed like fools. Because of the insult to the trunk, a great and wonderful sin was incurred. Provoked by this, the yogi became angry, his eyes turning red.

The great yogi Bhrushundi said to the two low-born Brahmins: "Seeing my trunk, you laughed; therefore, become trees! Whoever insults the trunk of the elephant-faced Lord is undoubtedly my enemy." The two Brahmins, filled with great sorrow, bowed to Bhrushundi and said: "O Yogi, please forgive the laughter caused by our ignorance. O Lord of Sages, tell us how to be released from this curse. We are your servants, there is no doubt. Please make us dear to the Lord of Ganas."

Then, the best of sages, filled with great compassion, said: "Because of the insult to the trunk, you have been cursed by me, there is no doubt. Therefore, the Lord who bears the trunk will be pleased, and then all your desired wishes will be fulfilled. Nothing is under my control; the Gajanana (Ganesha) is the giver of the curse. My anger, which is usually absent, arose because I was provoked." Having said this, the great yogi sat in his seat, and the two immediately turned into trees.

A month later, the Brahmin Shaunaka, filled with worry, took his disciples and went to the Brahmin Aurva in great distress. He asked him slowly, "Where is Mandara? I sent my son to bring Shami." Aurva, astonished and filled with grief, said: "After giving my daughter, I sent him to you immediately." Hearing this, Aurva and Shaunaka were filled with sorrow and set out quickly to find them. On the way, they asked all the villagers and forest dwellers, who told them that they had left a month ago.

Thinking this, they went to the excellent ashram of Bhrushundi. There, in the place where they rested, they saw the trees. Realizing they had not arrived, Shaunaka went into meditation and, learning of the curse by Bhrushundi, informed them of the events. Then, the great yogi left them to their penance. Shaunaka and Aurva, in great pain, became devoted to the worship of Ganesha. By abstaining from food and air, and through meditation, they pleased the Lord of Ganas for their liberation from sin.

After twelve years had passed, the Elephant-faced Lord, deeply moved by their suffering, came to grant them a boon. Seeing the Lord of Obstacles, they bowed, worshipped Him with joy, and praised the Lord of Ganas with folded hands.

Shaunaka and Aurva said: Salutations to the Elephant-faced, the Lord of Obstacles, the Supreme Soul. Salutations again and again to the Infinite, the Great Lord, Heramba. You are the savior from the ocean of worldly existence, the remover of the delusion of Maya. Salutations to the Lord of Brahman, the giver of yoga to Shiva and others. Salutations to the King of the elders, the worshipful form of all, the primordial God, the One who resides within. Salutations to the beginningless One, the Mother and Father of all, the Supreme Soul, the One who dwells in His own bliss, Ganesha. Salutations to the One with winnowing-basket ears, the brave, the pot-bellied, the seeker. You are the creator of obstacles for the non-devotees and the remover of obstacles for the devotees. Salutations to the Lord of Brahman, the giver of the state of Brahman. Salutations to the Lord of Yoga, the peaceful, the giver of peace. Even Shiva, Vishnu, and the other gods could not reach the end of Your qualities; what to speak of the yogis, and what to speak of the Nirguna (formless) aspect?

Be pleased, O Lord Gajanana. Please deliver my disciple Mandara, the son of Dhaumya, now. Deliver Shami, the daughter of Aurva, O Lord of Obstacles. Turn those two who are in the form of trees back into humans. Grant us devotion, O Heramba, by which delusion is destroyed. We are servants of Your lotus feet; always fulfill our desires.

Hearing their words, the Lord of Ganas, who is bound by the devotion of His devotees, said:

Shri Gajanana said: O great Brahmins, those two were cursed by Bhrushundi long ago, knowing that I was insulted by the trunk. There is no doubt. I never make the words of Bhrushundi, who is my great devotee, false; he is more than my own body. If people insult Bhrushundi, the best of sages does not get angry. He never tolerates any insult to me. Therefore, I will not make his words meaningless. Bound by your penance, I will do what is beneficial. Listen to my beautiful words; I will grant you a great boon.

I will remain motionless at the roots of the Mandara and Shami trees. In my form, these trees will be worshiped by all. Even the gods will bow to them, let alone other creatures. By seeing and touching them, they will become destroyers of sin. They will be my favorites in every way, and they will become the most beloved forms to the gods, especially the kings of trees, O great sages. Humans will worship me with Shami leaves, and I will always fulfill their eternal desires. If a person offers me a single Mandara flower, I will be pleased and grant them the desired fruit. Any worship of me done by humans without Durva grass is in vain; there is no doubt about this.

From today onwards, O lords of Brahmins, worship with Shami leaves and Mandara flowers will be fruitful. If a person offers a Shami leaf to me, it is considered greater than a hundred seasons of offerings. I will be constantly satisfied by the Shami leaf; who can describe the fruit of the Mandara flower? Whoever bows to, worships, or touches the Shami tree daily, along with his seven generations, will go to my abode of Swananda. Similarly, whoever bows to, worships, or touches the Mandara tree will go to Swananda with his seven generations.

Whoever performs circumambulation of the Shami and Mandara trees, it is as if he has circumambulated the earth with its seven islands. If a person performs circumambulation of the tree with devotion, he gains the merit equal to circumambulating a hundred lands. What more can be said? They are my forms among the trees; who is capable of describing their greatness?

The Brahmin who views the Shami or Mandara with the thought that they are merely trees will undoubtedly go to hell. The low-born person who sees the Shami or Mandara and does not bow, or does not perform circumambulation, loses all his merit. The great sinner who cuts the Shami or Mandara will undoubtedly fall into hell. The sinner who cuts a branch or leaf will surely go to hell and become sinful just by looking at it. Whoever insults the Shami or the excellent Mandara tree will be devoid of all fortune and will go to hell. Whoever sees the Shami and Mandara as my own form, after enjoying various pleasures, will eventually go to my abode of Swananda.

My devotees who take the root of the Mandara, make an image of me, and worship it, will easily attain me. An image made from the Mandara root is a quick granter of success; no other image of mine will be like it. One should worship the Mandara image with Shami leaves with devotion. The trinity of Durva, Mandara flowers, and Shami leaves is considered very rare. If a devotee uses Shami, Mandara, and Durva together, he will be equal to me in my worship. If one makes a garland of Shami and Mandara and performs Japa, he will undoubtedly enjoy infinite fruits. A person wearing a garland of Shami and Mandara will see obstacles and sins destroyed just by looking at his body. In the end, one who wears a Shami leaf, a Mandara flower, and a Durva leaf will not be held by Yama.

If a human worships me in the proximity of Shami and Mandara, he has performed countless worships to me, there is no doubt. Having said this, the Lord of Ganas said to them again: "If you, O lords of Brahmins, desire my devotion with a heart full of emotion, then make an image from the root of the Mandara tree with respect and perform worship according to the prescribed rites. I am satisfied with Shami, Mandara, and Durva; otherwise, it will not be completely pleasing to me. Always perform my worship without Tulsi, and always perform my Japa with garlands of Shami and Mandara."

Having said this, the Lord of Ganas disappeared right there. The two Brahmins were filled with special joy.

Thus ends the twenty-fifth chapter named the Granting of the Boon of Shami and Mandara in the Lambodara Charita of the fifth section of the Shri Mudgala Mahapurana.

Chapter 26 : Describing the Glory of Touching the Shami and Mandara Trees

Mudgala said: Then, Shaunaka, filled with joy, went to Dhaumya and narrated the entire incident. Dhaumya was also greatly delighted. He said, "Blessed is my son, who dwells in the tree, for he is the bestower of all accomplishments. He is Ganesha himself, worthy of worship by all with reverence." Then, Aurvaka (Shaunaka), filled with joy, stayed near the Shami tree and performed severe penance because of his association with it. At the time of his death, he resided within the Shami tree. He became the glorious Agni-Aurva, a devotee of Ganesha. At the end of the Kalpa, he attained the Lord of the Ganas through yoga. Shaunaka also worshipped Dhundhi (Ganesha) with great affection. He made an idol from the root of the Mandara tree and, devoted to worship, pleased the remover of obstacles with Shami, Mandara, and Durva grass. The best of Brahmins shone, adorned with garlands of Shami and Mandara. Dhaumya also attained the form of a Gaanapatya (a devotee of Ganesha). Hearing of this event, Bhrushundi was filled with joy; he came to his hermitage and bowed to the two excellent trees. He worshipped the Shami and Mandara daily with devotion and offered Shami, Mandara, and Durva to the remover of obstacles. Adorned with garlands of Shami and Mandara, the excellent sage shone among the devotees of Ganesha like another Lord of the Ganas. Thus, all those who were especially devoted to Ganesha shone, being engaged in the worship of Shami and Mandara. I have told you all about the glory arising from the Shami and Mandara; now listen to another supreme destroyer of sins.

In the Dravida country, there lived a Shudra who was a sinner. Greedy for wealth, he would go into the forest and kill people, being of wicked soul. Once, while in the forest, he was attacked by a tiger. Terrified, he fell down and was immediately devoured by it. By the power of the wind, a Shami leaf came there. By divine coincidence, O Prajapati, it touched him. The powerful messengers of Yama arrived to take him, but at the same time, the attendants of Ganesha arrived to claim the wretch. The attendants of Ganesha defied the messengers of Yama, seized the Shudra, and were about to depart when the messengers of Yama attacked them. The messengers of Yama were struck with maces and fell to the ground. The attendants of Ganesha took him and transformed him into a divine being. The messengers of Yama, filled with grief and sorrow, went to Yama and narrated the incident, overflowing with anger.

The messengers of Yama said: Lord, there are some who have made scriptures on this earth. Everything is under your command; where has that gone? You are the Lord of Dharma himself, the master who understands the meaning of the Vedas; therefore, everything is considered to be under your command. Gods like Shambhu and Vishnu are the foundations of Dharma; there is no doubt that they act according to the nature of Dharma. A great sinner, a Shudra named Vishalaksha, died. We went there to take him, O Lord, holding our nooses. Suddenly, some most wonderful beings arrived there, holding trunks, adorned with four arms. They defied us, seized that Shudra, and were about to depart, O son of the Sun, when we stood to fight. Having struck us down with their great power and maces, they took him and went away; we do not know where they are now, O wise one. Therefore, with effort, take away their pride. Having said this and bowed, they stood with folded hands.

Mudgala said: Hearing their words, the wise Yama became absorbed in meditation. Knowing everything, the glorious Yama felt fear. Meditating on the elephant-faced God, with hair standing on end and tears in his eyes, he bowed to Ganesha in his heart and said, trembling with fear: My attendants have committed a great offense, O Lord. Forgive them, O ocean of mercy, for they acted out of ignorance. A dispute with the highly fortunate devotees of Ganesha is not proper. I have received this only through the power of my lack of fortune. Having thus appeased the remover of obstacles, the son of the Sun called all his attendants and spoke beneficial words.

Yama said: Whoever has the mark of the Shami wood and Mandara garland on their body, and whoever carries their flowers and leaves, O brave ones, stay far away from them in fear. If someone sings the praises of the remover of obstacles while holding Durva, or performs worship of the Lord of the Ganas, stay far away from them in fear. Stay away in fear from those who say: "Ganesha, Heramba, Gajanana, Mahodara, revealer of one's own experience, supreme one, lover of accomplishments, Lord of wisdom." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Destroyer of many obstacles, Vakratunda, dweller in one's own self, four-armed one, Lord of poets, destroyer of the demon Antaka." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Son of Mahesh, enemy of the elephant demon, son of the excellent one, Vikata, three-eyed one, supreme Lord, supporter of the earth, Ekadanta." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Pramoda, Mode, destroyer of Naraka, destroyer of the six waves, elephant-eared one, Dhundhi, stabilizer of the ocean of duality." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Vinayaka, enemy of the destruction of knowledge, son of Parashara, son of Vishnu, beginningless, worshipped by the mouse, worshipped by all." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Son of Vidhi, Lambodara, smoke-colored one, protector of the peacock, rider of the peacock, whose lotus feet are served by gods and demons." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Excellent one, whose flag is the great mouse, Shurpakarna, Shiva's son, rider of the lion, infinite carrier, powerful one, Lord of obstacles, whose navel is the serpent Shesha." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Smaller than the small, greater than the great, son of the Sun, Lord of Yoga, best of kings, Lord of treasures, Lord of mantras, son of Shesha." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Bestower of boons, son of Aditi, giver of knowledge of Parashara, star-faced one, elder brother of Guha, son of Brahma, son of Parshu." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Enemy of the ocean, holder of the axe, Lord of the Shami, lover of flowers, remover of obstacles, God of gods worshipped with heaps of Durva." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Bestower of intellect, lover of Shami, bestower of great accomplishments, bestower of great peace, immeasurable, of infinite prowess." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Lover of the fourth day, son of Kashyapa, bestower of wealth, bestower of knowledge, Chintamani, dweller in the heart." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Enemy of Yama, enemy of ego, destroyer of Vidhi, son of Kapila, bodiless, Svananda, yoga of the unyoked." Stay away in fear from those who say: "Enemy of the Ganas, enemy of the lotus, knower of the state of equality, moon-crested one, one who moves in the beginning, middle, and end." Stay away in fear from those who say: "All-pervading, form of the universe, Lord of qualities, infinite one, Lord of nourishment, rider of the mouse, creator, preserver, and destroyer." Do not enter where those who read or listen to these one hundred and eight names are present. This hymn, which grants worldly enjoyment and liberation, increases wealth and grain, and leads to the state of Brahman, should be chanted daily with reverence. Wherever there are signs of Ganesha, O soldiers, do not enter there in fear. Having said this, Yama silenced all his attendants. All the messengers of Yama now worship the Lord of Ganesha with devotion. Whoever listens to or reads this holy glory arising from the Shami will attain the fruits of the Mandara.

Chapter 27 : The Story of the Origin of Durva

Daksha said: O Mudgala, please tell me the story of Durva, which brings great joy. Indeed, there is nothing as sacred as Durva for the worship of the Lord.

Mudgala said: In the beginning, when Lord Brahma, the creator of the universe, had created all living beings, he became deeply concerned about how to provide them with food. While meditating on Lord Gajanana (Ganesha), a goddess emerged from the pores of his body. She was radiant, with hands and feet in all directions, a thousand faces, and adorned with a thousand limbs. Seeing her, Brahma was filled with joy, recognizing her as the embodiment of food and the sustainer of all. She bowed to the Creator with folded hands and asked, "O Creator of the world, what shall I do?"

Brahma said: You shall be known as Durva, the provider of food for all. Perform penance and then create food for everyone according to the divine order. Agreeing to this, she performed great penance, meditating on Ganesha while remaining without food. After a thousand divine years, Ganesha, the giver of boons, appeared before her. Seeing him, she stood up, bowed, worshipped him, and praised him with the Atharvashirsha. Ganesha told her that she would create various forms of life and be worshipped by all.

Having said this, Ganesha disappeared. She then began her creation. From her head, food for the heavenly beings emerged; from her stomach, food for the beings of the earth; and from her feet, food for the beings of the netherworld. She created various types of food from her limbs, and all beings were satisfied. Seeing the people happy and nourished, Brahma performed her consecration with Vedic mantras. Having been consecrated as the mistress of food, she became especially honored. However, filled with pride and the intoxication of power, she forgot the mantra of Ganesha. She became arrogant and began to compete with Goddess Parvati, claiming that she herself was Annapurna (the goddess of food) and that Parvati was merely using the name.

Hearing this, the divine Parvati became angry. Once, during a grand festival at the moon's abode, Durva insulted the Mother of the Universe. Enraged, Parvati cursed her, saying, "You emerged from my pores; do not compete with me in vain. Fall and become a blade of grass." Thus, she became a blade of grass on earth. From that day on, Durva became the most sacred and worshipped by all.

Filled with sorrow, she remembered Ganesha and prayed for forgiveness for her arrogance and for neglecting his mantra. She went to the forest and performed intense penance. After a hundred years, Ganesha, pleased with her devotion, appeared before her. She praised him with various hymns and asked for his grace. Ganesha, pleased, said that he would forgive her and grant her whatever she desired. He promised that the hymn she composed would grant worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Durva asked for firm devotion and protection from the curse. Ganesha replied: "Do not worry, O Durva. You will remain a blade of grass on earth, but you will also retain your divine form in heaven. On earth, you will be the essence of nectar, ever-growing and sacred. You will be dear to all gods and worshipped by all. Without Durva, no one can worship me; those who worship me without Durva will not receive the fruits of their devotion. There is nothing as dear to me as Durva. Those who offer Durva to me have offered everything. I am conquered by the offering of Durva."

Having said this, Ganesha disappeared. Durva, filled with joy, continued to worship Ganesha. Following Ganesha's words, the gods and Parvati began to worship him with Durva. Thus, she became the remover of sins and a bringer of auspiciousness. This is the story of the origin of Durva, which grants both worldly happiness and liberation to those who hear or read it.

Chapter 28 : Describing the Glory of Touching a Durva Leaf

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Mudgala said: Here I shall narrate to you an ancient story, through which the supreme glory of the Durva grass is known to mankind. In the Dandaka forest, there lived a man born of a low caste named Pishuna, who was wicked, evil-minded, and devoted to sinful deeds. He was a thief who went into the forest and killed people cruelly. Sometimes, seeing a woman alone, the wicked man would molest her. Being obsessed with his own physical desires and hunger, he wandered in the forest, villages, and cities, always armed and wicked. He committed countless sins, including the killing of a Brahmin. O Daksha, it is not possible for me to describe his character fully.

Once, while in the forest, this great villain saw a Brahmin. He rushed out from a hollow tree, armed with a weapon. Seeing him approach like Yama, the bearer of the staff, the Brahmin fled in great terror, crying out for help. Hearing the cry of the Brahmin, five Kshatriyas who were traveling on that path by divine coincidence arrived from the forest. They struck the sinner with their weapons, and he died and fell to the ground. The Brahmin, filled with joy, returned to his hermitage.

Then, the messengers of Yama seized the wicked Pishuna, threw him into the Raurava hell, and began to punish him. On the fifth day, a miracle occurred; the hell became peaceful and devoid of all torment. Seeing this great wonder, the messengers of Yama went to Yama and reported the news of the sudden peace in hell. Hearing this, Dharma (Yama) was astonished and went into meditation. Having realized the truth, he remembered the Lord Lambodara (Ganesha) and spoke to them.

Dharma Raja said: This Pishuna is a great sinner and an outcaste, there is no doubt about it. O messengers, he is no longer fit for hell, for he has now become virtuous. Where he died, a messenger of Ganesha arrived, and a Durva leaf from his head fell upon Pishuna. O messengers, whoever is touched by that, whether it be bones or a corpse, all their sins will undoubtedly be destroyed. He becomes a storehouse of merit and is especially honored by all. Therefore, take him out and bring him into my presence.

Hearing the words of Yama, the messengers were amazed and went to carry out the order. As they prepared to remove him, a wonder occurred. A celestial chariot accompanied by the messengers of Ganesha arrived to take him. The attendants of Ganesha took him and departed for their abode, Svananda. They transformed Pishuna into a divine being, filled with joy, as he had become devoted through the touch of the Durva leaf on his former body.

Dharma, being greatly astonished, said to his servants: Oh, behold, O chiefs of the messengers, the intense greatness of the Durva. Blessed is this sinful Pishuna, upon whose body the Durva came, by which he attained the status of the yogis. Who can know the complete greatness of the Durva? Even by touching a corpse, it grants the status of Brahman, O chiefs of the messengers.

Having said this, the son of the Sun (Yama) had tears in his eyes. He constantly contemplates Ganesha and worships Him with devotion. Whoever hears or reads this wonderful greatness born of the Durva, Lord Ganesha always grants them their desired wishes.

Thus ends the twenty-eighth chapter of the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, titled "The Description of the Glory of Touching a Durva Leaf," in the Lambodara Charita.

Chapter 29 : The Description of the Story of Trishira

Mudgala said: Listen further to the greatness of the Durva grass, which bestows happiness upon itself and by which the Elephant-faced Lord became fully satisfied. Once, the yogi Narada went to the abode of King Janaka. Being worshipped, he spoke to the king with great joy. Narada said: You are blessed, O devotee of the Lord of the Ganas, for you are truly devoted. The Lord of the Ganas grants you whatever you desire. Hearing the words of Narada, the yogi-king Janaka smiled and replied to the great sage with wisdom. Janaka said: The auspicious Yoga taught to me by Yajnavalkya bestows complete peace, O Brahmana; there is no doubt about this. He graciously gave me the single-syllable mantra of Ganesha, which grants success, and through that practice, I have attained this state. O great yogi, through that knowledge, I have become a yogi and a guru by the grace of Ganesha. Therefore, the Lord of the Ganas and I are not different; where there is the grace of Ganesha, how can there be any division? Whatever I think, the Lord of Obstacles grants it; alas, to those who are not yogis, all this seems like an illusion. The five-fold mental modifications and their illuminator, the Chintamani itself, play within my heart. O King of Yogis, tell me now where Janaka is, and whether the grace of Ganesha is upon him. To say that "I am the destroyer, I am the creator, I am the protector" is like the speech of one who is deluded; it is not appropriate for you, O best of yogis.

Hearing the words of Janaka, Narada became angry and rebuked the king. Narada said: You are intoxicated by knowledge, O King; how can you be imperishable? You are a body-bound being, and your form is that of Ganesha, yet you are caught in illusion. Ganesha is known as Brahmanaspati according to the Vedas; that essence exists fully within his body. O King, that essence never exists in the body of a yogi; the wise say that Ganesha is the Lord of Yogis through Samadhi. Since a human yogi possesses a body born of past actions, there is no doubt that his essence is always like that of a human. O Janaka, because you are proud of your yogic powers, your pride will be broken by the grace of the Elephant-faced Lord. Having said this, the yogi went to Kailasa to the Lord of the Ganas, bowed to him, and narrated the entire incident. Then, bowing again to the Lord of Obstacles, the sage Narada, who was fond of singing the praises of the Lord of the Ganas, went on his way.

Then, the Elephant-faced Lord assumed the form of an old, leprous Brahmana, covered in worms and falling to the ground. He was trembling, surrounded by a foul odor, covered in pus, blood, and sweat, and swarmed by flies. By the order of the King, he was allowed to enter by the gatekeeper. The Brahmana begged the King for food that would satisfy him. The King worshipped the Brahmana, and he ate the food that was brought. He asked for more, and the King gave him a large amount. He ate that too, and the King, now filled with fear, gave him food enough for ten thousand people, which brought great satisfaction. After eating that, the great sage asked for more food. Then the King gave him raw food, which the best of Brahmanas ate. Then the King gave him all the food stored in the city and on the ground; he ate that too and asked the King for more. The King gathered food from all the villages on the outskirts of the city, and the Brahmana ate it all. The Brahmana asked the King for more food, and the King, filled with shame, said nothing. The great sage said to the King, smiling: You are Ganesha, there is no doubt; why are you devoid of power? O King, why do you stand silent when you are capable of doing, undoing, or changing anything? You are deluded by the pride of yoga, O King; there is no doubt that you are like a human, not Ganesha at all.

Having said this, the Lord of Ganesha, in the form of a Brahmana, went outside and begged for food from the people, being hungry. All the people said to him: The food gathered by the King has all been eaten by you. There is nothing left in our homes now, O Brahmana; go away. Who are you, that you eat everything? No one knows. Hearing this, the Brahmana laughed and wandered here and there. By chance, he went to the outskirts of the city and saw the house of a Brahmana. Trishira, a great sage who lived on what he received without asking, a leader among the devotees of Ganesha, was there with his wife, Virochana. The Lord of Ganesha saw him and entered the hermitage, which was devoid of any wealth or grain. He asked the great soul for food that would satisfy his hunger, saying: Give it to me, for I am hungry. Trishira said to the Brahmana: O best of Brahmanas, there is no grain in my house. I am the king of the poor, there is no doubt; no human is as poor as I am. Only Durva grass has been brought for the worship of Ganesha; there is nothing else, O Brahmana, do not think otherwise. Hearing these words, the hungry Brahmana said: O great soul, give me the Durva grass with devotion, for it will remove my hunger. Then Virochana, having bowed to the Elephant-faced Lord, gave the Durva grass to the Brahmana. Meditating on it, she transformed it into various kinds of food. The Brahmana ate what she gave with devotion and was satisfied.

Being pleased with their devotion, the Lord of the Ganas revealed his supreme form, adorned with the elephant face and other marks. Seeing the pot-bellied Lord, they bowed again and again. With tearful eyes, they worshipped him and prayed with folded hands. Virochana and Trishira said: Salutations to you, O Ganesha, salutations to you who are dear to all. Salutations to Brahman, the Lord of Brahman, the Lord of Obstacles. Salutations to Heramba, the Supreme Lord, the one whose flag bears a mouse. Salutations to you, who are both the Self and the non-Self, the pot-bellied one. Salutations to the one who is free from disease, the support of all, the one with a beautiful form. Salutations to the curved-trunk one, the one worthy of worship by all. Salutations to the one who is without beginning, middle, or end, the one who takes that form, the seeker. Salutations to the eldest of all, the mother and father of all. Salutations to the one with ears like winnowing fans, the perfect one, the supporter of the earth. Salutations to the one adorned with the serpent, the Chintamani. Salutations to the giver of success and wisdom, the one who dwells in his own bliss, the giver of peace to devotees. Salutations to the one who rides a mouse, the bestower of success and wisdom. Salutations to the Elephant-faced one, the one who grants the highest position to the eldest. Blessed are my mother, father, and lineage, for I have seen the Lord of the Ganas, who is beyond the reach of Vedanta. Even the Vedas, the yogis, Brahma, Shiva, and the knowers of the Vedas do not know you. We see this wonder, O Lord of the Ganas, that you are attained through peace; there is no doubt. You are the Supreme of the Supreme, the immeasurable one; how did you come to my house, O great soul? There is no one like me in this universe who has reached your feet, O Ganesha.

As the great sage and his wife prayed in this way, their throats choked with emotion due to the greatness of their devotion. He danced in supreme bliss, his hair standing on end, forgetting everything in his tearful state. Then the Lord of the Ganas, seeing their devotion, spoke words of benefit, himself tearful and with hair standing on end. Ganesha said: This hymn composed by you will be very pleasing to me, O sage, and will increase the devotion of people towards me. Any mortal who reads or hears this will attain everything. They will attain enjoyment, liberation, and the state of Brahman, and will always be dear to me. Ask for your desired boons, O great yogi Trishira; I am pleased by your devotion and will grant you everything, there is no doubt. Hearing the words of Ganesha, Trishira, with his wife, bowed again and said with humility. Trishira said: Why are you deluding me, O Lord? I do not ask for anything, O Elephant-faced one. Considering everything to be an illusion, I only ask for steady devotion to you. Hearing this, the Lord of Obstacles was amazed. Oh, how this Brahmana asks only for firm devotion! Ganesha said: You will attain complete devotion, O great yogi; there is no doubt. By that, I will always be under your control.

Having said this, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, disappeared. Trishira and his wife were filled with sorrow. By the grace of Ganesha, the house of the great sage became beautiful, adorned with jewels and golden walls. At the door, many people holding golden staffs stood, along with women and servants eager to serve. Seeing the house filled with various riches, he was amazed. The servants took him and served him with great devotion. Seeing this great wonder, the sage said to his wife: This illusion has been given by the Lord of Obstacles to destroy my devotion. Therefore, enjoy these pleasures with effort, O virtuous one. Thus, being free from pride, they enjoyed great happiness. He gave away various kinds of wealth through the path of charity, yet the wealth remained the same, and he was amazed. This is the great greatness of the Durva grass that I have told you, which pleases Ganesha and grants all success. In the pores of Ganesha's skin, there are many universes; the entire Brahman resides in his body, O King. Everything attains satisfaction when the Supreme Lord of Obstacles is satisfied. Who can describe the fruit of this, O giver of honor? The three worlds are not equal to the Durva grass. It is said to be the giver of immense merit and is pleasing to Ganesha. The greatness of the Durva grass, which grants Brahman and bestows enjoyment and liberation, has been told to you, O Daksha; those who read or hear it will attain everything.

Chapter 30 : The Description of the Glory of Durva

Daksha said: I have heard the glory of offering Durva, which grants all accomplishments; indeed, nothing in the worlds is seen to be equal to Durva. Yet, being devoted to the nectar of Yoga, I am not satisfied. Therefore, O King of Yogis, tell me the story of Janaka. How was the great Yogi Janaka deceived by Ganesha, and what did he do after the Lord of Obstacles departed? Tell me.

Mudgala said: Listen, O Prajapati, to the wonderful character of Janaka. After the Lord of Ganas departed, he became filled with sorrow. Disturbed in his heart, he lamented to himself, "Ah, what the sage Narada said is indeed true. The body is meant for devotion, but one who does not worship the Elephant-faced Lord after attaining this body is truly cheated by Maya. Those established in action should serve the Lord of Ganas for their own welfare; those established in penance should serve Him as the bestower of the fruits of penance; those established in knowledge should always serve Him as the bestower of the perfection of knowledge; and Yogis should serve Him, the Lord who is the embodiment of Brahman, as the giver of Yoga. He is the direct manifestation of the Lord of Speech (Brahmanaspati) mentioned in the Vedas. Deluded like a fool, I abandoned Ganesha and spoke falsely of Yoga, behaving like an ordinary man. The great soul who holds onto the worship of Ganesha is the true Guru of Yogis; there is nothing equal to that."

While Janaka was thus distressed in his home, the nine sons of Rishabha, the kings of Yogis, arrived. They were naked, always youthful, worshipped everywhere, moving freely in the three worlds, and always intent on the state of Brahman. They were like the embodiment of Yoga, devoid of injunctions and prohibitions. They were speaking to each other about the deeds of Ganesha, joyfully chanting His names. They were free from the delusion of "self" and "other," regarding a lump of clay, a stone, and gold as equal, devoid of distinctions, and beyond the divisions of caste and stage of life. They acted according to their own will, possessed knowledge by their own will, and were established in their own nature by their own will. Seeing them, King Janaka, with a mind filled with reverence, rose and bowed down to the ground like a staff. After worshipping them and feeding them, he served them by massaging their feet and spoke beneficial words.

Janaka said: My parents, my knowledge, and my studies are blessed by the sight of you, who are the embodiment of the Lord of Speech. You are undoubtedly free from desires in all aspects, yet I wish to serve you. If a human being remains even slightly under your command, he attains devotion and is never reborn. By insulting you, a man immediately becomes a dweller of hell. You have come out of compassion, being the very form of Yoga. There is no one in the three worlds equal to me, for I have attained the fruit of my life through the glory of my fortune by seeing you.

Mudgala said: Hearing the words of Janaka, the nine Yogis, who bestow peace, spoke to the King with great joy.

The Nine Yogis said: You are blessed, O King, you are truly Videha (disembodied/liberated). It is no wonder that you are established in virtue even while possessing a body. We came to your house desiring to see a saintly person. Having seen you as a perfect Yogi, we are satisfied. We are leaving now, O best of kings; we do not desire anything. We are pleased to see the worship you have offered.

Hearing their words, the King, filled with devotion, bowed and asked for their guidance.

Janaka said: Tell me, what should people always do for the perfection of Yoga, so that they may become wise and worthy of being worshipped by Yogis?

The Sage said: One should always be a follower of his own duty and intent on Yoga, abandoning Rajas and Tamas, and being filled with Sattva. A man should constantly perform Vedic and Smriti rites, especially vows, with the intention of offering them to Ganesha. All internal practices related to breath control should be performed with a focus on meditation. O King, always meditate on Ganesha in your heart. Even if one is established in Samprajnata Samadhi (meditation with consciousness), one should never abandon meditation on the Lord of Ganas. Even if one is in Asamprajnata Yoga (meditation without consciousness), one should always meditate on the Lord of Obstacles. By being joined with the meditation of Ganesha, one crosses the land of Yoga in a short time by the removal of obstacles. Then, the Yogi becomes peaceful. By constant practice, he becomes one with the Chintamani (the wish-fulfilling gem) in his heart. There is nothing in the worlds equal to Ganesha that grants Brahman. He assumed a body to grant Yoga to all. This is the worship that grants success in Yoga.

Janaka said: Why is Ganesha called Chintamani? Tell me how to perceive His form in the heart.

Hari said: The mind is said to be of five types, and the Chintamani resides there. Because He is the illuminator of the mind, He is called the Lord of Obstacles. The mind is known as Kshipa (distracted), Mudha (dull), Vikshipa (oscillating), Ekagra (focused), and Nirodhaka (restrained). Know that the Chintamani is the illuminator in all these states. When the mind is distracted, know that the Chintamani is the one who illuminates it; worship Him. When the mind is dull like a ghost due to lack of knowledge, the Chintamani is the one who plays there; worship Him. When the mind is oscillating, seeking happiness in the world without the experience of Brahman, the Chintamani is the one who makes it intent on practice; worship Him. When the mind is focused, the Chintamani is the direct illuminator; worship Him. When the mind is restrained, the Chintamani is the one who grants the state of Brahman. By abandoning these five states of the mind, one becomes a Yogi. The Chintamani Himself becomes the destroyer of the mind. By peace and constant devotion, one attains the Chintamani.

Janaka said: How did Ganesha, the Lord of Ganas, become embodied? How does such faith arise?

Antariksha said: The Rajasic aspect is on the left, the Tamasic on the right, and the Sattvic in the middle; the fourth state is the union of these. The body is fourfold, and the Lord of Ganas is the one who holds the ego. He from whom everything arises and to whom everything goes during the great dissolution is called the Gaja (Elephant). That which is never born and never dissolves is called the Brahman in the Vedas. That is the head of the great-souled Ganesha. In the union of these, the Lord of Ganas becomes manifest. The body below the neck is the form of Samprajnata, and the head above the neck is the form of Asamprajnata. In their union, Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, became embodied out of grace for His devotees. He is not a being of equal nature; He is the Lord of Ganas who assumed a body for the sake of devotion. There is nothing dearer to Him than the devotion of His devotees. For their sake, He abandons everything and becomes subservient to them.

Janaka said: What is the Maya of the Lord of Ganas that causes delusion?

Prabuddha said: Siddhi (Success) arose from His left side, and Buddhi (Intellect) from His right. They are the ones who cause delusion in those whose minds are caught by Maya. When men focus on sins, the result is hell; when they focus on virtues, the result is heaven; when they focus on Brahman, the result is Yoga. The Chintamani resides in the heart as the giver of focus. By His Maya, the Lord of Ganas plays freely. Siddhi is the deluder, and Buddhi is the one who removes the delusion after one understands through the intellect.

Janaka said: If Ganesha is fond of devotion, why does He not make His devotees free from delusion?

Pippalayana said: The world was created by Ganesha for the sake of play. It is twofold: one is the worldly path full of Maya, and the other is the Ganesha-Yoga path that grants peace and destroys Maya. By knowing the Lord of Ganas through Yoga, a man worships Him with undivided devotion. Whatever state a person has, the Lord of Obstacles does not interfere. He created the world as twofold to see the highest state of consciousness.

Janaka said: What is Karma (action), Akarma (inaction), and Vikarma (prohibited action)? What is Karma Yoga?

Avirhotra said: Action performed according to one's own duty is Karma, which grants bodily happiness but also birth and death. Action devoid of duty and rules is Vikarma, which is sinful. Action performed for the sake of liberation, with the attitude of offering it to Brahman, is Akarma. One who performs action as an offering to Brahman is a Karma Yogi.

Janaka said: What is Knowledge?

Drumila said: Knowledge is the pulsation of awareness in the heart. It is the Brahman that resides there as the giver of pulsation. It is devoid of name and form, eternal, and without beginning or end. That is the highest knowledge.

Janaka said: What is Bliss, the natural state, and the state of Brahman?

Chamasa said: Bliss exists everywhere, both internally and externally. The fourth state, which is beyond the three states of delusion, is called the natural state (Sahaja). By the command of the Lord, Brahman exists in these three states. The fourth state destroys all. By the union of the self-controlled, one attains the natural state of Brahman.

Mudgala said: Having said this, the nine Yogis took leave of Janaka and departed. Janaka, with a heart full of devotion, worshipped Ganesha day and night. He became a Yogi worshipped by other Yogis. Because of Ganesha's satisfaction, his lineage became full of wise and virtuous people. Later, Janaka heard the story of Ganesha's arrival from the sage Trishira. He went to the sage, bowed to him, and became a constant devotee of Durva. He never performed worship without Durva until his death. In the end, he attained the Lord of Ganas and resided in His own bliss. This glory of Janaka, if heard or read, grants both worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Chapter 31 : The Reason for the Exclusion of Tulsi

Daksha asked: You have stated that worship should be performed without Tulsi. Why did that sacred plant become tainted with such a defect? What did Tulsi do to offend Lord Ganesha, for which the Lord of the Immortals became angry and abandoned her, despite her glory?

Mudgala replied: There was a king named Dharmadhvaja who was righteous, generous, and highly respected by the wise. He had a daughter of unparalleled beauty and grace, named Vrinda. One day, she bowed to her father and spoke with deep emotion and wisdom. Vrinda said: "Father, I have decided that I will marry none other than Lord Vishnu. Please grant me your permission to seek Him." Dharmadhvaja replied: "My daughter, Lord Vishnu is the Supreme Being, the Lord of Lakshmi. How can you attain Him? Choose a husband from among men. We are mere mortals, while He is the Supreme Lord."

Despite being advised in many ways, she remained unmoved. She obtained a powerful mantra and went to the forest of Vishnu. Meditating on the four-armed Lord and observing strict fasting, she performed intense penance. She traveled to various holy places, practicing different forms of austerity, subsisting only on dry leaves, air, water, roots, and fruits to please the Lord. Thousands of years passed, but Janardana did not appear. Through the power of her penance, she attained inner wisdom and saw the entire universe as one with the Self.

One day, she saw Ganesha absorbed in deep meditation, worshipping the Lord within his heart. Seeing the elephant-headed son of Shiva near the Bhagirathi river, she was astonished. She became captivated by the Lord of the Ganas, thinking, "He is the Lord of all, the source of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva. He is the complete manifestation of the Divine." Realizing this, she decided to choose Ganesha as her husband, believing that by the power of her penance, this would not be an act of infidelity, as He is the embodiment of Vishnu.

She approached the meditating Ganesha and tried to break his concentration. She asked, "Why are you, the son of Shiva, meditating? Even the gods worship you. Why do you, the Lord of all, worship another?" Ganesha remained silent. She then sprinkled a drop of water on him, and when he did not stir, she touched him with her finger. Ganesha opened his eyes and asked, "Who are you, and why do you disturb a devotee's meditation? Such actions are not becoming of the virtuous."

Vrinda replied, "I am the daughter of Dharmadhvaja. I have performed great penance; be my husband." Ganesha laughed and said, "I do not accept a woman who is driven by desire. I am a servant of the Lord and remain detached. Go where your heart finds peace." Despite his refusal, she persisted, and in her obsession, she grabbed his anklet and tried to force him to rise. Angered, Ganesha cursed her, saying, "Because you have acted like a demoness and tried to force me, you shall be born in a lower form as a plant."

Terrified, Vrinda wept and begged for forgiveness. Seeing her distress, Ganesha softened and said, "Do not grieve. You will be seen by a demon in the forest and will be taken by him. Later, Lord Vishnu will take the form of Shankhachuda and marry you. When that demon is killed by Shiva, you will cast your body into the fire and return to your plant form. You will then be the beloved of Vishnu, and people will worship you with leaves and flowers. However, you will always be excluded from my worship. Go now, and have no fear."

Chapter 32 : The Granting of the Boon to Tulasi

Mudgala said: O Prajapati, it happened exactly as Gajanana had spoken. Having bowed to Him, Vrinda went to the forest to perform penance. Filled with anxiety and longing for Ganesha, the Goddess meditated upon Him in her heart and performed excellent penance. She chanted His name-mantra and worshipped Him with devotion. In this way, one hundred and fifty thousand years passed. Then, Ganesha, the Lord of the Ganas and the giver of boons, suddenly appeared before her. Having bowed to Him and worshipped Him, Vrinda praised Him with great joy.

Vrinda said: Salutations to the Lord of Obstacles, the remover of the obstacles of His devotees, and salutations again and again to the one who specifically places obstacles before those who are not His devotees. Salutations to You, the Supreme Lord of the supreme, the one who is higher than the highest, the Lord of devotees, and the one who is always fond of devotion. Salutations to You, the Lord of Brahma, the Lord of the Ganas, the embodiment of Brahman, the Lord of the Vedas, and the leader of the Ganas. Salutations to the immeasurable, the inconceivable, the one who always dwells in His own bliss, and the one who grants positions to Shiva, Vishnu, and others. Salutations to the Lord of the helpless, the Lord of all, the Supreme Soul, the one with the curved trunk, and the one who is worshipped first by all. Salutations to Vinayaka, the heroic one, the one with winnowing-basket ears, the seeker, the elephant-faced one, Chintamani, and Heramba. Salutations to the one who possesses infinite qualities, the one who engages in various plays, the Lord of the supreme, and the one who has the mouse as His emblem. Salutations to the one who is complete bliss, the mother and father of all, the eldest of the elders, and the granter of the highest position. How can I praise You, O Lord of the Ganas, who are the form of Yoga and higher than the highest? Therefore, I bow to You; may You be pleased with this, O Lord. Grant me the Yoga of Ganesha and make me a Gana-devotee. Forgive the sin I committed by transgressing against You. O Giver of boons, make me worthy of Your worship, even if I have offered only a little devotion. O Lord, I can never attain equality with You; please forgive this act which was done out of the pride of my penance.

Having praised the Lord of the Ganas in this way, she bowed to Him. O Prajapati, the Lord, who is skilled in speech, spoke to her.

Shri Ganesha said: You will attain My primordial Yoga; there is no doubt about this. O fortunate one, you will always be a Gana-devotee. On the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada, those who are devoted to the great festival and worship Me with devotion, I shall hold you there. When humans offer twenty-one leaves, one leaf among them will be acceptable to Me, O Goddess. There has never been, nor will there ever be, a sin equal to transgression. By the slightest transgression, you were abandoned by Me forever. I constantly abandon those who transgress against Me. Yet, bound by your penance, O Vrinda, I honor you. This hymn composed by you will grant worldly enjoyment and liberation. It will grant the state of Brahman and will be complete for those who read or hear it always. O daughter, those who offer Tulasi to Me, this hymn composed by you will be the remover of their sins. It will especially be the remover of the sin of transgression daily. For those who praise Me with this daily, nothing will be unattainable.

Having said this, the Lord of the Ganas Himself disappeared. O Prajapati, Tulasi became filled with joy. From that time on, that fortunate one became free from distress. Gradually, Tulasi became the pure wife of Vishnu.

Thus ends the thirty-second chapter titled The Granting of the Boon to Tulasi, in the Lambodara Charita of the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana.

Chapter 33 : The Description of the Prohibition of Offering Tulsi

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Daksha said: O great Yogi, tell me the fruit of worshipping the Lord of the Ganas (Ganesha) with Tulsi, whether done through ignorance or knowledge. O Lord, I am not satisfied even after hearing the nectar of your stories. Indeed, devotion to Ganesha is rare; there is no doubt about this.

Mudgala said: I will tell you a story that happened in the past. By hearing it, all your doubts will be destroyed. Once, a foolish Brahmin was born in the lineage of Kashyapa. He stubbornly worshipped Ganesha daily with Tulsi leaves. Other Brahmins there specifically forbade him, but he did not heed their words, speaking instead with a perverse intellect. He argued, "If Ganesha is the embodiment of all, then why do you impose rules and prohibitions? Do not speak to me of such ritualistic constraints."

Then, all those excellent Brahmins became anxious and fearful. O Prajapati, the devotees of Ganesha among them said to one another, "Alas! If Tulsi is seen on the body or head of the Lord of the Ganas, it must be removed immediately by the devotees. If others do not do so, they are undoubtedly headed for hell. Even if one is impure or wearing footwear, one must act quickly; otherwise, one becomes a participant in the sin."

They asked, "What should other Brahmins do? This wicked man must be dealt with." Just as mountains and others suffer when struck by a thunderbolt, Ganesha himself is pained by Tulsi. Having decided this, they expelled the great sinner from the Ganesha temple, and he remained in his own home, continuing his ways. After a short time, that wretch of the Kashyapa lineage died, and the messengers of Yama immediately cast him into the hells.

He remained in hell for a period equal to a Kalpa. After suffering all the consequences, he was born as a terrible demon. Always consumed by hunger and suffering from burning diseases, and unable to perform any tasks, he wandered in the forests. Once, the great sage Gargya, a chief devotee of Ganesha and a great Yogi, arrived there by his own will. Seeing him, the wicked demon rushed to devour him. Gargya sprinkled water sanctified by the Ganesha mantra upon him.

By the merit of the touch of that water and the sight of Gargya, the demon, who had been a destroyer of Dharma, suddenly remembered his past life. Filled with extreme sorrow, he wept, bowed down to the sage, and with folded hands, cried out in distress.

The demon said: Protect me, O tiger among sages, O glorious devotee of Ganesha. I am devoted to your feet; please liberate me from this ocean of worldly existence. The demon then narrated his entire past life to Gargya, trembling with fear. He said, "By your sight and the sanctified water, I have regained the memory of my past life. I tell you of my suffering."

Seeing him seeking refuge, the compassionate great sage gave him the Ganesha name-mantra, which is beneficial. The great Yogi said to him, "Chant this name-mantra daily, and also recite the hymn composed with Tulsi." Having said this, the highly sanctified Gargya disappeared. The wicked demon followed these instructions daily, and seeing his own misery, he became filled with fear.

Then, the hunger of that evil-minded one was pacified. He became free from disease, strong, and a beloved devotee of Ganesha. In the end, free from sorrow, he went to the Lord of the Ganas. There, through his yogic nature, he worshipped the Lord of Obstacles.

I have told you this entire story in brief, which destroys all doubts regarding the prohibition of Tulsi. The person who offers Tulsi to the Lord of the Ganas is undoubtedly like a Chandala and is destined for the lands of hell. His lineage is destroyed, his wealth is lost, and he is severely afflicted by diseases and other torments, eventually going to hell. One should not touch such a person; if one does, one must bathe. If one does not perform a bath with clothes on, one becomes a sinner, knowing this to be true. O Lord of the people, even his name should not be taken, as it brings sin. What more is there to say? He is remembered as the embodiment of sin.

I have told you everything you asked for exactly as it is. Do you wish to hear any other story now?

Chapter 34 : The Story of Rukmangada

Daksa said: Please describe in detail the worship of Ganesha, both mental and external, which grants all accomplishments. Tell me about the realm of Ganesha and its greatness. What is this realm like, O Sage, and by whom is it attained?

Mudgala said: I shall tell you an ancient, auspicious, and highly significant history that grants all accomplishments. Long ago, I told you about the place of the great Ganesha, which originated from the boon of Kartikeya and consists of one hundred thousand forms of Ganesha. There was a king named Aila, who was well-versed in all scriptures, knowledgeable in the science of weapons, righteous, and truthful. He was a constant devotee of Ganesha and worshipped the Lord of Obstacles. He was a famous emperor and a great lover of the Ganesha tradition.

The sage Gargya, who was knowledgeable in all matters, arrived there. The illustrious King Aila bowed to him, worshipped him, and asked: O Master, you are a devotee of the Lord of Ganas and possess such great yoga. Are you not Ganesha himself in person? There is no doubt. In the incarnation of Mayuresha, you remained with Mayuresha, and you, O Brahmin, have witnessed Mayuresha. You are blessed among yogis, for you remain in great joy, having made the Lord of Ganas your friend. By the command of Mayuresha, O best of knowers of yoga, you composed the commentary on the Ganesha Gita, which bestows supreme peace. Through your commentary, the Brahmins and others who know the Gita have become followers of Ganesha and are considered your disciples, O Sage. Just as it was spoken by the Lord of Ganas, you have understood it; there is no doubt that you are considered Ganesha himself. The essence of the Ganesha Gita and Ganesha himself, the most excellent one—you three are considered by the scriptures to be known through complete yoga. By whatever blessed fortune I have seen your lotus feet, my education, vows, penance, charity, parents, and lineage are all blessed. My fame, kingdom, and everything are blessed by the sight of your feet. One does not get to see yogis through little merit. Now, O Brahmin, tell me the method of worshipping Ganesha, both external and internal, according to the Ganesha tradition.

Being asked thus, the great yogi Gargya, who was knowledgeable in all matters, spoke to him with great joy. Gargya said: I shall tell you an ancient history, O King, concerning the life of the Brahmin Gritsamada. He was a man of great penance and knew the supreme truth. His wife, Mukunda, was beautiful and youthful. While they were both in the forest, King Rukmangada, whose mind was intent on hunting, arrived there by chance. Hungry and thirsty, the king had lost his companions; he entered the hermitage, bowed to the Brahmin, and stayed there with his wife. At that time, the illustrious Gritsamada left the hermitage for his midday duties. After the sage had gone, the king said, "Give me water, Mother Mukunda, for I am exhausted and distressed by hunger and thirst." Hearing his words, the sage's wife was overwhelmed. Seeing the noble king, she was struck by the arrows of desire and said: O Prince, I have never seen a man like you anywhere in the three worlds. Therefore, O noble one, enjoy me, for I am overwhelmed. If not, my life will depart, struck by the arrows of desire. Having said this, she tried to kiss the king by force.

The king was greatly agitated; he pushed her away and spoke these words to the sage's wife, who was intent on adultery: O Goddess, Brahmins are the gurus of all; there is no doubt about this. We are all like your sons, O sinner. Those who are attached to the Lord of Obstacles never deviate; when they see another man's wife, they are taught to view her as a mother. Having rebuked her thus, the king left, filled with sorrow. Deluded by desire, she cursed the king: "Become a leper, you wicked man, for you reject a woman who desires you. You are hard-hearted and evil-minded." By the power of her curse, he became a white leper. Rukmangada went into the forest and remembered the elephant-faced Lord.

Then, at night, the great yogi Narada arrived there. Seeing him, the king bowed first and asked with humility: O Lord, you who know all dharma and are dear to the Ganesha tradition, tell me of a pilgrimage site, a place, or a medicine that can cure leprosy. Narada said: Lord Ganesha was worshipped by Indra through penance. He became the bestower of boons to destroy the thousand marks of sin. By the boon of Ganesha, the great Indra became filled with splendor and, endowed with yoga, protected the three worlds, both moving and stationary. There is a famous pilgrimage site marked by the feet of Ganesha, where the Chintamani Ganesha was established. Go there, O King, and bathe immediately. You will become free of leprosy and golden-hued; there is no doubt. By the power of that pilgrimage, those who are free of leprosy go to Svananda; there is no doubt, O King. Having said this, the great yogi Narada, remembering the Lord of Ganas, departed, and the king became filled with joy.

Then, in the morning, the king's army arrived. Accompanied by them, he went to Vidarbha, seeking the pilgrimage site. Seeing the pilgrimage site of Chintamani, O King, he became free of leprosy and everyone rejoiced. Then, having bathed with his army according to the prescribed rites, the prince worshipped Chintamani with joy. Having worshipped him according to the rules and given all kinds of charity, the king remained in the pavilion, filled with devotion to Ganesha. Suddenly, a chariot arrived, bestowing happiness. The messengers of Ganesha said to the noble king: "O best of kings, board this chariot with your army and go to the realm of Svananda, through the power of bathing in this pilgrimage site."

The illustrious and wise king, a follower of Ganesha, bowed to them and said: "How can I leave my father, my beautiful mother, and the Lord of Obstacles to go in such a hurry?" The messengers spoke to him specifically about the Lord of Ganas: "O King, perform the bath for them in this pilgrimage site now. We will take them too; do not worry, O best of men. This pilgrimage site is said to grant Svananda by merely bathing." Then, the king, filled with joy, took Kusha grass, tied a knot in their names, and performed the bath according to the rules. Then, the people of the city, from children to the lowest, were filled with joy and also bathed. Then, they boarded the chariot and went to Kaundinya city. Having given merit to all of them, they took them and went to Svananda. Thus, by the power of the pilgrimage, the people of the city, O King, went, saw the Lord of Obstacles, and became one with the Brahman.

The righteous Rukmangada, a devotee of Ganesha, became free of obstacles in a short time, even though he had been deceived. Those wicked people who deceive a staunch devotee of Ganesha have no redemption and suffer terrible misery. Hear, O King, the fate that befell Mukunda after she deceived Rukmangada.

Chapter 35 : The Meeting of Gritsamada and Ganesha

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Gargya said: Mukunda, scorched by the fire of lust, fell to the earth. Nothing in the world brought her any comfort. Overwhelmed by deep sorrow, she remained lost in thought until she fell asleep. Then, the Lord of the Gods (Indra) awakened her. To grant her peace, he himself had come there in the form of Rukmangada and enjoyed her company. Filled with joy and carrying a child in her womb, she returned home, and Indra, the King of Gods, returned to heaven through the sky.

The sage Vachaknavi returned after completing his daily rituals, unaware of Mukunda’s story, as he was absorbed in his penance. When the pregnancy reached its term, she gave birth to an excellent son. The great sage performed all the birth rituals and named the child Gritsamada, who brought joy to all. In the fifth year, he joyfully performed the sacred thread ceremony. He taught him the Vedas, and for the sake of his learning, he himself initiated him into the mantra beginning with "Gananam Tva."

Meditating on the Lord of Obstacles with that mantra, the sage Gritsamada remained devoted to daily chanting and pleased Him with his sincerity. Once, King Magadha invited the virtuous, ascetic, and illustrious Gritsamada to his father’s funeral rites. There, sages like Vashistha, who were masters of yoga, also arrived and were duly honored. During a discussion on the scriptures, Gritsamada spoke about yoga while in a state of non-yogic detachment. Hearing this, Atri said to him, "You are a non-yogi among yogis, and you are the son of Rukmangada. You are not fit to sit in this assembly; go back to your hermitage."

Filled with great anger, Gritsamada went to his hermitage and, with bloodshot eyes, asked his mother, "Tell me, who is my father? You shameless, lustful woman! If you do not tell me, I shall curse you." Fearing the curse, she trembled and narrated the entire story of her past affair with Rukmangada. Hearing this, the son, consumed by rage, cursed his mother, "Mother, may you become a thorny tree, obsessed with wicked deeds. People will not eat your bitter fruits, and you shall always be despised in the forest, suffering for the sins you committed."

Then, the mother, filled with anger, also cursed her son, "A very terrible and great demon will be born from you." O King, Mukunda then abandoned her body and became a thorny, dark-colored tree, addicted to sinful acts. Because she had caused a disturbance to a devotee of Ganesha, she had incurred a great sin, and due to that, she remained cursed for a long time. In every kalpa, due to that curse, she was reborn as a tree. O King, behold the terrifying nature of obstacles.

Gritsamada, deeply disturbed, prepared to abandon his own life. Then, a voice from the sky spoke to him with affection, "You were born from Indra, who had assumed the form of Rukmangada. Worship the Lambodara (Ganesha) daily, and you shall attain happiness." Hearing this, he went to the forest and performed severe penance. Remaining without food, he constantly worshipped Ganesha. He bathed the deity, who resided in the mandala, with the Brahmanaspati hymns and chanted with devotion to attain the status of a Brahmin.

Aila asked: "O Lord, what is the nature of the mandala of the Brahmanaspati hymns? What fruit does a human attain from it? Tell me the procedure, for you are all-knowing and compassionate, so that the Lord of Obstacles may be pleased and grant all desires."

Gargya said: "Listen, O wise one, to the method of the Brahmanaspati hymns, through which Gritsamada attained perfection. O King, one should perform the nyasa on sixteen parts of the body with the 'Gananam Tva' mantra and then perform the worship and the ritual bathing. O King, twenty-one repetitions of the hymns are prescribed for both the bathing and the chanting. One should know that a mandala consists of twenty-one such sets of twenty-one repetitions. If there are twenty-one such mandalas, the wise call it a 'Maha-Mandala.' O best of kings, whether used in bathing or chanting, this Maha-Mandala is considered by the gods to be the bestower of all perfections. It is excellent for achieving dharma, wealth, desire, and liberation, and it pleases Ganesha, leading one to the state of Brahman.

The great-souled Gritsamada performed this Maha-Mandala of hymns to attain the status of a Brahmin. I have told you this, O King; now listen to the story of how Gritsamada attained the Lord of Obstacles and had his sins removed. Gritsamada himself worshipped the Lord of Obstacles daily according to the rules, using the 'Gananam Tva' mantra. O King, he performed twenty-one such rituals daily for the bathing and then became absorbed in chanting. He chanted the 'Gananam Tva' mantra one hundred and twenty-one times daily, with his gaze fixed on the tip of his nose, and through his intense penance, he pleased the Lord of Obstacles.

After a thousand divine years, the Lord of Obstacles, moved by his love for his devotee, appeared before the sage who was absorbed in meditation."

Shri Ganesha said: "Behold me, O best of sages, I have come as Brahmanaspati. Ask for boons from me, and I shall grant them to you with respect."

Hearing the words of Ganesha, Gritsamada was overjoyed. Opening his eyes, he saw Dhundhi (Ganesha) standing nearby. Filled with great joy, he bowed to Vinayaka, worshipped him with devotion, and praised him with tear-filled eyes.

Chapter 36 : The Granting of Boons to Gritsamada

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Gritsamada said: Salutations to You, O Lord of the Brahmanas, the very form of Brahman, the Lord of Speech (Brahmanaspati), O Ganesha, salutations to You again and again. Salutations to You, the King of the eldest, the bestower of the highest status, the form of the eldest among the elders, and the one worthy of worship by all. Salutations to You, the form of the doer among the doers, the poet, the leader of poets, the giver of power to the doers, and the King of poets. Salutations to You, the knower of all Brahmanas in the worlds, the provider of various enjoyments, the consumer of offerings, and the consumer of Brahman. Salutations to You, who are always associated with illusion and the creator of illusion, the life-force, the Lord of the great, and Brihaspati. Salutations to You, who always dwell in the bliss of Brahman, the form of the Supreme Soul, who dwell in the heart, the knower of Sankhya, and the friend of Brihaspati. Salutations to You, who are the form of self-realization, the one who holds yoga in self-bliss, and the union of all living beings and Brahman. Salutations to You, who are Truth, the form of yoga, beyond mind and speech, the auspicious one, and the Truth of Truths. Salutations to You, the nourisher of all, the nectar-formed Soma, the giver of Soma to the Somas, and the Lord of nourishment. Salutations to You, the creator of the rising creation, the protector, the destroyer, the embodiment of the three Vedas, the fourth state, and beyond the fourth state. Salutations to You, the helper of Indra and other gods, the essence of the protector of Dharma, and the Lord of Dharma. Salutations to You, the giver of kingdoms to all, the King of kings, the one without a kingdom, the Supreme Lord, and the savior across the ocean of worldly existence. Salutations to You, the giver of yoga to the yogis, the yoga of yoga, the giver of peace, and the Lord of those who dwell in eternal peace. Thus, there are many forms of Brahman according to the Vedas; salutations to You, the Lord of all these forms. O Lord of the Ganas, how can I praise You, the embodiment of Brahmanaspati? Be pleased, O Lord of Brahman, salutations to You by Your grace.

Having praised Ganesha in this manner, O King, Gritsamada, with eyes blooming with joy, danced, overwhelmed by love. Seeing him, the Lord of the Ganas spoke with a deep voice: "Satisfied by this hymn, I grant you a boon; ask for it now. This hymn composed by you will be the bestower of all accomplishments, and there is no doubt it will increase devotion to Me. By becoming peaceful, you will worship Me, and through this hymn, you will become one with Brahman. By reading or hearing this, O Sage, one will attain the increase of sons, grandsons, wealth, and grain."

Hearing these words, the sage became attentive. Controlling his intense joy, he bowed to the Elephant-faced Lord. With faltering words, the great sage Gritsamada, the king of devotees, spoke to Ganesha.

Gritsamada said: My parents are blessed, my birth is blessed, and my penance and age are fruitful because of the sight of Your lotus feet. Oh, the Lord of Ganesha, the Lord of Brahman, has appeared before my eyes. You are beyond mind and speech, yet You are not devoid of them. Seeing You, the Lord of Ganesha, what shall I ask for? Still, I ask for a boon to fulfill Your command. The wise have declared that those who are established in Brahman are Brahmanas; therefore, O Lord, make me a Brahmana, a sage among yogis, and worthy of worship. Just as You are the Lord of Brahmanas, may I become a leader of those who are established in Brahman, a guru of the gurus of yogis. O Elephant-faced Lord, grant me firm devotion to Your lotus feet and keep me always in the company of those devoted to Ganesha. Make me famous among the devotees of Ganesha; there is no one like Gritsamada who holds the nature of a devotee of Ganesha.

Having said this, the great sage bowed to the Lord of Ganesha. Seeing his wonderful devotion, Ganesha spoke.

Shri Ganesha said: You have performed the japa of the mantra "Ganaanaam Tvaa," and you shall be the chief among those who use that hymn. Become the Rishi of that mantra and that hymn, and especially, become the best of Brahmanas. In the beginning of the sacrifice, people will remember you, and then they will remember Me, the deity; thus, become the chief in My worship. O Great Sage, there shall be no one equal to you among My devotees. The Brahmanas, led by Atri, will undoubtedly honor you. If one does not remember you while chanting My Vedic mantra, their chanting will be fruitless. Shiva, Vishnu, Brahma, the Brahmanas, Shesha, and the demons—all will always bow to you. If anyone insults you, I, being angry, will burn the entire universe. I will always dwell in your heart, longing for your devotion. The place where you performed penance will be the best, My own sacred place, the "Pushpaka," which grants all accomplishments. It nourishes enjoyment, liberation, the state of Brahman, and devotion; therefore, this place is known as "Pushpaka," sheltered by Me. Whatever you think of, O wise one, will be fruitful. Upon your remembrance, I will manifest for great tasks.

Having said this, the Lord of Brahmanaspati vanished. Gritsamada remained there, feeling a sense of loss. Then, along with the Brahmanas, he installed the Lord of Obstacles, naming Him "Brahmanaspati," and established Him in that sacred place. Then, the yogi Gritsamada became honored by all, a devotee of Brahmanaspati, and was worshipped by the Brahmanas. Everything happened just as the Elephant-faced Lord had said. The sage worshipped that Vinayaka there, O King. He performed mental and external worship daily. Gritsamada, the great devotee of Ganesha, shone everywhere. This story of Gritsamada, which grants all accomplishments and destroys all sins, is holy; by hearing or reading it, men attain everything.

Chapter 37 : Description of the Daily Rituals of Gritsamada

Aila said: O Great Yogi, how did the foremost among the devotees of Ganesha, the great sage Gritsamada, perform his daily worship in two ways? Tell me this in detail, for he is indeed blessed among Brahmins, having been made a sage of his own hymn by Ganesha himself.

Gargya said: Listen to the conduct of that great sage, which is rooted in devotion, and how he worshipped the elephant-faced Lord daily with reverence. Rising in the morning, he would bow to his Guru in his mind and worship the Lord of the Ganas within his heart. Remembering the Ganesha-devotees who embody divine glory, he would seek forgiveness from the Earth before placing his feet upon her. He performed all his purificatory rites according to the scriptures, cleaned his teeth, and bathed according to the prescribed rules.

Listen, O King, to how he performed the internal bath, which I shall describe as it grants perfection and destroys the impurities of ignorance. He would meditate on the elephant-faced, disease-free Lord seated in the thousand-petaled lotus (Sahasrara), the four-armed Lord who is the size of a thumb-joint. He meditated on Him holding the noose and the goad, complete with the tusk and the gesture of fearlessness, three-eyed, adorned with ornaments and priceless garments. He would visualize a supreme, holy river originating from the tip of the Lord's great toe, flowing everywhere. With this, the wise sage would wash away all the internal impurities of ignorance from his mind. The noble person who performs this internal bath becomes free from all stains and attains both worldly enjoyment and supreme liberation.

Thereafter, the external bath is described as twofold: Vedic and Tantric. First, one should perform the Vedic bath according to the rules prescribed in one's own branch of the Vedas. Then, one should perform the Tantric bath using one's own root mantra. Thus purified, one becomes fit for all rituals and equal to the gods. O best of kings, the great sage Gritsamada performed all these rituals daily, filled with devotion, desiring only the pleasure of Ganesha.

Wearing clean clothes, he performed the Vedic Sandhya, and then the Ganesha-Sandhya derived from the Agamas, which grants all perfections. The wise have declared the sipping of water (Achamana) to be of three kinds: Vedic, Tantric, and Puranic, all of which grant great success. The Vedic is performed with the Vyahritis, the Tantric with the root mantra, and the Puranic with the name of Ganesha ending in the dative case.

Listen now to the Puranic method, which is highly purifying, as performed by the illustrious sage Gritsamada: Ganesha, Dhundhiraja, Heramba, Vakratunda, Shurpakarna, Vighnesha, Chintamani, Gajanana, Lambodara, Ekadanta, Brahmanaspati, Vinayaka, Jyeshtharaja, Vikata, Kapila, Dharanidhara, Ashapuraka, Mahodara, Dhumraketu, Mayuresha, and Svanandavasakaraka.

With three names, one should sip water; with two, wash the hands; with two, touch the lips; with one, touch the mouth; then touch the feet, head, and chin with the names, O King. With two, touch the right and left nostrils; with two, the eyes and ears; with one, the navel and the heart; and with one name each, touch the forehead and the arms. The person who performs this Achamana while reciting these names truly invokes Ganesha himself upon this earth.

Thereafter, he would perform the Nyasa, install the water vessel (Kalasha), perform the worship of the seat (Pitha-puja), and then the Mantra-nyasa. O King, the great sage Gritsamada, though himself god-like, performed all these rituals daily according to the path laid down in the Agamas. Meditating on the elephant-faced Lord in his heart, the great yogi, well-versed in the rituals of Ganesha, performed his mental worship with a steady mind. A steady mind is considered the supreme cause of mental worship, and there is no distinction of caste or stage of life in this, O best of kings. In the scriptures, those with a steady mind—whether they belong to a caste or stage of life or are beyond them—always have the right to perform this.

Thus ends the thirty-seventh chapter of the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, titled "The Description of the Daily Rituals of Gritsamada."

Chapter 38 : The Description of Mental Worship as Told by Gṛtsamada

Salutations to Lord Gaṇeśa.

Gṛtsamada said: O Lord of Obstacles, hearing the wonderful praises sung by the bards and eulogists, O Elephant-faced One, arise! Make this world auspicious at the dawn of the day.

Having been prayed to by me in this way, Lord Gaṇeśa rises from the heart and steps outside. Seeing Him depart, the gods led by Śiva, the foremost of yogis, and I myself, bow down to Him.

O Heramba, I mentally prepare water for Your ablutions and for the cleansing of Your teeth. After wiping Your lotus-like face with a cloth, I seat You in the assembly.

Having seen You greeted by the sages like Śuka and others, and having conversed with You, I prepare a beautiful pavilion and seat You there.

I see the Lord of Beginnings reflected in the light of brilliant gems within my mind. I conceive of a throne adorned with gold and jewels and seat the Lord upon it.

O Lord of Obstacles, along with Siddhi and Buddhi, please accept with love the water for Your feet, offered by me mentally, which is fragrant and pleasantly warm.

O Gaṇeśa, I wipe Your feet with a pure cloth and worship You with sacred grass and other offerings. O Friend of the distressed, who loves devotion, make my heart dissolve into Your lotus feet.

O Elephant-faced One, please accept the water scented with camphor and cardamom that I have prepared. Having sipped it, look upon me quickly with Your compassionate glance.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, O Dhundhi, please accept this priceless and fruitful offering (Arghya) made of coral, pearls, and necklaces, prepared with inner devotion.

O Vināyaka, please accept the pure Madhuparka (honey-mixture) that I have conceived with devotion. Having sipped water, quickly protect Your devotees, O Lord of the faithful.

O Dhundhi, I have prepared oil mixed with substances like Champaka and Jātī flowers. Please accept this offering; I massage Your entire body with it for Your pleasure.

O Dhundhi, I bathe You mentally with pure, warm water brought from all the sacred rivers. Please accept this bath offered by me with a pure heart.

Then, O Gaṇeśa, please accept a bath of milk, followed by a bath of water, and then a bath of curd, O Lord who removes all ailments.

Then, O Destroyer of Obstacles, please accept a bath of ghee from sacred waters. Be pleased, and accept this bath I have mentally prepared, followed by a bath of honey and water.

O Dhundhi, please accept a bath of sugar-water prepared by me. Then, O Gaṇeśa, accept a bath of pure water to wash away all sins and illusions.

O Lord of Lords, please accept a bath of sacred clay, and then a bath of pure water from the Kamandalu (water pot) that I have prepared for You.

I bathe You with milk and sacred waters while reciting hymns, worshipping You with sacred grass and countless other offerings, and performing an ablution with the power of the Bṛhaspati mantras.

Then, O Lord of Obstacles, please accept the drying of Your body with a fine cloth that I have mentally prepared, and then sip pure water, O Dhundhi.

Please accept the garments I have mentally offered, which are purer than fire and beyond price. Wearing them, O Mayūreśa, protect all people.

O Lord of Obstacles, having sipped water, please accept the upper garment I have offered with devotion. Protect it as the sky protects the stars.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, please accept the sacred thread (Yajñopavīta) made of gold and threads, representing the three qualities, offered by me for Your sustenance.

O Dhundhi, having sipped water from the sacred pot I offered, protect the universe with Your divine play.

O Heramba, please accept the vermilion I have mentally offered, which shines like the rising sun. Apply it to Your entire body with care.

O Lord of Brahmā, please accept the golden crown I have mentally offered, adorned with countless gems, to grace Your head.

O Dhundhi, please accept the various earrings I have mentally offered, adorned with gold and strange gems, to decorate Your ears.

O Lord, please accept the golden armor adorned with gems that I have offered for Your trunk; make it fruitful.

O Dhundhi, please accept the ornaments for Your single tusk, made of gold and gems, which I have offered for Your beauty.

O Lord, Destroyer of Obstacles, please accept the four bracelets I have prepared and offered for Your four arms.

O Lord, please accept the rings I have offered for Your fingers, adorned with various gems and gold.

O Lord of the Pramathas, please accept the armlets (Keyūras) I have prepared and offered for Your arms, adorned with various gems and gold.

O Lord of Obstacles, please accept the various necklaces made of coral, pearls, and gems strung on gold threads, which I have offered to adorn Your chest and belly.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, adorn Your forehead with the moon, which is free from waxing and waning, and reveal Your love for Your devotees.

O Lord, place the wish-fulfilling Cintāmaṇi gem upon Your heart to make it radiant. O Lord of Obstacles, protect the distressed with this gem that grants eternal joy.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, place the thousand-headed serpent around Your waist as a girdle. Protect Your devotee with this ornament.

O Lord of Compassion, I mentally place a golden, gem-studded girdle around Your waist. Protect me, Your devotee.

O Gaṇeśa, please accept the anklets and bells I have offered for Your feet, which chime beautifully.

In this way, I mentally decorate Your limbs with various ornaments made of gems and metals according to my devotion.

O Single-tusked One, please accept the red sandalwood paste, ground with eight types of fragrances, which I have prepared for Your body.

I create beautiful patterns on Your limbs with the eight fragrances. O Vināyaka, protect me, and apply the round red mark on Your forehead.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, O Friend of the distressed, please accept the red rice grains mixed with ghee and saffron that I have offered on Your forehead. Protect Your devotees.

O Gaṇeśa, please accept the Champaka, Mālatī, and lotus flowers, as well as the red flowers and Mallikā blossoms I have offered.

O Heramba, please accept the Mandāra and Śamī leaves I have mentally offered, which are countless and represent the form of Praṇava (Om).

O Lord of Obstacles, please accept the sacred grass (Dūrvā) I have mentally offered, with three or five leaves, which are white and fresh.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, please accept the incense I have offered to the fire. Protect me, O Lord, and accept it along with Siddhi and Buddhi.

O Lord of unerring vision, please accept the lamp I have offered with devotion, made of camphor and oil.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, please accept the vast and varied offerings of food—eatable, lickable, drinkable, and suckable—that I have mentally prepared for Your nourishment.

O Gaṇeśa, please drink the scented water I have offered in the middle of the meal. O Satisfier of the universe, drink it.

Then, O Dhundhi, please accept the fragrant paste for cleansing Your mouth, prepared with devotion.

O Lord of all, having sipped the scented water I offered, please accept the cleaning of Your hands and face.

O Dhundhi, please accept the fruits I have offered—grapes, bananas, mangoes, dates, jujubes, and pomegranates—which are full of flavor.

O Gaṇeśa, I mentally wash Your hands again with water. Please drink the scented water I have offered.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, please accept the betel leaf (Tāmbūla) I have offered for the purification of Your mouth.

O Lord, please rest on the great throne I have prepared, covered with priceless cloths of cotton and Śamī-fiber.

O Lord of the helpless, I mentally worship Your attendants with various offerings out of devotion.

O Lambodara, please accept the countless offerings of gold and gems I have mentally given as Dakṣiṇā (offering). Protect this entire universe.

O Lord of the Gaṇas, please accept the royal honors I have offered—the umbrella, horses, elephants, and all moving and non-moving beings.

O Heramba, please accept the various forms of charity I have offered—cows, land, and all else. Deliver me from the illusion of the world.

O Heramba, Lambodara, Protector of the distressed, I offer You Mandāra flowers, Śamī leaves, and Dūrvā grass. Accept me and keep me at Your feet.

O Lord of Brahmā, please accept the turmeric, red powder, and vermilion I have offered for Your beauty.

Then, the sages led by Śuka, the gods led by Viṣṇu and Śiva, and the serpent-lords bow down to You as You sit in the assembly.

Siddhi, who is the very form of illusion and ultimate reality, serves You with devotion on Your left side.

Buddhi, the embodiment of knowledge and the arts, serves You on Your right side, O Lord of Brahmā.

The attendants led by Pramoda serve You from behind, while the devotees led by Mudgala, Śiva, and Śuka serve You from the front.

The Gandharvas sing sweet songs, and the Apsarās perform beautiful dances before You.

In this way, I worship the Lord of Obstacles, who is served by all. I perform the waving of lights (Āratī) while meditating upon You.

I meditate upon You, O Gaṇeśa, who has four arms, holding the noose, the goad, and the tusk, having three eyes, a large belly, and an elephant face, bestowing fearlessness.

I meditate upon You, adorned with a serpent-thread, elephant ears, and lotus feet served by all, worshipped with all powers.

Then, I perform the Japa of Your root mantra mentally and offer the count to You, O Dhundhi.

I perform the Āratī with countless lamps of camphor. O Lambodara, accept it, for it removes the darkness of ignorance for Your own people.

O Gaṇeśa, please accept the flowers offered with the sacred mantras, which I have mentally presented to You.

O Single-tusked One, please accept the hymns I have mentally composed from the Vedas, Smṛtis, and Purāṇas.

O Lambodara, please accept the countless mental circumambulations I offer. Protect Your servants from the ocean of worldly existence.

O Lord of Obstacles, please accept my prostrations, both the eight-limbed and others, which I have performed mentally. Protect me along with Siddhi and Buddhi.

O Lord of Obstacles, if there is any deficiency in my worship, please accept these Dūrvā sprouts and make my worship complete.

O Lord of Obstacles, forgive my various faults. Make my devotion fruitful, I pray to You mentally, O Gaṇeśa.

I bow my head to the Prasad (blessed offering) given by the pleased Elephant-faced One and place it upon my head. I bow to You, O Lord of Obstacles.

O Gaṇeśa, having risen and left the assembly, You depart to Your divine abode. I bow to You and meditate upon You.

Having bowed to all, I worship the Lord of the Gaṇas with devotion. I return to my place and play with the great devotees of Gaṇeśa.

In this way, I worship the Lord of the Gaṇas mentally three times a day. May He be pleased and grant me great devotion.

May the water from Your feet, the remnants of Your sandalwood paste, the flowers, and the remnants of Your meal be my sustenance.

May everything I do be an offering to You, O Lord. Be pleased, O merciful Lord of Brahmā, and make me a devotee of Your feet.

I prepare a priceless bed adorned with Mandāra flowers and cotton cloths. O Lord of Obstacles, please accept it and rest.

Seeing You resting with Siddhi and Buddhi, I go to my own place and sleep, meditating upon You in my heart.

O Lord of unerring power, grant me such mental happiness. By this, I become fulfilled and relish the nectar of devotion.

Gārgya said: Thus, O King, the illustrious Gṛtsamada, the teacher of the greatest yogis, performed this mental worship daily.

Any excellent person who performs this mental worship or reads it will become a true devotee of Gaṇeśa.

Any mortal who listens to this with devotion or causes others to listen will, in due course, become one with Brahman.

Whatever they desire will be fulfilled, and in the end, they will attain the abode of supreme bliss, worshipped even by the yogis.

Thus ends the thirty-eighth chapter of the Lambodara-carita in the fifth section of the Mudgala-purāṇa, titled "The Description of Mental Worship as Told by Gṛtsamada."

Chapter 39: Description of the External Worship as Taught by Gritsamada

Aila asked: O Lord, please describe to me the external worship as taught by Gritsamada, so that I may constantly worship the Lord of Obstacles (Ganesha) in that manner.

Gargya replied: After performing the mental worship, the sage Gritsamada performed the external worship according to the prescribed rites. Listen to this, for it bestows happiness. Meditating upon Ganesha along with his divine family within his heart, he brought the deity out through the path of his nostrils to the external form. First, he recited the Vedic mantra "Ganaanaam Tva," and then, chanting the following verses, he worshipped the Lord of Obstacles.

Gritsamada said: I meditate upon the four-armed, three-eyed, elephant-faced Lord, who is of red complexion, adorned with a noose and a goad, and endowed with divine Maya. O Lord of the Vedas, worshipped by the best of gods and demons, come here along with Siddhi and Buddhi, for I am eager to receive your grace. I am truly blessed by your arrival, O Lord; may my life be fruitful, now that I am graced by the Lord of Obstacles.

O Lord of the Ganas, please accept this jeweled throne. Having seated yourself there, O Lord of Obstacles, protect your devotees especially. O Lord, I wash your feet with fragrant, tempered water; please accept this excellent foot-wash (padya). O Lord of the Ganas, please perform your sipping (achamana) with this water brought from all sacred rivers and scented with fine substances. O Heramba, O Elephant-faced Lord, please accept this offering (arghya) made of jewels, corals, and pearls, which brings you joy. O Elephant-faced Lord, please accept this honey-mixture (madhuparka) made of curd, honey, and ghee, offered by me with devotion; salutations to you. After the foot-wash, honey-mixture, bath, clothing, sacred thread, and meal, please perform your sipping again.

O Lord of the Ganas, O Lord of all, O Lambodara, please accept this anointing with excellent oils scented with Champaka and other flowers; salutations to you. O Lord of Obstacles, lover of devotees, please accept this massage with Yaksha-kardama (a fragrant paste) offered by me. O Dhundhi, please accept the bath I offer, consisting of water from various sacred rivers, warmed to a comfortable temperature and poured from a Kamandalu. O Heramba, you who know the ultimate truth, please bathe in this most holy milk from the Kamadhenu. O Lord of Obstacles, O Ocean of Mercy, bathe repeatedly with the five nectars (panchamrita) and the water from the sacred rivers of the divine Kamandalu. O Lord, please accept this curd from the divine cow for your bath, which removes all impurities. O Dhundhi, please bathe with this ghee from the divine cow, which brings satisfaction and removes great impurities. O Vinayaka, please accept this honey, purified by bees and mixed with nectar, for your bath; salutations to you. O Lord of the Ganas, please accept this sugar derived from sugarcane juice, which destroys impurities, and bathe with it. O Lord of the Ganas, please bathe with the Yaksha-kardama paste, which is the final purifier and brings all fragrance.

O Supreme Lord, I offer you sandalwood paste, unbroken rice grains (akshata), and sprouts of Durva grass; please accept them. O Elephant-faced Lord, I perform your ritual bathing (abhisheka) twenty-one times with the Brahmana-spatya hymns; please accept it. O Lord of the Ganas, perform your sipping with the water from all sacred rivers, scented with fine substances. O Lord of Obstacles, please accept these two priceless red garments, which cover the shame of the world; salutations to you. O Lord of all perfections, please accept this multi-colored upper garment, resembling the star-studded sky, which I offer with great devotion. O Lord of the Ganas, please accept this sacred thread, which represents the three Gunas and is tied with the knot of the Pranava (Om).

O Lord of the Ganas, please accept this vermilion for anointing your limbs, O you who increase eternal bliss. O Lord, please accept these various ornaments made of gold and jewels to adorn your entire body. O Elephant-faced Lord, I apply the eight-fold fragrant paste (ashtagandha) and red sandalwood paste on your twelve limbs. O Lord of the Ganas, please accept these unbroken rice grains mixed with red sandalwood or saffron on your forehead. O Elephant-faced Lord, please accept these red flowers like the lotus and Champaka, as well as Shami, Mandara, and Durva grass. O Vinayaka, O Mahodara, please accept this ten-fold incense (guggulu) which brings all fragrance. O Lord of the Ganas, please accept this lamp of various kinds, which is the form of light that destroys the darkness of ignorance and mental impurities. O Lord of Obstacles, I offer you this meal consisting of four types of food, including sweet laddus; please eat. O Lord of Lords, please accept this fragrant water brought from sacred rivers for drinking during your meal; salutations to you. O Lord of the Ganas, after the meal, perform your cleansing with the fragrant paste and drink this scented water. O Lord of Lords, please accept these various sweet fruits such as dates, pomegranates, grapes, and bananas. O Lord of Obstacles, please accept this eight-fold betel leaf (tambula) for mouth-freshening, which I offer to you especially. O Dhundhi, O beloved of all, please accept this sacrificial fee (dakshina) consisting of gold and various precious metals and gems. O Lord of Obstacles, please accept these various royal offerings and gifts that I have presented to you.

O Lord, I now worship your attendants (aavaranas) according to the prescribed rites; may you be pleased with these various offerings. O Dhundhi, for the sake of completeness, please accept these twenty-one sprouts of Durva grass, out of your love for your devotees. O Elephant-faced Lord, please accept this waving of lights (nirajana) offered with devotion, which destroys all ignorance. O Lord of the Ganas, accept this thousand-fold chanting of the "Ganaanaam Tva" mantra and be the bestower of all perfections. O Lord of lights, I perform this waving of camphor lamps and other lights before you; please accept it. I perform the waving of lights four times at your feet, twice at your navel, and once at your face, and then seven times around your entire body. O Elephant-faced Lord, please accept these flowers and leaves consecrated with the Vedic mantras of the Ganapatya tradition. O Lord of the Ganas, I praise you with the five types of Ganapatya hymns; be pleased with this, O bestower of devotion. O Elephant-faced Lord, please accept these twenty-one or three circumambulations (pradakshina), performed with the realization of the unity of Brahman and the Lord of Brahman. O Heramba, O you who are worshipped by all, please accept this full prostration (sashtanga pranama) performed by me twenty-one times. O Lord, I offer these few extra sprouts of Durva grass to compensate for any deficiencies; please accept my worship as complete. I contemplate the remnants of your offering (nirmalya) as given by your own hand; I wear it on my crest, for it is always the bestower of all boons.

O Lord of the Ganas, forgive my countless offenses and make me your devotee, always dear to your feet. You are my mother, you are my father, you are my friend and relative; you are my family deity, and there is no doubt that you are everything to me. I offer to Ganesha all the actions performed by my body, speech, and mind, whether in waking, dreaming, or deep sleep, or through association. By bowing one's head to the holy water of Ganesha's feet, all external and grave sins are destroyed. By drinking the water from Ganesha's feet, a man is instantly cleansed of all sins, even those beyond counting. One who applies the fragrant paste offered to Ganesha on his own twelve limbs becomes equal to Ganesha in form and destroys all sins by his very sight. If one does not have the fragrant paste offered to Ganesha, one should perform the ritual by chanting his name-mantra on the twelve limbs; by doing so, one becomes equal to Ganesha on this earth. One should worship the Lord of the Ganas on the head, the Lord of Obstacles on the forehead, the Curved-trunk Lord (Vakratunda) at the base of the right ear, the Single-tusked Lord (Ekadanta) at the base of the left ear, the Pot-bellied Lord (Lambodara) on the throat, the Wish-fulfilling Gem (Chintamani) on the heart, Heramba on the right arm, the Twisted-form Lord (Vikata) on the left arm, and Vinayaka on the navel. One should worship the Peacock-lord (Mayuresha) on the right side of the belly, the Elephant-faced Lord on the left side, and the Dweller of Svananda (Svananda-vasin) on the back. Anointing the entire body with the eight-fold fragrant paste is highly recommended for the devotees of Ganesha, as it brings all auspiciousness. I consume the remnants of the food offered to Ganesha, for it is the bestower of worldly enjoyment and liberation, and it destroys all sins. By remembering Ganesha, I conquer time itself; may I always dwell in the Ganapatya state, O Elephant-faced Lord.

Gargya said: Thus, the sage Gritsamada performed the external worship three times a day, always filled with devotion. O King, you too perform it in this manner, and you shall become a devotee of Ganesha; just as Gritsamada attained him, you too shall surely attain him.

Chapter 40 : Description of Svananda Loka

Salutations to Shri Ganesha.

Aila said: Having heard the story of Gritsamada, which grants all desires, I am not satisfied, O great Yogi; it is like drinking nectar. I do not see anyone equal to Gritsamada in the realm of Brahma. O giver of honor, he is blessed among yogis and followers of Ganesha. Now, O Lord of Yogis, describe the realm of Ganesha. What is this personal realm of Ganesha like, especially?

Gargya said: I will tell you an ancient history here, containing the dialogue between the sage Mudgala and Kapila. Once, O King, the yogi Mudgala, the foremost among the followers of Ganesha, went to the ashram of Kapila, situated on the banks of the Ganges. After bowing down, the foremost of the Angiras clan, with folded hands and filled with joy, spoke to Kapila.

Mudgala said: You are the direct embodiment of Vishnu, O pure one, the giver of all meanings. You are the chief among the followers of Ganesha and the ultimate destination of the Sankhyas. By the merit of seeing you, I am instantly fulfilled. My lineage, wealth, fame, knowledge, and wisdom are blessed today by your sight.

As he spoke thus, Kapila bowed to him and worshipped the emotion-filled Mudgala with great joy. Then the yogi Kapila spoke these beneficial words: O son, I am purified today by your sight. You alone are the perfect devotee of Ganesha, O great sage. You are truly the master of the Mudgara (hammer) weapon, a follower of Ganesha, there is no doubt. Just as a man, by crushing substances with a hammer, makes them devoid of name and form, so too, O great yogi, have you crushed your ego through penance and yoga, leaving it without a root. Many yogis try to crush their ego through yoga, yet it remains in the heart. But you, O Mudgala, are the only yogi in the world who has truly achieved this. We are not like that, O son, as we follow a single path. Your Puranic work, the Angirasa, will surely be one that is respected by all and will grant yoga, without doubt. Hear, O Mudgala, the reason why Puranas and scriptures are filled with ego. Even when they know the secret, they hide their own opinion and state the truth in a special way. Although you are a devotee of Ganesha, you never keep the Lord of Ganas a secret for special purposes. Your words are supreme and accepted by all, without doubt, because they are devoid of ego and reveal the secrets of the Vedas. The Purana you have created will be the final one and will be the greatest of all Puranas, O Mudgala, you who are the very form of Mudgala. Whoever insults what you have said will be destined for hell, and the fool himself will fall into hell. The Mudgala Purana, created by you, will surely be a great scripture due to its lack of ego. There is no yogi equal to Mudgala, and there will never be a follower of Ganesha as great as him in the three times, this is certain. Welcome, O great one, stay in my presence. I am delighted by your sight, O beloved of Ganapati.

O King, having been honored by Kapila, the great yogi Mudgala stayed there with great devotion. Once, while Kapila, the best of yogis, was sitting comfortably, Mudgala asked him with joy, being humble.

Mudgala said: O Master, the Chintamani has come to your house in the form of a son, the supreme Lord of Brahmas. You have worshipped the Lord of all with constant devotion. You alone know his secret, there is no doubt. You are a great Siddha, equal to none, you are the direct embodiment of Vishnu. You are a follower of Ganesha and the progenitor of Ganesha. Therefore, I ask you, O great Lord, tell me everything. People like you exist for the salvation of others. What is the Svananda abode like, O Master? What is the happiness there? What is its extent, and by what yoga is it attained? No one knows the secret of that realm except you. O Lord of all, tell me about the realm of the follower of Ganesha in detail.

Gargya said: Being asked thus, the great Siddha Kapila smiled, praised him, and spoke with joy.

Kapila said: Listen, Mudgala, I will tell you the greatness of the Svananda realm in brief, which is complete, the essence, and accepted everywhere. When the Lord of Ganas, having killed the invincible Ganasura, was ready to go to his own realm, I prayed to him with great devotion. The Lord of Ganas said to me, "O Yogi, ask for a boon, I will grant it." Then, O Brahmin, I bowed to Chintamani and said with great sorrow, "O Lord of Ganas, tell me the greatness of the Svananda realm. I have heard of many realms, O Lord, but no one knows the Svananda realm where you reside eternally."

Chintamani said: The Svananda, which is the Brahman in the Vedas, O best of yogis, that itself became a great city by Maya in the beginning. It is attained only through self-realized yoga and not otherwise, or by my vows and worship, O great sage. Know, O best of sages, that my realm is not attained by chanting, penance, charity, or various rituals. Vishnu, Shiva, and other yogis like Shuka do not go there because it is pathless and is always considered inaccessible. It is never attained by the four types of emotions; it is undoubtedly inaccessible to them, O wise one. Its extent is ten thousand yojanas, and above that, the same measure is declared everywhere. It is circular and rests on its own foundation. It is always full of light and is called the light of lights. There is nothing with attributes (Saguna) there, nor does the attribute-less (Nirguna) exist. There is no man, no neuter, and no woman there, O best of sages. It is self-realized and situated in my own self, near me. Know, O wise one, that in all four types, it exists with Maya, always wandering as its own form. That which is devoid of delusion in them, which always exists as a witness, is always the supreme, undivided, and attribute-less nature. In the union of the two, there is the bliss of the self, the Dhundhi realm. My devotees stay there with devotion to me. In my realm, O great one, there is no hunger or thirst. My servants always live in the enjoyment of Svananda. There is no artificial happiness there, nor is there non-artificial happiness. Immersed in Svananda, they enjoy everything eternally. They enjoy both Saguna and Nirguna happiness, born of Svananda, at the same time, O wise one. Everything that exists here in various forms, O sage, exists in my realm in the form of light. That which is not here, the Nirguna, exists there eternally in the form of light. Everything born of Svananda exists there eternally. Because of my Maya, it always appears full of wonder in my realm. There is no Saguna there, yet Saguna exists there. There is no Nirguna, yet the Nirguna state exists in my realm. It is always devoid of both Saguna and Nirguna, and therefore my realm is especially full of wonder. I am not there, nor is my nature there, nor am I devoid of emotion. My beloveds always stay in my realm, O great yogi. That which is the nectar of Saguna gives enjoyment, and that which is the nectar of Nirguna is the cause of enjoyment in nectar. In the union of the two, O great yogi, is the nectar of one's own form. The ocean of that essence exists there on its own foundation. From that, various sweet things emerge, which are Saguna; because they are Nirguna, it is called the Ocean of Sugarcane. The water there is of one's own form, red in color. By drinking it, the Nirguna and Saguna nature vanishes. From that, a stream emerged, which is called the stream of honey. Through it, both Saguna and Nirguna exist with satisfaction. There, I always play with Siddhi and Buddhi. My Ganas, such as Brahmapriya, live there. I am always adorned with the trunk and other signs, O sage. My other Ganas and devotees also live there in their own chosen forms. I have told you everything about the great happiness of my realm. See me in your heart, both with and without attributes, O sage.

Thus ends the fortieth chapter of the fifth section of the Mudgala Purana, titled "Description of Svananda Loka."

Chapter 41 : Description of the State of Svananda

Kapila said: O Gajanana, please tell me by what path do those men who are endowed with devotion to You, and who have attained Svananda (the bliss of the Self), travel?

Chintamani said: Those who are engaged in my worship, who are devoted to me, and who are intent upon me, remember me at the end of their lives and attain my realm. O great sage, I send a celestial chariot, adorned with my own form, which is invisible to the gods like Shiva and Vishnu, from my own realm. The devotee, while still in his physical body, ascends it accompanied by my attendants, and is struck with wonder as he beholds all the heavens. Because they lack the eyes of Svananda and are bound by mortal bodies, the gods and yogis cannot see the devotee who is seated in that chariot.

Passing beyond the realm of Shakti, then the solar and Vaishnava realms, and then the Shaiva realm, he finally enters my abode. At first, a darkness born of ignorance is seen, which is not perceived by the man or the attendants due to the nature of the body. By the radiance of the chariot and by remembering me, after traveling ten thousand yojanas, light becomes visible. There, the goddess Bhramari, possessing a fierce form, shines as a luminous entity, striking fear into the ignorant. Know that all fear of birth and death, and all the illusions that create various divisions, are fully constructed by this Maya of mine.

That Bhramari, who herself resides in the foundation of Svananda, causes all beings to revolve. Seeing her, the devotee becomes fearful and remembers me specifically. Then, comforted by the attendants, the best of men is led forward. The Shakti that resides on her head is the foundation of light, which is always fearful to the ignorant. It is the form of the Jiva (individual soul), which always holds the delusion of all differences, and it is also the form of Shiva, the witness, which acts as the foundation.

O great yogi, the foundation of the body and other things is called the Jiva; because it is itself without a foundation, it is the Supreme Self. O great sage, the entire foundation of all is established by her; thus, she is known as the Adhara-Shakti (Foundation Power). Through her, I always appear in two ways in the minds of the ignorant: as the Jivatma (individual soul) and the Paramatma (Supreme Soul), and there, the supreme Maya shines. By meditating on me, the human being transcends this and is led forward by the attendants to behold me.

Then, the Shakti that grants desires, which is of the form of extreme light, shines on her head, O sage, bestowing joy. The union of the soul with that which is full of birth, death, and various differences is known as the Yoga-form. Similarly, O sage, that which is associated with desire is always twofold; thus, the union of the body and the soul became mutually connected. It is known to be full of creation, sustenance, and destruction, full of illusions, full of various divisions, and associated with decay and growth. The soul is always the foundation of immortality, devoid of birth, death, decay, and growth, as the knowers of the Vedas declare.

Why does one desire the soul to be always full of differences? O giver of honor, observe the diversity of the soul when there is opposition. By the word "Asi" (sword/non-existence), that which is associated with desire is always placed in duality; through the influence of that delusion, the world is mutually beneficial. Therefore, that Shakti is called the "Kamada" (grantor of desires), the great enchantress, the sword-like, the expert, and my great Maya. No Shakti other than the Bhramari is seen here as the cause of rotation. Know that the great Maya residing in the "Tvam" (Thou) word is such by its power.

O great yogi, there is no foundation other than the foundation itself. Where is the Shakti? Therefore, she is said to be established in that position. What Shakti is there for the Kamada? There is no one other than the Kamada. She shines even in the position of the "Padamayi" (the word-form), as you are always the form of everything. Know that the realm on her head is my own form, created by my Maya, and is of the nature of my own experience.

Having arrived there, the great devotee drinks the water born from the ocean of sugarcane juice. By that, he immediately assumes a luminous form. Becoming devoid of difference and non-difference, he then beholds me. Through the yoga born of my vision, the man becomes Brahman. Residing in his own realm by his own will, he can wander anywhere. Becoming both Saguna (with attributes) and Nirguna (without attributes), he is always established in the bliss of the Self. O best of sages, after that, he does not have delusion again, as is confirmed by the knowers of the Vedas for hundreds of millions of kalpas.

In the Great Dissolution, the four types of beings attain oneness in me. All those who are associated with ignorance are created by me again. O sage, I have told you all this about the arrival at my realm; know this briefly as the path that leads to supreme accomplishment.

Kapila said: O Gajanana, many people attain Your realm by performing Your worship, and so do the yogis. If they are known as Brahman and never fall, then, O Lord, this world would gradually become depleted. O Lord, why do You create again and again as it depletes? You always play in this complete universe effortlessly.

Chintamani said: Just as I, O sage, especially during the time of war, become infinite in form to protect the world through my Maya, and after killing all the demons and others, I again assume my single form, knowing all states, and assume an avatar for the sake of play; in the same way, I create a new world through the power of Maya. This world is never less or more at any time.

Kapila said: Men and women who have become Brahman shine, established in Svananda. They are infinite, and through Your devotion, they again become travelers to Svananda. O Lord, tell me where they reside, as there must surely be congestion if they always dwell there.

Chintamani said: They dwell in Svananda with reverence until the end of the kalpa. When the Great Dissolution occurs, they become merged in my body. O best of sages, at the beginning of the yuga, I make everything separate, and at the end of the yuga, through the state of yoga, it becomes my form. Those who have come through ignorance are merged into my body through the state of yoga; the wise call that my "Yoganidra" (divine sleep). All the people in Svananda, being endowed with knowledge, merge into my body through the state of yoga, O best of yogis.

O sage, in the creation yuga, the world emerges again effortlessly. There is no doubt that it was merged due to ignorance by my Maya. Those who have come through knowledge into my body never become separate again, nor are they affected by the state of yoga at the beginning of creation. Thus, those who dwell there in Svananda, O best of sages, attain union with my body in every kalpa.

Kapila said: O Gajanana, those who desire Your devotion with reverence always, and not through yoga, but through the feeling of love—tell me where their position is. Where do those highly fortunate devotees, who are countless, reside in every kalpa?

Chintamani said: The measurement of ten thousand yojanas is declared for yogis and those seeking liberation, but never for the devotees. My realm is undoubtedly of infinite yojanas. There, my devotees, eager to serve, dwell in Svananda. My devotees are countless, always immersed in the nectar of love. They worship me in nine ways in every kalpa, having shed all sorrows. O great sage, those who were, who are, and who will be my devotees, always dwell in my presence, bestowing delusion.

O highly fortunate one, there is nothing as dear to me as devotion. Being subservient to devotion, I wander in the presence of my devotees with great bliss. Wherever my devotees stay, I always stay there with all my heart, protecting them, overwhelmed with love. With a nine-fold state of mind, I worship the greatness of the devotee in the proximity of the devotee, always acting as if enchanted. Neither Svananda, nor success, nor wisdom, nor sons are as dear to me as my devotees.

Kapila said: Those attendants of Svananda who have come to take the devotees—they are of the form of light. How are they seen by the devotees?

Chintamani said: O great yogi, what is difficult for my Maya? Tell me. Through my grace, my devotees see them with their physical eyes here. Svananda is of all forms; I will tell you the path there. My attendants are seen as divided by those who are full of division. O sage, to those who are devoid of division and established in the Self, my attendants are seen as Nirguna (without attributes), and they are also seen as self-realized by those established in their own Self. Just as an image appears in water, so they are seen in the entire circle of my realm. I have told you everything about the activities of my realm. Now, O great sage, I shall go to my own realm.

Chapter 42 : Describing the Disappearance of Chintamani

Salutations to Sri Ganesha.

Kapila said: O Leader of the Ganas, if everyone were to possess such great devotion as yours, what would become of the world? When people recognize you as the supreme being, they worship you with their whole heart, there is no doubt about it. Those who seek superior results worship you for quick attainment; why then, O Brahma-knower, do the lords of the gods abandon you and worship others?

Chintamani said: O Sage, I have created this entire world, which is filled with various states of being. Because they are caught up in these various states, people do not always worship me. They first perform excellent worship of the four chief deities, and only after their inner selves are purified by that, do they worship me. Furthermore, hear my words: I have manifested Maya. Deluded by it, they do not know me as the most supreme. When I cover my form with Maya, those who are free from delusion worship me as the Supreme.

I have taken many incarnations in the worlds of the gods and others, and they speak of me according to those forms. They say that the status of being worshipped first and being worshipped by all was given to Ganesha by Shiva. Deluded by my Maya, they say I am born of Parvati, the son of Shiva, and established by Shiva himself. They say that I am merely a form of Shiva's attendant, incapable of granting liberation, and that as the Lord of the Ganas, I am only a fulfiller of desires for the sake of worldly success.

O Sage, I have manifested many such states for the sake of play. Because of this, they abandon me and consider others to be superior. If this were not so, everyone would undoubtedly attain their own bliss, and without delusion, the world would not function until the end of the Kalpa. Furthermore, the entire universe, along with its people, is bound by karma and, due to various determinations, remains caught in the delusion of diverse states. O Great Sage, at the end, those with pure hearts who know the essence of the Vedas and Shastras attain my state by themselves.

I am the Lord of Intellect, and by stirring the intellect, I hide the true form of Ganesha in the hearts of living beings. Because of this, people never truly know me as the Supreme. Hear carefully as I tell you the path of discernment regarding this. In the Krita Yuga, all humans worship me with complete devotion; free from the veil of ignorance, they worship me as the Supreme. In the Treta Yuga, O Sage, people worship me with one foot less; they speak of five superior deities in the form of the Panchayatana. There, they worship me as equal to all, and my knowledge, which declares me superior to the four, is lost. In the Dvapara Yuga, the gods and others worship me with two feet less; they speak of the son of Shankara as inferior to the four. Humans consider others superior in the Vedas, Shastras, and Puranas; due to the delusion of the Yuga, they speak of me as inferior.

What happens when the Vedas say "Everything is Brahman"? The reality is the same, and so is this Leader of the Ganas. In the Kali Yuga, they worship me with knowledge that is three feet less; people say that this Gajanana is born of filth. They say that this Lord of the Ganas is worshipped first only due to Shiva's boon, just as demons and other creatures are also endowed with boons. Because the Vedas say "Everything is Brahman," the great sages describe me as the Remover of Obstacles only by the power of Shiva's boon. This Chintamani is never a god with his own independent reality; he became the first only by the power of Shiva-devotion and the boons he received.

At the end of the Kali Yuga, O Sage, people will not worship me at all. The devotion consisting of one foot will certainly perish. Due to differences of opinion and delusion, they will abandon me at the beginning and worship Shiva, Vishnu, and others as the ones to be worshipped first. Consequently, my status as the one to be worshipped first by all will be lost. In the end, all those burdened with obstacles will rot in hell.

O Great Sage, I have established these divisions of karma, non-karma, and forbidden karma here for the sake of the Yuga cycle. If I did not do this, then my boons, the karmas, and the Yugas would all become fruitless. O Great Sage, the hells and heavens described are all fruitless; I play this game for that reason. Everything, whether auspicious or inauspicious, has been established by me through my own boons; because of that delusion, they do not know me as the root cause. I have told you all this, my great endeavor. Do not doubt, O Sage; worship me with devotion.

Kapila said: Having said this, the Lord of Ganesha disappeared from my sight. Filled with sorrow, I searched for him in my heart. I, the Brahmin, established this Chintamani, the Supreme Lord, and I worship and serve him daily with a heart full of devotion. I have told you all the secrets of that wise one. Worship him with devotion, as has been told to the world. You are undoubtedly a Gana-patya (a devotee of Ganesha) in human form; by your mere sight, everyone becomes purified.

Gargya said: Hearing this, the Yogi Mudgala, who had attained the world of his own bliss, was astonished and bowed to the great sage. After worshipping Chintamani and bowing to Kapila again, Mudgala, O King, returned to his hermitage, absorbed in the Lord of Ganas. I have told you all this; worship him with devotion. Through this material body, you will attain your own bliss, O King.

Mudgala said: Having said this to King Aila, the great sage Gargya, with the King's permission, went to his own hermitage, O Vidhi. Aila also, constantly serving the Lord of Ganas known as Laksha, became absorbed in his own bliss and worshipped him, O Lord of the People.

Thus ends the forty-second chapter in the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Mahapurana, which describes the disappearance of Chintamani.

Chapter 43 : Description of the Path of Devotion

Daksa said: I am blessed and my purpose is fulfilled after hearing the story born of your own bliss. O King of Yogis, I am astonished, for there is nothing equal to it. Now, tell me everything about the glory of King Aila that was born of devotion. What did he practice after being instructed by Gargya, the best of kings? How did he attain the realm of Ganesha? Tell me that story. How does one worship the Lord of Ganesha here and there?

Mudgala said: The best of kings was instructed by Gargya in the path of Ganapatya. Following the path of Ganapatya, he worshipped the Lord of the Ganas. A deity named Laksha was installed there by Skanda, and the king served that remover of obstacles daily with single-minded devotion. The best of kings knew nothing beyond Ganesha. Being single-minded by nature, he worshipped that Lord Laksha.

Daksa said: O Mudgala, tell me about this single-minded devotion and other forms of worship by which the Lord of Obstacles becomes directly pleased with men.

Mudgala said: The worship of the five deities (Panchayatana), the service of fire, and the daily duties of the twice-born are universally accepted. Know that the form of an obstacle is very terrible; it is a bond in this world and the next, and it always acts as a barrier to the path of Brahman. A man of excellence should conquer this by worshipping the Lord of Obstacles. For the sake of knowledge, one should worship Shankara daily with reverence; for the sake of fame, Vishnu; for health, the Sun; and for prosperity, the Mother of the Universe. One should worship fire through rituals like Homa for the success of actions, as it is the source of satisfaction for all the gods.

A mortal who acts with this understanding, offering all his duties to Brahman, or even one who acts with desires but offers all his actions to the deities, is considered righteous. O Prajapati, all these paths are considered equal, and the performance of one's own duty is known to be the best and the giver of all happiness. In this, one should always place the Lord of Obstacles in the center, worship Vishnu in the northeast, Shambhu in the southeast, the Mother of the Universe in the southwest, and the Sun in the northwest. One should worship these five deities in the form of the Panchayatana. Because the Lord of Obstacles is the object of worship for all and is the first to be worshipped, and because He is the form of the eldest Lord, He remains in the center. Alternatively, one may take the mantra of one of the five deities and install that one in the center. Everything is a part of the Panchayatana; when these five are worshipped, the entire universe is worshipped.

In the past, various deities were created by the Divine for the success of tasks; all of them are His forms and should be worshipped by man. O Prajapati, this devotion, which encompasses everything, has been described. Through the feeling of one's chosen deity, one meditates on the Supreme everywhere. Now, O Daksa, listen to the single-minded devotion that is most wonderful, which grants success in a short time and is most dear. The entire universe is established within one's chosen deity; by worshipping Him alone, everything is worshipped. Because the Divine exists everywhere in infinite forms and parts, it is impossible for embodied beings to worship all of them. The Divine is composed of the moving and non-moving; therefore, the moving and non-moving should be worshipped. O best of Brahmins, this is impossible for anyone due to the infinite nature of creation. Others say that this entire universe is merely a cluster of universes within the pores of His skin. Since there are crores of universes in the pores of His skin, the worship of His entire body is never possible. Therefore, the Divine took on an embodied form for the sake of His love for devotees; by worshipping Him, everything is worshipped.

With this conviction, one should worship one's chosen deity. One should not desire the agitation or satisfaction of others in one's heart. O Daksa, one should not criticize others, nor should one praise them, but remain established in oneness. Being devoted to one's chosen deity, one should perform devotion specifically. O Prajapati, a man who is always united with the Lord of Obstacles and worships his chosen deity attains the supreme success. This single-minded devotion is never broken. I will tell you the path that destroys all doubts. Just as one performs Sandhya and other rites according to one's own Vedic branch, one should worship the Lord of the Ganas as the one to be worshipped first. According to the Vedas, He is the first to be worshipped by all; otherwise, due to contradiction with the Vedas, all actions are destroyed. There is no first-worship for Shiva, Vishnu, and others. Therefore, they should be worshipped after Him, but the Elephant-faced Lord should never be neglected.

Hear another statement of mine: Ganesha, accompanied by Siddhi and Buddhi, is the giver of fruits to all; there is no doubt in this. O Prajapati, according to the Vedas, Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha, and the state of Brahman are all dependent on success (Siddhi). The doer is endowed with intelligence, and the chosen deity is endowed with that intelligence. The entire world, knowledge, and the body are dependent on intelligence. The Lord of both is Ganesha, who is the Lord of Speech (Brahmanaspati). A man who does not worship Him first becomes a dweller of hell. O Daksa, when Ganesha is not worshipped first, the chaste Siddhi and Buddhi do not come there. One becomes devoid of all success and filled with evil thoughts; there is no doubt that at the end of life, such a person rots in hell. He is declared the eldest among Shiva, Vishnu, and others. By merely transgressing Him, the gods will cause one to fall. Just as Vishnu was ready to bind the great Vaishnava Bali in hell due to his enmity toward Ganesha, one should understand this.

Like the Sandhya rituals, He is accepted by the wise as the one to be worshipped first. By His worship alone, single-mindedness is not broken. If Ganesha is the one-pointed focus, then Ganesha is always the only one to be worshipped; there is no doubt, as the necessity for others is negated. There is no single-minded devotion other than this. Brahman is manifold, and therefore the universe is worshipped through devotion. The Divine, who is united with the world and Brahman, is one's chosen deity; when He is worshipped in full, everything is satisfied. O giver of honor, just as the devotee is single-minded, so is the Divine. By becoming dependent on the single devotee, He remains established there. O Daksa, I have told you this three-fold worship. By this path of devotion, a man attains whatever he desires.

Chapter 44 : Procedure of Meditation and Worship with Durva Grass and Other Offerings

Mudgala said: In this way, that royal sage Aila, being devoted to a single goal, worshipped Ganesha daily, just as Gritsamada did. Meditating upon the elephant-faced deity in solitude, with his entire being absorbed in Him, he became indifferent to worldly enjoyments and royal pleasures. Having renounced his kingdom in favor of his son, he worshipped the elephant-faced Lord and remained there constantly, absorbed in meditation by his very nature.

Daksha said: Please tell me now the meditation on Ganesha as performed by Aila, so that I, too, may worship Him constantly with such meditation.

Mudgala said: O respected one, listen to the meditation by which that best of kings, Aila, worshipped the Lord of the Ganas after learning about Him from Gargya.

Aila said: I bow to the lotus feet of the Lord of Obstacles, which are beautifully marked and grant everything to those who belong to Him. They are adorned with the signs of the flag, sword, lotus, fearlessness, and thunderbolt, and are extremely delicate, marked by the battle-axe. With reddish toes and soft, radiant nails, they are highly resplendent. Possessing well-formed ankles and soft, fleshy contours, they dispel the darkness of ignorance residing in the heart. I meditate upon His shins, which are reddish and beautiful, and on His soft, fleshy thighs, which are covered with fine hair and curved at the tips. I contemplate His thighs, which are plump, resembling banana stalks, and highly radiant; they are reddish, born of heart-felt contemplation, and grant devotion to the remover of obstacles. I always contemplate the waist of the Lord of the Ganas, which is fleshy, reddish, circular, and situated in the middle, as He is the one who always bestows happiness and bliss. I contemplate the garment of deep red color, of infinite value, situated on His waist, and His navel, which is circular, endowed with supreme beauty, and the abode of Shesha. I meditate upon His great belly, which is fleshy, covered with fine hair, and radiant; it is the source of all light, the root of the three worlds, eternal and supreme. I contemplate the vast form of the Lord with the curved trunk, His chest which is reddish and fleshy, and His heart with two breasts; I meditate upon the Chintamani gem there, which is the supreme bestower of happiness. I contemplate His neck, which has three lines, is fleshy, reddish, and endowed with the radiance of Ganesha. I meditate in my heart upon His shoulders, which are like those of a bull, soft, and reddish. I contemplate His arms, which are always fleshy and radiant, bestowing great joy to the mind of the Lord of Self-Bliss, and His hands, which are endowed with reddish brilliance—the left ones and the right ones, I contemplate in my heart. I contemplate in my heart the twenty fingers of the Lord of the Ganas, which are circular, adorned with reddish nails, radiant, and protectors of His devotees. I contemplate His face, which is adorned with a trunk and three eyes, and is the supreme illuminator. I contemplate His single tusk, which is sharp and grants happiness to His devotees through the nectar of love. I contemplate in my heart His vast ears, which grant both worldly desires and liberation to the devotee; they are shaped like winnowing baskets, ever-moving, and are the very form of the Vedas. I contemplate in my heart the cheeks of the Lord of the Ganas, which are constantly surrounded by sages in the form of bees, seeking the nectar of His divine intoxication, and resonating with sound. I contemplate the forehead of the Lord Dhundhiraja, which is full of radiance and resembles the moon; it is decorated with eight types of red sandal paste, dots, and grains of rice. I contemplate the head of the remover of obstacles, covered with hair; the Lord residing at the root of the universe, and the crown of His head, which is the abode of the bliss of Brahma. I contemplate the body of Ganesha, adorned with various wonderful ornaments and beautiful, priceless garments, always resplendent as the bestower of the boon of Brahma. I contemplate the Lord of the Ganas, who is anointed with various fragrant substances, decorated by the best of devotees and yogis, and who always resides in the heart, longing for the devotion of His own. I contemplate in my heart the Lord of the Ganas, who is always praised by the noose, goad, and other weapons, and by Pramoda and others; He is worshipped by the best of gods and the king of mice. I contemplate Him as being praised by the Vedas, Puranas, Smritis, and Shastras, and by embodied beings; He is praised with devotion by the countless Ganas led by Brahma-priya, and by the great sages led by Mudgala. I contemplate in my heart Ganesha, who is endowed with the beauty of all limbs, and is served by Siddhi, who is surrounded by her own powers and Maya. I contemplate in my heart the Lord of the Ganas, who is served by Buddhi, the arts, and all branches of knowledge; I contemplate Him with His own Maya, which beautifies all His limbs and adorns His right side. I contemplate the Lord of Obstacles, who is served by Laksha and Labha, who are beautiful and auspicious; I contemplate Him as the Lord of Brahma, always worshipped by devotees who have attained His abode. I contemplate His form, which is described by infinite mantras, as it is impossible to describe; He resides in the heart, is fond of meditation, and is the supreme Lord of the Ganas, the embodiment of Brahma.

Mudgala said: In this way, that royal sage Aila, engaged in the worship of Ganesha, always remained in meditation, having turned away from worldly pursuits. Rising early in the morning, that king, along with his wife, performed his daily duties according to Dharma. Then, with devotion, he worshipped the Lord of the Ganas, just as he had been instructed by Gargya. With devotion, he worshipped Him with Shami leaves, Mandara flowers, and Durva grass. The king would gather the sprouts, leaves, and flowers after praying to them.

Daksha said: O best of sages, tell me about the worship of Shami, Mandara, and Durva, and about the sons of Ganesha, Laksha and Labha, and the daughters, Siddhi and Buddhi. What is the prayer for Shami, Mandara, and Durva? After performing that, I shall gather the flowers and leaves according to your instructions. With which mantra, O best of yogis, should a man offer Shami and Mandara to the supreme Lord Ganesha?

Mudgala said: Listen, O wise Daksha, devoted to the path of Ganesha. I am pleased with your question and shall tell you. These are considered worthy of worship with the mantra of Ganesha himself; they become satisfied when offered with items belonging to Ganesha. O Daksha, there is no distinction of male or female gender in this regard. The letter 'Ga' is the form of Siddhi, and the letter 'Na' is called Buddhi. Ganesha is the master of both and appears in a threefold form. His Ganas are also to be worshipped with the mantras of Ganesha. From the letter 'Ga', the son Laksha, who is of the nature of an image, is described, and from the letter 'Ga', Labha is also considered to be of that same nature. O Daksha, Shami and Mandara, due to the boon granted, are specifically considered to be the very form of Ganesha; have no doubt about this. A man should worship the Durva grass with the mantra of Ganesha, or he may worship it with Vedic mantras such as the Kanda-rishi mantras. A Shudra should worship them with the name-mantra with all his heart. This is the secret I have told you clearly. Listen, O wise Daksha, I shall tell you the prayer. After bathing, go to the forest or to your own temple in the city. Wherever the Durva, Mandara, and Shami are, go there, bow down first, and pray with devotion: "O Durva, you are the form of the food-Brahma, the form of nectar, and possess a hundred sprouts. I gather you to please Ganesha; please forgive me." "O Shami, you are the bestower of everything, O Goddess, you are the form of Ganesha. I gather you to please Ganesha, and I bow to you." "O Mandara, you are the bestower of desired objects, O form of the Lord of Obstacles. Please forgive me; I gather you to please Ganesha." Having prayed thus, O Lord of subjects, one should bring the Shami, Mandara, and Durva leaves and flowers and worship the Lord. A man should offer them with the thousand names, the hundred and eight names, or the twenty-one names. Or, one may offer the Durva and other items with the root mantra, the name-mantra, or various mantras of Ganesha. I have told you this entire secret, which is purifying and grants the status of a Gana-patya. What more do you wish to hear?

Thus ends the forty-fourth chapter in the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which describes the procedure of meditation and worship with Durva grass and other offerings.

Chapter 45 : The Description of the Glory of Hearing

Salutations to Lord Ganesha.

Daksha said: O Brahmin, tell me the Ganesha Keelakam (the pinning or locking mantra), which grants all desires and, when practiced with complete devotion, bestows perfection upon mantras and other spiritual practices.

Mudgala said: Mantras without the Keelakam do not provide happiness. Therefore, one should first recite the Keelakam before performing Japa (repetition of mantras). When done this way, the mantras become potent and grant various types of success; there is no doubt about this. I shall tell you what I have heard. This most secret and supreme knowledge was imparted to me by Angiras. Listen, O bestower of honor, to the Ganesha Keelakam, which grants perfection.

The Seer of this Ganesha Keelakam is Shiva, the meter is Anushtup, and the deity is Lord Ganapati. Om Gam Yogaya Svaha is the mantra. Om Gam is the seed. It is used in Japa for the pleasure of the Lord of the Ganas, who is the power of both knowledge and ignorance. One should first perform the Nyasas (ritual placement of mantras) of the Seer, meter, and others. O Daksha, the wise person should perform the six-limbed Nyasa of the single-syllable mantra.

Then, one should meditate on the Lord of Ganesha, who is the embodiment of supreme light, beyond mind and speech, and adorned with four arms. His face, with the trunk, is so complete that it cannot be fully perceived. He is accompanied by knowledge and ignorance and is worshipped with all divine glories. Having meditated on the Lord of Ganesha in this way, one should worship Him mentally with five offerings and then perform the Japa. O Prajapati, one should perform the Japa twenty-one times. After that, recite the hymn and then complete the ritual.

O Elephant-faced Lord, grant me beauty, strength, wealth, fame, and vigor. O Lord of Obstacles, I bow to you, grant me intellect, wisdom, and glory. When all the gods were rendered powerless by the demons, you destroyed them and restored their vigor. Similarly, O Lord of Ganesha, when mantras are rendered powerless by wicked people or curses, please restore their potency; I bow to you again and again. O Lord, all powers rendered ineffective are made full of knowledge and vigor by your remembrance; I bow to you, O Lord of Obstacles. When the entire moving and unmoving world becomes devoid of existence, it is restored to existence by your remembrance, O Dhundhi (Ganesha). When the elements become devoid of vigor, O Lord of Obstacles, they are reborn with vigor through your remembrance; I bow to you. When the Vedas become devoid of yoga, they are restored to yoga by your remembrance, O Lord of Ganesha; I bow to you. Everything in this world becomes endowed with existence by your remembrance, O Lord; I bow to the Lord of Obstacles again and again. Similarly, O Lord of Ganesha, when mantras become devoid of vigor, please restore them to potency by your remembrance, O Dhundhi. May all my mantras, worship, and other rituals be endowed with existence through your name. I bow to you, O Vakratunda (the curved-trunk Lord).

Unlock the great mantras through Japa and the recitation of this hymn. May the mantras grant all success through your grace. I bow to you, O Ganesha, O Heramba, O One-Tusked Lord, O dweller of Svananda, O Lord of the Vedas. O Prajapati, this Ganesha Keelakam, spoken by Shiva, is the supreme lock-breaker of mantras. Whoever recites this with devotion after chanting the supreme mantra attains all success arising from various mantras. One who chants the Ganesha mantra daily without this Keelakam becomes devoid of all fruits; there is no doubt about this. This Keelakam is declared to be the bestower of all success and is highly miraculous. In ancient times, Shambhu (Shiva) himself attained the success of mantras through this. Vishnu, Brahma, and other gods, sages, and supreme yogis attained mantra-siddhi through this, O Prajapati. Aila, having performed this primary Keelakam and being devoted to the mantra, went to Svananda-puri and became the king of devotees. By the vision of Ganesha, he became a form of light while still in his physical body, along with his wife, and beheld the entire universe.

Daksha said: How did Aila, while in his physical body, behold the universe along with the gods? Tell me this, as I am curious. How did he, a storehouse of merit, see the hells and those who are fit to be seen by sinners?

Mudgala said: O Lord, the King, while seated in his celestial chariot, saw them by His grace. Even while in a physical body, he saw the Lords of Ganesha, Shiva, Vishnu, and others. O Daksha, those who reside in the Svananda chariot see everything clearly through their yogic form. I have told you the auspicious story of Aila. Whoever hears this, that mortal will surely attain both worldly enjoyment and liberation.

Whoever, being a noble person, hears this section of Lambodara (Ganesha), reads it, or causes others to hear it, becomes divine himself. There is nothing equal to this for granting enjoyment and liberation, where the yogic form of the great Lambodara is praised. The fruit one gets from hearing various Shruti and Smriti texts is obtained here by hearing this single section. Those who give various donations and bathe in holy rivers obtain a hundred times more merit by taking refuge in this. O Lord, a human who performs all vows and sacrifices obtains a hundred times more merit just by hearing this. Whoever performs various austerities repeatedly obtains boundless merit just by hearing this. There is no doubt that a person becomes endowed with sons, grandsons, wealth, and grain immediately by hearing this. O Lord of the people, whatever one desires becomes fruitful in an instant by hearing this section; there is no doubt. What more is there to say? This section, which I have told you, O Prajapati, is the supreme bestower of yoga and all success. This story of Lambodara, which grants all success, has been told to you briefly. What more do you wish to hear?

Suta said: O foremost of the Bhrigus, just as Mudgala revealed this to Daksha, I have told it to you. O Shaunaka and others, you have heard the story of Lambodara. I am blessed that, through your association, I am engaged in the story of Ganesha. Even Shiva, the Vedas, and Shesha are unable to describe it in detail; who am I? Whatever story Vyasa told in the past, I have told you the same. O Brahmin, what more do you wish to hear?

Thus ends the forty-fifth chapter of the fifth section of the glorious Mudgala Purana, which is the description of the glory of hearing the story of Lambodara.

Dedicated to Lord Gajanana.

Thus ends the fifth section of the Shri Mudgala Purana.

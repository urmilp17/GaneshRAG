Shloka 521: Aṣṭaiśvaryapradāyakaḥ (अष्टैश्वर्यप्रदायकः)
दाताऽणिमादिसिद्धीनामष्टैश्वर्यप्रदायकः ॥
He who is the giver of the eight great lordly powers (Aṣṭaiśvarya) — Aṇimā (minuteness), Laghimā (lightness), Prāpti (attainment), Prākāmya (irresistible will), Mahimā (greatness), Īśitva (lordship), Vaśitva (control), and Kāmāvasāyitā (suppression of desires) — is Aṣṭaiśvaryapradāyakaḥ (The Bestower of the Eight Lordly Powers).


अष्टपीठोपपीठ श्रीरष्टमातृसमावृतः ॥ 
अष्टभैरवसेव्योऽष्टवसुवन्द्योऽष्टमूर्तिभृत् ॥ १४७ ॥


Shloka 522: Aṣṭapīṭhopapīṭhaśrīḥ (अष्टपीठोपपीठ श्रीः)
अष्टपीठोपपीठश्रीर्महापीठोपपीठयोः ॥ ५२२॥ पुराणे स्फुटयोर्भूरिसङ्ख्ययोः संपदात्मकः॥
The eight great sacred seats (Mahāpīṭhas) and their subordinate seats (Upapīṭhas) are clearly described in the Purāṇas. He who is the embodiment of the glory (Śrī) and prosperity of these is Aṣṭapīṭhopapīṭhaśrīḥ (The Glory of the Eight Pīṭhas and Upapīṭhas).


Shloka 523: Aṣṭamātṛsamāvṛtaḥ (अष्टमातृसमावृतः)
अष्टामी तु महालक्ष्मीर्बा-ह्याद्याः सप्त मातरः ॥ ५२३ ॥ आवृता यस्य तिष्ठन्ति सोऽ-ष्टमातृसमावृतः॥
The eight Mothers (Mātṛs): the eighth (aṣṭamī) is Mahālakṣmī, and the other seven (sapta mātaraḥ) are the outer ones (Brāhmī, Māheśvarī, etc.). He who is surrounded (Samāvṛta) by them is Aṣṭamātṛsamāvṛtaḥ (The One Encompassed by the Eight Mothers).


Shloka 524: Aṣṭabhairavasevyaḥ (अष्टभैरवसेव्यः)
अष्टभैरवसेव्योऽष्टौ भैरवा बटुकादयः ॥ ५२४ ॥ तैः सेव्यो॥
The eight Bhairavas – such as Batuka (Bhairava), and others (Asitāṅga, Ruru, Caṇḍa, Krodha, Unmatta, Kapāla, and Bhīṣaṇa) – are his servants. He who is served (Sevya) by them is Aṣṭabhairavasevyaḥ (The One Served by the Eight Bhairavas).


Shloka 525: Aṣṭavasuvandyaḥ (अष्टवसुवन्द्यः)
वसवोऽष्टौ तु प्रभासांता धरादयः । तैर्वन्दितो भवानष्टवसुवंद्यः प्रकीर्तितः ॥ ५२५ ॥
The eight Vasus – Dhara, Dhruva, Soma, Āpa, Anala, Anila, Pratyūṣa, and Prabhāsa – are the bestowers of wealth. He who is praised (Vandya) by them is proclaimed as Aṣṭavasuvandyaḥ (The One Worshipped by the Eight Vasus).


Shloka 525: Aṣṭamūrtibhṛt (अष्टमूर्तिभृत्)
भूतानि पुण्यवन्तौ स्वरे तद्रूपोऽष्टमूर्तिभृत् ॥
He who bears (Bhṛt) the eight forms (Aṣṭamūrti) – the five elements (earth, water, fire, air, ether), the sun, the moon, and the sacrificer (or soul) – is Aṣṭamūrtibhṛt (The Bearer of the Eight Forms).






अष्टचक्रस्फुरन्मूर्तिरष्टद्रव्यहविःप्रियः ॥ 
नवनागासनाध्यासी नवनिध्यनुशासिता ॥१४८॥


Shloka 526: Aṣṭacakrasphuranmūrtiḥ (अष्टचक्रस्फुरन्मूर्तिः)
अष्टचक्रा नवद्वारा देवानां पूरिति श्रुतेः ॥ ५२६ ॥ यंत्रे तिष्ठन्नष्टचक्रस्फुरन्मूर्तिरिति स्मृतः ॥
According to the Śruti, "The city of the gods has eight wheels and nine gates." He who resides in that body-yantra (mystical diagram) and whose form radiates (Sphuran) with the eight cakras (energy centers) is remembered as Aṣṭacakrasphuranmūrtiḥ (The One Whose Form Radiates with the Eight Wheels/Cakras).


Shloka 527: Aṣṭadravyahaviḥpriyaḥ (अष्टद्रव्यहविःप्रियः)
तिलेषु सक्तुचिपिटः कदल्यो मोदकास्तिलाः ॥ ५२७ ॥ नारिकेलघृताक्तानीत्यष्टद्रव्यहविः प्रियः ॥
The eight substances (Aṣṭadravya) used as oblations (Havis) are: sesame seeds, parched grain, flattened rice, plantain, Modakas, sesame cakes, coconut, and ghee (or items mixed with ghee). He who is fond of these eight oblations is Aṣṭadravyahaviḥpriyaḥ (The One Fond of the Eight Oblatory Substances).


Shloka 528: Navanāgāsanādhyāsī (नवनागासनाध्यासी)
नवनागासनाध्यासी नागैः कर्कोटका-दिभिः ॥ ५२८ ॥ पत्रेष्वष्टसु मध्ये च यस्यासनमधिष्ठितम् ॥
The nine serpents (Navanāga) – such as Karkoṭaka, Vāsuki, Śeṣa, etc. – serve as a couch or seat. He who is seated upon (Adhyāsī) that seat (Āsana) formed by the eight petals (of the lotus) and the pericarp in the middle, is Navanāgāsanādhyāsī (The One Seated on the Throne of Nine Serpents).


Shloka 529: Navanidhyanuśāsitā (नवनिध्यनुशासिता)
शासच्छंखमुकुंदादीन् नवनिध्यनुशासिता ॥ ५२९ ॥
He who rules over (Anuśāsitā) the nine treasures (Navanidhi) – such as Śaṅkha, Mukunda, Mahāpadma, Nīla, Makara, Kacchapa, Padma, Kunda, and Kharva – and commands them, is Navanidhyanuśāsitā (The Ruler of the Nine Treasures).














नवद्वारपुराधारो नवाधारनिकेतनः ॥ 
नवनारायणस्तुत्यो नवदुर्गानिषेवितः ॥१४९॥


Shloka 530: Navadvārapurādhāraḥ (नवद्वारपुराधारः)
नवद्वार पुराधारो जीवरूपेण देहभृत् । कुलाकुलसहस्रारे मूलाद्याज्ञांतिमानि षट् ॥ ५३० ॥
The body (Pura) has nine gates (Navadvāra): two eyes, two ears, two nostrils, mouth, anus, and genitals. He who is the support (Ādhāra) of this city (Pura) in the form of the individual soul (Jīva) is Navadvārapurādhāraḥ (The Supporter of the Nine-Gated City).


Shloka 530: Navādhāraniketanaḥ (नवाधारनिकेतनः)
लंबिकेति नवस्वास्से नवा-धारनिकेतनः ॥
He whose abode (Niketana) is in the nine supports (Navādhāra) — the six cakras (Mūlādhāra, Svādhiṣṭhāna, Maṇipūra, Anāhata, Viśuddhi, Ājñā) plus Lāmbikā (possibly the Ājñā's end), and two others (perhaps Sahasrāra and another) — is Navādhāraniketanaḥ (The One Whose Abode is the Nine Supports).


Shloka 531-532: Navanārāyaṇastutyaḥ (नवनारायणस्तुत्यः)
धर्माद्यनन्तबदरी रूपशङ्करसुन्दरैः ॥५३१॥ लक्ष्म्या साध्येनोपपदैर्युक्ता नारायणा नव । नवनारायणस्तुत्यस्तैः स्तुत्यः स भवानिति ॥
The nine Nārāyaṇas (Nav Nārāyaṇa) are: Dharma (Yama), Ananta (Śeṣa), Badarī, Rūpa, Śaṅkara, Sundara, Lakṣmī, Sādhya, and Upapada (?). He who is praised (Stutya) by these nine Nārāyaṇas is Navanārāyaṇastutyaḥ (The One Praised by the Nine Nārāyaṇas).


Shloka 533: Navadurgāniṣevitaḥ (नवदुर्गानिषेवितः)
दुर्गाया नवकूष्माण्ड-चन्द्रघण्टादयोऽभिधाः । प्रसिद्धास्ताभिरीड्यत्वान्नवदुर्गा-निषेवितः ॥ ५३३ ॥
The nine Durgās (Navadurgā) are well-known: Śailaputrī, Brahmacāriṇī, Chandraghaṇṭā, Kūṣmāṇḍā, Skandamātā, Kātyāyanī, Kālarātrī, Mahāgaurī, and Siddhidātrī. He who is served (Niṣevita) by these nine Durgās is Navadurgāniṣevitaḥ (The One Served by the Nine Durgās).












नवनाथमहानाथो नवनागविभूषणः ॥ 
नवरत्नविचित्राङ्गो नवशक्तिशिरोधृतः ॥१५०॥


Shloka 534: Navanāthamahānāthaḥ (नवनाथमहानाथः)
ज्ञानप्रकाशसत्यानन्द विमर्शस्वभावसुभगानाम् । नवनाथ-महानाथो नाथत्वात्प्रतिभपूर्णयोश्च भवान् ॥ ५३४ ॥
The nine Nāthas (Masters) are: Jñāna, Prakāśa, Satya, Ānanda, Vimarśa, Svabhāva, Subhaga, and two others (?). He who is the great master (Mahānātha) of these nine Nāthas, because of his lordship over them and his fullness of brilliance, is Navanāthamahānāthaḥ (The Great Lord of the Nine Lords).


Shloka 534: Navanāgavibhūṣaṇaḥ (नवनागविभूषणः)
कर्कोटकादिमण्ड्यत्वान्नवनागविभूषणः ॥ ५३५ ॥
He who is adorned (Vibhūṣaṇa) by the nine serpents (Navanāga) such as Karkoṭaka, Vāsuki, Śeṣa, etc., wearing them as ornaments, is Navanāgavibhūṣaṇaḥ (The One Adorned with Nine Serpents).


Shloka 535: Navaratnavicitrāṅgaḥ (नवरत्नविचित्राङ्गः)
नवरत्नविचि-त्राङ्गो वज्रमुक्तादिभूषितः ॥
He whose limbs (Aṅga) are variegated (Vicitra) with the nine jewels (Navaratna) — such as Vajra (diamond), Muktā (pearl), Ruby, Emerald, Sapphire, Topaz, Coral, Cat's eye, and Coral (or others) — is Navaratnavicitrāṅgaḥ (The One with Limbs Adorned with Nine Jewels).


Shloka 535: Navaśaktiśirodhṛtaḥ (नवशक्तिशिरोधृतः)
तीब्राद्यानवते पीठे नवशक्तिशिरोधृतः ॥
The nine Śaktis (powers) reside in the nine seats (Pīṭha). He who is borne on the head (Śirodhṛta) by these nine Śaktis (as a crest) is Navaśaktiśirodhṛtaḥ (The One Who is the Head-Ornament of the Nine Powers).
















दशात्मको दशभुजो दशदिक्पतिवन्दितः ॥ 
दशाध्यायो दशप्राणो दशेन्द्रियनियामकः ॥ १५१ ॥


Shloka 536: Daśātmakaḥ (दशात्मकः)
दिशो दश तदात्मा यो व्यापकः स दशात्मकः ॥ ५३६ ॥
He who is the Self (Ātmā) of the ten directions (Daśa Diś) — pervading East, South-East, South, South-West, West, North-West, North, North-East, Up, and Down — is Daśātmakaḥ (The One Whose Self is the Ten Directions).


Shloka 536: Daśabhujaḥ (दशभुजः)
महागणपतेर्मूर्त्या भवान् दशभुजोऽभवत् ॥
You, in the form of Mahāgaṇapati, possess ten arms. Thus, He is Daśabhujaḥ (The Ten-Armed One).


Shloka 537: Daśadikpativanditaḥ (दशदिक्पतिवन्दितः)
इन्द्रादयोऽष्टौ दिक्पाला ऊर्ध्वाधोदिगधीश्वरौ ॥ ५३७ ॥ क्रमाद्ब्रह्मानन्त इति दशदिक्पतिवन्दितः ॥
The eight guardians of the directions (Dikpālas) are: Indra (East), Agni (South-East), Yama (South), Nirṛti (South-West), Varuṇa (West), Vāyu (North-West), Kubera (North), and Īśāna (North-East). Added to the lords of the directions Up (Brahmā) and Down (Ananta), these are the ten Dikpatis. He who is praised (Vandita) by these ten guardians is Daśadikpativanditaḥ (The One Worshipped by the Ten Guardians of the Directions).


Shloka 538: Daśādhyāyaḥ (दशाध्यायः)
दशाध्यायश्चतुर्वेदी षडङ्गय-ध्ययनान्मतः ॥ ५३८ ॥
He who is the master of the four Vedas and the six Vedāṅgas, together comprising ten chapters (or subjects) of study, is considered Daśādhyāyaḥ (The One of Ten Chapters / The Master of the Tenfold Scripture).


Shloka 538: Daśaprāṇaḥ (दशप्राणः)
दशप्राणः पंचपंचप्राणनागादिरूपवान् ॥
He who possesses the ten vital airs (Daśaprāṇa): the five primary ones (Prāṇa, Apāna, Samāna, Udāna, Vyāna) and the five secondary ones (Nāga, Kūrma, Kṛkara, Devadatta, Dhanañjaya). Thus, He is Daśaprāṇaḥ (The One with Ten Vital Airs).




Shloka 539: Daśendriyaniyāmakaḥ (दशेन्द्रियनियामकः)
वागाद्यात्मत्वगाद्यात्मदशेन्द्रियनियामकः ॥ ५३९ ॥
He is the controller (Niyāmaka) of the ten Indriyas (senses): five Jñānendriyas (organs of knowledge: speech, hands, feet, anus, genitals) and five Karmendriyas (organs of action: ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose). Thus, He is Daśendriyaniyāmakaḥ (The Regulator of the Ten Senses).


दशाक्षरमहामन्त्रो दशाशाव्यापिविग्रहः ॥ 
एकादशादिभी रुद्रैः स्तुत एकादशाक्षरः ॥ १५२ ॥


Shloka 540: Daśākṣaramahāmantraḥ (दशाक्षरमहामन्त्रः)
उक्तो हस्तिपिशाचीति मन्त्राश्चेत्प्रणवादिमः। दशाक्षर-महामन्त्रस्त्वदैवत्यस्त्वमेव सः ॥ ५४० ॥
The mantra beginning with Praṇava (Oṁ) known as the Hastipiśācī (or other ten-syllabled mantras consisting of ten syllables such as "Oṁ Gaṁ Gaṇapataye Namaḥ") is the great ten-syllabled mantra (Daśākṣaramahāmantra). Its deity is you, and you alone are that mantra. Thus, He is Daśākṣaramahāmantraḥ (The Great Ten-Syllabled Mantra).


Shloka 540: Daśāśāvyāpivigrahaḥ (दशाशाव्यापिविग्रहः)
दशदिग्व्यापि-देहत्वाद्दशाशाव्यापिविग्रहः ॥ ५४१ ॥
Because his form (Vigraha) pervades (Vyāpi) the ten directions (Daśāśā), he is Daśāśāvyāpivigrahaḥ (The One Whose Form Pervades the Ten Directions).


Shloka 541: Ekādaśādibhirudraiḥ Stutaḥ (एकादशादिभी रुद्रैः स्तुतः)
रुद्रा एकादशारभ्य सह-स्राणि सहस्रशः ॥ ५४१ ॥ एकादशादिभी रुद्रैः स्तुतस्तैः सकलैर्नुतः ॥
The Rudras, beginning from the eleven (Ekādaśa) and up to thousands upon thousands, praise him. He who is praised (Stuta) by all these Rudras (from the eleven upwards) is Ekādaśādibhirudraiḥ Stutaḥ (The One Praised by the Eleven and Other Rudras).


Shloka 542: Ekādaśākṣaraḥ (एकादशाक्षरः)
तारशक्ती विघ्नशक्ती वशमानय उद्वयम् ॥५४२॥ एकादशाक्षरो मन्त्रस्त्वदैवत्यस्त्वमेव सः ॥
The eleven-syllabled mantra (Ekādaśākṣara), such as "Oṁ Gaṁ Gaṇapataye Namaḥ" (eleven syllables) or other mantras of eleven syllables, has you as its deity, and you are that mantra. Thus, He is Ekādaśākṣaraḥ (The One Who is the Eleven-Syllabled Mantra).




द्वादशोद्दण्डदोर्दण्डो द्वादशान्तनिकेतनः ॥ 
त्रयोदशभिधाभिन्नविश्वेदेवाधिदैवतम् ॥१५३॥


Shloka 543: Dvādaśoddaṇḍadordaṇḍaḥ (द्वादशोद्दण्डदोर्दण्डः)
द्वादशो इंडदोर्दडो गाडैर्द्वादशभिर्भुजैः ॥ ५४३ ॥
He who possesses twelve mighty (Uddaṇḍa) arm-like rods (Dordaṇḍa) — i.e., twelve powerful arms — is Dvādaśoddaṇḍadordaṇḍaḥ (The One with Twelve Uplifted Strong Arms).


Shloka 544: Dvādaśāntaniketanaḥ (द्वादशान्तनिकेतनः)
द्वादशांतं ललाटोवं ब्रह्मरंध्रावसानकम् । प्रकटो योगिनां तत्र द्वाद-शांतनिकेतनः ॥ ५४४ ॥
He whose abode (Niketana) is the Dvādaśānta (the twelfth end) — which is the end of the Brahma-randhra (aperture of Brahman) located at the forehead — and who manifests there to the yogis, is Dvādaśāntaniketanaḥ (The One Whose Abode is the Twelfth End).


Shloka 545: Trayodaśābhidhābhinna-viśvedevādhidaivatam (त्रयोदशभिधाभिन्नविश्वेदेवाधिदैवतम्)
पुरूरवप्रभृतिभिः पूज्यस्त्वं विश्वदै-वतैः । त्रयोदशाभिधाभिन्नविश्वेदेवाधिदैवतम् ॥
You are worshipped by the Viśvedevas (all the gods) who are thirteen in number according to their distinct names. You are the super-deity (Adhidaivata) of the Viśvedevas. Thus, He is Trayodaśābhidhābhinna-viśvedevādhidaivatam (The Presiding Deity of the Viśvedevas, Who are Distinguished by Thirteen Names).


चतुर्दशेन्द्रवरदश्चतुर्दशमनुप्रभुः । 
चतुर्दशादिविद्याढ्यश्चतुर्दशजगत्प्रभुः ॥ १५४ ॥


Shloka 546: Caturdaśendravaradaḥ (चतुर्दशेन्द्रवरदः)
चतुर्दशानामिन्द्राणां मनूनां वरदोऽधिपः । चतुर्दशेंद्र-वरदश्चतुर्दशमनुप्रभुः ॥ ५४६ ॥
He is the bestower of boons (Varada) to the fourteen Indras (Caturdaśa Indra), who rule over the fourteen Manvantaras. Thus, He is Caturdaśendravaradaḥ (The Bestower of Boons to the Fourteen Indras).








Shloka 546: Caturdaśamanuprabhuḥ (चतुर्दशमनुप्रभुः)
He is also the lord (Prabhu) of the fourteen Manus (Caturdaśa Manu) — Svayambhuva, Svarociṣa, Auttami, Tāmasa, Raivata, Cākṣuṣa, Vaivasvata, Sāvarṇi, Dakṣasāvarṇi, Brahma-sāvarṇi, Dharma-sāvarṇi, Rudra-sāvarṇi, Deva-sāvarṇi, and Indra-sāvarṇi. Thus, He is Caturdaśamanuprabhuḥ (The Lord of the Fourteen Manus).


Shloka 547: Caturdaśādividyāḍhyaḥ (चतुर्दशादिविद्याढ्यः)
चतुर्दशादिविद्याख्यो वेदाद्याः सोपवेदकाः । चतुर्दशाष्ट दश च प्रसिद्धास्तासु पण्डितः ॥ ५४७॥
He is endowed with the fourteen (Caturdaśa) principal branches of knowledge (Vidyā) — the four Vedas (Ṛg, Yajus, Sāma, Atharva), the six Vedāṅgas (Śikṣā, Kalpa, Vyākaraṇa, Nirukta, Chandas, Jyotiṣa), and the four Upavedas (Āyurveda, Dhanurveda, Gandharvaveda, Sthāpatyaveda). Thus, He is Caturdaśādividyāḍhyaḥ (The One Possessing the Fourteen Sciences).


Shloka 547: Caturdaśajagatprabhuḥ (चतुर्दशजगत्प्रभुः)
आपातालादा सत्याच्चतुर्दशजगत्प्रभुः ॥
He is the lord (Prabhu) of the fourteen worlds (Caturdaśa Jagat) — the seven upper worlds (Bhūḥ, Bhuvaḥ, Svaḥ, Mahaḥ, Janaḥ, Tapaḥ, Satya) and the seven lower worlds (Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Rasātala, Talātala, Mahātala, Pātāla). Thus, He is Caturdaśajagatprabhuḥ (The Lord of the Fourteen Worlds).


सामपञ्चदशः पञ्चदशीशीतांशुनिर्मलः । 
षोडशाधारनिलयः षोडशस्वरमातृकः॥ १५५ ॥


Shloka 548: Sāmapañcadaśaḥ (सामपञ्चदशः)
यत्पंचदशभिः स्तोमै राज्यस्तोत्रचतुष्टयम् ॥ ५४८ ॥ साम-युक्तं तदात्मत्वात् सामपंचदशो भवान् ॥
The four royal hymns (Rājya-stotra) are connected with the fifteen Stomas (Sāma chants). Because you are the embodiment of that Sāma (Veda) with its fifteen-fold divisions, you are Sāmapañcadaśaḥ (The Fifteenfold Sāmaveda / One with Fifteen Sāma Chants).


Shloka 549: Pañcadaśīśītāṁśunirmalaḥ (पञ्चदशीशीतांशुनिर्मलः)
स्वच्छो राकें-दुवत्पंचदशीशीतांशुनिर्मलः ॥ ५४९ ॥
You are as spotless (Nirmala) as the fifteen-day-old full moon (Pañcadaśī) which is the cool-rayed one (Śītāṁśu). Thus, He is Pañcadaśīśītāṁśunirmalaḥ (The One as Spotless as the Fifteen-Day-Old Moon).




Shloka 550: Ṣoḍaśādhāranilayaḥ (षोडशाधारनिलयः)
षोडशाधारनिल-योऽस्यंतश्चक्रेषु संस्थितः । षडाधारनवाधारषोडशाधारलक्षणम् ॥ ५५० ॥ वेद्यं गुरुमुखादेवेत्यागमे शिवशासनम् ॥
He whose abode (Nilaya) is in the sixteen supports (Ṣoḍaśādhāra) — which are the six cakras (Mūlādhāra, Svādhiṣṭhāna, Maṇipūra, Anāhata, Viśuddhi, Ājñā) plus ten others (the four petals of Ājñā and others — described as the sixteen Ādhāras).


Shloka 551: Ṣoḍaśasvaramātṛkaḥ (षोडशस्वरमातृकः)
स्वरान्मिमीते मानेन षोडशखरमातृकः ॥ ५५१ ॥
He who is the creator or measurer of the sixteen vowels (Ṣoḍaśa Svara) — a, ā, i, ī, u, ū, ṛ, ṝ, ḷ, ḹ, e, ai, o, au, aṁ, aḥ — is the mother (Mātṛka) of these sixteen sounds. Thus, He is Ṣoḍaśasvaramātṛkaḥ (The Source of the Sixteen Vowels).


षोडशांतपदावासः षोडशेन्दुकलात्मकः ॥ 
कलासप्तदशी सप्तदशः सप्तदशाक्षरः ॥ १५६॥


Shloka 552: Ṣoḍaśāntapadāvāsaḥ (षोडशांतपदावासः)
षोडशांतपदावसो ब्रह्मरंध्राजकर्णिकाम् ॥ ५५२ ॥ आरभ्योर्ध्व षोडशांत मुन्मन्याः परतः स्थितः ॥
He whose dwelling place (Āvāsa) is the state beyond the sixteenth (Ṣoḍaśānta) — which is the end of the sixteen (starting from the pericarp of the thousand-petaled lotus at the Brahma-randhra and extending above to the state beyond the mind) — is Ṣoḍaśāntapadāvāsaḥ (The One Whose Abode is the Limit of the Sixteenth).


Shloka 553: Ṣoḍaśendukalātmakaḥ (षोडशेन्दुकलात्मकः)
अमृता मानि-नीत्यादिषोडशेंदुकलात्मकः ॥ ५५३ ॥
He who is the embodiment (Ātmaka) of the sixteen digits (Kalā) of the moon (Indu) — Amṛtā, Māninī, and others — is Ṣoḍaśendukalātmakaḥ (The One Whose Self is the Sixteen Moon-Digits).


Shloka 554: Kalāsaptadaśī (कलासप्तदशी)
कला सप्तदशी नाम प्रसिद्धा त्रिपुरागमे । त्रयोदशाक्षरी तुर्यविद्याख्या त्वं तदात्मकः ॥ ५५४ ॥
In the Tripurāgama, the seventeenth digit (Kalāsaptadaśī) is well-known as the thirteenth syllable and is called the Turīya Vidyā. You are the embodiment of that. Thus, He is Kalāsaptadaśī (The Seventeenth Digit).


Shloka 554: Saptadaśaḥ (सप्तदशः)
यत्तु सप्तदशस्तोमं पृष्ठस्तोत्रचतुष्टयम् । सामयुक्तं तदात्मत्वाद्भवान् सप्तदशो मतः ॥ ५५५ ॥
The Sāma Veda, combined with the four Pṛṣṭha Stotras (Vāk, Praṣṭha, Āśvina, and others), constitutes the seventeen-fold Stoma (Saptadaśa Stoma). You are the embodiment of that; therefore, you are considered Saptadaśaḥ (The Seventeenfold One).


Shloka 554: Saptadaśākṣaraḥ (सप्तदशाक्षरः)
वौषडाश्रावययजास्तु श्रौषडूयेयजामहे । इति त्वं आप्नुषे यज्ञानिति सप्तदशाक्षरः ॥
The mantra containing seventeen syllables, such as "Vauṣaṭ, Āśrāvaya, Yajā, etc., Śrauṣaṭ, Yajāmahe" — by which you attain the sacrifices — you are that. Thus, He is Saptadaśākṣaraḥ (The Seventeen-Syllabled Mantra).


अष्टादशद्वीपपतिरष्टादशपुराणकृत् ॥ 
अष्टादशौषधीसृष्टिरष्टादशविधिः स्मृतः ॥ १५७ ॥


Shloka 556: Aṣṭādaśadvīpapatiḥ (अष्टादशद्वीपपतिः)
अष्टादशद्वीपपतिः सप्तैकादशसंख्ययोः ॥ ५५६ ॥ द्वीपोपद्वीपयोजैबुसिंहलाद्योरधीश्वरः ॥
He is the lord (Pati) of the eighteen continents (Aṣṭādaśadvīpa) — the seven principal continents (Jambu, Plakṣa, Śālmali, Kuśa, Krauñca, Śāka, Puṣkara) and the eleven subordinate islands (Upadvīpa) such as Jaiyabu, Siṃhala, etc. Thus, He is Aṣṭādaśadvīpapatiḥ (The Lord of the Eighteen Continents).


Shloka 557: Aṣṭādaśapurāṇakṛt (अष्टादशपुराणकृत्)
मात्स्यकौर्मब्राह्मपाद्मा-द्यष्टादशपुराणकृत् ॥ ५५७ ॥
He is the creator (Kṛt) of the eighteen Purāṇas (Aṣṭādaśa Purāṇa): Matsya, Kūrma, Brahmāṇḍa, Padma, Viṣṇu, Vāyu, Bhāgavata, Nārada, Mārkaṇḍeya, Agni, Bhaviṣya, Brahmavaivarta, Liṅga, Varāha, Skanda, Vāmana, Kālikā, and Śiva. Thus, He is Aṣṭādaśapurāṇakṛt (The Author of the Eighteen Purāṇas).


Shloka 558: Aṣṭādaśauṣadhīsṛṣṭiḥ (अष्टादशौषधीसृष्टिः)
अष्टादशौषधीसृष्टिर्धान्य-सृष्टिविचक्षणः । मुख्यधान्योपधान्यानि तेषु द्वादश षट् क्रमात् ॥ ५५८ ॥
He is the creator (Sṛṣṭiḥ) of the eighteen medicinal herbs (Aṣṭādaśauṣadhī) and the grains. The principal grains are twelve, and the secondary grains are six. Thus, He is Aṣṭādaśauṣadhīsṛṣṭiḥ (The Creator of the Eighteen Herbs).


Shloka 559: Aṣṭādaśavidhiḥ (अष्टादशविधिः)
विधयो पूर्वनियमपरिसंख्याः पृथक् पृथक् । प्रयोगविनियोगे त्याख्याभिर्नव ते द्विधा ॥ ५५९ ॥ गुण-प्रधानभेदादित्यष्टादशविधिः स्मृतः ॥
The Vidhis (ritual injunctions) are classified as: Pūrva (primary), Niyama (restrictions), Parisankhyā (exclusive alternatives), etc. According to differences in application and usage, there are nine types, which are further divided into two (principal and secondary) based on being chief or subordinate. Thus, the total is eighteen. He who embodies these is remembered as Aṣṭādaśavidhiḥ (The One Who is the Eighteenfold Ritual Injunction).


अष्टादशलिपिव्यष्टिसमष्टिज्ञानकोविदः ॥ 
एकविंशः पुमानेकविंशत्यंगुलिपल्लवः ॥१५८॥


Shloka 560-561: Aṣṭādaśalipivyaṣṭisamaṣṭijñānakovidaḥ (अष्टादशलिपिव्यष्टिसमष्टिज्ञानकोविदः)
नागरद्राविडांध्राद्याः स्वतंत्रा लिपयो भुवि ॥ ५६०॥ अष्टा-दश तदंशोत्था अवान्तरभिदा अपि । देशभेदेन बहवस्त-ज्ज्ञाने त्वं विचक्षणः ॥ ५६१ ॥ अष्टादशलिपिव्यष्टिस-मष्टिज्ञानकोविदः ॥
The independent scripts (Lipis) in the world — Nāgarī, Drāviḍī, Āndhrī, etc. — are eighteen (Aṣṭādaśa). Their subdivisions (Vyasti) and the totality of scripts (Samaṣṭi) are known to you. You are expert in this knowledge. Thus, He is Aṣṭādaśalipivyaṣṭisamaṣṭijñānakovidaḥ (The One Expert in the Knowledge of the Eighteen Scripts, Their Individual Forms, and Their Collective Whole).


Shloka 562-563: Ekaviṁśaḥ Pumān (एकविंशः पुमान्)
दशहस्त्या अङ्गुलयः पद्मा अंगुलयो दश ॥ ५६२ ॥ एकविंशः पुमानात्मेत्यामनंत्यैतरेयिणः ॥
Ten fingers on the hands (Daśahastya Aṅgulayaḥ) and ten toes on the feet (Padmā Aṅgulayaḥ) make twenty. The Self (Ātmā) is the twenty-first. As the Aitareya Upaniṣad declares, "The Puruṣa (Pumān) is the twenty-first." Thus, He is Ekaviṁśaḥ Pumān (The Twenty-First Person/Puruṣa).


Shloka 563: Ekaviṁśatyanguḷipallavaḥ (एकविंशत्यंगुलिपल्लवः)
सङ्घ शुण्डाञ्चले नैकविंशत्येङ्गुगुलिपल्लवः ॥ ५६३ ॥
He whose trunk tip (Śuṇḍāñcala) has the sprout (Pallava) of twenty-one fingers' breadth (Ekaviṁśati Aṅguli) in length is Ekaviṁśatyanguḷipallavaḥ (The One Whose Trunk Sprout is Twenty-One Fingers Long).






चतुर्विंशतितत्त्वात्मा पञ्चविंशाख्यपूरुषः ॥ 
सप्तविंशतितारेशः सप्तविंशतियोगकृत् ॥१५९॥


Shloka 564: Caturviṁśatitattvātmā (चतुर्विंशतितत्त्वात्मा) & Pañcaviṁśākhyapūruṣaḥ (पञ्चविंशाख्यपूरुषः)
तत्त्वानि जिनसंख्यानि तदूर्ध्वः पुरुषो भवान् । चतुर्विं-शतितत्त्वात्मा पंचविंशाख्यपूरुषः ॥ ५६४ ॥
The twenty-four principles (Tattvas) — from Prakṛti (primordial nature) to the five gross elements, including the eleven senses, the three internal organs (mind, intellect, ego), and the five subtle elements — are you. Above them, you are the twenty-fifth principle, the Puruṣa. Thus, He is Caturviṁśatitattvātmā (The One Who is the Twenty-Four Tattvas) and Pañcaviṁśākhyapūruṣaḥ (The One Known as the Twenty-Fifth Person/Puruṣa).


Shloka 565: Saptaviṁśatitāreśaḥ (सप्तविंशतितारेशः) & Saptaviṁśatiyogakṛt (सप्तविंशतियोगकृत्)
नक्षत्राणां च योगानां ईशः कर्ता क्रमाद्भवान् । सप्तविंशतितारेशः सप्तविंशतियोगकृत् ॥ ५६५ ॥
You are the Lord (Īśa) of the twenty-seven stars (Tārās/Nakṣatras) — Aśvinī, Bharaṇī, Kṛttikā, etc. You are the creator (Kṛt) of the twenty-seven Yogas (astrological combinations) — Viṣkambha, Prīti, Āyuṣmān, etc. Thus, He is Saptaviṁśatitāreśaḥ (The Lord of the Twenty-Seven Stars) and Saptaviṁśatiyogakṛt (The Creator of the Twenty-Seven Yogas).


द्वात्रिंशद्भैरवाधीशश्चतुस्त्रिंशन्महाहृदः । 
षट्त्रिंशत्तत्त्वसम्भूतिरष्टात्रिंशत्कलातनुः ॥ १६०॥


Shloka 566: Dvātriṁśadbhairavādhīśaḥ (द्वात्रिंशद्भैरवाधीशः)
द्वात्रिंशद्वैर वाधीशश्चत्वारो भैरवाष्टकाः । असितांगा-दयो ये ये तेषां तेषामधीश्वरः ॥ ५६६॥
There are eight groups of Bhairavas, each group containing four Bhairavas – thus making thirty-two Bhairavas in total, starting with Asitāṅga. He who is the supreme lord (Adhīśa) over all of them is Dvātriṁśadbhairavādhīśaḥ (The Lord of the Thirty-Two Bhairavas).


Shloka 567: Catustriṁśanmahāhradaḥ (चतुस्त्रिंशन्महाहृदः)
पुष्कराद्या देवखाताः संति पुण्या महाहदाः। तदात्मकत्वेन भवांश्चतुस्त्रिंशन्महा-हृदः ॥ ५६७ ॥
There are thirty-four holy great lakes (Mahāhrada) — Puṣkara, Mānasa, etc., which are divine reservoirs. You, being the embodiment of them, are Catustriṁśanmahāhradaḥ (The One Who is the Thirty-Four Great Lakes).


Shloka 568: Ṣaṭtriṁśattattvasambhūtiḥ (षट्त्रिंशत्तत्त्वसम्भूतिः)
षट्त्रिंशत्तत्त्वसंभूतिः शैवतंत्रोक्तया दिशा । शिवादिक्षितिपर्यंतषत्रिंशत्तत्त्वकारणम् ॥ ५६८ ॥
According to the Śaiva Tantras, the thirty-six Tattvas (principles) range from Śiva at the top down to the Earth (Kṣiti) at the bottom. He is the cause (Kāraṇam) or the very origin (Sambhūtiḥ) of these thirty-six Tattvas. Thus, He is Ṣaṭtriṁśattattvasambhūtiḥ (The Origin of the Thirty-Six Tattvas).


Shloka 569: Aṣṭātriṁśatkalātanuḥ (अष्टात्रिंशत्कलातनुः)
वहेः सूर्यस्य चन्द्रस्य दश द्वादश षोडश । कलाः सन्ति तदात्मत्वादष्टात्रिंशत्कलातनुः ॥ ५६९ ॥
Fire (Vahni) has ten Kalās (digits), the Sun (Sūrya) has twelve, and the Moon (Candra) has sixteen — totaling thirty-eight Kalās. You, being the embodiment of these, are Aṣṭātriṁśatkalātanuḥ (The One Whose Body is the Thirty-Eight Digits).


नमदेकोनपञ्चाशन्मरुद्वर्गनिरर्गलः ॥ 
पञ्चाशदक्षरश्रेणी पञ्चाशद्रुद्रविग्रहः ॥ १६१ ॥
Shloka 570: Namadekonapañcāśanmarudvarganirargalaḥ (नमदेकोनपञ्चाशन्मरुद्वर्गनिरर्गलः)
मरुद्वर्गनतश्चासौ निष्प्रत्यूहगतिश्च सः । नमदेकोनपंचा-शन्मरुद्वर्गनिरर्गलः ॥ ५७० ॥
The group of forty-nine Maruts (Deities of wind/storms) bows down (Namad) to him. He is unobstructed (Nirargala) in his movement. Thus, He is Namadekonapañcāśanmarudvarganirargalaḥ (The One to Whom the Forty-Nine Maruts Bow and Who is Without Any Obstruction).


Shloka 571: Pañcāśadakṣaraśreṇī (पञ्चाशदक्षरश्रेणी)
पंचाशदक्षरश्रेणीत्युच्यते मातृकात्मना ।
The series (Śreṇī) of fifty letters (Pañcāśat Akṣara) is the Mātṛkā (matrix of phonemes). He is that. Thus, He is Pañcāśadakṣaraśreṇī (The One Who is the Fifty-Letter Syllabic Series).


Shloka 571: Pañcāśadrudravigrahaḥ (पञ्चाशद्रुद्रविग्रहः)
श्रीकंठादिशिवात्मत्वात् पंचाशद्रुद्रविग्रहः ॥ ५७१ ॥ केशवाद्याश्च कीर्त्याद्या विष्णवस्तस्य शक्तयः ॥
He is the embodiment (Vigraha) of the fifty Rudras (Pañcāśad Rudra) – Śrīkaṇṭha, etc., and also of the Viṣṇus – Keśava, etc., and of the Śaktis known as Kīrti, etc. Thus, He is Pañcāśadrudravigrahaḥ (The One Who Has the Form of the Fifty Rudras).








पञ्चाशद्विष्णुशक्तीशः पञ्चाशन्मातृकालयः ॥ 
द्विपञ्चाशद्वपुःश्रेणी त्रिषष्ट्यक्षरसंश्रयः ॥१६२॥


Shloka 572: Pañcāśadviṣṇuśaktīśaḥ (पञ्चाशद्विष्णुशक्तीशः)
पञ्चाशद्विष्णुशक्तीशस्तदीशत्वेन कथ्यसे ॥ ५७२ ॥
You are declared as the lord (Īśa) of the fifty Viṣṇus and the fifty Śaktis (Pañcāśad Viṣṇu and Pañcāśad Śakti). Thus, He is Pañcāśadviṣṇuśaktīśaḥ (The Lord of the Fifty Viṣṇus and Fifty Śaktis).


Shloka 573: Pañcāśanmātṛkālayaḥ (पञ्चाशन्मातृकालयः)
एकपञ्चाशतोर्णानामालयो लय एव वा । नाद एव तदात्मत्वा-त्पञ्चाशन्मातृकालयः ॥ ५७३ ॥
He is the abode (Ālaya) or the place of dissolution (Laya) of the fifty-one letters (Ekapañcāśat varṇa). Being of the nature of Nāda (primordial sound) itself, he is Pañcāśanmātṛkālayaḥ (The Abode of the Fifty Mātṛkās – The Fifty-One Phonemes).


Shloka 574-575: Dvipañcāśadvapuḥśreṇī (द्विपञ्चाशद्वपुःश्रेणी) & Triṣaṣṭyakṣarasaṃśrayaḥ (त्रिषष्ट्यक्षरसंश्रयः)
लैङ्गववर्णितपाशानामविद्यापञ्चपर्वणि । अष्टाष्टदिग्वसुधृतिभिन्नानां देहदायिनाम् ॥ ५७४ ॥ त्वद्रूपत्वाद्विपश्चाशद्वपुःश्रेणीति गीयसे । त्रिष-ष्टिश्चतुःषष्टिर्वा वर्णाः शंभुमते मताः ॥ ५७५ ॥ कला अपि चतुःषष्टिः प्रसिद्धास्तत्समाश्रयः । तन्निर्णेता तत्खनिस्त्वं त्रिषष्ट्यक्षरसंश्रयः ॥
The series of fifty-two forms (Dvipañcāśad Vapuḥ) — divided as eight (Dik), eight (Vasu), etc. — are of your nature. Thus, you are praised as Dvipañcāśadvapuḥśreṇī (The Series of Fifty-Two Forms). In the Śaiva tradition, the letters (Varṇas) are considered to be sixty-three or sixty-four. The sixty-four arts (Kalā) are also well-known. You are the abode (Samāśraya) of these sixty-three letters, their knower (Nirṇetā), and their mine (Khanin). Thus, He is Triṣaṣṭyakṣarasaṃśrayaḥ (The Abode of the Sixty-Three Syllables).


















चतुःषष्ट्यर्णनिर्णेता चतुःषष्टिकलानिधिः ॥ 
चतुःषष्टिमहासिद्धयोगिनीवृंदवंदितः ॥ १६३॥


Shloka 576-577: Catuḥṣaṣṭyarṇanirṇetā (चतुःषष्ट्यर्णनिर्णेता) & Catuḥṣaṣṭikalanidhiḥ (चतुःषष्टिकलानिधिः)
चतुःषष्ट्यर्णनिर्णेता चतुःषष्टिकलानिधिः । अक्षोभ्या-दिचतुःषष्टिमिथुनैर्वदितो भवान् ॥ ५७७ ॥
He is the ascertainer (Nirṇetā) of the sixty-four letters (Catuḥṣaṣṭi Varṇa) — the fifty-one Mātṛkās plus the thirteen additional vowels or modified forms. He is the repository (Nidhi) of the sixty-four arts (Kalā). You are spoken of as having sixty-four pairs (Mithuna) starting with Akṣobhya. 
Thus, He is Catuḥṣaṣṭyarṇanirṇetā (The One Who Ascertains the Sixty-Four Syllables) and Catuḥṣaṣṭikalanidhiḥ (The Treasure-House of the Sixty-Four Arts).


Shloka 577: Catuḥṣaṣṭimahāsiddhayoginīvṛndavanditaḥ (चतुःषष्टिमहासिद्धयोगिनीवृंदवंदितः)
चतुःषष्टिमहा-सिद्धयोगिनीवृंदवंदितः ॥
He who is worshipped (Vandita) by the hosts (Vṛnda) of the sixty-four great Siddhas and the sixty-four Yoginīs is Catuḥṣaṣṭimahāsiddhayoginīvṛndavanditaḥ (The One Worshipped by the Hosts of the Sixty-Four Great Siddhas and Yoginīs).


अष्टषष्टिमहातीर्थक्षेत्र भैरवभावनः ॥ 
चतुर्नवतिमंत्रात्मा षण्णवत्यधिकप्रभुः ॥१६४॥


Shloka 579: Aṣṭaṣaṣṭimahātīrthakṣetra-bhairavabhāvanaḥ (अष्टषष्टिमहातीर्थक्षेत्र भैरवभावनः)
शैवतीर्थान्यष्टषष्टिः श्रीकाशीखंडपाद्मयोः ॥ ५७८ ॥ 
उक्तानि तेषु क्षेत्रेषु भावयन् भैरवं शिवम् । अष्टषष्टिमहातीर्थ-क्षेत्रभैरवभावनः ॥ ५७९ ॥
Sixty-eight sacred Śaiva Tīrthas (pilgrimage sites) are described in the Kāśīkhaṇḍa and Padma Purāṇa. Meditating upon Lord Bhairava (Śiva) in those holy places (Kṣetra) and filling them with his essence, He is Aṣṭaṣaṣṭimahātīrthakṣetra-bhairavabhāvanaḥ (The One Who Contemplates Bhairava in the Sixty-Eight Great Sacred Tīrthas and Kṣetras).












Shloka 581: Caturnavatimantrātmā (चतुर्नवतिमंत्रात्मा)
अष्टात्रिंशत्कला मंत्राः पञ्चाश-न्मातृकाः कलाः । हंसः शुचिप्रतद्विष्णुर्विष्णुर्योनि त्रियंबकम् ॥ ५८० ॥ तद्विष्णोर्मूलविद्येति चतुर्नवतिसंज्ञकाः । द्रव्य-शुद्ध्यै कलासूत्रे जामदग्येन वर्णिताः ॥ ५८१ ॥ चतुर्नवति-मंत्रात्मा तावन्मंत्रात्मको भवान् ॥
The thirty-eight Kalā mantras, the fifty Mātṛkā Kalās, and the mantras Haṁsa, Śuci, Pratad Viṣṇu, Viṣṇur Yoni, Tryambakam, Tad Viṣṇoḥ, and Mūlavidyā — together these comprise the ninety-four mantras (Caturnavati Mantra). You are the embodiment (Ātmā) of these ninety-four mantras. Thus, He is Caturnavatimantrātmā (The One Who is the Essence of the Ninety-Four Mantras).


Shloka 583: Ṣaṇṇavatyadhikaprabhuḥ (षण्णवत्यधिकप्रभुः)
तंत्रराजे षण्ण वतिः प्रोक्ताः श्रीचक्रदेवताः ॥ ५८२ ॥ विद्यागणेशयोर्योगा-ज्जायतेऽधिकदेवताः । तासामीशः समस्तानां षण्णवत्य-धिकप्रभुः ॥ ५८३ ॥
In the Tantrarāja, the deities of the Śrīcakra are said to be ninety-six (Ṣaṇṇavati). By the union of Vidyā and Gaṇeśa, additional deities are born. He who is the lord (Prabhu) of all those ninety-six and more is Ṣaṇṇavatyadhikaprabhuḥ (The Lord of Ninety-Six and More).
(The commentary rejects an alternative interpretation of this name involving 360 days of the year as contextually inconsistent and contrary to mantra tradition.)


शतानन्दः शतधृतिः शतपत्रायतेक्षणः ॥ 
शतानीकः शतमखः शतधारावरायुधः ॥ १६५ ॥


Shloka 584: Śatānandaḥ (शतानन्दः)
शतानन्दः शतगुणा आनन्दा मानुषादयः । ब्रह्मानन्दा-दिमाये ये ते सर्वेऽपि तदात्मकाः ॥ ५८४ ॥
The forms of bliss (Ānanda) are manifold: human bliss, divine bliss, and the bliss of Brahmā, etc. These are multiplied a hundredfold (Śataguṇa). You are the embodiment of all these. Thus, He is Śatānandaḥ (The Hundredfold Bliss / The One Whose Bliss is Hundredfold).


Shloka 584: Śatadhṛtiḥ (शतधृतिः)
ब्रह्मांडानामन-न्तानां धर्ता शतधृतिर्भवान् ॥
You are the bearer (Dhṛti) of innumerable universes (Brahmāṇḍas). Thus, He is Śatadhṛtiḥ (The Hundredfold Supporter / One with Hundred Supports).








Shloka 585: Śatapatrāyatekṣaṇaḥ (शतपत्रायतेक्षणः)
पद्मपत्रायताक्षस्त्वं शत-पत्रायतेक्षणः ॥ ५८५ ॥
Your eyes are long and beautiful like the petals of the lotus (Padmapatra). Thus, He is Śatapatrāyatekṣaṇaḥ (The One Whose Eyes are Long Like a Hundred Lotus Petals / The Lotus-Eyed One).


Shloka 586: Śatānīkaḥ (शतानीकः)
शतानीको भूरिसैन्यः शक्रः शतमखो भवान् । अथवा बहुसंख्यानि पूजनादीनि यस्य सः ॥ ५८६ ॥
He who possesses a great army or a hundred leaders (Śatanīka), or he who is Indra (Śakra) performing a hundred sacrifices (Śatamakha). He to whom countless forms of worship (Pūjana) are offered. Thus, He is Śatānīkaḥ (The One with a Hundred Armies/The One of Hundred Sacrifices) and Śatamakhaḥ (The One Who Performed a Hundred Sacrifices — Indra).


Shloka 587: Śatadhārāvarāyudhaḥ (शतधारावरायुधः)
शतं धारा अरास्तीक्षा यदायुधवरस्य सः । वज्रो-त्कृष्टायुधो वाऽपि शतधारावरायुधः ॥ ५८७ ॥
The best of weapons (Varāyudha) — the thunderbolt (Vajra) — has a hundred edges or sharp points (Śatadhāra). He who possesses that supreme weapon is Śatadhārāvarāyudhaḥ (The One Whose Excellent Weapon Has a Hundred Edges / The Bearer of the Vajra).


सहस्रपत्रनिलयः सहस्रफणभूषणः ॥ 
सहस्रशीर्षापुरुषः सहस्राक्षः सहस्रपात् ॥ १६६॥


Shloka 588: Sahasrapatranilayaḥ (सहस्रपत्रनिलयः)
सहस्रपत्रनिलयो ब्रह्मरंध्रस्थपद्मगः ॥
He who abides (Nilaya) in the lotus of a thousand petals (Sahasrapatra) situated in the Brahma-randhra (the aperture at the crown of the head) is Sahasrapatranilayaḥ (The One Who Dwells in the Thousand-Petaled Lotus).


Shloka 588: Sahasraphaṇabhūṣaṇaḥ (सहस्रफणभूषणः)
फण। सहस्रवद्भूष्यः सहस्रफणभूषणः ॥ ५८८ ॥
He who is adorned (Bhūṣaṇa) with a thousand serpent hoods (Sahasraphaṇa) — like Śeṣa or other serpents — is Sahasraphaṇabhūṣaṇaḥ (The One Adorned with a Thousand Serpent Hoods).




Shloka 589: Sahasraśīrṣāpuruṣaḥ (सहस्रशीर्षापुरुषः)
सहस्रशीर्षापुरुष इत्येकं नाम ते स्फुटम् ॥
"The Puruṣa (Cosmic Being) with a thousand heads" — this is clearly one name. As stated in the Puruṣa Sūkta: "Sahasraśīrṣā Puruṣaḥ" — Sahasraśīrṣāpuruṣaḥ (The Thousand-Headed Cosmic Person).


Shloka 589: Sahasrākṣaḥ (सहस्राक्षः) & Sahasrapāt (सहस्रपात्)
अनंतनेत्रचरणः सहस्राक्षः सहस्रपात् ॥ ५८९ ॥
He who has a thousand eyes (Sahasrākṣa) and a thousand feet (Sahasrapāt). Thus, He is Sahasrākṣaḥ (The Thousand-Eyed One) and Sahasrapāt (The Thousand-Footed One).


सहस्रनामसंस्तुत्यः सहस्राक्षबलापहः ॥ 
दशसाहस्रफणभृत्फणिराजकृतासनः ॥ १६७ ॥


Shloka 590: Sahasranāmasaṃstutyaḥ (सहस्रनामसंस्तुत्यः)
सहस्रनामसंस्तुत्यः स्तुत्यर्थोऽसंख्यनामभिः । यमा-विशन्ति नामानि सर्वाणीति किल श्रुतेः ॥ ५९० ॥
He who is praised (Saṃstutya) by a thousand names (Sahasranāma) — indeed, all names enter into you, as the Śruti says. Thus, He is Sahasranāmasaṃstutyaḥ (The One Praised by a Thousand Names).


Shloka 590: Sahasrākṣabalāpahaḥ (सहस्राक्षबलापहः)
प्रसिद्धं तत्पुराणे यत् सहस्राक्षबलापहः ।
It is well-known in the Purāṇa that he is the destroyer (Apaha) of the strength (Bala) of the thousand-eyed (Sahasrākṣa) Indra. Thus, He is Sahasrākṣabalāpahaḥ (The One Who Destroyed the Power of Thousand-Eyed Indra).


Shloka 591: Daśasāhasraphaṇabhṛt-phaṇirājakṛtāsanaḥ (दशसाहस्रफणभृत्फणिराजकृतासनः)
दशसाहस्रफणभृ-त्फणिराजकृतासनः ॥ ५९१ ॥ इच्छया सृष्टिनिर्मातुः किमसाध्यं तव प्रभो ॥
He who is seated (Āsana) upon the King of Serpents (Phaṇirāja) — Śeṣa — who bears ten thousand hoods (Daśasāhasraphaṇa). Thus, He is Daśasāhasraphaṇabhṛt-phaṇirājakṛtāsanaḥ (The One Who Has Made a Seat of the Serpent-King Who Bears Ten Thousand Hoods).










अष्टाशीतिसहस्राद्यमहर्षिस्तोत्रयंत्रितः ॥ 
लक्षाधीशप्रियाधारो लक्षाधारमनोमयः ॥ १६८ ॥


Shloka 593: Aṣṭāśītisahasrādya-maharṣistotrayantritaḥ (अष्टाशीतिसहस्राद्यमहर्षिस्तोत्रयंत्रितः)
सर्वविद्या कलाराशिरक्षणैकप्रयोजनः ॥ ५९२ ॥ विष्णुना हिमवत्पृष्ठे स्थापिता गृहमेधिनः । पुराणे वैष्णवे प्रोक्तास्तैः स्तु-त्योऽसि नियंत्रितः॥५९३॥ अष्टाशीतिसहस्राद्यमहर्षिस्तो-त्रयंत्रितः ॥
In the Viṣṇu Purāṇa, the eighty-eight thousand great sages (Aṣṭāśītisahasra Maharṣi) were established by Viṣṇu on the slopes of the Himalayas. You are praised (Stotra) and controlled (Yantrita) by them through their hymns. Thus, He is Aṣṭāśītisahasrādya-maharṣistotrayantritaḥ (The One Who is Praised and Worshipped through the Hymns of the Eighty-Eight Thousand Great Sages).


Shloka 594: Lakṣādhīśapriyādhāraḥ (लक्षाधीशप्रियाधारः)
लक्षाधीशप्रियाधारो लक्षाधीशा धनेश्वराः ॥ ५९४ ॥ तेषां प्रियः सन्नाधारो धनवृद्ध्यर्थमाश्रितः ॥
The lords of hundreds of thousands (Lakṣādhīśa) are the lords of wealth (Dhaneśvara), i.e., Kubera and his attendants. He is dear (Priya) to them and is their support (Ādhāra), resorted to for the increase of wealth. Thus, He is Lakṣādhīśapriyādhāraḥ (The One Who is the Beloved Support of the Lords of Hundreds of Thousands).


Shloka 595: Lakṣādhāramanomayaḥ (लक्षाधारमनोमयः)
लक्षाणि चित्तस्थैर्यार्थ प्रसिद्धानि तदाश्रितं ॥ ५९५ ॥ मनो यस्य तदात्त्मा त्वं लक्षाधारमनोमयः ॥
The hundreds of thousands (Lakṣa) of mantras or objects are well-known for steadying the mind. That mind rests upon him, and he is of the nature of that mind. You are that. Thus, He is Lakṣādhāramanomayaḥ (The One Whose Mind is the Support of Hundreds of Thousands [of Mantras or Thoughts]).


चतुर्लक्षजपप्रीतश्चतुर्लक्षप्रकाशितः ॥ 
चतुरशीतिलक्षाणां जीवानां देहसंस्थितः ॥ १६९ ॥


Shloka 596: Caturlakṣajapaprītaḥ (चतुर्लक्षजपप्रीतश्चतुर्लक्षप्रकाशितः)
चतुर्लक्षजपप्रीनः पुरश्चर्या हि सा मनोः ॥ ५९६ ॥
He who is pleased (Prīta) by the repetition (Japa) of a mantra four hundred thousand times (Caturlakṣa) — that being the preliminary rite (Puraścaraṇa) for the mantra — is Caturlakṣajapaprītaḥ (The One Pleased by Four Hundred Thousand Japa Repetitions).
Shloka 597: Caturlakṣaprakāśitaḥ (चतुर्लक्षप्रकाशितः)
पुराणाष्टादशग्रंथसंख्या लक्षचतुष्टयम् । तैः प्रकाशित रूपस्त्वं चतुर्लक्षप्रकाशितः ॥ ५९७ ॥
The eighteen Purāṇas consist of four hundred thousand (Caturlakṣa) verses. You, whose form is revealed (Prakāśita) by these, are Caturlakṣaprakāśitaḥ (The One Revealed by the Four Hundred Thousand [Verses of the Purāṇas]).


Shloka 598: Caturśītilakṣāṇāṁ Jīvānāṁ Dehasaṁsthitaḥ (चतुरशीतिलक्षाणां जीवानां देहसंस्थितः)
सर्वजीवात्मतेयत्तानवच्छेदश्च रोचिषः। कलंकतापासंपर्कः कथ्यते नामभिस्त्रिभिः ॥ ५९८ ॥ चतुरशीतिलक्षाणां जीवानां देहसंस्थितः ॥
He who dwells (Dehasaṁsthitaḥ) within the bodies of the eighty-four hundred thousand (Caturśītilakṣa) species of living beings (Jīvas) is Caturśītilakṣāṇāṁ Jīvānāṁ Dehasaṁsthitaḥ (The One Who Resides in the Bodies of Eighty-Four Lakh Species of Souls).

Shloka 599: Koṭisūryapratīkāśaḥ (कोटिसूर्यप्रतीकाशः)
कोटिसूर्यप्रतीकाशः कोटिचंद्रांशुनिर्मलः ॥ ५९९ ॥
He who shines (Pratīkāśa) like ten million suns (Koṭi Sūrya) is Koṭisūryapratīkāśaḥ (The One Who Resembles Ten Million Suns).


Shloka 599: Koṭicandrāṁśunirmalaḥ (कोटिचन्द्रांशुनिर्मलः)
He who is as spotless (Nirmala) as the rays of ten million moons (Koṭi Candra) is Koṭicandrāṁśunirmalaḥ (The One as Spotless as the Rays of Ten Million Moons).


Shloka 600: Śivā-bhavādhyuṣṭa-koṭi-vināyaka-dhuraṁdharaḥ (शिवाभवाध्युष्टकोटिविनायकधुरंधरः)
ग्रहा विनायकाभिख्याः सार्धकोटित्रयात्मकाः । भवस्य च शिवायाश्च प्रत्येकं संति तत्पतिः ॥ ६०० ॥ शिवाभवाध्युष्ट-कोटिविनायकधुरंधरः ॥
There are groups of Vināyakas (obstacle-creating deities) numbering three and a half crores (Sārdha-koṭi-traya) each for Bhava (Śiva) and for Śivā (Pārvatī). He who is the bearer of the burden (Dhuraṁdhara) of the crores of Vināyakas presided over (Adhyuṣṭa) by Śivā and Bhava is Śivā-bhavādhyuṣṭa-koṭi-vināyaka-dhuraṁdharaḥ (The One Who Bears the Burden of the Crores of Vināyakas Ruled Over by Śivā and Bhava).


सप्तकोटिमहामंत्रमंत्रितावयवद्युतिः ॥ 
त्रयस्त्रिंशत्कोटिसुर श्रेणीप्रणतपादुकः ॥ १७१ ॥


Shloka 602: Saptakoṭimahāmantramantritāvayavadyutiḥ (सप्तकोटिमहामंत्रमंत्रितावयवद्युतिः)
विश्वरूपस्य तेऽङ्गानि सर्वमंत्राधिदेवताः ॥ ६०१ ॥ अतः संजातमंत्रैस्तैर्घोतसे ऽवयवैरपि । सप्तकोटिमहामंत्रमंत्रिता-वयवद्युतिः ॥ ६०२॥
The limbs of your universal form are the presiding deities of all mantras. Therefore, you shine through those mantras that have arisen from your limbs. He whose limbs are consecrated (Mantrita) by the seven crore (Saptakoṭi) great mantras and whose splendor (Dyuti) radiates from them is Saptakoṭimahāmantramantritāvayavadyutiḥ (The One Whose Limbs are Illuminated by the Seven Crore Great Mantras).


Shloka 603: Trayastriṁśatkoṭisuraśreṇīpraṇatapādukaḥ (त्रयस्त्रिंशत्कोटिसुर श्रेणीप्रणतपादुकः)
ईदृशान्नाधिकस्त्वत्तः कोऽपि देवस्ततो भवान् । त्रयस्त्रिशत्कोटिसुरश्रेणीप्रणतपादुकः ॥ ६०३
There is no deity greater than you. Therefore, the entire host of thirty-three crore (Trayastriṃśatkoṭi) gods bow down (Praṇata) at your feet. Thus, He is Trayastriṁśatkoṭisuraśreṇīpraṇatapādukaḥ (The One Whose Feet are Bowed to by the Host of Thirty-Three Crore Gods).


अनन्तनामानन्तश्रीरनन्तानन्तसौख्यदः ॥
ॐ इति वैनायकं नाम्नां सहस्रमिदमीरितम् ॥ १७२ ॥


Shloka 604: Anantanāmā (अनन्तनामा)
अनंतनामा सर्वेऽपि शब्दास्त्वन्नामतां गताः । यावत्प्रति-ष्ठितं ब्रह्म तावती वागिति श्रुतेः ॥ ६०४ ॥
He who has infinite names (Anantanāmā) — for all words have become your names, as the Śruti says: "As far as Brahman extends, so far does speech extend." Thus, He is Anantanāmā (The One with Infinite Names).


Shloka 605: Anantaśrīḥ (अनन्तश्रीः)
अनंतश्रीर-संख्यास्ते विद्यासंपत्तिकीर्तयः । प्रातिस्विकमनं तानामानन्त्ये गणना तु या ॥ ६०५ ॥
He whose glory (Śrī) is infinite (Ananta) — your knowledge, wealth, and fame are countless; the reckoning of their infinity is beyond enumeration. Thus, He is Anantaśrīḥ (The One of Infinite Glory).




Shloka 606: Anantānantasaukhyadaḥ (अनन्तानन्तसौख्यदः)
तावंतं दददानन्द मनन्तानन्त-सौख्यदः । अनंतानंददानं तु स्वाद्वैते पर्यवस्यति ॥ ६०६ ॥
He who bestows (Da) happiness (Saukhya) that is infinite (Ananta) and endless (Ananta) is Anantānantasaukhyadaḥ (The Bestower of Boundless and Endless Happiness). The giving of infinite bliss culminates in the state of non-duality (Advaita).

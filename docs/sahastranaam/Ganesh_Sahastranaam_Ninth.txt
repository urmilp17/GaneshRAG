Shloka 456: Mokṣaḥ (मोक्षः)
(The destruction of that illusion is liberation. He is that as well.) Thus, He is Mokṣaḥ (Liberation).


Shloka 457: Sukham (सुखम्) & Bhogaḥ (भोगः)
क्षस्त्वविद्याविध्वंसस्ततो यदवशिष्यते । तत्सुखं प्रत्य-गानन्दो भोगः सोऽनुभवात्मकः ॥ ४५७ ॥
What remains after the destruction of ignorance is Sukha (Bliss). That inner Ānanda is Bhoga (Experience), which is of the nature of direct perception. Thus, He is Sukham (Bliss) and Bhogaḥ (Experience/Enjoyment).

Shloka 457: Ayogaḥ (अयोगः)
अयोगः संग-रहितः ।
He who is free from all attachment, or the state of non-union (disconnection), is Ayogaḥ. Thus, He is Ayogaḥ (Non-Attachment/Disunion).


Shloka 458: Satyam (सत्यम्)
तद्धि सत्यमबाधितम् ।
That which is uncontradicted is Satyam (Truth). Thus, He is Satyam (Truth).


Shloka 458: Aṇuḥ (अणुः)
स एवाणुर्मनःषष्ठेन्द्रियागोचरतावशात् ॥ ४५८ ॥
He alone is the Aṇuḥ (Minute/Atom) because He is beyond the reach of the mind and the five senses (and the sixth, the intellect). Thus, He is Aṇuḥ (The Subtle/Atom).


Shloka 458: Mahān (महान्)
ततोऽधिकस्य सौख्यस्याभावादेव स वै महान् ॥
Because there is no greater bliss than Him, He is indeed Mahān (The Great). 
Thus, He is Mahān (The Great).


Shloka 459: Svasti (स्वस्ति)
तदेव सम्यगस्तीति स्वस्ति नास्ति ततः परम् ॥ ४५९ ॥
That which truly exists is Svasti (Well-being/Auspiciousness). Nothing exists beyond it. Thus, He is Svasti (Auspiciousness).


Shloka 460: Huṁ (हुं) & Phaṭ (फट्)
अन्यस्य दूरीकरणात्तदेव ब्रह्म हुं मतम् । दूरो-त्सारणमात्रं न किंतु नाशोऽपि तेन फटू ॥ ४६० ॥
That very Brahman is considered Huṁ, because it repels everything else. It is not merely the act of keeping away, but also the destruction; therefore, Phaṭ. Thus, He is Huṁ (The Repelling Seed) and Phaṭ (The Destroying Mantra).


Shloka 461: Svadhā (स्वधा), Svāhā (स्वाहा), Śrauṣaṭ (श्रौषट्), Vauṣaṭ (वौषट्), Vaṣaṭ (वषट्), Namaḥ (नमः)
खधेति पितृदैवत्यं स्वाहेत्यन्नन्यादिदैवतम् । औषडित्यादयस्तत्तच्छब्दयुक्रर्मलक्षकाः ॥ ४६१ ॥
Svadhā is the invocation for the ancestors (Pitṛs). Svāhā is for Agni and other deities. Śrauṣaṭ, Vauṣaṭ, Vaṣaṭ, and Namaḥ are ritual utterances that characterize various Vedic actions (karma). Thus, He is Svadhā (The Offering to Ancestors), Svāhā (The Offering to Fire), Śrauṣaṭ (The Mantra for Praise), Vauṣaṭ (The Mantra for Pouring), Vaṣaṭ (The Mantra of Offering), and Namaḥ (The Mantra of Salutation).


Shloka 462: Mahāphale (महाफले) / Summary
सर्वेषां कर्मणामिष्टसिद्ध्यर्थत्वान्महाफले । सुखे लब्धे तु तत्रैव तत्सर्व पर्यवस्यति ॥ ४६२ ॥ तेन कर्मापि सकलं भवेदेकात्मकं सुखम् ॥
Since all rituals aim to attain desired results, their culmination is the great fruit (Mahāphale). When bliss (Sukham) is attained, everything resolves into that alone. Thus, all action (Karma) becomes one with that bliss. He is that ultimate fruit. Thus, He is Mahāphale (The Great Fruit).


ज्ञानं विज्ञानमानन्दो बोधः संविच्छमो यमः ॥ 
एक एकाक्षराधार एकाक्षरपरायणः ॥ १३२ ॥


Shloka 463: Jñānam (ज्ञानम्) & Vijñānam (विज्ञानम्)
मोक्षे धीर्ज्ञानमन्यत्र विज्ञानं तद्वयं भवान् ॥ ४६३ ॥
The knowledge that leads to liberation is Jñānam. Knowledge of everything else (worldly matters) is Vijñānam. You are both of these. Thus, He is Jñānam (Supreme Knowledge) and Vijñānam (Applied Knowledge).


Shloka 464: Ānandaḥ (आनन्दः) & Bodhaḥ (बोधः)
त्वमेव प्रत्यगानन्दः प्रत्यग्बोधस्त्वमेव हि । योगिभिः सेव्यते संविद्वाह्यवृत्तिनिरासिका ॥ ४६४ ॥
You indeed are inner Ānanda (Bliss). You indeed are inner Bodha (Awareness/Consciousness). That which is served by Yogis, the Consciousness (Saṃvit) that removes external mental states, is you. Thus, He is Ānandaḥ (Bliss) and Bodhaḥ (Awakening/Consciousness).


Shloka 464: Saṃvit (संवित्)
Thus, He is Saṃvit (Pure Consciousness).


Shloka 465: Śamaḥ (शमः) & Yamaḥ (यमः)
सर्वस्य शमनात्सर्वोपरमात्त्वं शमो यमः ॥ ४६५ ॥
Because you are the allayer (Śamana) of everything and the cessation (Uparama) of everything, you are Śamaḥ (Tranquility/Control of Mind) and Yamaḥ (Restraint/Regulation). Thus, He is Śamaḥ (Inner Calmness) and Yamaḥ (Self-Control).


Shloka 466: Ekaḥ (एकः)
एक एवाद्वितीयस्त्वं भेदत्रैविध्यशून्यतः ॥ ४६६॥
You alone are One (Ekaḥ) without a second, devoid of the three kinds of differences (variations within itself, variations from others, variations of its own nature). Thus, He is Ekaḥ (The One).


Shloka 466: Ekākṣarādhāraḥ (एकाक्षराधारः)
गंबीजान्तस्थितेरेकाक्षराधार इति स्मृतः ॥
Because you are established in the seed-syllable 'Gaṁ', you are remembered as Ekākṣarādhāraḥ (The Support of the Single Syllable).


Shloka 466: Ekākṣaraparāyaṇaḥ (एकाक्षरपरायणः)
ॐमित्ये-काक्षरस्थत्वादेकाक्षरपरायणः ॥
Because you are established in the single syllable 'Oṁ' (which is of the nature of Brahman), you are Ekākṣaraparāyaṇaḥ (The One Devoted to / Abiding as the Single Syllable).


एकाग्रधीरेकवीर एकानेकस्वरूपधृक् ॥ 
द्विरूपो द्विभुजो द्यक्षो द्विरदो द्वीपरक्षकः ॥ १३३ ॥


Shloka 467: Ekāgrasdhīḥ (एकाग्रधीः)
एकाग्रधीः स्वात्ममात्रप्रवणापि मतिर्भवान् ॥ ४६७ ॥
You are Ekāgrasdhīḥ (The One of Unwavering Intellect) — the intellect that is single-pointed and inclined only towards the Self.


Shloka 468: Ekavīraḥ (एकवीरः)
अहमि प्रलयं कुर्वन्निदमः प्रतियोगिनी । पराक्रमपरोऽस्येक-वीरस्त्वत्तो न चापरः ॥ ४६८ ॥
Destroying the ego (Aham) and its counterpart "this" (Idam), you are the supreme hero of valor. There is no other hero like you. Thus, He is Ekavīraḥ (The Sole Hero).


Shloka 469: Ekānekasvarūpadhṛk (एकानेकस्वरूपधृक्)
प्रतिभूतमवस्थानादेका-नेकस्वरूपधृक् । एकधा बहुधा चैव दृश्यसे जलचन्द्रवत् ॥ ४६९ ॥
Because you reside in every living being, you assume both one and many forms. You are seen as one and as many, like the moon reflected in water. Thus, He is Ekānekasvarūpadhṛk (The One Who Assumes Both Single and Multiple Forms).


Shloka 470: Dvirūpaḥ (द्विरूपः)
द्वे ब्रह्मणी इति श्रुत्या द्विरूपोऽस्यपरं परम् । त्वमेव द्विभुजो यक्षो द्विरदः प्रकटार्थकः ॥ ४७० ॥
According to the Śruti, "There are two Brahmans" (the Absolute and the conditioned). Therefore, you possess two forms (the transcendent and the immanent). Thus, He is Dvirūpaḥ (The Two-Formed One).


Shloka 470: Dvibhujaḥ (द्विभुजः)
Thus, He is Dvibhujaḥ (The Two-Armed One), as the meaning is clear.


Shloka 470: Dvakṣaḥ (द्व्यक्षः / द्व्यक्षः)
Thus, He is Dvakṣaḥ (The Two-Eyed One).


Shloka 470: Dviradaḥ (द्विरदः)
Thus, He is Dviradaḥ (The Two-Tusked One / Elephant).


Shloka 470: Dvīparakṣakaḥ (द्वीपरक्षकः)
सप्त-द्वीपाधिपत्यस्य प्रदानाद्वीपरक्षकः ॥
Because you bestow sovereignty over the seven continents (Dvīpa), you are their protector. Thus, He is Dvīparakṣakaḥ (The Protector of the Continents).


द्वैमातुरो द्विवदनो द्वन्द्वातीतो द्वयातिगः ॥ 
त्रिधामा त्रिकरस्त्रेतात्रिवर्गफलदायकः ॥१३४॥


Shloka 471: Dvaimāturaḥ (द्वैमातुरः)
द्वैमातुरोऽस्युमागङ्गे मातरौ तव विश्श्रुते ॥ ४७१ ॥
You are celebrated as Dvaimāturaḥ (The One with Two Mothers) — Umā (Pārvatī) and Gaṅgā are your two mothers.


Shloka 471: Dvivadanaḥ (द्विवदनः)
तवैव मूर्तिरग्न्याख्या तेन द्विवदनो भवान् ॥
The form called Agni (Fire) is indeed yours; therefore, you are Dvivadanaḥ (The Two-Faced One).

Shloka 472: Dvandvātītaḥ (द्वन्द्वातीतः)
शीतोष्णादीन्यति-क्रान्तो द्वन्द्वातीत इति श्रुतः ॥ ४७२ ॥
Having transcended dualities such as cold and heat, you are known as Dvandvātītaḥ (The One Beyond Duality).


Shloka 472: Dvayātigaḥ (द्वयातिगः)
रजस्तमश्चा-तिलङ्घय वर्तमानो द्वयातिगः ॥
Transcending the two qualities of Rajas and Tamas (and also Sattva), you are Dvayātigaḥ (The One Who Has Surpassed the Pair of Opposites).


Shloka 473: Tridhāmā (त्रिधामा)
त्रिधामा सूर्यचन्द्राग्नितेज-स्त्रितयमूर्तिमान् ॥ ४७३ ॥
You are Tridhāmā (The Possessor of Three Luminaries) — embodied as the three forms of radiance: the sun, the moon, and fire.


Shloka 473: Trikaraḥ (त्रिकरः)
त्रयाणामपि लोकानां करणात्रि-करो भवान् ॥
Because you are the agent (Kara) or the hand (Kara) for the three worlds, you are Trikaraḥ (The Three-Handed One).


Shloka 474: Tretātrivargaphaladāyakaḥ (त्रेतात्रिवर्गफलदायकः)
नर्याद्यग्नित्रया काङ्गधर्मकामार्थकर्मणाम् ॥४७४॥ फलप्रद इति त्रेतात्रिवर्गफलदायकः ॥
You bestow the fruits of the three goals of life (Trivarga: Dharma, Artha, Kāma) in the Tretā Yuga (and by extension, all ages). Thus, He is Tretātrivargaphaladāyakaḥ (The Bestower of the Fruits of the Three Goals in the Tretā Age).


त्रिगुणात्मा त्रिलोकादिस्त्रिशक्तीशस्त्रिलोचनः ॥ 
चतुर्बाहुश्चतुर्दन्तश्चतुरात्मा चतुर्मुखः ॥१३५॥


Shloka 475: Trigunātmā (त्रिगुणात्मा)
गुणत्रयवती मूलप्रकृतिस्त्रिगुणात्मता ॥ ४७५ ॥ तस्या धृतिस्त्वमेवेति त्रिगुणात्मेति गीयसे ॥
The primordial nature (Mūlaprakṛti) possessing the three qualities (Guṇas) is the state of being Trigunātmā. You are the sustainer of that; therefore, you are sung as Trigunātmā (The One Whose Self is the Three Qualities).
Shloka 476: Trilokādiḥ (त्रिलोकादिः)
त्रयाणामपि लोकानां त्रिलोकादिश्च कारणम् ॥ ४७६ ॥
The creator (Ādi) and cause of the three worlds: thus, He is Trilokādiḥ (The Origin of the Three Worlds).


Shloka 477: Triśaktīśaḥ (त्रिशक्तीशः)
रमालज्जानङ्गयोग स्त्रि-शक्तिः कथ्यते मनुः । प्राभावौत्साहमान्च्या वा त्रिशक्तीशस्तदीश्वरः ॥ ४७७ ॥
The triad of powers (Triśakti) is spoken of as: Ramā (Lakṣmī), Lajjā (Modesty), and Aṅgayoga? or as power of manifestation (Prabhāva), enthusiasm (Utsāha), and wisdom? He is the lord (Īśa) of those three powers. Thus, He is Triśaktīśaḥ (The Lord of the Three Powers).


Shloka 477: Trilocanaḥ (त्रिलोचनः)
त्रिलोचनः... इति स्फुटम् ॥
He who has three eyes: thus, Trilocanaḥ (The Three-Eyed One) is clear.


Shloka 478: Caturbāhuḥ (चतुर्बाहुः)
चतुर्बाहुः... इति स्फुटम् ॥
He who has four arms: thus, Caturbāhuḥ (The Four-Armed One) is clear.


Shloka 478: Caturdantaḥ (चतुर्दन्तः)
चतुर्दन्त... इति स्फुटम् ॥
He who has four tusks: thus, Caturdantaḥ (The Four-Tusked One) is clear.


Shloka 478: Caturātmā (चतुरात्मा)
आत्मान्तरात्मा ज्ञानात्मा परमात्मेति भेदतः ॥४७८॥ आत्मोपनिषदि प्रोक्तश्चतुरात्मा त्वमेव हि ॥
The different forms of the Self: the individual self (Ātmā), the inner self (Antarātmā), the knowledge self (Jñānātmā?), and the supreme self (Paramātmā). As proclaimed in the Ātmopaniṣad, you indeed are Caturātmā (The Fourfold Self).


Shloka 479: Caturmukhaḥ (चतुर्मुखः)
चतुर्विधा मुखे वेदा यस्य स त्वं चतुर्मुखः ॥
The four Vedas exist on your face; therefore, you are Caturmukhaḥ (The Four-Faced One).

चतुर्विधोपायमयश्चतुर्वर्णाश्रमाश्रयः ॥
चतुर्विधवचोवृत्तिपरिवृत्तिप्रवर्तकः ॥ १३६ ॥


Shloka 480: Caturvidhopāyamayaḥ (चतुर्विधोपायमयः)
भेदो दण्डः साम दानमित्युपायचतुष्टयम् । चतुर्विधो-पायमयस्तज्जन्य फलसाधकः ॥ ४८० ॥
The four means (Upāya) are: Bheda (creating dissension), Daṇḍa (punishment), Sāma (conciliation), and Dāna (gift). He who embodies these four means and accomplishes the fruit born of them is Caturvidhopāyamayaḥ (The One Who is the Embodiment of the Four Means).


Shloka 481: Caturvarṇāśramāśrayaḥ (चतुर्वर्णाश्रमाश्रयः)
ब्राह्मणब्रह्मचर्यादि-वर्णाश्रमविभागशः । विहितैः कर्मभिः प्राप्यश्चतुर्वर्णाश्रमा-श्रयः ॥ ४८१॥
He who is attained through the prescribed duties (Karmas) according to the division of the four Varṇas (castes: Brāhmaṇa, Kṣatriya, Vaiśya, Śūdra) and the four Āśramas (stages of life: Brahmacarya, Gārhasthya, Vānaprastha, Saṃnyāsa) is Caturvarṇāśramāśrayaḥ (The Refuge of the Four Castes and Four Stages of Life).


Shloka 482: Caturvidhavacovṛttiparivṛttipravartakaḥ (चतुर्विधवचोवृत्तिपरिवृत्तिप्रवर्तकः)
गुहायां वर्तयंस्तिस्त्रः पश्यंति मध्यमा परा । वैखरी परितो बाह्यवर्तनेन प्रवर्तयन् ॥ ४८२ ॥ चतुर्विध-बचोवृत्तिपरिवृत्तिप्रवर्तकः ॥
He who activates the fourfold stages of speech (Vāc): operating within the cavity (of the heart) are the three – Paśyantī, Madhyamā, and Parā, while activating Vaikharī externally in the world through sequential expression is Caturvidhavacovṛttiparivṛttipravartakaḥ (The One Who Sets in Motion the Cycle of the Fourfold Functions of Speech).


चतुर्थीपूजनप्रीतश्चतुर्थीतिथिसंभवः ॥ 
पञ्चाक्षरात्मा पञ्चात्मा पञ्चास्यः पञ्चकृत्यकृत् ॥ १३७ ॥


Shloka 483: Caturthīpūjanaprītaḥ (चतुर्थीपूजनप्रीत) & Caturthītithisaṁbhavaḥ (चतुर्थीतिथिसंभवः)
चतुर्थीपूजनप्रीतश्चतुर्थीतिथिसंभवः । इति नाम द्वयं स्पष्टं स्याद्गणेशपुराणतः ॥ ४८३ ॥
He is delighted by worship (Pūjana) on the fourth lunar day (Caturthī), and he is born from (or manifests on) the fourth lunar day. These two names are clear from the Gaṇeśa Purāṇa. Thus, He is Caturthīpūjanaprītaḥ (The One Pleased by Worship on the Fourth Day) and Caturthītithisaṁbhavaḥ (The One Who Manifests on the Fourth Day).
Shloka 484: Pañcākṣarātmā (पञ्चाक्षरात्मा)
नादबिन्दुमका रोकारा-काराः प्रणवस्थिताः ॥ ४८४ ॥ त्वच्छरीरमतः पञ्चाक्षरात्मा परिकीर्त्यसे ॥
The Nāda (sound), Bindu (dot), the letter 'Ma', the letter 'kāra' (A?), and the letter 'Rokāra'? – these five are established in Praṇava (Oṁ). These constitute your body; therefore, you are proclaimed as Pañcākṣarātmā (The One Whose Self is the Five Syllables).


Shloka 485: Pañcātmā (पञ्चात्मा)
ब्रह्मा विष्णुश्च रुद्रश्च ईश्वरश्च सदाशिवः ॥४८५॥ यस्यैते विग्रहाः पञ्च स पञ्चात्मेति गीयसे ॥
He whose five forms (Vigrahas) are Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, Īśvara, and Sadāśiva is sung as Pañcātmā (The One Whose Self is Fivefold).


Shloka 486: Pañcāsyaḥ (पञ्चास्यः)
विस्तीर्ण वदनं यस्य ब्रह्माण्डकवलक्षमम् ॥ ४८६ ॥ पचेर्विस्तारवाचित्वात्स पञ्चास्य इति स्मृतः ॥
He whose face (Vadana) is vast enough to swallow the cosmic egg (Brahmāṇḍa) is remembered as Pañcāsyaḥ (The Five-Faced One / The Wide-Mouthed One) – because the root 'Pañc' indicates expansion.


Shloka 487: Pañcakṛtyakṛt (पञ्चकृत्यकृत्)
सृष्टिः स्थितिश्च संहारस्तिरोधानमनु-ग्रहः ॥ ४८७ ॥ ब्रह्मादिरूपैरेतानि कुर्वाणः पञ्चकृत्यकृत् ॥
He who performs the five acts (Pañcakṛtya) – creation (Sṛṣṭi), preservation (Sthiti), dissolution (Saṁhāra), concealment (Tirodhāna), and blessing (Anugraha) – through forms beginning with Brahmā is Pañcakṛtyakṛt (The Performer of the Five Acts).


पञ्चाधारः पञ्चवर्णः पञ्चाक्षरपरायणः ॥ 
पश्चतालः पञ्चकरः पञ्चप्रणवभावितः ॥ १३८ ॥


Shloka 488: Pañcādhāraḥ (पञ्चाधारः)
पञ्चाधारोऽसि भूतानां पञ्चानामपि धारकः ॥ ४८८ ॥
You are Pañcādhāraḥ (The Fivefold Support) because you are the bearer (Dhāraka) of the five great elements (Bhūtas).

Shloka 489: Pañcavarṇaḥ (पञ्चवर्णः)
सर्वदा सूर्यकोटिप्रभः सन् कृतत्रेतयोश्चन्द्रवर्णोऽर्जुनद्रुच्छविः । 
द्वापरे शक्रगोपोपमोऽभूत्कलौ धूम्रवर्णोऽभवत्पञ्चवर्णो भवान् ॥ ४८९ ॥
You always possess the effulgence of ten million suns. Yet in the Kṛta and Tretā Yugas, you have the color of the moon (white) and the shine of silver; in the Dvāpara Yuga, you are like the Indragopa insect (red); in the Kali Yuga, you are smoke-colored. Thus, you are Pañcavarṇaḥ (The One of Five Colors).


Shloka 489: Pañcākṣaraparāyaṇaḥ (पञ्चाक्षरपरायणः)
जपन् पञ्चाक्षरं शैवं पञ्चाक्षरपरायणः ॥
He who is devoted to (or is the goal of) the five-syllabled Śaiva mantra ("Namaḥ Śivāya") is Pañcākṣaraparāyaṇaḥ (The One Devoted to the Five-Syllabled Mantra).


Shloka 490-491: Pañcatālaḥ (पञ्चतालः)
आमध्यमाग्रादङ्गुष्ठात्प्रमाणं ताल उच्यते ॥ ४९० ॥ ताल-पञ्चकदेहाढ्यः पञ्चतालोऽसि वामनः ॥
The measurement from the tip of the middle finger to the thumb is called one Tāla. You possess a body endowed with five such Tālas (a measure of 5:4 proportion), and you are the dwarf (Vāmana). Thus, He is Pañcatālaḥ (The One of Five Tālas in Height).


Shloka 491: Pañcakaraḥ (पञ्चकरः)
पञ्चहस्तमितः प्रांशुरपि पञ्चकरो भवान् ॥ ४९१॥
You are five Hasta (cubits) in height, or you possess five hands (Karas). Thus, He is Pañcakaraḥ (The Five-Handed One / Five-Cubits Tall).


Shloka 492: Pañcapraṇavabhāvitaḥ (पञ्चप्रणवभावितः)
तारवाग्भवलैज्जाभाः प्रसादपरया सह । प्रणवाः पञ्च तैर्वाच्यः पञ्चप्रणवभावितः ॥ ४९२ ॥
The five Praṇavas (sacred syllables Oṁ) are: Tāra (Oṁ), Vāgbhava (Aiṁ?), Laijjābha (Hrīṁ?), Prasāda (Śrīṁ?), and Parā (or another). He who is invoked (or contemplated upon) by these five is Pañcapraṇavabhāvitaḥ (The One Meditated Upon with the Five Oṁs / The One Who Contains the Five Praṇavas).

पञ्चब्रह्ममयस्फूर्तिः पञ्चावरणवारितः ॥ 
पञ्चभक्ष्यप्रियः पञ्चबाणः पश्चशिवात्मकः ॥१३९॥


Shloka 493: Pañcabrahmanaya-sphūrtiḥ (पञ्चब्रह्ममयस्फूर्तिः)
सद्योजातवामदेवाघोरतत्पुरुषेश्वरैः । पञ्चब्रह्ममयस्फूर्ति-रभिन्नस्फुरणात्मकः ॥ ४९३ ॥
Your manifestation (Sphūrti) consists of the five Brahmans (Pañcabrahman): Sadyojāta, Vāmadeva, Aghora, Tatpuruṣa, and Īśāna. You are of the nature of an undivided, radiant manifestation. Thus, He is Pañcabrahmanaya-sphūrtiḥ (The One Whose Manifestation is the Five Brahmans).


Shloka 494: Pañcāvaraṇavāritaḥ (पञ्चावरणवारितः)
पञ्चावृतिस्तवप्रोक्तैरङ्गाद्या-वरणैर्वृतः । छन्नो वा पञ्चभिः कोशैः पञ्चावरणवारितः ॥४९४॥
He who is surrounded (Vārita) by the five enclosures (Āvaraṇas) – such as the Aṅga Āvaraṇa – as described in your Tantra, or he who is covered by the five sheaths (Kośas), is Pañcāvaraṇavāritaḥ (The One Encompassed by the Five Enclosures).


Shloka 495: Pañcabhakṣyapriyaḥ (पञ्चभक्ष्यप्रियः)
लड्डूक मंडकपूरी फेणीवटकाभिधानि भक्ष्याणि । यस्य प्रियाणि पञ्चापि पञ्चभक्ष्यप्रियः स भवान् ॥ ४९५ ॥
The five types of food (Bhakṣya) – Laḍḍuka, Maṇḍaka, Pūrī, Pheṇī, and Vaṭaka – are dear to you. Therefore, you are Pañcabhakṣyapriyaḥ (The One Fond of the Five Eatable Offerings).


Shloka 496: Pañcabāṇaḥ (पञ्चबाणः)
बा-णैश्वर्यभिधाम्बायाः कामेश्वरशिवस्य च । प्रत्येकं पञ्चबीजानि सिद्धान्येव तु तद्विदाम् ॥ ४९६ ॥ तदात्मकतया पञ्चवाणः पञ्चशिवात्मकः ॥
For the goddess Kāmeśvarī (associated with the power of arrows) and for the god Kāmeśvara Śiva, each has five perfected Bījas (seed-syllables) known to the wise. Being of that nature, He is Pañcabāṇaḥ (The Five-Arrowed One / The One who is the Five Bījas).


Shloka 496: Pañcaśivātmakaḥ (पञ्चशिवात्मकः)
Thus, He is Pañcaśivātmakaḥ (The One Whose Self is the Five Śivas).

षट्कोणपीठः षट्चक्रधामा षड्ग्रन्थिभेदकः । 
षडध्वध्वान्तविध्वंसी षडगुलमहाह्रदः ॥ १४० ॥


Shloka 497: Ṣaṭkoṇapīṭhaḥ (षट्कोणपीठः)
षट्‌कोणपीठः कोणाः षद् पूजास्थानानि यस्य सः ॥ ४९७ ॥
He whose seat (Pīṭha) is the six-angled (hexagonal) figure (representing the six petals or the six worship places) is Ṣaṭkoṇapīṭhaḥ (The One Whose Seat is the Hexagon).


Shloka 498: Ṣaṭcakradhāmā (षट्चक्रधामा)
षट्चक्रधामा त्वं मूलाधिष्ठानमणिपूरकाः । अनाहतविशु-ङ्ख्याज्ञावासस्थानानि यस्य सः ॥ ४९८ ॥
You are the abode of the six Cakras: Mūlādhāra, Svādhiṣṭhāna, Maṇipūra, Anāhata, Viśuddhi, and Ājñā. Thus, He is Ṣaṭcakradhāmā (The One Whose Abode is the Six Cakras).


Shloka 499: Ṣaḍgranthibhedakaḥ (षड्ग्रन्थिभेदकः)
मूलाज्ञामणिपूरेषु द्वौ द्वौ ग्रंथी भिनत्ति यः । वह्निकुंडलिनी सैव भवान् षड्ग्रंथि-भेदकः ॥ ४९९ ॥
He who pierces the two knots (Granthis) each in Mūlādhāra, Maṇipūra, and Ājñā (or the six knots in total) is called Ṣaḍgranthibhedakaḥ (The One Who Pierces the Six Knots).


Shloka 500: Ṣaḍadhvadhvāntavidhvaṁsī (षडध्वध्वान्तविध्वंसी)
पदभुवनवर्णतत्त्वकलामंत्राख्याः षडध्वानः । तच्छोधनकैर्तृत्वेन षडंध्वध्वांतविध्वंसी ॥ ५०० ॥
The six spiritual paths (Ṣaḍadhvan) are: Pada, Bhuvana, Varṇa, Tattva, Kalā, and Mantra. He who destroys the darkness (Ajñāna) of these six paths by purifying them is Ṣaḍadhvadhvāntavidhvaṁsī (The Destroyer of the Darkness of the Six Spiritual Paths).


Shloka 501: Ṣaḍaṅgulamahāhradaḥ (षडगुलमहाह्रदः)
महा-हृदो नाभिरूपो गांभीर्येण षडङ्गुलः । पिचण्डिलत्वाद्यस्यैष षडङ्गुलमहाहदः ॥ ५०१ ॥
He whose great lake (Mahāhrada) is the navel region, which is six Aṅgulas (finger-widths) deep, is Ṣaḍaṅgulamahāhradaḥ (The Great Lake of Six Aṅgulas Depth).

षण्मुखः षण्मुखभ्राता षट्शक्तिपरिवारितः ॥ 
षड्डेरिवर्गविध्वंसी षडूर्मिभयभञ्जनः ॥ १४१ ॥


Shloka 502: Ṣaṇmukhaḥ (षण्मुखः)
षट्संख्याकानि शास्त्राणि मुखे यस्य स षण्मुखः । स भवान् षण्मुखभ्राता सेनानीर्यस्य तेऽनुजः ॥ ५०२ ॥
He who has the six categories of scriptures (or six faces) is Ṣaṇmukhaḥ (the six-faced one, Skanda). You are that Lord, and also the brother of Ṣaṇmukha. Thus, He is Ṣaṇmukhaḥ (The Six-Faced One) and Ṣaṇmukhabhrātā (The Brother of the Six-Faced One).
(Some texts interpret the second name as "He whose brother is Ṣaṇmukha" i.e., Gaṇeśa as the elder brother of Skanda).


Shloka 502: Ṣaṭśaktiparivāritaḥ (षट्शक्तिपरिवारितः)
षडङ्ग-युवतिच्छन्नः षट्शक्तिपरिवारितः ॥
He who is surrounded (Parivārita) by the six Śaktis (powers) is Ṣaṭśaktiparivāritaḥ (The One Attended by Six Powers). (These are identified with the six Aṅgas or six female energies).


Shloka 503: Ṣaḍḍerivargavidhvaṁsī (षड्डेरिवर्गविध्वंसी)
षङ्गैरिवर्गविध्वंसी कामक्रोधौ च मत्सरम् ॥ ५०३ ॥ लोभमोहमदान् हंसि षडिमान्वैरिशब्दितान् ॥
You destroy the six enemies (Ṣaḍḍerivarga) – Kāma (desire), Krodha (anger), Matsara (jealousy), Lobha (greed), Moha (delusion), and Mada (pride). Thus, He is Ṣaḍḍerivargavidhvaṁsī (The Destroyer of the Six Enemies).


Shloka 504: Ṣaḍūrmibhayabhañjanaḥ (षडूर्मिभयभञ्जनः)
अशनायां पिपासां च शोकं मोहं जरां मृतिम् ॥५०४॥ षडूर्मिशब्दितान्निघ्नन् षड्डूर्मिभयभञ्जनः ॥
You also destroy the six waves (Ṣaḍūrmi) – hunger (Aśanāyā), thirst (Pipāsā), sorrow (Śoka), delusion (Moha), old age (Jarā), and death (Mṛti). Thus, He is Ṣaḍūrmibhayabhañjanaḥ (The Shatterer of the Fear of the Six Waves).

षट्तर्कदूरः षट्कर्मनिरतः षद्साश्रयः ॥ 
सप्तपातालचरणः सप्तद्वीपोरुमण्डलः ॥ १४२॥


Shloka 505: Ṣaṭtarkadūraḥ (षट्तर्कदूरः)
षड्दर्शनोदितैस्तकैर्युक्तिभिर्यो न गृह्यते ॥ ५०५ ॥ वाग-तीतः स षटूतर्कदूर इत्यभिधीयते ॥
He who cannot be apprehended by the logics (Tarka) and arguments produced by the six philosophical systems (Ṣaḍdarśana) and who is beyond speech is called Ṣaṭtarkadūraḥ (The One Beyond the Six Schools of Logic).


Shloka 506: Ṣaṭkarmanirataḥ (षट्कर्मनिरतः)
षट्‌कर्मनिरतः सक्तः षट्सु यागादिकर्मसु ॥ ५०६ ॥
He who is devoted (Nirata) to the six prescribed duties (Ṣaṭkarma) – such as sacrifice (Yāga) and others – is Ṣaṭkarmanirataḥ (The One Devoted to the Six Rituals).


Shloka 506: Ṣaḍrasāśrayaḥ (षड्रसाश्रयः)
मधुरादिरसानामप्याधारः षड्रमाश्रयः ॥
He who is the support (Āśraya) of the six tastes (Ṣaḍrasa) – sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter, and astringent – is Ṣaḍrasāśrayaḥ (The Abode of the Six Tastes).


Shloka 507: Saptapātālacaraṇaḥ (सप्तपातालचरणः)
अतलाद्या अधोऽधःस्थाः सप्त लोका यदंघ्रिगाः ॥ ५०७ ॥
The seven netherworlds (Pātāla) – Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Talātala, Rasātala, Mahātala, and Pātāla – are situated below his feet. Thus, He is Saptapātālacaraṇaḥ (The One for Whom the Seven Netherworlds are His Feet).


Shloka 508: Saptadvīporumaṇḍalaḥ (सप्तद्वीपोरुमण्डलः)
सप्तजम्ब्वादयो द्वीपा यस्य सक्थिभुवर्मुखाः । ऊर्ध्वगाः सप्तगोलोकावधयो यस्य मूर्ध्नि सः ॥ ५०८ ॥ सप्त-पातालचरणः सप्तद्वीपोरुमंडलः ॥
The seven continents (Dvīpa) – Jambu, Plakṣa, Śālmali, Kuśa, Krauñca, Śāka, and Puṣkara – are situated on his thighs. Thus, He is Saptadvīporumaṇḍalaḥ (The One Whose Thigh-Region is the Seven Continents).

सप्तस्वर्लोकमुकुटः सप्तसप्तिवरप्रदः ॥ 
सप्ताङ्गराज्यसुखदः सप्तर्षिगणमण्डितः ॥१४३॥

Shloka 509: Saptasvarlokamukuṭaḥ (सप्तस्वर्लोकमुकुटः)
सप्तस्वर्लोकमुकुट इति नामत्रयेरितः ॥ ५०९ ॥ ताभिर्ज-टाभिः सहितः सप्त इत्युच्यते शिवः ॥
This name is expressed through three (or as a single name). He whose crown (Mukuṭa) consists of the seven heavenly worlds (Svarloka) is Saptasvarlokamukuṭaḥ (The One for Whom the Seven Heavens are the Crest). (The commentary links this to Śiva's matted locks containing the seven worlds.)


Shloka 510: Saptasaptivarapradaḥ (सप्तसप्तिवरप्रदः)
मणिमल्ल मृधे तस्मै भवान् प्रादाद्धयोत्तमम् ॥५१०॥ सूर्याय वा वरं तेन सप्तसप्तिवर-प्रदः ॥
In the fight with Maṇimalla, you granted an excellent horse to (someone). Or, you granted a boon to the sun (Sūrya) for his seven horses. Thus, He is Saptasaptivarapradaḥ (The Bestower of the Boon of Seven Horses).


Shloka 511: Saptāṅgarājyasukhadaḥ (सप्ताङ्गराज्यसुखदः)
स्वाम्यमात्यादिसप्ताङ्गयुगराज्यात् सुखं ददत् ॥ ५११॥ सप्ताङ्गराज्यसुखदः ॥
He who bestows the happiness (Sukha) of a kingdom (Rājya) endowed with the seven constituents (Aṅgas) – the king (Svāmin), minister (Amātya), allies (Suhṛt), treasury (Kośa), army (Daṇḍa), territory (Rāṣṭra), and fortress (Durga) – is Saptāṅgarājyasukhadaḥ (The Bestower of Happiness of a Seven-Limbed Kingdom).


Shloka 512: Saptarṣigaṇamaṇḍitaḥ (सप्तर्षिगणमण्डितः)
सप्तभिः कश्यपादिभिः । गणदेवैश्च सेव्यत्वात् सप्तर्षिगणमंडितः ॥ ५१२ ॥
He who is adorned (Maṇḍita) by the group (Gaṇa) of the seven sages (Saptarṣi) – Kaśyapa, Atri, Vasiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra, Gautama, Jamadagni, and Bharadvāja – and who is served by them is Saptarṣigaṇamaṇḍitaḥ (The One Adorned by the Host of Seven Sages).

सप्तच्छन्दोनिधिः सप्तहोता सप्तस्वराश्रयः ॥ 
सप्ताब्धिकेलिकासारः सप्तमातृनिषेवितः ॥ १४४ ॥


Shloka 513: Saptacchandonidhiḥ (सप्तच्छन्दोनिधिः)
सप्तछन्दोनिधिः पथ्यछन्दः सप्तकसंश्रयः । होत्रादयो स्थावाकांता होतारः सप्त सौमिकाः ॥५१३॥
He who is the repository (Nidhi) of the seven metres (Chandas) – Gāyatrī, Uṣṇik, Anuṣṭubh, Bṛhatī, Paṅkti, Triṣṭubh, and Jagatī – is Saptacchandonidhiḥ (The Repository of the Seven Metres).


Shloka 513: Saptahotā (सप्तहोता)
महाविहर्मुनिर्वापि सप्तहोता तदात्मकः ॥
The seven sacrificers (Hotā) – Hotṛ, Potṛ, Neṣṭṛ, Agnīdhra, Prastotṛ, Subrahmaṇya, and Brāhmaṇācchaṁsin – or the great sage (Mahāvira). He who is the embodiment of these is Saptahotā (The Sevenfold Sacrificer / The Seven Hotā Priests).


Shloka 514: Saptasvarāśrayaḥ (सप्तस्वराश्रयः)
षड्जादिस्वरवान्नाद इति सप्तस्वराश्रयः ॥ ५१४॥
He who possesses the seven musical notes (Svara) – Ṣaḍja, Ṛṣabha, Gāndhāra, Madhyama, Pañcama, Dhaivata, and Niṣāda – and is the support of Nāda (primordial sound) is Saptasvarāśrayaḥ (The Abode of the Seven Notes).


Shloka 514: Saptābdhikelikāsāraḥ (सप्ताब्धिकेलिकासारः)
सप्ताब्धिकेलिकासारो यस्य क्रीडासरोऽर्णवः ॥
He whose playground (Kelikāsāra) or sporting lake is the seven oceans (Saptābdhi) – of salt, sugarcane juice, wine, ghee, curd, milk, and sweet water – is Saptābdhikelikāsāraḥ (The One Whose Pleasure-Lake is the Seven Oceans).


Shloka 515: Saptamātṛniṣevitaḥ (सप्तमातृनिषेवितः)
ब्राहृयादिमातृसेव्यत्वात् सप्तमातृनिषेवितः ॥ ५१५ ॥
He who is served (Niṣevita) by the seven Mothers (Saptamātṛ) – Brāhmī, Māheśvarī, Kaumārī, Vaiṣṇavī, Vārāhī, Indrāṇī, and Cāmuṇḍā – is Saptamātṛniṣevitaḥ (The One Attended by the Seven Mothers).

सप्तच्छन्दोमोदमदः सप्तच्छन्दोमखप्रभुः ॥ 
अष्टमूर्तिध्येयमूर्तिरष्टप्रकृतिकारणम् ॥ १४५ ॥


Shloka 516: Saptacchandomodamadaḥ (सप्तच्छन्दोमोदमदः)
पथ्यानि सप्तच्छन्दांसि तेषां मोदेन यो मदः । तेनैव घूर्ण-नात् सप्तच्छन्दोमोदमदः स्मृतः ॥ ५१६॥
The seven metres (Chanda) are the pathways. He whose intoxication (Mada) arises from the delight (Moda) of those metres, and by which he is made to reel, is called Saptacchandomodamadaḥ (The One Intoxicated by the Delight of the Seven Metres).


Shloka 516: Saptacchandomakhaprabhuḥ (सप्तच्छन्दोमखप्रभुः)
पेथ्याद्यक्रतुपूज्यत्वात् सप्तच्छन्दोमखप्रभुः ॥
Because he is worshipped in sacrifices (Makha) such as the Pathyā (Soma sacrifice) and others that involve the seven metres, he is Saptacchandomakhaprabhuḥ (The Lord of the Sacrifice of the Seven Metres).


Shloka 517: Aṣṭamūrtidhyeyamūrtiḥ (अष्टमूर्तिध्येयमूर्तिः)
अष्टमूर्तिशिवस्तेन यन्मूर्ति-हृदि चिन्त्यते ॥ ५१७ ॥ सोऽष्टमूर्तिध्र्येयमूर्तिध्र्येयो वा पृथगष्टभिः ॥
Śiva has eight forms (Aṣṭamūrti). The form (Mūrti) of you that is meditated upon in the heart is Aṣṭamūrtidhyeyamūrtiḥ (The One Whose Meditated Form Consists of Eight Forms).


Shloka 518: Aṣṭaprakṛtikāraṇam (अष्टप्रकृतिकारणम्)
प्रकृत्यहङ्कृतिमहत्पञ्चतन्मात्रसंज्ञिताः ॥ ५१८ ॥ प्रकृतीर्यो जनयति सोऽष्टप्रकृतिकारणम् ॥
He who generates the eight Prakṛtis (primordial elements) – Prakṛti (nature), Ahaṅkāra (ego), Mahat (intellect), and the five Tanmātras (subtle elements: sound, touch, form, taste, smell) – is Aṣṭaprakṛtikāraṇam (The Cause of the Eightfold Primordial Nature).

अष्टाङ्गयोगफल भूरष्टपत्राम्बुजासनः ॥
अष्टशक्तिसमृद्धश्रीरष्टैश्वर्यप्रदायकः ॥ १४६ ॥


Shloka 519: Aṣṭāṅgayogaphalabhūḥ (अष्टाङ्गयोगफल भूः)
अष्टाङ्गयोगफलभूः समाध्यन्तैर्यमादिभिः ॥ ५१९ ॥ अङ्गैर्युक्तस्य हृद्वृत्तिरोधस्य फलदायकः ॥
He who is the ground (Bhūḥ) or bestower of the fruit (Phala) of the eight-limbed Yoga (Aṣṭāṅga Yoga) — which consists of Yama, Niyama, Āsana, Prāṇāyāma, Pratyāhāra, Dhāraṇā, Dhyāna, and Samādhi — and who grants the fruit of restraining the modifications of the heart to one endowed with these limbs, is Aṣṭāṅgayogaphalabhūḥ (The Ground/Bestower of the Fruit of Eight-Limbed Yoga).


Shloka 520: Aṣṭapatrāmabujāsanaḥ (अष्टपत्राम्बुजासनः)
पङ्कजेऽष्टदले तिष्ठन् अष्टपत्राम्बुजासनः ॥ ५२० ॥
He who is seated (Āsanaḥ) on the lotus (Ambuja) with eight petals (Aṣṭapatra) — the heart-lotus — is Aṣṭapatrāmabujāsanaḥ (The One Seated on the Eight-Petaled Lotus).


Shloka 521: Aṣṭaśaktisamṛddhaśrīḥ (अष्टशक्तिसमृद्धश्रीः)
अष्टशक्तिसमृद्ध-श्रीर्दलाष्टकनिवासिना । तीब्रादिशक्त्यष्टकेन परितः सेवितो भवान् ।। ५२१ ॥
He whose glory (Śrī) is enriched (Samṛddha) by the eight Śaktis (powers) — such as Tīvrā and others — who reside in the eight petals and who serve (him) from all sides, is Aṣṭaśaktisamṛddhaśrīḥ (The One Whose Glory is Enriched by the Eight Powers).

Ganesha Purva Tapini Upanishad (Varada Purva Tapini)

First Upanishad

I worship Ganesha, the lord of the celestial attendants, beyond all attributes yet possessing all attributes, all-pervading, to whose supreme state the yogins attain—that beloved son of Gauri. Om, salutations unto the granter of boons, the remover of obstacles. Now, therefore, we shall expound the Upanishad of Brahman. He is Brahma among the gods, Savitr among the seers, the Rishi among the learned sages, the buffalo among wild beasts, the sustainer among the Vasus, and Surabhi among created beings. Salutations unto Brahman, the son of Atharvan, the bountiful bestower. He is the Ordainer, the primeval consciousness of the gods, who projected the worlds like vast forests through His mind alone. Salutations unto You, O Brahman, the son of Brahma, the eldest son of Atharvan, armed with the bow.

Om. Prajapati created living beings. Having been created, they said, "How shall we obtain sustenance?" He divided the universe into three realms: the earth (Bhur), the atmosphere (Bhuvas), and the heavens (Svar). He performed intense meditative austerity (tapas). He became Brahma, He became Vishnu, He became Shiva, He became Prajapati, He became Indra, and He became Agni. Meditating silently within His mind on how these beings might obtain food, He beheld within His own Self the Divine Lord bearing an elephant form, radiant as the moon, endowed with four arms—the one from whom indeed all these beings are born, by whom they live and breathe, and into whom they dissolve. That verily is the Imperishable Supreme Brahman. From Him originate the vital life-force (prana), the mind, all the senses, the ether, air, fire, water, and the earth which sustains the cosmos. All this existence is the Purusha alone, the supreme austerity, Brahman, the immortal nectar.

He offered praise: "Salutations to Brahman, salutations to the knowers of Brahman, salutations to the Vedas, salutations to the Rishis, salutations to the sacred traditions and lineages. Salutations to the Sun and to the Supreme Creator, salutations to the object of experience, to the most eminent one, to the one with matted locks, to the universal wheel and the wielder of the discus, to food and the Lord of food, to Shiva, to Sadashiva, to the transcendent Fourth state (Turiya). Salutations to the Lord of Earth, Atmosphere, and Heaven, the Lord of wealth, the Lord of vigor, the Lord of cattle, the Master of the Rig, Yajur, Sama, and Atharvangirasa Vedas, salutations to the Son of Brahman!"

The Lord said, "I am the Granter of Boons." Prajapati asked, "How may these beings be nourished?" The illustrious son of Brahma, who had performed tapas in the sacred realm, said, "You are the proclaimer of all, the knower of all." He then instructed them, "Inquire of the son of Bhrigu, who is engaged in tapas in the forest of accomplished sages." They approached him, and he asked, "What brings you here?" They replied, "How may we obtain food and sustenance?" Meditating silently within his mind on how they might be nourished, he perceived this Anushtubh meter, through which all beings depart and enter. Regarding this, it is declared: Anushtubh is the first, Anushtubh is the highest. Anushtubh is speech itself; through speech they depart, through speech they arise. Supreme indeed among the sacred meters is Anushtubh; everything is Anushtubh. Whoever realizes this king of mantras (Mantra-raja) truly sees. He attains both worldly enjoyment and spiritual liberation, and all knowledge dawns within him. Regarding this, there is the sacred verse: "The one Divine Lord who bestows treasures, endowed with auspicious splendor, all-auspicious in every direction, the Lord of Maya, deep and unassailable in might, who destroys the adversaries of Brahman—unto that south-facing Deity, I offer praise. O Lord, gather for us abundant and wonderful wealth; like an elephant with its great right trunk, bring it forth unto us." Thus he praised Him a thousand times.

Then he beheld the Great God (Mahadeva), adorned with divine majesty, exhilarated with bliss, worshipped by great yogins such as Sanaka and the master knowers of the Vedas. He shone amidst the gods headed by Brahma, who surrounded Him like bees around a lotus. He possessed radiant ears, the Great God assuming an elephant form, the Auspicious Shiva. The Lord said, "I am the Granter of Boons." In his mind, he chose his desired boon silently. The Lord replied, "So be it." Regarding this, there is the verse: "Being thus praised, the Divine Son of the Supreme Lord, well-pleased, spoke to the son of Bhrigu: 'Know Me, O Bhargava, as Vakratunda, the curved-trunked one, the refuge of the refuge-less, the embodiment of the three Gunas, the Auspicious Shiva.'" Then His six auxiliary limbs manifested. He declared, "Chant the royal Anushtubh mantra consisting of six feet and six syllables. He who chants this becomes endowed with all prosperity, and all of you shall become nourished." Regarding this, there is the sacred verse: "We invoke You, the Lord of the celestial hosts, the King of the gods, the Seer among seers, the embodiment of supreme intellect, the Sovereign of the elders, the Supreme One, the sole Banner of truth—hearing our prayers, come with Your protective blessings and be seated eternally."

They asked, "How are we to understand this king of mantras in the Anushtubh meter?" He then expounded this six-footed royal Anushtubh mantra. That mantra is verily the Sama Veda. The Rig is Gayatri, the Yajus is Ushnih, and the Sama is Anushtubh. It is Aditya, the cosmic Sun. The Rig is the Vasus, the Yajus is the Rudras, and the Sama is the Adityas. It possesses six feet, for the Sama is six-footed. One should know the earth with its oceans, seven continents, and mountains as the first foot of that Sama: Rayasposhasya data (The Bestower of the increase of wealth). Through it, one becomes the sovereign of the seven continents and attains mastery over the earthly realm. One should know the intermediate space, inhabited by the hosts of Yakshas, Gandharvas, and Apsarases, as the second foot: Nidhidata (The Bestower of divine treasures). Through it, one becomes the master of the cardinal directions headed by Kubera and attains mastery over the atmosphere. One should know the celestial heaven, served by the Vasus, Rudras, Adityas, and all the gods, as the third foot of that Sama: Annado matah (Regarded as the Giver of sustenance). Through it, one attains sovereignty over the gods and dominion over heaven. One should know Brahmaloka, served by the hosts of the Rig, Yajur, Sama, and Atharvangirasa Vedas, as the fourth foot: Rakshohana (The Destroyer of afflictions and demonic forces). Through it, one attains supremacy among gods and dominion over the realm of Brahma. One should know Vishnuloka, served by the four divine emanations headed by Vasudeva, as the fifth foot of that Sama: Balagahana (Deep and unassailable in power). Through it, one attains sovereignty over all deities and dominion over the realm of Vishnu. One should know the pristine, unconditioned nature of Brahman, dwelling in the supreme ether, as the sixth foot of that Sama. Whoever realizes this as Vakratundaya Hum (Unto the curved-trunked Lord, Hum) attains immortality and achieves dominion over Satyaloka, the highest realm of truth.

The four Vedas—Rig, Yajus, Sama, and Atharva—constitute its four quarters. Rayasposhasya data is the first quarter, and the Rig Veda is verily this first quarter. Nidhidata annado matah is the second quarter, and the Yajur Veda is verily this second quarter. Rakshohano vo balagahana is the third quarter, and the Sama Veda is verily this third quarter. Vakratundaya Hum is the fourth quarter, and the Atharva Veda is verily this fourth quarter—the Atharva is verily the fourth quarter.

Thus ends the First Upanishad of the Ganesha Purva Tapini Upanishad.

Second Upanishad

Prajapati said: Agni is verily the Vedas, all this universe, all created beings, the cosmic sovereign, the self-ruler, and the supreme monarch; one should know this as the first foot of that Sama. The Sun, who is the embodiment of the Rig, Yajur, Sama, and Atharva Vedas, the golden Person dwelling within the solar sphere, should be known as the second foot of that Sama. Soma, the lord of the stars and the generator of all medicinal herbs and plants, should be known as the third foot of that Sama. He who is Brahma should be known as the fourth foot of that Sama. He who is Hari should be known as the fifth foot of that Sama. He who is Shiva, the Supreme Brahman, should be known as the final foot of that Sama. Whoever realizes this attains immortality and becomes the Supreme Brahman Itself. Therefore, this Anushtubh Sama should not be casually disclosed anywhere; if one desires to bestow it, one should impart it only to a devoted, obedient son or to a qualified disciple.

Verily, it has six auxiliary limbs: Om Hridayaya Namah (Salutations to the Heart), Shirase Svaha (Unto the Head, Svaha), Shikhayai Vashat (Unto the Crown, Vashat), Kavacaya Hum (Unto the Armor, Hum), Netratrayaya Vaushat (Unto the Three Eyes, Vaushat), and Astraya Phat (Unto the Weapon, Phat)—assigning the first limb with the first part, the second with the second, the third with the third, the fourth with the fourth, the fifth with the fifth, and the sixth with the sixth. Enclosing each syllable on both sides are the divine energies of Maya and Lakshmi. This Vainayaki Maya creates all this, protects all this, and dissolves all this; therefore, one should know this Maya as the supreme Shakti. One who knows this conquers death, transcends all sin, attains immense spiritual and material prosperity, becomes accomplished in speech, attains mastery over the six esoteric rites, and reaches immortality. The knowers of Brahman deliberate whether it is short, long, or protracted: if it is short, one crosses beyond all sin and attains immortality; if it is long, one obtains vast wealth and attains immortality. Regarding this, the Rishi has stated the illustration: "Protect him who offers the soma pressing, the swift Rudra; protect Sri, Lakshmi, the Mother of stone, the sacred cow, and Shashthi whom they call Indrasena—that wisdom which is the primal source of Brahman." For longevity in this world, I take refuge in Her. One should meditate on the Divine Lord reclining upon the ocean of milk, seated beneath the wish-fulfilling tree, the granter of boons, whose nature is cosmic space, wielding a fierce staff with mighty arms, possessing the form of Vakratunda, accompanied by the wish-fulfilling cow beside Him, the all-pervading son of Shiva and Uma. His radiance is like the sky shimmering with gold, red-hued, four-armed, adorned with matted locks, auspicious, peaceful, and bestowing fearlessness upon His devotees. He has elevated big toes, concealed ankles, well-formed heels, stout calves, concealed knees, broad thighs, a noble waist, a deep navel, a conch-like neck, extended ears, and an elongated trunk, lovingly embraced by both Siddhi and Buddhi, His lotus-face blooming with grace. Such is the divine communion.

Now follows the sacred meter and the presiding Deity. The meter is Anushtubh, consisting of thirty-two syllables. By Anushtubh all this was created; by Anushtubh all this is dissolved. The supreme boon-giving Deity is the Supreme Self united with Shiva and Uma. They asked, "How is He united with Shiva and Uma?" The son of Bhrigu replied: "He is composed of both Prakriti (nature) and Purusha (consciousness), the bestower of wealth; Prakriti is Maya, and Purusha is Shiva. Thus, this Supreme Soul of the cosmos is the presiding Deity." Regarding this, there is the illustration: "Indra, invoked by many, is praised through His wondrous powers of Maya; Sharva sustained the cosmos through Maya. That unborn One sleeps through Maya within the cave of the heart; the entire universe is placed within Him, and Vishnu alone is uniquely realized." This is the swan-like divine consciousness (Hamsa) of the gods. Verily, space is the supreme refuge and ultimate goal of all these beings. All these beings are born from space alone, having been born they live in space, and they depart and dissolve back into space. Therefore, one should know the seed of space as Shiva. Regarding this, there is the sacred verse: "He is the swan dwelling in the pure heavens, the Vasu dwelling in the mid-region, the priest dwelling at the altar, the guest dwelling in the home; He dwells in humans, in the highest gods, in the eternal cosmic order, in the vast sky; He is born of water, born of earth, born of truth, born of the mountains—the Great Cosmic Truth."

Now follows the sacred seat of adoration (Adhishthana): in the center is the central point (Bindu), then the triangle, followed by the six-pointed hexagon, the eight-petaled lotus, the twelve-spoked circle, and the sixteen-petaled perimeter. In the center, one should worship the Deity who is the embodiment of the seed-syllable. On His left and right are Siddhi and Buddhi; in front is the wish-yielding cow, Kamadhenu. In the six angles of the hexagon reside the six Vinayakas headed by Sumukha. In the eight petals reside the eight Vinayakas headed by Vakratunda. In the twelve spokes reside Batuka, Vamana, Mahadashaka, Mahodara, Subhadra, Mali, Vara, Rama, Uma, Shiva, Skanda, and Nandi. Outside this circle reside the yogic accomplishments such as Anima. In the sixteen petals reside the guardians of the directions bearing their respective weapons.

Now follows the ritual application: whoever offers in oblations one hundred thousand sweet cakes (apupas) with this mantra on the fourth lunar day (Chaturthi) of both fortnights becomes instantaneously wealthy like Kubera. He who, with single-minded devotion, offers consecrated cooked rice into the sacred fire for three months, or offers flattened rice for the same duration, becomes verily like Kubera in person. One may perform rites of expulsion with the fruits of the Vibhitaka tree, eradicate destructive forces with products of the poisonous tree, attract and harmonize through offerings of lotus flowers, and one seeking wealth should offer oblations of sweet dumplings (modakas). Knowing this truth, a practitioner accomplishes all undertakings—verily, one fulfills all undertakings.

Thus ends the Second Upanishad of the Ganesha Purva Tapini Upanishad.

Third Upanishad

Now the son of Bhrigu said: "The sacred esoteric design (Tantra) must be deeply inquired into and realized. At the center and base, one should recognize the supreme void (Shunya). That void is verily the Supreme Brahman. There, one should project the Sama mantra accompanied by the primal sound Tara (Om) and the seed of Maya (Hrim). It is bounded by three lines forming a triangle, for these cosmic realms are three and these Vedas are three. The Rig Veda is the earthly realm (Bhur), which is Maya; the Yajur Veda is the mid-region (Bhuvas), which is Shiva; and the Sama Veda is the celestial heaven (Svar), which is Hiranyagarbha. Next is the six-pointed hexagon, representing the six cosmic regions and the six seasons. There, one should inscribe in succession the sacred seeds of Tara (Om), Maya (Hrim), Rama (Shrim), Mara (Klim), Vishvesha (Glum), and Dharani (Gam). Beyond this is the eight-petaled lotus, corresponding to the eight syllables of the Gayatri foot; upon it, one should project the Brahma-Gayatri. Then follows the twelve-petaled lotus, representing the twelve solar deities (Adityas), who are the primordial vowel-sounds (svaras). Realizing these divine sound-forms, one attains the realm of the Sun. Encircling this is the sixteen-petaled lotus; the cosmic Person (Purusha) verily consists of sixteen constituent parts (kalas), and all the articulated letters of the alphabet are indeed the Purusha. He is established as the foundation of all the worlds, and the Anushtubh is verily the Purusha."

The son of Bhrigu said: "Whoever realizes this supreme king of mantras in the Anushtubh meter along with its auxiliary limbs (anga), its practical ritual applications (prasara), its cosmic power of Maya, its sacred seat of installation (adhishthana), and its esoteric geometry (tantra), becomes endowed with all spiritual and material glory; he attains immortality—verily, he attains immortality."

Thus ends the Third Upanishad of the Ganesha Purva Tapini Upanishad.

Thus concludes the Ganesha Purva Tapini Upanishad belonging to the Atharva Veda.

Ganesha Uttara Tapini Upanishad (Varada Uttara Tapini)

First Upanishad

Om. The single imperishable syllable Om is Brahman, all this universe. Here is its thorough exposition: all that has been, all that is, and all that will be in the future—everything is verily the sacred syllable Om. That which transcends the three dimensions of time is also verily the syllable Om. All this manifest cosmos is indeed Ganesha; this innermost Self is Brahman. This very Self is endowed with four quarters (padas).

Established in the waking state (jagrat), conscious of the external world, having seven cosmic limbs and nineteen portals of perception, experiencing gross objects—this is Vaishvanara, the first quarter. Established in the dream state (svapna), conscious of the internal subtle world, having seven limbs and nineteen portals, experiencing subtle objects in solitude—this is Taijasa, the radiant one, the second quarter. That state wherein one is asleep and desires no desire whatsoever, nor experiences any dream, is deep dreamless sleep (sushupti). Established in deep sleep, unified into oneness, a concentrated mass of undifferentiated consciousness, experiencing unalloyed bliss, whose doorway is pure awareness—this is Prajna, the third quarter. He is the Lord of all, He is the Omniscient, He is the Inner Controller (Antaryamin), He is the womb of all, the origin and dissolution of all beings.

Neither conscious of the internal subjective world, nor conscious of the external objective world, nor conscious of both together; neither a concentrated mass of cognition, nor simple consciousness, nor unconsciousness; unseen by the senses, beyond worldly interaction, ungraspable by the organs of action, without distinguishing physical marks, unthinkable by the mind, indescribable in words, the pure essence of the conviction of the single Self, the complete dissolution of the phenomenal universe, peaceful, auspicious, and non-dual—thus is the Fourth state (Turiya) realized. That is Ganesha; that is the Self to be known.

Ever-luminous, devoid of ignorance and its manifold effects, free from any bondage to one's individual self, free from all impurities, the very form of supreme bliss, the foundation of all existence, pure Being alone, completely dispelling the darkness, ignorance, and delusion of Maya—contemplating Him in this manner, one should realize with one's mind: "I am Om Tat Sat, the Supreme Brahman; I am the Lord of Obstacles whose very essence is pure Consciousness; I am That, the supreme Vinayaka, the transcendental Light and absolute nectar"—thus merging one's individual self into non-dual identity with Brahman.

Those who proclaim in absolute truth, "I am Vinayaka," are certainly no longer bound to transmigratory existence; they attain supreme spiritual delight without a doubt. Such is the secret teaching of the Upanishad. He who knows this becomes the foremost of all, so declares the sage Yajnavalkya—verily, so declares Yajnavalkya.

This alone is the highest meditation, this alone is the supreme austerity; this realization and adoration of Vinayaka is the ultimate liberation from the cycle of worldly existence. A thousand horse sacrifices (Ashvamedha) and a hundred Vajapeya sacrifices do not equal even a sixteenth part of this single yoga of non-dual meditation.

Thus ends the First Upanishad of the Ganesha Uttara Tapini Upanishad.

Second Upanishad

Om. He is Vishnu, He is Shiva, He is Brahma, He is Indra, He is the Moon, He is the Sun, He is the Wind, and He is the Fire. He is Brahman; the wise contemplate this very Self as all the gods, the Self of all created beings, the universal Soul. Om, "I am He; Om, I am He; Om, I am He." Om, "O Brahman; Om, O Brahman; Om, O Brahman." Om, "O Shiva; Om, O Shiva; Om, O Shiva." That Ganesha, that supreme Ganesha is the most exalted. Om, You are the Lord of hosts among the celestial assemblies, the Lord of beloveds among all that is cherished, the Lord of treasures among all wealth. Om, we know that Supreme Person, we meditate upon the Lord with the curved trunk; may that tusked Lord inspire and illumine our intellect. Om, That is Ganesha; Om, absolute Truth is Ganesha; Om, the Supreme is Ganesha; Om, Brahman is Ganesha. The primordial resonance (Nada) is His very form; all this universe is Nada, all forms are Nada, this manifest form is Nada—it is the Great Cosmic Sound.

That Ganesha is vast and immeasurable, yet He is the subtlest atom. He is adorable, He is the foremost, He is the worthy object of all worship. He possesses divine form, yet He is utterly formless. He manifests as duality, yet He is non-dual. He assumes the nature of the immovable, and He assumes the nature of the moving. He is the conscious spirit and the insentient matter; He is everything. That Ganesha is the unmanifest, the infinitesimal point, the most excellent, and the swiftest of the swift. He is neither short nor measurable, yet exceedingly subtle and minute; He is neither gross nor dense, transcending all physical materiality.

Om, He is neither air, nor fire, nor space, nor water, nor earth. He is not an object of sight, unseen by worldly eyes, beyond the realm of visibility. He is neither cold, nor heat, nor rain. He is neither yellow, nor white, nor red, nor black. Devoid of color, devoid of name, and devoid of empirical attributes, yet He is realized as the ultimate Ganesha. Pure, pure, eternally pure is Ganesha. Brahman, Brahman, Supreme Brahman is Ganesha. Shiva, Shiva, Auspicious Shiva is Ganesha. Indra is Ganesha, Vishnu is Ganesha, Surya is Ganesha—all this totality is Ganesha. He is without material qualities (nirguna), free from ego (nirahamkara), beyond mental conceptualization (nirvikalpa), desireless (niriha), formless (nirakara), the very embodiment of supreme bliss, of transcendent light, ineffable, immeasurable, and proclaimed as the ancient, primeval Ganesha. He is the First Cause, the Imperishable, the Infinite, the Immutable Great Person (Mahan Purusha).

From that pure and qualified reality arise Prakriti (nature) and the Mahat (cosmic intelligence). From that arises the cosmic ego (Ahamkara) along with the five subtle elements (Tanmatras). From those arise the five great cosmic elements: earth, water, fire, air, and space. From the earth arise all plants and herbs; from plants comes food; from food comes the vital seed; from that arises the living being; and from that emerges everything—verily everything, the entire universe. All created beings are born; the gods indeed are born; and through Him they live and breathe. The gods live by Him, the sacred sacrifices are sustained by Him, everything lives and moves by Him. That Ganesha is the innermost Self to be known and realized. Such is the secret teaching of the Upanishad. He who knows this becomes the foremost of all, so declares the sage Yajnavalkya—verily, so declares Yajnavalkya.

Thus ends the Second Upanishad of the Ganesha Uttara Tapini Upanishad.

Third Upanishad

Om. Verily, Ganesha is Brahman; thus should one know. Whatever exists—all that has been, all that is to come, and all that is currently being born—they declare all this to be Him alone. There is nothing whatsoever beyond or higher than Him. Whoever truly knows this knows Brahman and attains Brahman alone; all this is proclaimed to be His manifestation. He is declared to be the Sovereign Lord presiding over all celestial hosts headed by Brahma and Vishnu; that is Ganesha. He is declared to be the Transcendent Supreme, whom neither the earth, nor radiant celestial powers, nor all gathered beings can fully encompass or comprehend. Speech along with the mind cannot reach Him; neither fire, nor earth, nor light, nor air, nor cosmic space, nor water can grasp Him. He is beyond the senses, beyond the physical body, beyond name, and beyond form. He is neither white, nor red, nor yellow, nor black. He is neither the waking state, nor the dream state, nor deep sleep, nor even the conditioned notion of the Fourth state. That Supreme Reality is pristine, ever ungraspable, unknowable, and beyond all conceptual divisions. Endowed with inherent divine power (Shakti), bearing an elephant's face and an elephant's majestic form, He encompasses and pervades the entire universe.

Encompassing the celestial heavens with infinite heads, the cardinal directions with infinite arms, the cosmic sky with an infinite belly, and the earth with infinite feet, He abides pervading both the external and internal realms through His own supreme radiance—so it is declared. That verily is the Supreme Brahman; thus do the wise contemplate the innermost Self as Ganesha. In the beginning, He looked all around on every side and beheld nothing other than Himself. Then arose the realization, "I am He" (So'ham). Thinking, "Solitude is not fitting," He generated the three cosmic qualities (Gunas): from His navel arose Rajas, which became Brahma; from His face arose Sattva, which became Vishnu; and from His eye arose Tamas, which became Hara (Shiva). He instructed Brahma: "O Brahma, bring forth creation." Brahma replied: "I do not know how." Ganesha said: "Behold within My cosmic body the entire universe situated within, and fashion creation in that very likeness."

Thereupon Brahma, through the divine portal of origin, beheld the universe situated within the Lord's cosmic body: the oceans, rivers, mountains, forests, earth, heaven, netherworld, humans, domestic animals, wild beasts, serpents, horses, herds of cattle, the sun and moon, constellations, fires, winds, and directions; and from that vision, he brought forth the creation. Then Brahma thought within himself: "There is nothing higher than me; I alone am the sovereign Lord of all." No sooner did he entertain this thought than terrifying, ferocious beings arose. Possessing colossal bodies, licking the earth with their tongues, filling the sky with their fangs, and making thunderous roars, they rushed forward intent on slaying Brahma. Seeing them, Brahma was terrified and remembered the Supreme Lord. Thereupon, before his eyes, he beheld the Blissful One, radiant like ten million suns, bearing an elephant's countenance. He then praised the Lord of Hosts: "You are the creator of mountains, rivers, oceans, and all moving and unmoving beings. There is nothing higher than You, O Lord of the universe! You are the creator of all the cosmos, its sustainer, and the cause of its dissolution. You abide completely pervading this entire universe." Having been thus praised, the Lord spoke to Brahma: "O Brahma, perform tapas, perform tapas!" Having spoken, He vanished, and Brahma engaged in deep austerity.

After some time had passed while Brahma remained absorbed in tapas, the Lord manifested before him and said: "I am well-pleased, I am well-pleased! Choose your boons." Hearing these words, Brahma opened his eyes and, looking ahead, beheld Ganesha. He offered praise: "You are Brahma, You are Vishnu, You are Hara, You are Prajapati, You are Indra, You are Surya, You are Soma, You are Ganesha. All the moving and unmoving creation is pervaded by You; without You, nothing exists." Then Ganesha said: "You and I are verily not different. Bring forth creation, O Prajapati! Receive the power bestowed by Me for the task of creating the cosmos." Receiving that divine Shakti, Brahma's cosmic creation unfolded: the Brahmana was born from His mouth, the Kshatriya from His arms, the Vaishya from His thighs, and the Shudra from His feet; the Sun was born from His eye, the Moon from His mind, Agni from His mouth, Vayu from His breath, cosmic space from His navel, heaven from His head, the earth from His feet, and the cardinal directions from His ears. In this manner, he fashioned all the worlds.

Then the Lord spoke to the embodiment of Sattva: "You are verily Vishnu; protect, protect the entire universe." Vishnu replied: "I do not possess the power." The Lord said: "Receive this sacred wisdom (Vidya)." Taking that divine wisdom, Sattva (Vishnu) sustained and protected the cosmos. The Lord then spoke to Hara: "Perform dissolution, O Hara. By drawing back and resolving the universe, be known as Hara." But Hara thought to himself: "There is nothing higher than me; I am the primordial origin of the universe, I am Hara"—and harbored pride. No sooner had he done so than the sky was filled with elephant-faced apparitions uttering deafening roars, poised to destroy Hara. Seeing them, Hara wept aloud; from that weeping (rodana), he came to be known as Rudra. Remembering that Supreme Person, he offered praise: "You are Brahma, You are the Supreme Doer, You are the Primal Cause; You create, protect, and dissolve the worlds. You are the support of the universe, yet You are unsupported, uncontainable, indescribable, beyond logical speculation; pervading all this, You abide!" Through this hymn of praise, he beheld Vinayaka and bowed before Him. Ganesha said: "Perform dissolution, O Hara." Thereupon, Rudra became the cosmic dissolver. Whoever realizes this truth becomes Ganesha Himself. Such is the secret teaching of the Upanishad.

Thus ends the Third Upanishad of the Ganesha Uttara Tapini Upanishad.

Fourth Upanishad

Om. Verily, Ganesha manifested as Pure Being (Sat); That is verily the Supreme Brahman. The knower of That attains the Supreme. Regarding this, it is declared in the sacred verse: That which is beginningless, infinite in form, the embodiment of pure consciousness, whom all the gods worship as the Eldest Brahman—for Him there is neither physical body nor sensory instruments, nor is there seen anyone equal to Him or superior to Him. From fear of Him the Sun rises; from fear of Him the Wind blows; from fear of Him Fire burns. That is the nature of pure Consciousness, changeless and non-dual. That very reality is declared to have become variegated through Maya. Through this, as the primal unmanifest state arose, there sounded the sacred resonance Om. That sound-form verily assumed the shape of an elephant. That ineffable Maya is proclaimed to be the seed of the universe. She alone is known as Prakriti, as Ganesha, as Pradhana, and as the reality qualified by Maya. From this arose the cosmic intelligence (Mahat-tattva). Then, with the tip of His trunk, He created the cosmic ego (Ahamkara). That ego is threefold: Sattvika, Rajasa, and Tamasa. The Sattvika aspect is the power of knowledge (Jnana-shakti); the Rajasa aspect is the power of action (Kriya-shakti); and the Tamasa aspect is the power of materialization (Dravya-shakti). From the Tamasic aspect arose the five subtle elements (Tanmatras) and the five gross elements. From the Rajasic aspect arose the five organs of perception, the five organs of action, and the five vital breaths. From the Sattvic aspect arose the presiding deities of the cognitive senses—the Directions, the Wind, the Sun, Varuna, and the Ashvins—along with the presiding deities of the active organs—Fire, Indra, Vishnu, Prajapati, and Mitra. This is the cosmic manifestation of the Primordial Person (Adi-Purusha). This is verily declared to be the subtle body (Sukshma-sharira) of the Supreme Self.

Now follows the second manifestation: taking the five subtle elements and subtle proto-elements, through the process of quintuplication (Panchikarana), the five gross great elements were produced. At the dawn of the cosmic cycle, upon the division of the elements, the entry of pure Consciousness into them gave rise to the cosmic sense of self-awareness (Aham-abhimana). Therefore, You are addressed as the Primordial Ganesha (Adi-Ganesha). Then, from the elements, the fourteen cosmic worlds were brought forth. The vast multitudes of living beings residing within them along with their gross physical bodies are called the Cosmic Physical Person (Virat). Such is the second manifestation. Brahma is endowed with Rajas, Vishnu with Sattva, and Hara with Tamas. Coming together, the triad said to one another, "I alone am the Sovereign Lord of all." Unable to bear each other's claims, they ascended into the heights, but there they saw nothing. Wandering downward through the ten cardinal directions, they still beheld nothing. Thereupon, they sat absorbed in deep meditation.

Within the cave of their hearts, they beheld the Great Person, elephant-faced, endowed with countless heads, countless feet, and infinite hands, pervading all the worlds with His dazzling radiance. Brahman was His crown, the cardinal directions His ears, the cosmic egg His temples, the ether of consciousness His palate, and the realm of truth His origin. The opening and closing of His eyes marked the creation and dissolution of the universe. The Moon, Sun, and Fire were His three eyes; the King of Mountains His majestic tusk; merit and demerit His upper and lower lips; the planets and stellar constellations His teeth; Sarasvati His tongue; Indra His trunk; the sacred ancestral lineages His shoulders; Soma His neck; Hara His hair; the holy rivers and streams His arms; cosmic serpents His fingers; the constellations His nails; Sri His heart; the ether of cosmic desire His navel; the great oceans His belly; the earth His waist; creation itself His procreative organ; the great mountains His thighs; the Ashvins His knees; while within His belly dwelt the Yakshas, Gandharvas, Rakshasas, Kinnaras, and humans. The netherworlds formed His shanks, the holy sages His feet, Time His big toe, and the vast cluster of stars His tail. Beholding Him, they offered praise: "From You indeed all these beings are born; from You originate fire, earth, water, light, and air; from the tip of Your trunk Brahma, Vishnu, and Rudra were born; from You arise the oceans, rivers, mountains, and all moving and unmoving existence!" Pleased with their hymn of adoration, the Lord spoke: "I am the Sovereign Lord of all. From Me originate all beings; from Me emerges all that moves and moves not. You are verily not different from Me, for you are indeed My own cosmic attributes—of this there is no doubt. Contemplating Me in your hearts as Ganesha, the Lord of the Gunas (Gunesha), do you, O Rajasa Brahma, create the world; do you, O Sattvika Vishnu, sustain it; and do you, O Tamasa Hara, dissolve it!" Having spoken thus, He vanished. He is verily Ganesha. He is to be known as the Universal Soul, the one Supreme Self of all the deities. Whoever realizes this truth becomes Ganesha Himself. Such is the secret teaching of the Upanishad.

Thus ends the Fourth Upanishad of the Ganesha Uttara Tapini Upanishad.

Fifth Upanishad

Om. The gods verily spoke to Rudra: "How is His worship performed?" Rudra replied: "The seer (Rishi) is Ganaka, the meter is Nicrid-Gayatri, and the presiding Deity is Sri Ganapati. He who knows the auxiliary limbs (angas) of this king of mantras (Mantra-raja)—which, due to essential non-difference, is identical in nature with the sacred syllable Om (Pranava) and the Supreme Self—he truly knows, and he attains immortality. Whoever studies this crosses beyond all limitations. He who daily chants this all-bestowing Mantra-raja of Ganapati immobilizes fire, immobilizes water, immobilizes the wind, immobilizes the sun, immobilizes all the gods, neutralizes poison, and halts all afflictions and disturbances. Such is the secret teaching."

Whoever recites this Mantra-raja daily draws and subdues all obstacles, gods, Yakshas, diseases, planetary afflictions, and human beings. He conquers the earthly realm (Bhurloka), he conquers the atmospheric realm (Bhuvarloka), the heavenly realm (Svarloka), Maharloka, Janaloka, Tapoloka, and Satyaloka—he conquers all seven worlds, verily he conquers all realms. He attains the merit of having sacrificed with the Agnishtoma, the Atyagnishtoma, the Ukthya, the Shodashi, the Vajapeya, the Atiratra, the Aptoryama, and indeed all sacred Vedic rites.

Whoever recites this sovereign Mantra of the Lord of Obstacles daily is deemed to have studied the Rig verses, the Yajus formulas, the Saman chants, the Atharvana and Angirasa hymns, all Vedic recensions (shakhas), the Puranas, the Kalpa sutras, the sacred songs, the eulogies of ancient heroes (Narashamsis), and the sacred Pranava. Whoever knows this sovereign Mantra of Ganesha knows everything—verily, he knows all things. He becomes equal to the Vedas, equal to the sages, equal to the divine serpents, equal to the Sun, and equal to Agni.

The spiritual merit of one hundred and one initiated students, one hundred and one householders, and one hundred and one forest-dwelling hermits equals the merit of a single chanter of Sri Rudram. The merit of one hundred and one ascetics (yatis) equals that of a teacher of the Atharvashiras and Atharvashikha Upanishads. The merit of one hundred and one chanters of Sri Rudram and one hundred and one teachers of the Atharvashiras and Atharvashikha Upanishads equals the spiritual fruit of a single teacher of this Ganesha Tapaniya Upanishad.

For the practitioner of this sovereign Mantra-raja, there is that supreme realm where neither the sun nor the moon shines, where the wind blows not and the stellar constellations do not gleam, where fire does not burn and death cannot enter, where there is neither delusion nor sorrow, but ever-abiding bliss, transcendent delight, absolute equanimity, eternal reality, Sadashiva, worshipped by Brahma and all the gods, the supreme state meditated upon by yogins, pure Consciousness alone, Brahmanaspati, the one single Imperishable Syllable. Having internalized this Supreme Self within the cave of the heart after practicing outward contemplation, and having performed japa for a brief time, there remains no further need for chanting, nor rosary beads, nor ritual seat, nor meditation, nor ceremonial invocation. This Self, having directly manifested as Brahman within oneself, is realized as: "I am That Self" (So'ham Atma), endowed with four quarters.

Experiencing gross objects through external awareness is the waking state; the inward-knowing dreamer who experiences subtle objects is Taijasa; that state wherein one asleep desires no desire whatsoever and sees no dream is deep sleep (sushupti); unified therein as a concentrated mass of pure consciousness, experiencing supreme bliss, whose doorway is pure awareness, He is Prajna. He is the Sovereign Lord of all, the Inner Controller of all, the womb and source of all beings. Neither conscious of the external world, nor conscious of the internal, nor conscious of both, nor a mass of cognition; indescribable, beyond empirical interaction, ungraspable, without physical characteristics, unthinkable, the essence of the conviction of the single Self, the dissolution of phenomenal existence, peaceful, auspicious, and non-dual—meditating thus upon this four-quartered reality, one becomes that very Self.

That Self is to be known: ever-luminous, devoid of ignorance and its effects, free from individual bondage, free from duality, completely dispelling the darkness of ignorance, delusion, and egoity. Realizing with deep conviction, "I alone am everything, the Primal Sovereign," one delights, delights, delights, and ever delights in the Supreme Brahman—the immortal, radiant, supreme light of the Lord of Obstacles, full of eternal bliss, self-luminous, ever-risen, eternal, pure, liberated, the all-knowing Sovereign Lord. Whoever thus realizes the Ganesha Tapaniya Upanishad crosses beyond worldly transmigration, crosses beyond terrifying perils, crosses beyond sorrow, crosses beyond all obstacles, and transcends great calamities. He becomes Bliss itself, he becomes eternal, he becomes pure, he becomes liberated, he becomes self-effulgent, he becomes the Supreme Lord, he becomes the foremost, he becomes Vaishvanara, he becomes Taijasa, he becomes Prajna, he becomes the Witness (Sakshi), he becomes That verily, he becomes All—verily, he becomes All. Such is the sacred Upanishad. Om, may He protect us together.

Thus ends the Fifth Upanishad of the Ganesha Uttara Tapini Upanishad.

Sixth Upanishad

Om. Now the divine Goddess Gauri said verily unto Rudra: "Declare unto me the ritual procedure of practice (anushthana-vidhi) for this King of Mantras." Rudra spoke: "Having obtained the grace of the Guru and the Deity, offering mental flowers, one begins with the expulsion of obstructive spirits (bhuta-utsarana), assuming the firm meditative posture (asana-bandha), performing self-protection (atma-raksha), observant discipline, elemental purification (bhuta-shuddhi), infusing the vital breath (prana-pratishtha), repetition of the Pranava, adoration of the Mother energies, internal placement of sacred sounds (antar-matrika), and inner spiritual sacrifice (antar-yaga). Here, some practitioners proceed according to the foundational Vedic rites accompanied by mantras, performing the invocation, dedication, and offering externally; otherwise, installing the precious auspicious conch upon a tripod worshipped with sandal-paste and perfumes, one arranges the ritual vessels starting from the right—the vessels for foot-washing (padya), water offering (arghya), sipping water (achamana), honey mixture (madhuparka), secondary sipping (punar-achamana), and food offerings (nivedana)—placed in their respective positions according to sacred injunction on the fourth lunar tithi (Chaturthi) or festival occasions, cleansing them before offering. Touching the five nectar vessels (panchamrita) and the main offering vessel with the root mantra, sprinkling oneself, the vessels, and all ritual articles with the consecrated Arghya water, purifying the hands with the sacred water, regulating the breath with the root mantra, and recollecting the prescribed Rishi, meter, Deity, and ritual application (viniyoga).

In daily practice, japa is an integral part of worship and worship is an integral part of japa. Having performed the pervasive placement (nyasa) from the thumbs across all fingers, along with the eight-limbed, staff (dandi), and head (mundi) nyasas, beholding one's inner countenance and contemplating oneself as the very embodiment of the Divine, placing a flower upon the crown of the head, worshipping the sacred pedestal (pitha), offering the seat, and meditating upon the Deity within the heart-lotus, yogins perform japa here. Invoking the Lord from the lotus of one's own heart, displaying the divine gestures (mudras), offering the internal seal for total communion with the Deity, presenting the ritual vessels with the root mantra, combining the five nectars in the vessel, bathing (abhisheka) the Lord with them five times or once, pleasing Him with daily libations, and bathing Him with the root mantra alongside hymn recitations, the Purusha Sukta, Sri Rudram, and peace chants; dedicating complete worship, waving the sacred light three times in a clockwise direction, offering the grand food presentation (maha-naivedya), and visualizing the withdrawal into the pedestal enclosures.

After offering betel leaves (tambula), repeating the root mantra briefly, offering incense, lamps, and the three kinds of sacred foods once more, one displays the mudras for all sixteen services, reciting: 'Salutations, this is the seat (Idam asanam namah)'; 'Salutations, this is water for the feet (Padyam namah)'; 'This is Arghya, Svaha (Esho'rghyah svaha)' into the right hand; 'This is Svadha' in front for the three offerings; 'Salutations' in the mouth for sacred baths; 'This is sacred fragrance, salutations (Esha gandho namah)'; 'Salutations with unbroken rice grains (Akshateshu namah)'; 'Om, flowers, salutations (Om pushpani namah)'; 'This incense and lamp, salutations (Esha dhupo dipo namah)'; 'I surrender (Samarpayami)' for the food offerings, fruits, and betel leaves; 'I dedicate, salutations (Nivedayami namah)' for gold and dakshina; and 'This floral tribute, salutations (Esha pushpanjalirnamah)' for the offering of garlands. Whoever knows this supreme, ineffable secret seed-syllable knows all—verily, he knows all.

Whether one chants while contemplating the meaning of the sacred syllables, the meaning of the mantra, or focusing upon the attendant deities of the pedestal, such a practitioner truly chants. Having secured a suitable seat—soft, made of clean washed cloth, safflower-dyed, madder-dyed, red wool, or skins of dappled deer or tiger—or seated upon other recommended mats, one chants using rosaries made of crystal, lotus seeds, bhadraksha, precious gems, pearls, coral, rudraksha, or knotted kusha grass; he who chants thus truly chants. The kusha rosary requires daily purification, smearing with sandalwood paste, consecration with incense, separate energizing with the five Sadyojata mantras, infusion of life-force, vitality, and libation, kept concealed and touched with the left hand at its base, never exposed to ordinary sight.

Thus in the month of Shravana with the sacred thread (pavitra), in Chaitra with Damana leaves and rosaries, on the Great Ninth (Mahanavami), on Magha Chaturthi with sesame sweets (tila-ladduka), on Saptami with cooling sandalwood paste, on Shivaratri with garlands of Bilva leaves, and on other great sacred occasions, they worship Him with immense devotion. One offers oblations into the sacred fire with modakas, flattened rice, puffed rice, roasted barley flour, plantains, sugar cane, coconuts, sweet cakes (apupas), and other prescribed offerings. Japa is performed facing east, and fire oblations are offered with profound devotion. Whoever performs this attains immortality, achieves enduring stability, attains liberation, enjoys worldly fulfillment, speaks words of truth and power, and attains renown. He who knows this secret truly knows; he who studies this truly studies. He becomes Bliss, he becomes Eternal, he becomes Immaculate, he becomes Liberated, he becomes Effulgent, he becomes Compassionate, he becomes endowed with Knowledge, endowed with Bliss, endowed with Supreme Wisdom, the very Bliss of Transcendent Realization; he attains immortality—verily, he attains immortality.

Om, may He protect us together. Peace.

Thus ends the Sixth Upanishad of the Ganesha Uttara Tapini Upanishad.

Thus concludes the Ganesha Tapini Upanishad belonging to the Atharva Veda.
